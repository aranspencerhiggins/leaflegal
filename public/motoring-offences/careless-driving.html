Careless driving in brief

Careless driving, or driving without due care and attention, carries 3 to 9 points or a discretionary ban. The outcome often depends on the evidence of how the driving fell below the standard expected.

A careless driving solicitor can test the evidence and advise on your plea. Leaflegal introduces you to one regulated motoring firm.

Careless driving explained

Pick a topic to read more.

Careless driving, sometimes called driving without due care and attention, is one of the most common motoring charges heard in the magistrates' courts of England and Wales. It is set out in section 3 of the Road Traffic Act 1988. The same section also covers inconsiderate driving, which means driving without reasonable consideration for other road users.

Many careless driving cases begin with a collision. A driver pulls out of a junction, misjudges a gap, clips a parked car or runs into the back of the car in front. Others come from something a police officer saw, such as lane hogging on a motorway, tailgating, using a phone in a way that is not covered by the specific phone offence, or overtaking on a bend. Some start with a complaint from another driver who has dashcam footage.

The charge does not require the police to prove that you meant to drive badly or that you knew you were doing so. The question is whether your driving fell below the standard of a competent and careful driver. That is an objective test. It looks at the driving itself rather than at how experienced you are or how you felt at the time.

Because the test is about a standard, two drivers can see the same event very differently. One might say a manoeuvre was a moment of bad luck on a wet road. The police might say it was a clear lapse of attention. Much of a solicitor's work in these cases is looking closely at the evidence to see which account it really supports.

  • The driver already has points and is worried about a ban
  • The driver passed their test recently and fears losing their licence
  • The driver needs a licence for work or caring responsibilities
  • The police have offered a course or fixed penalty and the driver is unsure what it means

It helps to understand what the prosecution has to show, because it explains why the evidence matters so much in careless driving cases.

Driving below the standard

The prosecution must prove that you were driving a mechanically propelled vehicle on a road or other public place, and that your driving fell below what would be expected of a competent and careful driver. The law treats breaking certain rules in the Highway Code as something the court can take into account, but a breach of the Highway Code does not automatically mean the driving was careless.

Inconsiderate driving

Inconsiderate driving is a separate way of committing the same offence. It applies where other people were actually inconvenienced by the driving, for example by driving through a puddle to splash pedestrians, flashing lights to force another driver out of the way, or driving too close behind to pressure someone. The penalties are the same.

Careless or dangerous

Careless driving is less serious than dangerous driving. Dangerous driving means driving that falls far below the expected standard where it would be obvious to a careful driver that driving that way was dangerous. Sometimes a driver is charged with dangerous driving and the case ends with a conviction for careless driving instead, because the court decides the driving was poor but not far below the standard.

When someone is hurt or killed

Where careless driving causes the death of another person, or serious injury, separate and more serious offences may apply. Those cases are handled very differently, can be heard in the Crown Court and can carry prison sentences. If anyone was badly hurt in your accident, tell the firm at the start so that the right specialist deals with it.

Related charges

Careless driving is often charged alongside other offences arising from the same incident. Examples include failing to stop after an accident, failing to report an accident, or driving without the right licence or insurance. Each charge has its own rules and penalties, and a solicitor will look at all of them together.

Most careless driving cases follow a similar route through the courts. The steps below give a general picture. A solicitor will explain exactly what applies to your case.

1. Notice of intended prosecution

For careless driving, the police usually have to warn the driver that a prosecution is being considered. This is done by a verbal warning at the scene, or by a notice of intended prosecution sent to the registered keeper within 14 days. There is an exception where an accident happened and the driver was aware of it at the time, which is why many drivers involved in a collision receive no notice at all.

2. Interview or written questions

Some drivers are asked to attend a voluntary interview under caution at a police station. Others are sent written questions. Anything said can be used as evidence. Free legal advice is available at the police station, and many people speak to a solicitor before answering questions in writing.

3. Course, fixed penalty or court

In some cases the police offer a driver awareness course or a fixed penalty instead of a prosecution. Whether these are offered depends on the circumstances and local policy, and there is no right to them. If neither is offered or the driver does not accept, the case goes to court.

4. The summons or requisition

The case usually starts with a postal requisition or summons telling you the date of your first hearing at the magistrates' court. Before that hearing the prosecution should provide initial details of its case, often including a summary of the evidence and witness statements.

5. First hearing and plea

At the first hearing you are asked whether you plead guilty or not guilty. If you plead guilty, the court will usually sentence you that day or adjourn for more information. If you plead not guilty, the court sets a trial date and gives directions about evidence and witnesses.

6. Trial

Careless driving trials take place in the magistrates' court before a bench of magistrates or a district judge. Witnesses give evidence and can be cross examined. You can give evidence yourself. The court decides whether the prosecution has proved the case beyond reasonable doubt.

7. Sentence and appeal

If you are convicted, the court decides the penalty. You can appeal to the Crown Court against conviction or sentence, but there are strict time limits, so speak to a solicitor quickly if you are thinking about this.

Careless driving carries a range of penalties. The court looks at how serious the driving was, what harm it caused or risked, and any personal circumstances you put forward.

Fines

A fine is the most common penalty. The court uses sentencing guidelines that relate the fine to the seriousness of the driving and to your income. You will also usually be ordered to pay prosecution costs and a victim surcharge.

Penalty points

If the court does not disqualify you, it must endorse your licence with between 3 and 9 penalty points. The number depends on how bad the driving was. Points usually stay on your driving record for four years, and count towards a totting up ban for three years from the date of the offence.

Disqualification

The court can disqualify you from driving for careless driving, although it does not have to. A ban is more likely where the driving was at the more serious end of the scale. The court can also order you to take an extended driving test before getting your licence back.

Totting up

If the new points bring your total to 12 or more within three years, the court must usually disqualify you for at least six months. You can ask the court not to ban you, or to ban you for less, on the grounds of exceptional hardship. This needs evidence, and the hardship usually has to fall on other people as well as you.

New drivers

If you passed your test within the last two years and reach six or more points, the DVLA will revoke your licence. You would then need to apply for a provisional licence and pass the theory and practical tests again. Courts cannot use exceptional hardship to stop this, which is why the number of points matters so much to new drivers.

Other orders

If damage or injury was caused, the court can sometimes make a compensation order, although many cases leave compensation to the insurers. A conviction will also need to be declared to your motor insurer, and many people find their premiums rise for several years. Some employers and professional bodies require drivers to report motoring convictions, so check any contract or code of conduct that applies to you.

There are two kinds of cost in a careless driving case. One is what the court may order you to pay if you are convicted. The other is what you pay your own solicitor.

Court costs

If you are convicted, the court may order you to pay a fine, a contribution to prosecution costs and a victim surcharge. The amounts depend on the case and change over time. If you are acquitted after paying privately for your defence, you may be able to recover some of your legal costs from public funds, but usually only at set rates that are often lower than what you paid.

Legal aid

Legal advice at the police station is free for everyone. Legal aid for court proceedings depends on your means and on whether the interests of justice require it. For careless driving, legal aid is often not available because there is no real risk of prison, so most people pay privately.

Solicitor fees

Many motoring firms offer fixed fees for different stages, such as advice before the first hearing, a guilty plea with mitigation, an exceptional hardship argument or a full trial. A trial with witnesses and expert evidence will cost more than a straightforward plea. Ask the firm to explain exactly what is included, whether a barrister may be needed and how any extra work would be charged.

Every case is different, and only a solicitor who has seen the evidence can tell you which route makes sense for you. These are the general options people in this position usually talk through with their solicitor.

Pleading not guilty

If you believe your driving was not careless, you can plead not guilty and the prosecution must prove its case. Evidence such as dashcam footage, photographs of the scene, independent witnesses, vehicle data or road layout can all be relevant. A solicitor can tell you how strong the evidence looks and whether an expert report might help.

Pleading guilty

If you accept that your driving fell below the standard, a guilty plea usually earns a reduction in the sentence, and the earlier the plea the bigger the reduction. A solicitor can still help by presenting mitigation, which is information about you and the incident that may reduce the penalty.

Arguing about the facts

Sometimes a driver accepts they were careless but disputes part of what the prosecution says, such as their speed or how long they were distracted. This can affect the number of points. The court may hold a hearing to decide the disputed facts.

Avoiding a totting up ban

Where the points would lead to a ban, a solicitor can prepare an exceptional hardship application. This involves gathering evidence about your work, family and other people who depend on you being able to drive.

Acting early

Evidence can disappear quickly. Dashcam files are overwritten, CCTV is deleted and witnesses forget. Getting advice soon after the incident gives a solicitor the best chance of securing what is needed.

Keeping your employer informed

If you drive for work, your contract may require you to tell your employer about a prosecution. Your solicitor can talk you through how the possible outcomes might affect your job and what evidence about your work could be useful to the court.

Careless driving questions and answers

Will I get points or a ban?
It depends on the circumstances. Points are common, and a ban is possible.
Is dashcam footage useful?
It can be. Keep it safe and tell your solicitor.
Can I defend the charge?
A solicitor will tell you whether there is a defence after reviewing the evidence.
Which areas do you cover?
Motoring offences dealt with in the courts of England and Wales.
Is there legal aid for motoring offences?
Usually not, unless you are at risk of prison. We only introduce people looking for paid legal help.
How quickly will someone contact me?
The firm aims to contact you quickly, especially if you have a court date coming up. Please give a mobile number you can answer.
What does careless driving actually mean?
It means driving that falls below the standard expected of a competent and careful driver. The court does not need to find that you were reckless or that you knew you were driving badly. It looks at what a careful driver would have done in the same situation and compares that with what you did. It is a summary offence, which means it is dealt with in the magistrates' court.
Is inconsiderate driving the same offence?
It is part of the same section of the law and carries the same penalties. Inconsiderate driving is where your driving inconvenienced other people, such as pedestrians, cyclists or other drivers. Typical examples include driving through a puddle and soaking people at a bus stop, or tailgating to force another driver to move over. The prosecution has to show that someone was actually inconvenienced.
Can I be charged if nobody was hurt?
Yes. Careless driving does not require anyone to be injured or any damage to be caused. A police officer who sees poor driving can report the driver even where nothing happened as a result. That said, the harm caused or risked is relevant to how serious the court considers the offence to be, which affects the penalty.
The other driver was also at fault. Does that help me?
It may be relevant. The question for the court is whether your driving fell below the standard, not whether someone else was more to blame. However, the other driver's actions can explain why an incident happened and may show that your driving was reasonable in the circumstances. A solicitor will look at the evidence of both drivers' actions.
What if I was charged with dangerous driving instead?
Dangerous driving is a more serious offence with a compulsory ban. If you have been charged with it, the court can convict you of careless driving instead if it decides the driving was poor but not far below the standard. A solicitor dealing with a dangerous driving charge will usually consider whether this is a realistic outcome.
How long do the police have to bring a case?
There are time limits for starting a prosecution for careless driving, and there are rules about warning the driver within 14 days unless an accident happened that the driver knew about. Whether these rules were followed depends on the facts. If you think the case has been brought late or you were never warned, ask a solicitor to check.
What is a notice of intended prosecution?
It is a formal warning that the police are considering prosecuting a driver for certain offences, including careless driving. It is usually sent to the registered keeper within 14 days of the incident, or given verbally at the scene. Where there was an accident that the driver was aware of, no notice is usually needed. The notice is not a charge, and receiving one does not mean a case will definitely follow.
Can the police offer a course instead of court?
Sometimes. For less serious careless driving, the police may offer a driver awareness course or a fixed penalty with points. These are offered at the discretion of the police and depend on the circumstances, your history and local policy. There is no right to be offered one. If you are offered a course, the paperwork will explain the conditions and the deadline for accepting it.
Do I have to go to court?
For careless driving you will usually be asked to attend the first hearing. If you are pleading not guilty, you will need to attend the trial. Your solicitor can tell you whether you need to be there for other hearings. If the court decides to disqualify you, it may want you present. Not attending when you are required to can lead to a warrant.
What happens at the first hearing?
You confirm your name and details and are asked to enter a plea. If you plead guilty, the court will hear the facts from the prosecution and any mitigation, then decide the sentence. If you plead not guilty, the court will fix a trial date and decide what evidence and witnesses are needed. A solicitor can attend with you or on your behalf for parts of the process.
Who decides the case?
Careless driving is decided in the magistrates' court. The case will be heard either by a bench of usually three lay magistrates, who are trained volunteers, or by a district judge, who is a legally qualified judge sitting alone. They are advised on the law by a legal adviser. There is no jury.
What evidence will the prosecution use?
It depends on the case. Common evidence includes statements from the other driver and witnesses, police officer accounts, photographs, dashcam or CCTV footage, collision investigation reports and anything you said in interview or in writing. The prosecution should provide initial details of its case before the first hearing, and your solicitor will review it.
Can I give my side of the story?
Yes. If you plead not guilty, you can give evidence at your trial and be questioned by the prosecution. You can also call your own witnesses. Your solicitor will advise you on whether giving evidence is likely to help and will prepare you for what to expect.
Can I appeal if I am convicted?
You can appeal to the Crown Court against your conviction, your sentence or both. The time limit for lodging an appeal is short, usually 21 days from sentence. In some cases you can also ask the magistrates to reopen the case. A solicitor can advise on whether an appeal has reasonable prospects.
What is mitigation?
Mitigation is information put before the court to explain the circumstances of the offence and your personal situation, with the aim of reducing the sentence. It might cover your previous driving record, why the incident happened, any remorse you have shown, and how a ban or high fine would affect you and others. A solicitor knows what courts tend to find relevant and how to present it clearly.
How many points will I get?
If you are not disqualified, the court must give between 3 and 9 points. The number depends on how serious the court considers the driving to be, following sentencing guidelines. Minor lapses tend to be at the lower end, while more prolonged or risky driving attracts more points. A solicitor can explain where your case might fall.
Could I be banned from driving?
Yes. Careless driving does not carry a compulsory ban, but the court can disqualify you if it thinks the driving was serious enough. You can also face a ban if the points you receive take you to 12 or more within three years. A solicitor can tell you how likely a ban is and what can be argued.
I passed my test recently. What happens?
If you are within two years of passing your test and your total reaches six or more points, the DVLA will revoke your licence. You would have to go back to a provisional licence and pass the theory and practical tests again. Points from before you passed can count too. This makes the number of points especially important for new drivers.
What is exceptional hardship?
If the new points would take you to 12 or more, the court must normally disqualify you for at least six months. You can ask the court not to do this because it would cause exceptional hardship. Ordinary inconvenience such as losing a job is often not enough on its own. The court looks closely at the effect on other people, such as employees, family members or people you care for.
How long will the points stay on my licence?
Points for careless driving usually stay on your driving record for four years from the date of the offence. They count towards a totting up ban for three years. Insurers often ask about convictions for several years, so the effect on your premiums can last a while.
Will I have to retake my test?
The court can order you to take an extended driving test if it disqualifies you, although this is not automatic for careless driving. New drivers whose licence is revoked under the new driver rules must also retake the standard tests. A solicitor can explain whether a retest is a realistic possibility in your case.
Can I get legal aid?
Advice at the police station is free for everyone. For court, legal aid depends on a means test and on whether the interests of justice require it. Careless driving rarely carries a risk of prison, so legal aid is often refused. Most people instructing a solicitor for careless driving pay privately. The firm can explain what funding might be available to you.
How do solicitors charge for careless driving cases?
Many firms offer fixed fees for separate stages, such as advising before plea, representing you on a guilty plea, preparing an exceptional hardship application or conducting a trial. Trials cost more because of the preparation, witness work and time at court. Ask for a written breakdown of what is included before you agree to anything.
Will I have to pay prosecution costs?
If you are convicted, the court will usually order you to contribute to the prosecution's costs and to pay a victim surcharge as well as any fine. The amounts vary from case to case. If you are acquitted, you will not pay prosecution costs, and you may be able to claim back some of your own legal costs.
Does my motor insurance cover legal costs?
Some motor policies include legal expenses cover that may help with defending a motoring prosecution, though many exclude it or limit it. Check your policy documents and any separate legal expenses policy. If you have cover, the insurer may have its own panel of firms, so read the terms carefully.
Should I answer the police questions in writing?
Anything you say can be used as evidence. Some drivers answer written questions or attend a voluntary interview without advice and later wish they had spoken to a solicitor first. Leaflegal cannot tell you what to do. The firm we introduce you to can look at the questions and explain the possible consequences of how you respond.
What should I keep after an accident?
Keep any dashcam footage somewhere safe, along with photographs, the names of witnesses, the other driver's details and any letters from the police or insurers. Write down what happened while you still remember it clearly. Note the weather, road layout and lighting. Your solicitor will tell you what is useful.
Will the solicitor speak to my insurer?
Your insurer deals with the civil claim for the damage, while the solicitor deals with the criminal case. The two are separate, but what happens in one can affect the other. Tell your solicitor about any insurance claim and tell your insurer about the prosecution. Your solicitor can explain how the two interact.
What will the solicitor ask me?
Expect questions about the incident, the route, the conditions, your vehicle, any passengers and anything you said at the scene. They will ask about your driving record, how many points you have and how much you rely on driving. Having your court papers and any police letters to hand will help.

No. Leaflegal is an introduction service and does not give legal advice. We pass your enquiry to one regulated law firm that handles motoring cases. Any advice comes from that firm, and you are free to decide whether to instruct them.
What is the difference between dangerous and careless driving?
Careless driving is driving below the standard of a competent and careful driver. Dangerous driving is driving far below that standard, where it would be obvious to a careful driver that the driving was dangerous. The difference is one of degree, and many contested cases turn on which side of that line the driving falls. The penalties for dangerous driving are much heavier.
Can I be convicted if I did not think I was driving dangerously?
Yes. The test is objective. It asks what would have been obvious to a competent and careful driver, not what you believed at the time. However, the circumstances you were in, such as the road, the weather and what other drivers were doing, are part of the picture the court looks at.
Can a vehicle defect lead to a dangerous driving charge?
It can. If it would have been obvious to a competent and careful driver that driving the vehicle in its condition was dangerous, that can amount to dangerous driving. Examples include an insecure load or serious defects with brakes or tyres. If the defect was hidden and not something you could reasonably have known about, that is likely to be important.
Is a police chase always dangerous driving?
Not automatically. The prosecution must still prove that the driving itself fell far below the expected standard. Failing to stop for the police is a separate offence. In practice, driving during a pursuit is often closely examined using police camera footage, and the court looks at the speed, the roads and the risk to other people.
Can the charge be reduced to careless driving?
Sometimes. The court can convict of careless driving as an alternative if it is not sure the driving was dangerous. The prosecution may also accept a plea to careless driving in some cases. Whether this is realistic depends on the evidence, and a solicitor can tell you once they have reviewed it.
What if someone was injured?
If dangerous driving causes serious injury or death, different and more serious offences apply. These are always heard in the Crown Court and carry much longer sentences. Tell the firm straight away if anyone was hurt, even if the police have not yet said what charge they are considering.
Do I have to receive a warning before being prosecuted?
For dangerous driving, the police usually have to warn the driver that a prosecution is being considered, either at the scene or by a notice of intended prosecution sent within 14 days. There is an exception where an accident happened that the driver was aware of. Whether the rules were followed depends on the facts, and a solicitor can check this when reviewing the case.
Should I have a solicitor at my police interview?
Leaflegal cannot advise you on that. What we can say is that everyone interviewed under caution has a right to free legal advice, whatever their income. A solicitor can find out what the police allege before the interview and talk through how to approach it. The firm we introduce you to can explain this in more detail.
