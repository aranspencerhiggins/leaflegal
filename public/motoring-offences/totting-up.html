About totting up bans

If you reach 12 or more points within three years, you will usually be banned for at least six months under the totting up rules.

You may be able to avoid the ban by showing it would cause exceptional hardship. A solicitor will prepare the evidence and present the argument at court.

A specialist motoring solicitor knows the procedure, the sentencing guidelines and the local courts, and can often spot problems with the paperwork or evidence. When we introduce you, the firm will look at your situation and explain your options before you decide anything.

Fees and costs

Most motoring solicitors charge fixed fees, often one for advice and preparation and another for each court hearing. Some offer a free first call to look at your paperwork and explain your options.

Legal aid is rarely available for motoring offences, so most drivers pay privately. A clear written quote before the firm starts work means you know where you stand.

Leaflegal is free for you to use. The firm pays us a fee for the introduction, and they will tell you about it in writing.

Questions to ask the firm

  • Is your fee fixed, and does it include the hearing?
  • Who will represent me at court?
  • What do you need from me, and by when?
  • Have you handled cases like mine before?
  • What happens if the hearing is adjourned?

When to get help

  • As soon as you receive a notice of intended prosecution
  • When you have a court date or a Single Justice Procedure notice
  • If a ban would affect your job or family
  • If you are close to 12 points

Things to know

Deadlines matter

Notices and court dates have strict deadlines. Contact a solicitor as soon as you can, and tell them any dates on your paperwork.

Exceptional hardship

If a ban would cause exceptional hardship to you or others, a court may decide not to ban you under the totting up rules. A solicitor can tell you whether that argument could apply.

Fees

Many motoring firms offer fixed fees for each stage. Ask what is included, such as attending court.

Totting up bans questions and answers

How long is a totting up ban?
Usually at least six months, longer if you have had a previous ban.
What is exceptional hardship?
Hardship beyond the normal consequences of a ban, often affecting other people such as family, employees or patients.
Can I argue it more than once?
The court can’t accept the same reasons again within three years.
Which areas do you cover?
Motoring offences dealt with in the courts of England and Wales.
Is there legal aid for motoring offences?
Usually not, unless you are at risk of prison. We only introduce people looking for paid legal help.
How quickly will someone contact me?
The firm aims to contact you quickly, especially if you have a court date coming up. Please give a mobile number you can answer.
