The basics

Online retailers often pay for stock long before customers pay for it. Manufacturers and overseas suppliers may want deposits or payment in full before goods are shipped, and stock can spend weeks in transit before it is ready to sell. Marketplaces may hold back payouts, and advertising has to be paid for before it brings in sales. When a product sells well, the business can run out of stock and cash at the same time, just as demand is at its highest.

Ecommerce businesses use finance to buy stock in larger quantities, pay for marketing campaigns, launch new products and invest in fulfilment. Revolving facilities can be drawn on when a big order is due. Some lenders base the amount on online sales data and take repayments as a share of revenue. Fixed-term loans can suit a one-off investment such as a warehouse move. A broker can explain which options lenders are likely to consider for an online business.

Things to consider

Sales data

Lenders that work with online businesses may ask to connect to your store, marketplace or payment provider accounts to see sales history. Consistent, well-kept data helps.

Stock overseas and in transit

If stock is held abroad or in transit for long periods, lenders may look at how quickly it turns into cash and how much is tied up at once. Shipping delays are worth allowing for when you plan repayments.

Seasonality and returns

Peaks around events such as Black Friday, and return rates in some categories, can make income uneven. Make sure repayments would be affordable in quieter months.

Questions and answers

Can an online-only business get a loan?
Yes. Many lenders work with ecommerce businesses and look at online sales history, marketplace payouts and bank statements. As with any business using Loanleaf, the company needs to be a UK limited company or LLP, trading for at least six months with annual revenue of £50,000 or more.
What is revenue-based finance?
It is finance where repayments are taken as a share of your sales, so you repay more in busy periods and less in quiet ones. Some lenders offer this to online businesses using data from their store or payment provider. Compare the total cost with a standard loan before deciding.
Can I borrow to buy stock from overseas suppliers?
Often, yes. Revolving facilities and short-term loans are commonly used to pay supplier deposits and balances. Some lenders offer trade finance aimed specifically at importing stock. A broker can explain which options might suit how and where you buy, and how long lenders allow for stock in transit.
Do marketplace sellers qualify?
Many lenders consider businesses that sell through marketplaces such as Amazon or eBay, as well as their own website. They may look at payout history, account health and how long you have been selling on each platform, alongside your bank statements and filed accounts. Payouts that are held back or delayed can affect how lenders view cash flow.
Can I borrow to pay a VAT bill?
Yes. VAT loans spread a quarterly VAT bill over a few months. They can help online retailers whose VAT falls due after a busy quarter, when much of the cash has already been spent on new stock. Check the total cost and any fees before you decide.

You answer one short form about your company and what you need, and we match you with a specialist finance broker who works with online retailers and ecommerce brands.
