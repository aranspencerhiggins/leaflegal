About ending a civil partnership

Ending a civil partnership is called dissolution. It follows the same no fault process as divorce, with the same minimum waiting periods.

Financial and children matters are dealt with in the same way as for married couples. A family solicitor can handle the dissolution and help you agree the rest.

A good family solicitor listens first, explains your options calmly and helps you find the least stressful way forward, which often means agreeing things without going to court. When we introduce you, the firm will explain how they can help and what it is likely to cost.

Civil partnership dissolution in brief

Civil partnership dissolution follows the same no fault process as divorce. A family solicitor can guide you through the application and help you agree finances and arrangements for children.

As with divorce, a financial order such as a consent order is needed to make a financial settlement binding.

Ending a civil partnership explained

Pick a topic to read more.

A civil partnership is a legal relationship registered between two people. It gives civil partners rights and responsibilities very similar to those of a married couple. When a civil partnership ends, the legal process is called dissolution rather than divorce, but the steps and the waiting periods are almost the same.

Civil partnerships were introduced for same sex couples in 2005. Since the end of 2019, opposite sex couples in England and Wales have also been able to form one. Whoever you are and however long you have been together, the dissolution process works in the same way.

Since April 2022, dissolution has been no fault. One or both civil partners tell the court that the relationship has broken down irretrievably, and the court accepts that statement as proof. Nobody has to blame the other or prove anything about their behaviour. This was designed to reduce conflict at what is already a painful time.

Ending the civil partnership itself is only one part of the picture. The dissolution does not, on its own, decide what happens to your home, savings, pensions or arrangements for children. Those are separate questions that often take more time and care. A family solicitor can deal with the dissolution and help you work through the rest.

It is natural to feel a mix of emotions, from relief to grief or anger, even when the decision is mutual. You do not need to have everything worked out before speaking to a solicitor. A first conversation is simply a chance to understand the process and what lies ahead.

The dissolution process is run through the family court and is mostly done online. Your solicitor will guide you through each stage. This is a general outline of how it usually works.

Before you apply

A dissolution application cannot be made until the civil partnership has lasted at least one year. You will need your civil partnership certificate, or a certified copy, and an address for your civil partner if you are applying alone. If the civil partnership was registered abroad, a translation or extra documents may be needed.

1. The application

One civil partner can apply alone, called a sole application, or both can apply together, called a joint application. The application includes a statement that the civil partnership has broken down irretrievably. A court fee is payable, and the amount changes from time to time, so check GOV.UK or ask your solicitor.

2. Responding

In a sole application, the other civil partner is sent the papers and asked to complete an acknowledgement of service, usually within 14 days. They can only dispute the dissolution on limited grounds, such as the court not having jurisdiction or the civil partnership not being legally valid.

3. The 20 week period

There is a minimum period of 20 weeks between the start of proceedings and applying for the conditional order. It is intended as a time to reflect and, where possible, to agree arrangements for money and children. Many people use it to start financial disclosure and negotiations.

4. Conditional order

After the 20 weeks, the applicant or applicants can ask the court for a conditional order. This is a document saying the court sees no reason why the civil partnership cannot be dissolved. It is also the point from which a financial consent order can be approved by the court.

5. Final order

At least six weeks and one day after the conditional order, an application can be made for the final order. The final order legally ends the civil partnership. Many solicitors suggest waiting until a financial order has been made before applying, because the final order can affect rights such as pensions and inheritance.

Civil partners have the same financial claims against each other as married couples. When the relationship ends, everything from the home and savings to pensions and businesses may need to be considered. The finances are dealt with separately from the dissolution itself.

Every family's finances are different. A couple with a rented flat and modest savings faces different questions from one with a business, several properties and large pensions. Your solicitor will want to understand your whole financial picture before explaining how the law might apply. They will not be able to tell you the likely outcome until they have that information.

Agreeing the finances

Many couples reach agreement themselves, through solicitors or with the help of mediation. Before agreeing anything, each person usually gives full financial disclosure so that decisions are made on accurate information. Once agreed, the terms are normally put into a consent order and sent to the court for approval after the conditional order.

When agreement is not possible

Either civil partner can apply to court for a financial order. Before doing so, the applicant usually needs to attend a mediation information and assessment meeting unless an exemption applies. The court process involves several stages, including a hearing focused on helping the couple settle. If agreement is still not possible, a judge decides.

What the court considers

The court looks at a range of factors, including each person's income, earning capacity, property and other resources, their needs, the standard of living during the relationship, their ages, the length of the civil partnership and each person's contributions. The welfare of any child of the family under 18 comes first.

Pensions

Pensions are often one of the most valuable assets. They can be shared through a pension sharing order, offset against other assets or dealt with in other ways. Valuing pensions properly can need expert help, and your solicitor can explain the options.

Why a court order matters

Without a court order, financial claims between former civil partners can stay open, sometimes for years. A consent order can make a clean break, so that neither person can make further claims later. It is important to know that if you form a new civil partnership or marry before applying for a financial order, you may lose the right to make certain claims. Your solicitor can explain the timing.

When civil partners with children separate, the children's arrangements are usually the most important thing for everyone. Arrangements for children are separate from the dissolution and are decided on what is best for the children.

Who is a legal parent

Families are created in many ways, and the law on who is a legal parent depends on how a child was conceived or joined the family. Where a child was born through assisted conception during a civil partnership, both civil partners may be legal parents. In other families, one partner may be a step-parent with or without parental responsibility. Your solicitor will want to understand your family so they can explain each person's position.

Agreeing arrangements

Many parents agree where the children will live and how they will spend time with each parent, either between themselves or through mediation. A written parenting plan can help everyone know what to expect, especially for holidays and special occasions.

If you cannot agree

A parent, or someone with parental responsibility, can apply to court for a child arrangements order, usually after attending a mediation information meeting. The court's main concern is the child's welfare. A solicitor can explain the process and what to expect.

Supporting the children financially

Child maintenance is usually arranged through the Child Maintenance Service or by agreement. In a financial settlement, the court will also consider the housing and other needs of any child of the family, which can include a child who has been treated as part of your family even if they are not both partners' child.

The cost of ending a civil partnership depends far more on the finances and children's arrangements than on the dissolution itself. A straightforward dissolution where everything is agreed costs much less than one with a contested financial dispute.

How firms usually charge

Many firms offer a fixed fee for dealing with the dissolution application and orders. Some also offer fixed fees for drafting a consent order where the finances are agreed. Financial negotiations, mediation and court proceedings are usually charged by the hour, and barristers' fees are extra if a barrister is needed.

Court fees

There is a court fee for the dissolution application and separate fees for financial applications. These change from time to time, so check GOV.UK or ask your solicitor for the current amounts. Help with court fees may be available to people on low incomes.

Things that affect the cost

  • Whether you agree on the finances and children
  • The number and complexity of assets
  • Whether pensions or businesses need valuing
  • Whether either of you has assets or links abroad
  • How quickly disclosure is provided
  • Whether court proceedings are needed

Ask the firm to explain its fees in writing, what is included and what could cost extra. A good firm will keep you updated if the estimate changes.

Ending a civil partnership touches many areas of life beyond the court process. These are some practical points people often find helpful to think about. Your solicitor can explain how each one applies to you.

Wills

When a civil partnership is dissolved, any gift to your former civil partner in an existing will usually fails, and they are no longer treated as your executor. That does not happen on separation alone. Many people make a new will once they separate, so that their wishes are clear throughout the process.

Pensions and life insurance

Pension schemes and life insurance policies often pay out according to nomination forms. These are not always updated automatically when a civil partnership ends. It is worth checking who you have nominated.

The home

If you own your home jointly as joint tenants, the survivor automatically inherits if one of you dies. Some people change this after separating. If the home is in one name, the other civil partner may be able to register their right to occupy it. Your solicitor can explain what is possible.

Names and documents

If you changed your name when you formed the civil partnership, you can choose whether to keep it or change back. Banks, employers and passport offices each have their own requirements, and your final order or civil partnership certificate is often needed.

Overseas civil partnerships

If your civil partnership was registered abroad, or either of you lives abroad, there can be questions about which country's courts should deal with the dissolution. Acting quickly can sometimes matter in these cases, so mention it when you make your enquiry.

Your wellbeing

Ending a relationship is hard. Many people find it helps to have support from friends, family or a counsellor alongside legal advice. If you are worried about your safety or that of your children, tell the firm when they contact you. In an emergency, always call 999.

Ending a civil partnership questions and answers

Is dissolution the same as divorce?
The process is very similar, and both are no fault.
Are finances treated differently?
No. Civil partners have the same financial claims as spouses.
How long does it take?
At least 26 weeks from application to final order, longer if finances need resolving.
Which areas do you cover?
Family law enquiries in England and Wales.
I’m worried about my safety. Can you help?
If you are in danger, call 999. For confidential support, the National Domestic Abuse Helpline is free on 0808 2000 247, 24 hours a day.
Will my partner find out I made an enquiry?
No. We only share your details with the firm we introduce you to, and they contact you, not anyone else.
What is the difference between dissolution and divorce?
Dissolution is the legal process for ending a civil partnership, and divorce is the process for ending a marriage. The steps are almost identical. Both are no fault, both use the same online system and both have the same minimum waiting periods. The orders have the same names, and financial and children matters are dealt with in the same way.
How long do we need to have been civil partners before applying?
You cannot apply for dissolution until your civil partnership has lasted at least one year. If you separate before then, you can still make arrangements about money and children, for example through a separation agreement. Your solicitor can explain what is possible in the meantime and how to prepare for the application.
Do I need to give a reason for ending the civil partnership?
No. Since April 2022 you only need to state that the civil partnership has broken down irretrievably. You do not have to give details of behaviour or separation, and your civil partner is not blamed. This is intended to reduce conflict and allow couples to focus on practical arrangements. Nobody has to prove fault.
Can we apply together?
Yes. Civil partners can make a joint application if they both agree that the relationship has ended. A joint application can feel more cooperative. If one of you later decides not to continue jointly, the process can usually carry on as a sole application. Your solicitor can explain how this works in practice.
Can my civil partner stop the dissolution?
Only in limited circumstances. A civil partner can dispute the application on grounds such as the court not having jurisdiction, the civil partnership not being legally valid or it having already ended. They cannot dispute it simply because they do not want it to end. A solicitor can explain what to do if your civil partner objects.
What if I do not know where my civil partner is?
The court usually needs the papers to be served on your civil partner. If you cannot find them, there are ways to try to trace them or ask the court to allow another method of service. In some cases the court can dispense with service. Your solicitor can explain the options and what evidence might be needed.
Can opposite sex couples dissolve a civil partnership?
Yes. Opposite sex couples have been able to form civil partnerships in England and Wales since the end of 2019. The dissolution process is the same for every civil partnership, whatever the sex of the partners. The financial claims and arrangements for children are also the same. Your solicitor can explain the steps.
Is there an alternative to dissolution?
Some civil partners who do not want to end the relationship legally choose a separation agreement or apply for a separation order, which allows the court to deal with some financial matters without dissolving the civil partnership. In rare cases a civil partnership may be annulled. Your solicitor can explain whether any of these is relevant to you.
How long does dissolution take?
There is a minimum of 20 weeks from the start of proceedings to applying for the conditional order, and at least six weeks and one day from the conditional order to applying for the final order. That means at least about 26 weeks in total. Court delays, service problems or waiting for a financial order can make it longer.
What is the conditional order?
It is the court's confirmation that it sees no reason why the civil partnership cannot be dissolved. It does not end the civil partnership. It is an important stage because it is the earliest point at which a court can approve a financial consent order. Your solicitor can explain how the timing of the two fits together.
What is the final order?
The final order legally ends the civil partnership. It can be applied for at least six weeks and one day after the conditional order. Once it is made, you are free to marry or form another civil partnership. It is worth keeping a copy safe, as you may need it for future paperwork.
Should we wait for the finances before getting the final order?
Many solicitors suggest waiting until a financial order has been approved before applying for the final order. Once the civil partnership ends, a former partner may lose certain rights, for example to pension benefits payable on death. Your solicitor will explain whether waiting makes sense in your circumstances. It is a common question.
What if the applicant does not apply for the final order?
If the applicant does not apply, the other civil partner can usually apply once a further three months have passed after the date the applicant could first have applied. If more than 12 months have passed since the conditional order, an explanation for the delay is usually needed. Your solicitor can help if things have stalled.
Can we pause the process if we try again?
Yes. If you decide to try to save the relationship, the application can be paused or withdrawn. Many couples use the 20 week period to reflect. If you reconcile after the final order has been made, the civil partnership has already ended, so you would need to register a new one.
Does the date we separated matter?
Under the no fault process you do not need to have been separated for any period before applying. The separation date can still be relevant in financial discussions, for example when looking at assets built up after you stopped living together. Your solicitor may ask about it when they gather information about your finances and the history of your relationship.
Do civil partners have the same financial rights as married couples?
Yes. Civil partners can make the same financial claims as spouses, including claims for property, lump sums, maintenance and pension sharing. The court considers the same factors as it would in a divorce. Your solicitor can explain how these apply to your own circumstances and what a fair outcome might look like.
Does dissolution decide who gets the house?
No. The dissolution only ends the legal relationship. What happens to the home, savings and pensions is decided separately, either by agreement turned into a consent order or by the court on a financial application. Without an order, claims can remain open. A solicitor can explain the options for the home.
What is a consent order?
A consent order is a court order that records a financial agreement between civil partners. It is drafted by a solicitor, signed by both of you and approved by a judge after the conditional order. Once approved, it is legally binding and can be enforced. It can also achieve a clean break so no further claims can be made.
Do we need a financial order if we have very little?
Many solicitors recommend one even where assets are modest. Without a clean break order, a former civil partner may be able to make a claim in future, for example if one of you later receives an inheritance or builds up savings. A consent order can be relatively straightforward where finances are simple.
Can I lose my right to a financial claim?
Yes, in some circumstances. If you form a new civil partnership or marry before making a financial application, you may lose the right to make certain claims. There are also practical risks in delaying, as evidence becomes harder to gather. Your solicitor can explain how to protect your position. Acting early usually helps.
What happens to pensions?
Pensions can be shared through a pension sharing order, which transfers part of one person's pension to the other. They can also be offset against other assets. Pension values can be hard to compare, and specialist reports are sometimes needed. Your solicitor can explain the options and what information to gather.
What if my civil partner hides money?
Each person has a duty to give full and frank financial disclosure. If one person hides assets, the court can draw conclusions, make orders for further information and, in some cases, set aside an agreement made on false information. If you have concerns, tell your solicitor early so they can explain the steps available.
Can I stay in our home while things are sorted out?
A civil partner who does not legally own the home usually has home rights, which means a right to occupy it while the civil partnership continues. These rights can be protected by registering them at HM Land Registry. If there are safety concerns, the court can make orders about who lives in the home. A solicitor can explain your position.
How are arrangements for children decided?
Most parents agree arrangements themselves or with the help of mediation. If agreement is not possible, a parent or someone with parental responsibility can apply to court for a child arrangements order, usually after a mediation information meeting. The court's main concern is the child's welfare, and it will look at the child's needs and wishes.
Am I a legal parent of our child?
It depends on how your family was formed. Where a child was conceived through assisted conception during a civil partnership, both partners may be legal parents. In other situations, one partner may be a step-parent. Parental responsibility can sometimes be gained by agreement or court order. A solicitor will explain your position based on your family's circumstances.
Can I claim support for a child who is not biologically mine?
In financial proceedings, the court considers the needs of any child of the family. That can include a child who has been treated by both of you as part of your family. Child maintenance through the Child Maintenance Service is usually based on legal parentage. Your solicitor can explain how this applies to you.
Can I move away with the children?
If both parents have parental responsibility, moving a long way within the UK or abroad usually needs the other parent's agreement or a court order. Short holidays abroad may also need consent, depending on the arrangements in place. A solicitor can explain the rules before you make plans, so that you avoid problems later.
How do we tell the children?
Many parents find it helps to tell children together, in simple language suited to their ages, and to reassure them that both parents love them. Avoid involving children in adult disagreements where you can. Family mediators and children's organisations offer guidance, and your solicitor may be able to suggest sources of support.
Will the children have to go to court?
In most cases children do not attend court. If the court is asked to decide arrangements, a family court adviser from Cafcass may speak to the parents and sometimes to the children, depending on their ages, to understand their wishes and feelings. The aim is to keep children out of the conflict as much as possible.
Do I need a solicitor to end a civil partnership?
You can apply online yourself, and some people do. Many use a solicitor to make sure the application is correct, the timing of the final order is right and the finances are properly dealt with. Even if the dissolution is simple, advice on finances and children can make a real difference to the outcome.
My civil partner has a solicitor. Do I need one?
You are not obliged to have one, but it can help to have someone advising only you, especially on finances. A solicitor acting for your civil partner cannot advise you. If you receive letters or documents from their solicitor, it is a good idea to take advice before responding or signing anything.
How much will it cost?
It depends mostly on whether the finances and children's arrangements are agreed. Many firms offer a fixed fee for the dissolution itself, with financial work charged separately. Court fees are extra and change from time to time. Ask the firm for a written estimate setting out what is included before you commit.

Is Leaflegal a law firm?
No. Leaflegal is an introduction service and does not give legal advice. We introduce you to one regulated family law firm, and any advice comes from them. The firm must meet professional standards and has a complaints procedure. You are free to decide whether to instruct them after your first conversation.
How quickly will someone contact me?
The firm usually gets in touch within one working day of your enquiry, often sooner. If anything is urgent, such as a court deadline, papers you have received or worries about your safety, mention it in your enquiry so the firm knows to prioritise it. In an emergency, always call 999.
What should I have ready for the first conversation?
It helps to have your civil partnership certificate, your civil partner's address, a rough list of property, savings, pensions and debts, and details of any children. Bring any papers you have received from the court or your civil partner's solicitor. Do not worry if you do not have everything yet, as the firm will explain what is needed.
Can I get divorced if I have been married less than a year?
No. In England and Wales you must have been married for at least one year before applying for a divorce. If you separate within the first year, a solicitor can explain other options, such as a separation agreement, while you wait. In rare cases a marriage can be annulled, which is a different process.
Do I need to give a reason for the divorce?
No. Since April 2022 you only need to state that the marriage has broken down irretrievably. You do not have to explain why or blame anyone. The court treats the statement as conclusive evidence that the marriage has broken down. This means the application itself contains no allegations, which many people find makes the process less upsetting for everyone, including children.
Should we apply jointly or should one of us apply alone?
Both routes lead to the same result. A joint application can feel more cooperative and reduces paperwork for the respondent. A sole application can be simpler if one person is reluctant or slow to respond. A solicitor can talk through which is more practical for you, without deciding for you.
What if I cannot find my marriage certificate?
You can usually order a certified copy from the register office where you married, or from the General Register Office. If you married abroad, you may need to contact the authorities in that country. A photocopy is not normally accepted, and a certificate not in English usually needs a certified translation.
Can I divorce in England if we married abroad?
Often yes, as long as the marriage is legally recognised in the UK and you or your spouse have a sufficient connection to England and Wales, such as living here. International cases can raise questions about which country is the right place to divorce. A solicitor can explain how this works for you.
Can I apply online myself?
Yes. The court service has an online system for divorce applications, and many people use it without a solicitor. Some people still prefer a solicitor to handle the paperwork, or to advise on the finances and timing alongside it. The online service explains each step, and you can usually track progress through your account. If your situation is not straightforward, such as an overseas marriage or a spouse who cannot be found, advice can help avoid delay.
Does it matter who applies first?
Under no fault divorce there is very little practical difference. The applicant controls the timing of each step, which some people prefer. In cases with an international connection, applying first can sometimes matter, so it is worth asking a solicitor if either of you has links to another country. It can also help to think about timing.
Will my spouse find out where I live?
In a sole application you give your spouse's address, not the other way round, and you can give a correspondence address for yourself. If there are safety concerns, you can ask for your contact details to be kept confidential. If you are at risk of harm, contact the police on 999 in an emergency or a domestic abuse support service.
