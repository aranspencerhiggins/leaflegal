Leaflegal introduces people and businesses to regulated law firms in eight areas of law. Six of those areas cover matters in England and Wales. The other two, trademarks and related IP and introductions to regulated immigration advisers, are available anywhere in the UK. This page gives an overview of each area, the kinds of matters the firms help with and how to choose where to start.

You do not need to know which area your problem falls under before you begin. Many people start with a simple description, such as needing to sell a flat, having received a court summons or being owed money by a customer. If you are not sure, pick the closest area on the form or describe what is happening in your own words. The firm will explain what kind of help fits once it understands the details.

The eight areas

  • Conveyancing, for buying, selling and remortgaging property
  • Lease extensions, for leaseholders with a shortening lease
  • Family law, for divorce, finances, children and separation
  • Motoring offences, for drivers facing points, a ban or court
  • Licensing, for premises licences and related matters
  • Business debt recovery, for unpaid invoices between businesses
  • Trademarks and related IP, for registering and protecting a brand, copyright and designs
  • Immigration, for introductions to a regulated adviser

How the introduction works in every area

Whichever area you choose, the process is the same. You answer a short set of questions about your matter, we introduce your enquiry to one regulated firm that handles that kind of work and covers your area, and the firm contacts you to talk it through. Leaflegal is free to use. The firm pays us for the introduction, and that does not change what the firm charges you.

Leaflegal is not a law firm and does not give legal advice or immigration advice. The information on these pages explains in general terms how things usually work. The firm will look at your own situation and explain your options.

What is not covered

We do not cover personal injury, clinical negligence, housing disrepair or financial mis-selling claims, debts owed to a business by individual consumers, or matters only suitable for legal aid. Apart from immigration, we only cover matters in England and Wales.

How to use these pages

Each area has its own page, with a general explanation of how things usually work, what tends to affect the cost and the timescale, common problems and answers to frequent questions. Within each area there are also pages on specific matters, such as buying a leasehold flat, a financial settlement or a speeding allegation. Reading them before your first call can help you understand what the firm is telling you and think of the right questions to ask.

Finding a firm near you

For many matters the firm does not need to be close by, because most of the work is done remotely. Where local knowledge helps, such as a hearing at your local magistrates' court or an application to your local council, we take your location into account. Our solicitors near you pages list the towns and cities we cover.

Two of our areas deal with homes and other property. Both involve the transfer or change of legal rights in land, and both are areas where getting the paperwork right matters for years to come.

Conveyancing

Conveyancing is the legal work involved in buying, selling or remortgaging a property. The conveyancer checks that the seller owns what they are selling, carries out searches with the local authority and others, reviews the contract and any lease, deals with the lender, handles the money and registers the new owner with HM Land Registry.

Firms we introduce help with buying a home, selling a home, buying and selling at the same time, first time purchases, new build homes, leasehold flats, buy to let purchases, remortgages and transfers of equity. Most conveyancing is charged as a fixed fee plus disbursements, such as search fees and Land Registry fees, and any Stamp Duty Land Tax due on a purchase. Tax rates and official fees change, so the firm will confirm what applies and GOV.UK has the current rates.

Conveyancing can be done by solicitors regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority or licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. If you are buying with a mortgage, the firm usually needs to be on your lender's panel. You can read more on our conveyancing page.

Lease extensions

Many flats, and some houses, are owned on a lease that lasts for a fixed number of years. As the lease gets shorter, the property can become harder to sell or mortgage, and extending the lease usually becomes more expensive. A lease extension adds years to the lease, and often reduces the ground rent.

Firms we introduce help with statutory lease extensions, which for most qualifying flat owners add 90 years to the existing term, informal extensions agreed directly with the freeholder, and collective enfranchisement, where flat owners join together to buy the freehold. A valuation by a specialist surveyor is usually needed to work out the price. Whether you qualify for a particular route depends on your circumstances, which the firm will check. See our lease extensions page for more.

Where property overlaps with other areas

Property matters often connect with other parts of life. A sale may follow a separation, in which case a family solicitor and a conveyancer may need to work together. A short lease may need to be dealt with before a sale can go ahead. If your situation involves more than one of these, mention it when you make your enquiry so the firm has the full picture.

Family law covers the legal side of relationships starting, changing and ending. It is often an emotional area, and the right firm will be both practical and sensitive to what you are going through.

Divorce and dissolution

In England and Wales, divorce and the dissolution of a civil partnership are now no fault. One or both people apply to the court, there is a minimum period of 20 weeks between the start of the application and the conditional order, and then at least six weeks and one day before the final order. A solicitor can deal with the application and, just as importantly, help make sure the finances are dealt with properly.

Financial settlements

Ending a marriage or civil partnership does not by itself end financial claims between the couple. A financial settlement deals with the home, savings, pensions, debts and maintenance. Where a couple agree, a solicitor can turn the agreement into a consent order approved by the court. Where they do not, there may be negotiation, mediation or court proceedings.

Children

Arrangements for children after separation, such as where they live and how much time they spend with each parent, are often agreed between parents. Where they cannot be agreed, a family solicitor can help explore mediation and, if needed, an application to court for a child arrangements order.

Agreements and cohabitation

Firms also help with prenuptial agreements before marriage, separation agreements for couples who are separating without divorcing yet, and disputes between people who lived together without marrying. The law treats cohabiting couples very differently from married couples, so specialist help is often useful. Our family law page has more detail.

Mediation and other options

Many separating couples try to reach agreement through mediation before going to court, and in most cases a person must attend a mediation information and assessment meeting before applying to court about finances or children, though there are exceptions. Collaborative law and arbitration are other options some couples use. A family solicitor can explain how each one works and support you through whichever you choose.

These two areas are quite different, but both often involve dealing with the courts, the police or a local authority under time pressure.

Motoring offences

Motoring offences range from speeding to drink driving and dangerous driving. Many result in penalty points, a fine or a driving ban. Under the totting up rules, a driver who reaches 12 or more points within three years usually faces a ban of at least six months, unless the court accepts that a ban would cause exceptional hardship.

Firms we introduce help drivers with speeding allegations, drink driving, drug driving, careless and dangerous driving, driving without insurance, failing to identify the driver, totting up and exceptional hardship arguments. They can explain the process, the likely range of outcomes, and whether there are points worth raising with the court. For more, see our motoring offences page.

Licensing

Businesses that sell alcohol, provide regulated entertainment or serve hot food late at night usually need a premises licence from the local council under the Licensing Act 2003. Most premises selling alcohol also need a designated premises supervisor who holds a personal licence.

Firms we introduce help with new premises licence applications, variations to existing licences, transfers when a business changes hands, changing the designated premises supervisor and licence reviews, which can follow complaints from residents or the police. Licensing work often involves dealing with the council, the police and other responsible authorities, and sometimes a hearing before a licensing committee. See our licensing page for more.

Why early contact helps

Both areas tend to run to fixed deadlines. A driver may have a short time to respond to a notice of intended prosecution or a single justice procedure notice. A business may have a set period to respond to a representation about its licence. Making contact with a solicitor early gives them more time to look at the paperwork and explain the options before a deadline passes.

Unpaid invoices can put real strain on a business, especially a small one. Business debt recovery is about getting paid by another business that owes you money, using the right steps in the right order.

What the firms help with

Firms we introduce help businesses in England and Wales recover money owed by other businesses. That can include sending a formal letter before action, negotiating a payment plan, issuing a county court claim, applying for judgment and enforcing a judgment that has not been paid. In some cases, where the debt is undisputed, a statutory demand may be used as a step towards winding up or bankruptcy proceedings.

Things that affect the approach

  • Whether the debt is disputed or simply unpaid
  • How much is owed and how old it is
  • Whether there is a written contract or agreed terms
  • Whether the debtor is a company, a partnership or a sole trader
  • Whether the debtor appears able to pay

In general, a claim for an unpaid debt must be brought within six years. Businesses may also be able to claim statutory interest and compensation for late payment of commercial debts. A solicitor will explain what applies to your situation.

What we do not cover

We only cover debts owed by one business to another. Collecting debts from individual consumers is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, and we do not cover it. More information is on our debt recovery page.

Before you start

It helps to gather the invoices, any contract or terms and conditions, emails or letters about the debt, and a short timeline of what has happened. The firm will want to know whether the customer has raised any complaint about the goods or services, because a disputed debt is usually handled differently from one that is simply unpaid.

Leaflegal introduces individuals, families and employers anywhere in the UK to a regulated immigration adviser. Leaflegal does not give immigration advice or provide immigration services, and nothing on our website tells you which visa or route might apply to you.

Why regulation matters

In the UK, only people who are regulated can give immigration advice or provide immigration services. That includes solicitors regulated by the SRA, barristers, and advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority. It is an offence for anyone else to do so. This protects people from poor or dishonest advice at a time when the stakes can be very high.

Why people look for an adviser

People contact a regulated adviser about many things, such as joining family in the UK, working in the UK, staying longer, becoming a British citizen, or responding to a refusal or a letter from the Home Office. Employers contact advisers about sponsoring workers. In every case, the adviser will look at the person's own situation and explain how they could help.

How working with an adviser usually goes

The adviser usually starts with an initial conversation or consultation to understand what you need. They will explain how they charge, which is often a fixed fee for a defined piece of work. If you go ahead, they will usually ask for documents and information, prepare any application or response and keep you updated. If you have received a letter with a deadline, mention it straight away. Our immigration page explains more about how introductions work.

Choosing and checking an adviser

Ask the adviser who regulates them and check their registration. Ask how often they deal with matters like yours, who will do the work, how they charge and what is included. A good adviser will be clear about fees, will not promise a particular outcome and will explain what they need from you.

Questions and answers

What if I don't know which area I need?
That is common, and you do not need to know the legal name for your problem. Choose the area that seems closest, or describe what is happening in your own words. The firm will look at the details and explain what kind of help fits. If your matter is outside our areas, we will not pass it on.
What if my matter covers more than one area?
Some situations cross over, such as a separation that involves selling the family home, or a business that is owed money and also needs a licence. Start with the most pressing matter and mention the other in the notes. You can also complete the form separately for each matter.
Do you cover all areas of law?
No. We focus on eight areas so that we can introduce people to firms that do that work every day. We do not cover personal injury, clinical negligence, housing disrepair, financial mis-selling, consumer debt collection, employment disputes or criminal matters other than motoring offences.
Why are some areas England and Wales only?
England and Wales share one legal system, while Scotland and Northern Ireland each have their own. Property law, family law, motoring law, licensing and court procedures differ between them. Immigration law applies across the UK, so immigration introductions are available everywhere in the UK.
Is Leaflegal free for every area?
Yes. Leaflegal is free in every area we cover. The firm we introduce you to pays us for the introduction, and that does not change what the firm charges you. The firm explains its own fees and confirms them in writing before any work starts.
Do you give advice about my matter?
No. Leaflegal does not give legal advice or immigration advice. Our pages give general information about how things usually work. The regulated firm we introduce you to will look at your own situation and explain your options and the likely next steps.
Can I see a list of the specific matters you cover?
Yes. Each area page lists the specific matters the firms help with, from buying a home to licence reviews. If your matter is not listed, it may still be covered. Describe it on the form or in the notes and we will check before introducing a firm.
Do you cover commercial property?
Our conveyancing introductions focus on residential property, including homes, flats, new builds and buy to let purchases. If your matter involves commercial property, mention it in the notes. The firm will tell you whether it can help or whether you need a different kind of specialist.
Can a conveyancer anywhere in England and Wales act for me?
Usually yes. Most conveyancing is done by phone, email and post, so the firm does not need to be near the property. If you are buying with a mortgage, the firm will normally need to be on your lender's panel, which it can confirm when it quotes.
How do I know if my lease is getting short?
Your lease will state the length of the term and the date it started, and the title at HM Land Registry usually shows this too. Under the long standing rules, the cost of extending tends to rise more quickly once a lease drops below 80 years. Reforms are being introduced over time, so a solicitor can explain the current position.
Is a lease extension the same as buying the freehold?
No. A lease extension adds years to your own lease. Buying the freehold, often done jointly with other flat owners through collective enfranchisement, means owning the building itself. Each has different costs, processes and implications, and a solicitor can explain which might suit your situation.
Can I sell my home while extending the lease?
In some cases a seller starts the statutory lease extension process and passes the benefit of the notice to the buyer as part of the sale. Whether this works depends on the circumstances, and timing matters. A solicitor will explain the options and the risks for your sale.
Do you help with remortgages?
Yes. Firms we introduce help with remortgages and transfers of equity, where someone is added to or removed from the title. Many lenders offer their own free legal service for a remortgage, so it is worth checking that first. A firm can explain when a separate solicitor might be needed.
Do I need a solicitor to get divorced?
You can apply for a divorce yourself online. Many people still use a solicitor, particularly to deal with finances, pensions, property and children, where mistakes can be costly and hard to undo. A solicitor can also explain the court process and the timetable in your case.
How long does a divorce take?
There is a minimum of 20 weeks between the start of the application and applying for the conditional order, then at least six weeks and one day before the final order. In practice it often takes longer, especially when finances need to be resolved first. A solicitor can give a realistic estimate.
Can you help unmarried couples?
Yes. Firms we introduce help couples who lived together without marrying, including disputes about property and arrangements for children. The law treats cohabiting couples very differently from married couples, so it is worth speaking to a family solicitor about your situation.
Do family solicitors only deal with court cases?
No. Much family work happens outside court, such as negotiating agreements, drafting consent orders, preparing prenuptial and separation agreements and supporting clients through mediation. Many family solicitors try to help clients reach agreement where it is safe and possible to do so.
What if I have concerns about my safety?
If you are in immediate danger, call 999. If you have concerns about domestic abuse, organisations such as the National Domestic Abuse Helpline can offer support. Family solicitors can explain protective orders, and legal aid may be available in some cases, which GOV.UK can help you check.
Can a solicitor help with a prenuptial agreement?
Yes. Firms we introduce help couples prepare prenuptial agreements before marriage and postnuptial agreements afterwards. Each person usually has their own solicitor, and full financial disclosure is normally expected. A solicitor will explain how courts tend to approach these agreements in England and Wales.
Can you help if I have a court date soon?
Yes, but tell us the date in the form so the firm knows how urgent it is. The closer the hearing, the harder it can be to prepare, so it helps to make an enquiry as soon as possible after receiving a summons or a notice of intended prosecution.
What is exceptional hardship?
When a driver faces a ban under the totting up rules, the court can decide not to impose the ban, or to shorten it, if it accepts that a ban would cause exceptional hardship. The test is strict and the court looks closely at the evidence. A motoring solicitor can explain how it works.
Do I need a solicitor for a licence application?
Not always. Some straightforward applications are made without one. Many businesses use a licensing solicitor because applications must follow set procedures, and objections, conditions and hearings can be complex. A solicitor can also help if a licence review is called against your premises.
What happens if a licence review is called?
A review can be asked for by a responsible authority, such as the police, or by others, such as local residents. The council then holds a hearing and can take steps ranging from adding conditions to suspending or revoking the licence. A licensing solicitor can help you prepare and represent you.
Do I need a personal licence to run a pub?
A premises that sells alcohol usually needs a designated premises supervisor who holds a personal licence, and every sale of alcohol must be authorised by a personal licence holder. Not everyone working at the premises needs one. A licensing solicitor can explain how this applies to your business.
Can I deal with a speeding allegation myself?
Many drivers deal with minor speeding matters themselves, often by accepting a fixed penalty or a speed awareness course where offered. Others seek advice, particularly if they are close to 12 points, the speed was high or they want to dispute the allegation. A solicitor can explain the options.
Can you help my business recover a debt from a person?
No. We only cover debts owed by one business to another. Collecting debts from individual consumers is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority and is outside what we do. If your customer is a private individual, a solicitor who handles consumer matters or an FCA authorised firm may be able to help.
How old can a debt be?
In general, a claim for an unpaid debt in England and Wales must be brought within six years. There can be exceptions, for example where the debtor has acknowledged the debt in writing or made a payment. A solicitor will check the dates and documents in your case.
Do you give immigration advice?
No. Leaflegal does not give immigration advice or provide immigration services. We introduce you to a regulated adviser, who will look at your situation and explain how they can help. Nothing on our website tells you which visa or route might apply to you.
Do you cover immigration across the UK?
Yes. Immigration law applies across the whole of the UK, so we introduce people anywhere in England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland to a regulated immigration adviser. The adviser may work with you remotely by phone, video and email.
Can employers use Leaflegal for immigration?
Yes. Employers can be introduced to a regulated adviser for matters such as sponsoring workers. Leaflegal does not give immigration advice. The adviser will explain how they work with businesses, what they charge and what they need from you.
What if I have a deadline from the Home Office?
Mention the deadline clearly in the form so the adviser knows it is urgent. Deadlines in immigration matters can be short, so it is best to make an enquiry as soon as you receive a letter. The adviser will tell you what can be done in the time available.
Is a statutory demand the same as a court claim?
No. A statutory demand is a formal demand for payment that can be a step towards winding up a company or making an individual bankrupt. It is not a court claim and is not suitable where a debt is genuinely disputed. A solicitor will explain whether it is an appropriate tool.

More questions people ask

What is a leasehold flat?
When you own a leasehold flat, you own the right to live in it and sell it for the number of years set out in the lease. The freeholder owns the building and the land. Your lease sets out your rights and duties, such as paying ground rent and service charge, keeping the inside in repair and following rules about the building. When the lease ends, the flat goes back to the freeholder unless the lease has been extended.
Why does the length of my lease matter?
As a lease gets shorter, the flat usually becomes less valuable and harder to sell or mortgage. Many lenders have a minimum term they want left on the lease when the mortgage ends. Under the long standing rules, the cost of extending also tends to rise more quickly once the lease drops below 80 years. A solicitor can explain how this affects your flat.
How do I find out how many years are left?
Your lease states when it started and how long it was granted for. Subtract the years that have passed since the start date. If you do not have a copy of your lease, it can usually be obtained from HM Land Registry. A solicitor can check this for you as part of their first review, and it is worth doing as the start date is not always the date the lease was signed.
What is the difference between a lease extension and buying the freehold?
A lease extension gives you a new, longer lease of your own flat, but the freeholder still owns the building. Buying the freehold, usually with other flat owners, means you take over ownership of the building itself. Freehold ownership brings control over how the building is managed, along with the responsibilities that come with it.
Do these rights apply to houses?
Owners of leasehold houses have separate rights under the Leasehold Reform Act 1967, which can include extending the lease or buying the freehold of the house. The rules and valuation methods differ from those for flats. A solicitor who handles leasehold work can explain the position for a house.
Is Leaflegal a law firm?
No. Leaflegal is an introduction service. We do not give legal advice. We introduce you to one regulated firm that handles leasehold matters, and any advice you receive comes from that firm.
What is the Right to Manage?
The Right to Manage lets qualifying flat owners take over the management of their building from the freeholder through a company they set up, without buying the freehold. It does not extend anyone's lease or change who owns the building. It is a separate process with its own rules, and a solicitor can explain how it compares with buying the freehold.
Do I qualify for a statutory lease extension?
Most long leaseholders of flats do, but there are exceptions. The lease usually needs to have been granted for more than 21 years, and some landlords, such as certain charities, are treated differently. The old rule that you must have owned the flat for two years used to apply, but the 2024 Act removed it from 31 January 2025. A solicitor will confirm whether you qualify.
What is a section 42 notice?
It is the formal notice a leaseholder serves on the freeholder to start a statutory lease extension. It sets out the leaseholder's details, the property and the premium they propose to pay. The notice must contain the right information and be served correctly, which is one reason most people use a solicitor. Once it is served, strict deadlines apply to both sides.
What happens if the freeholder disagrees with the price?
The freeholder replies with a counter notice, which may propose a different premium or different terms. The two sides then negotiate, usually through their surveyors. If they cannot agree, either side can apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) to decide. There are strict time limits for this, and missing them can mean the claim is treated as withdrawn.
What if I cannot find my freeholder?
Sometimes a freeholder cannot be traced, for example if a company has been dissolved. There is a separate court process for this situation, which can allow a lease extension or freehold purchase to go ahead without the freeholder's involvement. A solicitor can explain how it works and what it is likely to involve.
Do I need a surveyor as well as a solicitor?
Almost always. The premium is based on a valuation, and you need a specialist surveyor's report to decide what to put in the notice and how to negotiate. The solicitor handles the legal process and the surveyor handles the valuation. They usually work closely together.
Can I do it without a solicitor?
It is legally possible, but the process is technical and mistakes can be costly. An invalid notice can mean starting again, and in some cases there is a wait before a new notice can be served. Most people choose to use a solicitor who handles this work regularly.
Does my mortgage lender need to be involved?
Usually, yes. Your lender has security over your lease, so it normally needs to know about a new lease and may need to consent to it. The new lease is then registered with the lender's charge carried over. Your solicitor will contact the lender and deal with its requirements as part of the process.
Can I sell my flat while a lease extension is going on?
Often, yes. Under the statutory route, the benefit of a notice can usually be passed to a buyer when the flat is sold, so the buyer can carry on with the claim. This needs to be handled carefully at the time of the sale. Your solicitor will explain how it works and whether it suits your timescales.
How much does a lease extension cost?
There is the premium paid to the freeholder, your own solicitor's and surveyor's fees, and possibly some of the freeholder's costs. The premium depends on the value of the flat, the years left and the ground rent, among other things. Because the law on valuation is changing over time, your solicitor and surveyor will explain what applies and give you an estimate before you commit.
What is marriage value?
Under the long standing valuation rules, marriage value is the extra value created when a lease is extended. Where a lease has 80 years or fewer left, the leaseholder pays the freeholder a share of it, which can add considerably to the premium. The 2024 Act removes marriage value, but this depends on further regulations and is part of the reforms being brought in over time.
Will I pay my freeholder's costs?
Under the long standing statutory process, leaseholders usually pay certain reasonable legal and valuation costs of the freeholder. The 2024 Act changes this so that each side generally bears its own costs, with some exceptions. That change is being introduced over time, so your solicitor will confirm the rules that apply when you start.
Do solicitors offer fixed fees?
Many do for a straightforward lease extension. The fixed fee usually covers the notice, negotiation and completion of the new lease. Tribunal work, complicated leases or difficult freeholders may be charged separately. Ask for a written quote that sets out what is and is not included.

Is there Stamp Duty on a lease extension?
A new lease can sometimes be a taxable transaction, depending on the size of the premium and the rules at the time. Rates and thresholds change. Your solicitor will check whether anything is payable and deal with any return that needs to be filed.
Can I get a mortgage to pay for the premium?
Some people pay the premium from savings, while others borrow, often by remortgaging or arranging further lending from their existing lender. Whether this is possible depends on your lender and your finances. A mortgage broker or your lender can talk through the options, and your solicitor will deal with the legal side of any new borrowing.
