More questions people ask

What is a leasehold flat?
When you own a leasehold flat, you own the right to live in it and sell it for the number of years set out in the lease. The freeholder owns the building and the land. Your lease sets out your rights and duties, such as paying ground rent and service charge, keeping the inside in repair and following rules about the building. When the lease ends, the flat goes back to the freeholder unless the lease has been extended.
Why does the length of my lease matter?
As a lease gets shorter, the flat usually becomes less valuable and harder to sell or mortgage. Many lenders have a minimum term they want left on the lease when the mortgage ends. Under the long standing rules, the cost of extending also tends to rise more quickly once the lease drops below 80 years. A solicitor can explain how this affects your flat.
How do I find out how many years are left?
Your lease states when it started and how long it was granted for. Subtract the years that have passed since the start date. If you do not have a copy of your lease, it can usually be obtained from HM Land Registry. A solicitor can check this for you as part of their first review, and it is worth doing as the start date is not always the date the lease was signed.
What is the difference between a lease extension and buying the freehold?
A lease extension gives you a new, longer lease of your own flat, but the freeholder still owns the building. Buying the freehold, usually with other flat owners, means you take over ownership of the building itself. Freehold ownership brings control over how the building is managed, along with the responsibilities that come with it.
Do these rights apply to houses?
Owners of leasehold houses have separate rights under the Leasehold Reform Act 1967, which can include extending the lease or buying the freehold of the house. The rules and valuation methods differ from those for flats. A solicitor who handles leasehold work can explain the position for a house.

What is the Right to Manage?
The Right to Manage lets qualifying flat owners take over the management of their building from the freeholder through a company they set up, without buying the freehold. It does not extend anyone's lease or change who owns the building. It is a separate process with its own rules, and a solicitor can explain how it compares with buying the freehold.
Do I qualify for a statutory lease extension?
Most long leaseholders of flats do, but there are exceptions. The lease usually needs to have been granted for more than 21 years, and some landlords, such as certain charities, are treated differently. The old rule that you must have owned the flat for two years used to apply, but the 2024 Act removed it from 31 January 2025. A solicitor will confirm whether you qualify.
What is a section 42 notice?
It is the formal notice a leaseholder serves on the freeholder to start a statutory lease extension. It sets out the leaseholder's details, the property and the premium they propose to pay. The notice must contain the right information and be served correctly, which is one reason most people use a solicitor. Once it is served, strict deadlines apply to both sides.
What happens if the freeholder disagrees with the price?
The freeholder replies with a counter notice, which may propose a different premium or different terms. The two sides then negotiate, usually through their surveyors. If they cannot agree, either side can apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) to decide. There are strict time limits for this, and missing them can mean the claim is treated as withdrawn.
What if I cannot find my freeholder?
Sometimes a freeholder cannot be traced, for example if a company has been dissolved. There is a separate court process for this situation, which can allow a lease extension or freehold purchase to go ahead without the freeholder's involvement. A solicitor can explain how it works and what it is likely to involve.
Do I need a surveyor as well as a solicitor?
Almost always. The premium is based on a valuation, and you need a specialist surveyor's report to decide what to put in the notice and how to negotiate. The solicitor handles the legal process and the surveyor handles the valuation. They usually work closely together.
Can I do it without a solicitor?
It is legally possible, but the process is technical and mistakes can be costly. An invalid notice can mean starting again, and in some cases there is a wait before a new notice can be served. Most people choose to use a solicitor who handles this work regularly.
Does my mortgage lender need to be involved?
Usually, yes. Your lender has security over your lease, so it normally needs to know about a new lease and may need to consent to it. The new lease is then registered with the lender's charge carried over. Your solicitor will contact the lender and deal with its requirements as part of the process.
Can I sell my flat while a lease extension is going on?
Often, yes. Under the statutory route, the benefit of a notice can usually be passed to a buyer when the flat is sold, so the buyer can carry on with the claim. This needs to be handled carefully at the time of the sale. Your solicitor will explain how it works and whether it suits your timescales.
How much does a lease extension cost?
There is the premium paid to the freeholder, your own solicitor's and surveyor's fees, and possibly some of the freeholder's costs. The premium depends on the value of the flat, the years left and the ground rent, among other things. Because the law on valuation is changing over time, your solicitor and surveyor will explain what applies and give you an estimate before you commit.
What is marriage value?
Under the long standing valuation rules, marriage value is the extra value created when a lease is extended. Where a lease has 80 years or fewer left, the leaseholder pays the freeholder a share of it, which can add considerably to the premium. The 2024 Act removes marriage value, but this depends on further regulations and is part of the reforms being brought in over time.
Will I pay my freeholder's costs?
Under the long standing statutory process, leaseholders usually pay certain reasonable legal and valuation costs of the freeholder. The 2024 Act changes this so that each side generally bears its own costs, with some exceptions. That change is being introduced over time, so your solicitor will confirm the rules that apply when you start.
Do solicitors offer fixed fees?
Many do for a straightforward lease extension. The fixed fee usually covers the notice, negotiation and completion of the new lease. Tribunal work, complicated leases or difficult freeholders may be charged separately. Ask for a written quote that sets out what is and is not included.

Is there Stamp Duty on a lease extension?
A new lease can sometimes be a taxable transaction, depending on the size of the premium and the rules at the time. Rates and thresholds change. Your solicitor will check whether anything is payable and deal with any return that needs to be filed.
Can I get a mortgage to pay for the premium?
Some people pay the premium from savings, while others borrow, often by remortgaging or arranging further lending from their existing lender. Whether this is possible depends on your lender and your finances. A mortgage broker or your lender can talk through the options, and your solicitor will deal with the legal side of any new borrowing.
What is the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024?
It is a major reform of leasehold law in England and Wales. Among other things, it aims to make it easier and cheaper for leaseholders to extend their leases or buy their freehold. It received Royal Assent in 2024, but many of its changes need further regulations and are being brought into force over time. A solicitor will confirm what applies on the date you start.
Will statutory extensions become longer?
Under the long standing rules, a statutory extension for a flat adds 90 years to the remaining term. The 2024 Act provides for a much longer standard extension of 990 years. This is one of the reforms being introduced in stages, so your solicitor will tell you which length applies when you serve your notice.
Should I wait for the reforms before extending?
That depends on your circumstances and is a decision for you with your solicitor's advice. Waiting means the lease keeps getting shorter, and plans to sell or remortgage may not allow it. Timings for the reforms can also change. A solicitor can explain the possible pros and cons for your own flat.
Has the two year ownership rule gone?
The requirement to have owned a flat for two years before using the statutory right to extend used to apply, but the 2024 Act removed it from 31 January 2025. It means a new owner may be able to start the process straight away. A solicitor will confirm that the rule does not apply to you before serving a notice.
Will my ground rent change?
A statutory extension under the long standing rules reduces the ground rent to a peppercorn for the new lease. Separate rules limit ground rents on most new residential leases granted since 2022. The 2024 Act also includes provisions about buying out ground rent. Your solicitor can explain how these rules apply to your lease.
Do the reforms affect people who have already started?
Whether a claim is dealt with under the old or new rules can depend on when the notice was served and the transitional provisions in the regulations. If you have already started, ask your solicitor how the changes affect your claim.

What is conveyancing?
Conveyancing is the legal work involved in transferring the ownership of property, or registering a mortgage against it. It includes checking the title, carrying out searches, preparing or reviewing the contract, handling the money and registering the change at HM Land Registry. It applies to buying, selling, remortgaging and changing who owns a home.
Do I legally have to use a conveyancer?
It is possible to do your own conveyancing in some situations, but it is rare and difficult. If you have a mortgage, your lender will almost always insist that a regulated firm acts for it. Most people use a professional because the legal and financial risks of getting things wrong are high and hard to put right later.
What is the difference between freehold and leasehold?
Freehold means you own the building and the land it stands on with no time limit. Leasehold means you own the right to occupy the property for a set number of years under a lease, with a landlord who owns the freehold. Leasehold property usually comes with ground rent, service charges and extra checks during conveyancing.
What is HM Land Registry?
HM Land Registry keeps the official record of who owns most land and property in England and Wales. After a purchase, remortgage or transfer, your conveyancer applies to update the register. Some older properties are still unregistered, and the first sale or mortgage usually triggers an application for first registration, which takes more work.
What are searches?
Searches are enquiries made to the local authority, water companies and other bodies to find out about things that affect a property but may not be obvious from viewing it. They can reveal planning history, road adoption, drainage, flood risk, environmental issues and mining in some areas. Lenders usually require them when you buy with a mortgage.
When does a sale or purchase become legally binding?
In England and Wales, a sale or purchase only becomes binding when contracts are exchanged. Before that, either side can withdraw without penalty, even late in the process. Once contracts are exchanged, pulling out usually means losing the deposit or facing a claim for losses, so the timing of exchange matters a great deal.
What is the difference between a survey and a valuation?
A mortgage valuation is carried out for the lender to check the property is worth enough to lend against. It is not a detailed inspection and may not tell you about defects. A survey is commissioned by the buyer and looks at the condition of the property in more depth. Your conveyancer deals with the legal side and does not inspect the building, so many buyers arrange a survey as well.
What is a chain?
A chain is a series of linked sales and purchases that depend on each other. If you are selling to someone who is also selling, and you are buying from someone who is buying elsewhere, you are in a chain. Everyone in it usually needs to exchange contracts and complete on the same day, so a delay anywhere can affect everyone else.
