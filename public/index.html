Buying, selling or remortgaging a home or a buy to let property in England and Wales. This includes leasehold flats, new builds, transfers of equity and buying and selling at the same time. A conveyancer checks the title, runs searches, deals with the other side and registers the change of ownership.

Lease extensions

Help for flat and house owners whose lease is getting shorter. That includes extending a lease under the statutory process, agreeing an informal extension with the freeholder and joining with neighbours to buy the freehold.

Family law

Divorce, dissolution of a civil partnership, financial settlements, consent orders, child arrangements, separation agreements, prenuptial agreements and disputes between people who lived together without marrying.

Licensing

Premises licences for pubs, bars, restaurants, shops and venues. That covers new applications, variations, transfers, changes of designated premises supervisor and licence reviews.

Business debt recovery

Help for businesses that are owed money by other businesses, such as unpaid invoices. That includes letters before action, county court claims and statutory demands. We do not cover debts owed by individual consumers.

Immigration

Introductions to a regulated immigration adviser, for individuals, families and employers anywhere in the UK. Leaflegal does not give immigration advice and does not tell you which visa or route might apply to you. The adviser we introduce will talk that through with you.

What we do not cover

We do not handle personal injury, clinical negligence, housing disrepair or financial mis-selling claims. We also cannot help with matters that are only suitable for legal aid, or with debts owed to a business by private individuals. If your matter is outside our areas, organisations such as Citizens Advice and the Law Society's Find a Solicitor service can help you find the right kind of help.

Whether you use Leaflegal or find a firm another way, it helps to know what to look for. A good fit is not only about legal skill. It is also about how the firm communicates, how it charges and whether it has time for your matter.

Check they are regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can look up a solicitor or firm on the SRA register. Licensed conveyancers are regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Immigration advisers must be regulated too, either as solicitors or barristers or through the Immigration Advice Authority. Every firm we introduce is regulated, and it is still sensible to check for yourself.

Look for relevant experience

Ask how often the firm handles matters like yours. A firm that does a lot of lease extensions or licence reviews will know the usual process and the usual pitfalls. You can ask who will do the work day to day and how much experience they have.

Understand how they communicate

Some people want regular updates by phone. Others are happy with email and a call at key moments. Ask how the firm keeps clients informed and how quickly it usually replies. It is a fair question, and a good firm will be happy to answer it.

Get the fees in writing

Before any work starts, a regulated firm should explain how it charges and confirm that in writing. That might be a fixed fee, an hourly rate with an estimate, or a mix of both. Ask what is included, what is not and what might make the cost go up.

Think about location

For many matters, such as conveyancing or a lease extension, the firm does not need to be near you because most of the work is done by phone, email and post. For others, such as a hearing at a local magistrates' court or a licence application to your local council, local knowledge can be useful. We take your location into account when we make the introduction.

Trust your judgement

It is fine to take time to think after a first call. It is also fine to speak to more than one firm before you decide. Leaflegal introduces one firm, but there is no obligation to instruct them, and you remain free to look elsewhere.

How law firms usually charge

The cost of legal help depends on the type of matter, how complex it is and the firm. Some kinds of work are often done for a fixed fee, such as many house purchases, uncontested divorces or straightforward licence applications. Others are usually charged by the hour because it is hard to know at the start how much work will be needed, such as a contested financial settlement or a defended court case.

Many firms ask for some money on account before they start, especially where there are disbursements to pay early on. Others bill at set stages or at the end. The firm will explain how its billing works in its terms of business.

Disbursements and third party costs

On top of the firm's own fee there may be costs paid to other people. Examples include search fees and Land Registry fees on a property purchase, court fees on a claim, valuation fees on a lease extension or barristers' fees for a hearing. These are often called disbursements. They change from time to time, so the firm will tell you what applies to your matter and GOV.UK lists many official fees.

Questions worth asking about cost

  • Is the fee fixed, or charged by the hour
  • What does the fee include and leave out
  • What disbursements are likely
  • Is VAT included in the figures
  • When will I be asked to pay, and how much up front
  • What could make the cost go up, and will you warn me first

No. Leaflegal does not give legal advice or immigration advice, and nothing on this website is advice about your situation. Our pages explain how things usually work in general terms. A solicitor or regulated adviser will look at your own circumstances and tell you what your options are.
Can you help with personal injury claims?
No. Leaflegal does not cover personal injury, clinical negligence, housing disrepair or financial mis-selling claims. If you need help with one of these, the Law Society's Find a Solicitor service lists firms that do that kind of work, and Citizens Advice can explain where to start.
How do I complain about Leaflegal?
Email us with the word complaint in the subject line, telling us what went wrong and what you would like us to do. We acknowledge complaints within three working days and aim to give a full written reply within fifteen working days. Our complaints page has the details.
How do I complain about the firm?
Raise it with the firm first. Every regulated firm must have a complaints procedure. If the firm does not resolve a complaint about its service within eight weeks, you may be able to go to the Legal Ombudsman. Please tell us too, because we take how firms treat people seriously.
What should I have ready before the call?
Anything that explains your matter, such as letters, contracts, court papers, a memorandum of sale or invoices. A photo on your phone is fine at first. It also helps to note key dates and deadlines and a few questions about fees, timescales and who will handle your matter day to day.
Can I fill in the form for someone else?
You can make an enquiry on behalf of a family member or a business you work for, as long as they are happy for you to do so. The firm will usually need to speak to the person or business it is acting for directly, and it will need to carry out its own identity checks before taking instructions.
