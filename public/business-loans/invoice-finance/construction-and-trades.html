The basics

Construction invoicing is more complicated than a standard sale. Work is often paid through interim applications, a valuation is certified by the main contractor, and the final invoice comes weeks later. Retentions of 3% to 5% are held back for a year or more, and payment may be conditional on the main contractor being paid by the client. Standard invoice finance is built for simple invoices, so some providers are cautious about construction and others have specialist teams.

Where it works, invoice finance advances a large part of an approved invoice soon after it is issued, then pays the balance, less fees, when your customer pays. Lenders assess your customers’ credit more than your own, so a subcontractor working for a well-rated main contractor may qualify more easily than the size of the business suggests. A broker who knows the sector can point to providers who understand stage payments and retentions.

What businesses use it for

Waiting on main contractor payments

Release cash on certified applications for payment so wages, materials and subcontractors can be paid before the 45-day wait.

Supporting a growing order book

Take on more work from existing customers without running short of cash, since the finance grows as invoicing grows.

Covering CIS and payroll weeks

Smooth the timing between weekly labour costs and monthly payment cycles on larger projects.

Using one facility across several jobs

Apply finance only to selected invoices from specific customers, so you are not tied to financing your whole turnover.

How it fits

Advance rates and what is excluded

Providers typically advance a high percentage of eligible invoices, with the balance paid when the customer settles. Retentions, disputed amounts and work not yet certified are normally excluded or treated separately, so the amount you can draw is often smaller than your total invoicing.

Selective or whole turnover

Selective facilities let you finance chosen invoices and suit firms with a few large customers. Whole-ledger facilities cover all invoices and may cost less, but tie you in. For a firm that bills one main contractor heavily, a selective facility may be the easier fit.

What providers check

Customer credit quality, how long they take to pay, contract terms and any history of disputes. They want to see applications and certificates, not just invoices. A construction firm with clean paperwork and creditworthy customers is usually easier to place.

Things to consider

Retentions and pay-when-paid terms

Retentions remain tied up for months, and pay-when-paid clauses can delay settlement. Check how the provider treats these and whether the facility funds only the net amount, not the full invoice.

Customer knowledge

Some arrangements notify your customers that a lender is involved, while confidential facilities do not. Think about how your main contractor might react, and ask which arrangement the provider offers.

Costs and minimums

Fees are made up of a service charge and a discount charge, and may include minimum annual fees. Add these up against the benefit of faster cash, and compare with an unsecured loan or a revolving facility.

Questions and answers

Can I finance invoices that include retentions?
Usually the retention amount is excluded until it is released, though some providers can advance against retentions later or against invoices after practical completion. Ask the provider how it treats them. Some providers will also advance against an approved retention release, once the customer has confirmed it in writing.
Does the domestic reverse charge for VAT affect invoice finance?
It changes how VAT is shown on some construction invoices, since the customer accounts for the VAT. Providers who work in construction are used to this, and a broker can point you to those who are. Ask the provider how it handles invoices where VAT is not shown, so the advance is calculated on the correct amount.
Can I finance only one customer’s invoices?
Often yes, through a selective facility. This suits firms who are comfortable with the credit of one customer and prefer not to finance the rest. Fees may be slightly higher per invoice. Providers may set a minimum number or value of invoices each month, so ask what is expected.
What if my customer disputes an invoice?
Disputed invoices are generally excluded, or may have to be repaid to the lender if already advanced. Keep records of valuations, variations and approvals so disputes are minimal. A clear audit trail of applications, valuations and approvals makes disputes shorter and easier to resolve.
How long does set-up take?
Providers need to review your ledger, customers and contracts, and run credit checks, so a few weeks is common. Having a debtor list, recent accounts and sample invoices ready will help. Providers may also visit or phone your customers to verify invoices, so let your main contacts know it is coming.
