Referral arrangements

Leaflegal is an introducer. Under the SRA Code of Conduct, you must make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement you have with an introducer, and that the arrangement doesn’t affect your independence or your advice. Our consent wording explains to every client that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send you that wording with each lead so your client care letter can match it.

No prohibited referral fees

We only work in areas where paying for introductions is permitted. We don’t generate personal injury or clinical negligence enquiries, where referral fees are banned under LASPO, and we don’t handle financial mis-selling claims, which are regulated claims management.

The second gives consent for their details to be shared with a firm we match them with, and for that firm to contact them by phone, email or text about their matter. Are the leads exclusive?

What is included in each lead?
The client’s name, a UK mobile number and an email address, the practice area and type of matter, a short description in their own words, how urgent it is, where they are and the best time to call. You also get the source, a timestamp and the consent record.
Where do your leads come from?

More questions people ask

What makes family law marketing different?
Family clients are often in distress and are choosing someone to guide them through a very personal crisis. Trust, empathy, discretion and a fast human response matter more than clever advertising. Marketing that sounds aggressive or transactional can put people off. The firms that do best usually make clients feel understood quickly and are clear and honest about cost.
How do family clients choose a solicitor?
Many ask friends or family for a recommendation first, then look at websites and reviews. In a crisis, they often choose the first firm that responds quickly and makes them feel safe. They look for someone who listens, explains clearly, is honest about cost and has experience with situations like theirs. A calm, constructive approach reassures many clients.
Should we market ourselves as aggressive or constructive?
Most family clients want a fair outcome without making things worse, especially where children are involved. Marketing that sounds aggressive may attract a small group of clients but can put off many more. Many firms describe a constructive approach while making clear they will go to court when it is needed. It also helps to be consistent with how you actually work.
Which family matters are most valuable?
Contested financial remedy cases, complex children disputes and matters involving businesses, pensions or international elements usually generate the most work. But simple matters such as uncontested divorce can lead to further instructions and recommendations. It depends on your firm's capacity and expertise. Many firms choose a mix, and with Leaflegal you can select the matter types you want.
How important is specialism in family law marketing?
Quite important for complex work. Clients with a business, significant pensions, overseas assets or a relocation dispute want to know the firm has handled similar matters. You can show this through clear descriptions of the work your team does, articles and guides, and accreditations, while always respecting client confidentiality. Simpler matters benefit too, because clients like to know the firm handles their kind of case every week.
Should a small firm try to compete with larger family practices?
Yes. Many clients prefer a smaller firm where they deal directly with an experienced solicitor and feel personally looked after. Smaller firms can often respond faster and offer more continuity. Focus on what you do well, be clear about fees and make sure your intake is quick and warm. Choosing the matter types and locations that suit you also helps.
How long does family law marketing take to work?
It depends on the channel. Bought leads and paid search can bring enquiries within days of starting. Referral relationships and reputation build over months. SEO and content usually take longer still. Many firms combine faster channels with slower ones so that they have steady enquiries now while building long term sources that cost less over time.
Should we offer a free first consultation?
Some firms do and some do not. A free first call can attract more enquiries, but sometimes from people mainly looking for free information. A fixed fee initial consultation is easy to say yes to, sets expectations and values your time, and many family firms find it works well. You could also offer a short free call followed by a fixed fee consultation.
How should we talk about fees with family clients?
Clearly, early and kindly. Explain how you charge, give estimates for each stage and be honest about what could make costs rise, such as court proceedings. Confirm it in writing. Many clients have never paid a solicitor and are anxious about cost. Clear information often reassures them enough to take the first step.
Should we offer fixed fees in family law?
Where the work allows, fixed fees are popular with clients. They are common for uncontested divorce, consent orders and prenuptial agreements, and some firms offer fixed fees for defined stages of financial or children matters. For less predictable work, staged estimates give some certainty. Fixed fees can also bring in clients who later need further help.
What can we offer clients with a limited budget?
Options include staged fees, limited scope or unbundled help where the client represents themselves with your support, and payment plans for some matters. Some firms also explain other ways of funding fees where appropriate. Whatever you offer, explain the terms clearly in writing and make sure the client understands what is and is not included.
Do Leaflegal family leads include legal aid enquiries?
No. Every family lead has confirmed they are looking for paid legal help rather than legal aid or free advice. People who only want legal aid are pointed elsewhere before their enquiry is submitted. Budgets still vary, so being clear about cost on the first call remains important. Some clients will ask about staged fees or payment options, so it helps to have an answer ready.
Do we need to tell family clients about the referral fee?
Yes. The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement with an introducer, in writing, and that it does not affect your independence or advice. Most firms include it in the client care letter. Leaflegal's consent wording tells clients we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send it with each lead.
Which marketing channels work best for family law?
Most firms rely on a mix of recommendations, professional referrers, their website, search, reviews and, increasingly, bought leads. The right mix depends on your size, location and the work you want. Word of mouth tends to be strong for family firms with good client care, while leads and search can add volume you control.
Is SEO worth it for family law firms?
It can be, especially for local searches and specific questions people ask when separating. But family law search terms are competitive and results take time. Helpful, plain English content about common questions tends to work best. SEO is usually a long term investment that works alongside faster sources of enquiries such as leads or paid search.
Is paid search worth it for family law?
It can bring enquiries quickly, but family keywords can be expensive and many searches come from people looking for free information. Campaigns need careful targeting and regular management. If you use paid search, track the cost of each instruction, not each click, and compare it with your other channels. Negative keywords and careful location targeting can reduce wasted spend.
How can we build referrals from other professionals?
Get to know trusted financial advisers, accountants, mediators and counsellors in your area. Explain how you work and the kinds of clients you help. Refer clients to them where appropriate too. Any financial arrangement with a referrer must be disclosed to the client in writing and must not affect your independence.
How do we get reviews when family matters are so private?
Make it easy and entirely optional. Some clients are happy to leave a review once their matter is resolved, especially if they can do so without giving details. Never pressure anyone and respect those who decline. Even a small number of genuine reviews helps reassure new clients who are nervous about choosing a firm.
Should family firms use social media?
It can help keep your firm visible and show your approach, through short, helpful posts about common questions. Avoid anything that could identify clients or inflame disputes. Paid social advertising can reach people at the right moment but must follow your regulator's publicity rules and be handled sensitively given the subject matter.
