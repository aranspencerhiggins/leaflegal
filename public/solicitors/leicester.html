The right firm for you depends on the kind of matter you have, not just on being close by. It is worth asking any firm how it charges before work starts. Many matters are handled for a fixed fee, while others are charged by the hour. A good firm will explain the likely total, what could make it change and what is included, and confirm it in writing.

You can check that a solicitor is regulated on the Solicitors Regulation Authority register, and that a licensed conveyancer is regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. It is natural to want a solicitor who is close by. For some kinds of matter that is useful. For others, the location of the firm makes very little difference to the service you receive.

When local knowledge can help

Some matters involve a particular court, council or local authority. A motoring offence is usually heard at a magistrates' court in the area where the offence happened. A premises licence is applied for to the local council where the premises is, and the council's licensing policy and the views of local police and residents can matter. A family dispute may be heard at the local family court. In these cases a firm that knows the local procedures and people can be an advantage, and attending a hearing is easier if the firm is not far away.

When location matters less

Conveyancing, lease extensions and business debt recovery are now mostly handled by phone, email, post and online portals. Property searches and Land Registry applications are done electronically, county court claims for money are often issued online, and documents can be signed and returned without visiting an office. Many people use a firm in a different town and never meet in person, and are perfectly happy with the service.

Meeting in person

If meeting face to face matters to you, say so when you make your enquiry. Some people find it easier to discuss personal matters, such as a separation, in person. Others are more comfortable with video calls. Many firms offer both, and you can ask what the firm usually does on the first call.

Getting the balance right

In the end, experience in your type of matter, clear communication and fair fees usually matter more than the distance to the office. A nearby firm that rarely handles your kind of matter may not be the best choice, while a firm a few towns away that does it every day may suit you well. The firm we introduce handles your type of work and covers your area.

Area by area

Conveyancing firms routinely act for buyers and sellers all over England and Wales. What matters more is that the firm is on your lender's panel and responds quickly. Local knowledge can help with things like unusual local searches or properties affected by mining or flood risk, but a good conveyancer will know how to deal with those wherever the property is.

Lease extension work is mostly paperwork and negotiation with the freeholder's solicitor, supported by a valuation from a surveyor. The surveyor usually needs to know the local property market, but the solicitor can be based anywhere. If the matter goes to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber), hearings are often held remotely or at a regional venue.

Family solicitors often deal with the family court that covers the area where you or your children live. Many hearings now take place by video, but some are in person. Some people prefer a firm they can visit, especially during a difficult separation.

Motoring and licensing cases are the most local. Magistrates' courts, council licensing committees and local police all have their own ways of working. A firm that regularly appears before them can be a real help, and it may be able to attend a hearing at short notice.

Business debt recovery is usually handled through letters, negotiation and, if needed, the county court. Many money claims are issued and managed online, so the firm's location matters less than its experience with commercial debts.

Wales

England and Wales share one legal system, so firms in England can usually act for clients in Wales and the other way round. Some differences do apply in Wales, such as Land Transaction Tax instead of Stamp Duty Land Tax on property purchases, and some housing and planning rules. If you would like to deal with a firm in Welsh, mention it in the notes and the firm will tell you whether it can help.

Scotland and Northern Ireland

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own legal systems, so we do not cover matters there for conveyancing, family law, motoring offences, licensing, lease extensions or debt recovery. Immigration introductions are available across the whole of the UK.

Whether a firm is round the corner or at the other end of the country, it is worth checking it before you instruct it. This is easy to do and takes a few minutes.

Check the register

Solicitors and law firms in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can search for them on the SRA register. Licensed conveyancers are regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, which has its own register. Regulated immigration advisers can be checked with their regulator, such as the SRA or the Immigration Advice Authority.

Look for the regulatory information

Regulated law firms must show certain information on their websites, including who regulates them and their registration number. Many firms display the SRA's digital badge, which links to the firm's entry on the register. If a firm's website does not say who regulates it, ask.

Check the people, not just the firm

Ask who will handle your matter day to day. That might be a solicitor, a chartered legal executive, a licensed conveyancer or a paralegal working under supervision. All of these can be appropriate. It is reasonable to ask about their experience and who supervises them.

Read the paperwork

Before any work starts, the firm should send a client care letter and terms of business. These set out the fees, who will do the work, how complaints are handled and, if you were introduced by Leaflegal, the fee the firm pays for the introduction. Read them carefully and ask about anything you do not understand.

Every firm we introduce is regulated

Warning signs

Be cautious if anyone offering legal help will not say who regulates them, asks you to pay large sums before explaining their fees in writing, promises a particular result or pressures you to decide on the spot. Be especially careful with emails asking you to send money for a property purchase to new bank details. Always check bank details by phone using a number you already know before sending money to a firm.

Does a local firm cost more or less

Legal fees vary between firms, and location is only one factor. Firms in some large cities may have higher overheads, but that does not always mean higher prices for a particular piece of work. Fixed fee work, such as many house purchases, can be priced similarly across the country. The best way to understand the cost is to ask the firm for a written estimate or quote.

Travel and attendance

Where a solicitor needs to attend a court or hearing, the cost of travel and waiting time may be included in a fixed fee or charged separately. A local firm may charge less for travel to a local court. Ask how attendance is charged so you can compare like for like.

Disbursements

On top of the firm's own fees there may be disbursements, such as court fees, search fees, Land Registry fees, licensing application fees set by the council, or a barrister's fee. Official fees change from time to time, and GOV.UK lists many of them. The firm will tell you which apply.

Comparing quotes

If you speak to more than one firm, check that each quote covers the same work and includes the same items. A lower headline fee may leave out things that another firm includes. Ask each firm to confirm what is and is not included.

When is Stamp Duty or Land Transaction Tax paid?
Your conveyancer usually collects the tax from you before completion and submits the return and payment after completion, within a strict deadline. If the return is late, penalties and interest can apply. The amount depends on the price and your circumstances, and the rules change, so your firm will confirm the figure.
Why does my conveyancer need so much detail about my savings?
Regulated firms must check the source of funds for every purchase under money laundering rules. That usually means bank statements covering several months and an explanation of where larger sums came from, such as savings, a bonus, an inheritance or the sale of shares. It is a legal requirement and applies to every client, not just you.
What does exchange of contracts mean?
Exchange is the moment the contract becomes binding. Your conveyancer and the seller's conveyancer agree by phone that the signed contracts are exchanged, and your deposit is sent. From then on, if you do not complete, you could lose your deposit and be liable for the seller's losses.
Can I choose my completion date?
The completion date is agreed between buyer and seller before exchange, and in a chain everyone must agree the same date. You can suggest a date, but it needs to work for others too. Avoid booking removals until contracts have exchanged and the date is fixed.
What happens if completion is delayed?
If one side does not complete on the agreed date, the contract usually allows the other side to charge interest and, after a notice period, to cancel the contract and keep the deposit or claim losses. Delays are often short, but your conveyancer will explain what your contract says if it happens.
When do I get the keys?
You can usually collect the keys once the seller's conveyancer confirms they have received the money. The estate agent then releases the keys. In a chain, money often has to pass along several transactions, so the time you get the keys can vary on the day.
Can I visit the property between exchange and completion?
You have no automatic right to enter before completion, but sellers often agree to a visit to measure up or check something. Arrange it through the estate agent. Some buyers like to visit before completion to make sure fixtures and fittings agreed in the contract are still there.
Can I keep my pension if my spouse keeps the house?
This is called offsetting, and it is a common way of dealing with pensions. It can work well, but comparing a pension with a share of a house is not straightforward, because they are very different kinds of asset. A solicitor, and sometimes a pension expert, can help you understand what you would be giving up.
What is spousal maintenance?
It is regular payments from one former spouse to the other to help meet their income needs. It may be ordered for a fixed period or, less commonly, for longer. The court will consider whether the receiving person can adjust to independence over time. How much is paid depends on needs, income and earning capacity. It is not paid automatically, and in many cases there is a clean break instead.
How is child maintenance dealt with?
Child maintenance is usually arranged between parents or through the Child Maintenance Service, which uses a set formula. In some cases child maintenance can be included in a court order by agreement. Your solicitor can explain how it fits alongside a financial settlement. Child maintenance is separate from time with the children, and one does not depend on the other.
Can maintenance be changed later?
Usually yes. Maintenance orders can generally be varied if either person's circumstances change significantly. Some orders include terms that prevent the length being extended. Maintenance for a former spouse normally ends if they remarry. Changes are usually made by agreement or by applying to the court, and the court looks at all the circumstances, including any change in income, health or living arrangements. A solicitor can explain whether a variation might be possible.
What is a clean break?
A clean break means former spouses have no ongoing financial claims against each other once the order is carried out. The court must consider whether a clean break is possible. It is not always suitable, for example where one person cannot meet their needs without support. Where maintenance is needed, a clean break may still be possible later, once the maintenance term ends.
Can I get money to live on before the case ends?
Yes, in some cases. If agreement cannot be reached about temporary support, the court can order interim maintenance to cover essential needs until the case is finished. A solicitor can explain whether this might be appropriate and how it is applied for. Temporary arrangements can often be agreed without going to court.
How long does a financial case take?
If agreement is reached early, a settlement can sometimes be finalised in a few months. Cases that go through court can take a year or more, depending on complexity and court availability. Your solicitor will give you an idea once they understand your situation. Delays in disclosure and valuations are common causes of longer cases.
What is a high risk offender?
It is a category used by the DVLA for some drink driving offenders. It usually covers people with a reading of two and a half times the limit or more, people convicted of failing to provide a specimen, and people with two drink driving convictions within ten years. They must pass a medical assessment before their licence is returned.
Will my insurance go up after the ban?
Almost certainly. Insurers treat drink driving convictions seriously, and many will ask about them for several years. Some insurers decline cover, and specialist insurers may charge much more. Being honest with insurers when they ask is important, because failing to disclose a conviction can cause problems if you make a claim.
Can I drive abroad while banned in the UK?
A UK driving ban means you hold no valid licence during the ban, and an International Driving Permit relies on a valid licence. Different countries also have their own rules about recognising foreign bans. It is not something to assume. Check with the authorities of the country concerned and speak to a solicitor if you need to know more.
Can I appeal a drink driving ban?
There is usually a right of appeal to the Crown Court against conviction, sentence or both, within 21 days of sentence. In some cases you can apply for the ban to be suspended until the appeal is decided. Appeals need to have proper grounds. A solicitor can look at what happened and tell you whether an appeal is realistic.
Will my employer find out?
The court does not usually contact your employer. However, if you drive for work, your employer may check your licence or ask you to declare convictions, and some contracts require you to tell them. Certain professions also have reporting duties to a regulator. Your contract, staff handbook or professional body can tell you what applies to you.
How much does a drink driving solicitor cost?
Many firms offer a fixed fee for a guilty plea hearing, and a higher fee or hourly rate for a special reasons hearing or a trial. Expert evidence is usually extra. Fees vary between firms and areas. The firm will explain what is included and confirm it in writing before you agree to anything.
Is legal aid available?
Legal aid is not usually available for drink driving, because it depends mainly on whether there is a real risk of prison or other special factors. Most people pay privately. If your case is serious enough that legal aid might be available, the solicitor can tell you and explain how to apply.
What does the court look at on appeal?
The magistrates' court rehears the case and can consider evidence that was not before the committee, including events since the hearing. It gives weight to the council's decision, and the appellant usually has to persuade the court that the decision was wrong. The court can confirm, change or overturn the decision.
Will a review affect the sale of my business?
It can. A buyer will want to know about any review, its outcome and any conditions added. A revoked licence would usually need a new application. Even a review that ends with no action may be raised in due diligence. A solicitor can explain how the review is likely to be seen by buyers or landlords.
Will conditions added on review be permanent?
Conditions added on a review are usually permanent unless the committee says they apply only for a set period, which can be up to three months. Permanent conditions can later be changed through a variation, but councils will want to know why they are no longer needed. Your solicitor can help make sure any conditions offered are workable.
How much does help with a review cost?
It depends on how complex and contested the review is. Some firms offer a fixed fee for the whole review, while others charge in stages or by the hour. Extra hearings in a summary review, experts and appeals all add to the cost. Ask the firm for a written estimate and how they will update you.
Can I be ordered to pay costs?
Costs are not usually awarded at a council review hearing. On an appeal to the magistrates' court, the court can order the losing side to pay costs, and councils often ask for theirs if an appeal fails. Your solicitor will explain this risk before you decide to appeal.

How quickly can a firm help?
The firm usually contacts you within one working day, and often sooner. Reviews have fixed timetables, so it helps to get in touch as soon as you receive the application. If you have a hearing date or interim steps in place, say so on the form so the firm knows it is urgent.
Will the judgment be made public?
County Court judgments that are not paid within a set period are usually entered on the public Register of Judgments. This can affect the debtor's ability to get credit and is one reason many debtors pay promptly after judgment. Your solicitor can explain how registration works in your case.
Can a default judgment be overturned?
A debtor can apply to set it aside, for example if the claim was not properly served or if it has a real prospect of defending the claim and applies promptly. If that happens, your solicitor will explain the strength of the application and how to respond.
How long does the debtor have to pay after judgment?
A judgment usually orders payment either straight away or within a set period, often 14 days, unless the court has allowed instalments. If payment is not made by the date ordered, the creditor can start enforcement. Your solicitor will tell you the date in your judgment and what can be done once it passes.
Can I enforce a judgment against a sole trader?
Yes. A sole trader is personally liable for the debts of the business, so a judgment can be enforced against their personal assets as well as the business. Some methods, such as an attachment of earnings order, are only available against individuals. Your solicitor will explain which methods are available.
How much does a debt claim cost?
It depends on whether it is defended. Many firms offer fixed fees for issuing a claim and requesting default judgment. Defended claims cost more and are often charged by the hour or in stages. Court fees are payable on top. Ask the firm for a written estimate for each stage.
Will the debtor pay my legal costs?
If you win, the court usually orders the debtor to pay some of your costs, but in many debt claims the recoverable amount is fixed by the rules and may be well below what you have spent. On the small claims track, recoverable costs are very limited. Your solicitor will explain what to expect.
What if I lose?
If a claim fails, the claimant may be ordered to pay some of the debtor's legal costs, as well as bearing its own. This is one reason why a solicitor will look closely at the evidence and any dispute before recommending a claim, and will keep the risks under review as the case goes on.
How much does it cost to extend a short lease?
There is the premium paid to the freeholder, your own solicitor's and surveyor's fees, possibly some of the freeholder's costs and other fees such as Land Registry charges. For a short lease the premium is usually the largest part. A surveyor's valuation is the best way to get a realistic figure for your flat.
How long does an extension take?
Under the statutory route, the freeholder has at least two months to respond to the notice. If the premium is agreed, many extensions complete within a few months of the notice. If the case goes to the tribunal, it can take a year or longer. Your solicitor will give you an estimate based on your freeholder.
Is the premium fixed when the notice is served?
Under the statutory route, the valuation is generally carried out at the date the notice is served. This means the lease does not keep getting more expensive while the claim continues. It is one reason some owners with short leases choose to serve a notice sooner rather than later.

Can I borrow to pay for the extension?
Some owners pay from savings and others borrow, for example by arranging further lending from their current lender. With a short lease, some lenders may be cautious until the extension is complete, so it helps to speak to a mortgage broker early. Your solicitor will deal with the legal side of any borrowing linked to the new lease.
Will I have to pay my freeholder's costs?
Under the long standing statutory rules, a leaseholder usually pays certain reasonable legal and valuation costs of the freeholder, but not its tribunal costs. The 2024 Act moves towards each side paying its own costs, with exceptions, as part of the reforms being introduced over time. Your solicitor will confirm which rules apply to your claim.
Who should I speak to first?
Many people start with a solicitor who handles lease extensions, as they can check the lease, explain the options and recommend a specialist surveyor. If you are selling or remortgaging, your estate agent and mortgage broker will also need to know about the lease.
Why do advisers charge different amounts?
Fees vary depending on the adviser's experience, location, how complex the case is and what the fee includes. A higher fee may include more support, such as dealing with follow up requests. Comparing what is included, not just the headline price, gives a clearer picture.
How long will it take to get a decision?
Processing times vary and change over time. The Home Office publishes its current service standards on GOV.UK, and some applications may have a faster service available. Your adviser can tell you how long things are currently taking and what might affect the timing in your case.
Is legal aid available?
Legal aid is not usually available for most settlement applications, although it can be available in some limited circumstances. A regulated adviser can tell you whether any form of funding might apply to you.

Will I be charged for the first consultation?
Some advisers offer a short free first conversation and others charge a fixed fee for a full consultation. If there is a charge, it should be explained before the meeting. It is worth asking when the firm first contacts you so there are no surprises.
I have received a letter from the Home Office. What do I do?
Read it carefully and note the date it was sent and any deadline it mentions. If you already have an adviser, send it to them straight away. If not, many people contact a regulated adviser quickly, because some letters have short time limits.
