Choosing a solicitor in Bristol

The right firm for you depends on the kind of matter you have, not just on being close by. A conveyancing firm on the high street in Bristol may not handle a contested licence review, and a family law specialist will not usually deal with a speeding charge. It is worth asking any firm how it charges before work starts. Many matters are handled for a fixed fee, while others are charged by the hour. A good firm will explain the likely total, what could make it change and what is included, and confirm it in writing.

You can check that a solicitor is regulated on the Solicitors Regulation Authority register, and that a licensed conveyancer is regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. It is natural to want a solicitor who is close by. For some kinds of matter that is useful. For others, the location of the firm makes very little difference to the service you receive.

When local knowledge can help

Some matters involve a particular court, council or local authority. A motoring offence is usually heard at a magistrates' court in the area where the offence happened. A premises licence is applied for to the local council where the premises is, and the council's licensing policy and the views of local police and residents can matter. A family dispute may be heard at the local family court. In these cases a firm that knows the local procedures and people can be an advantage, and attending a hearing is easier if the firm is not far away.

When location matters less

Conveyancing, lease extensions and business debt recovery are now mostly handled by phone, email, post and online portals. Property searches and Land Registry applications are done electronically, county court claims for money are often issued online, and documents can be signed and returned without visiting an office. Many people use a firm in a different town and never meet in person, and are perfectly happy with the service.

Meeting in person

If meeting face to face matters to you, say so when you make your enquiry. Some people find it easier to discuss personal matters, such as a separation, in person. Others are more comfortable with video calls. Many firms offer both, and you can ask what the firm usually does on the first call.

Getting the balance right

In the end, experience in your type of matter, clear communication and fair fees usually matter more than the distance to the office. A nearby firm that rarely handles your kind of matter may not be the best choice, while a firm a few towns away that does it every day may suit you well. The firm we introduce handles your type of work and covers your area.

Area by area

Conveyancing firms routinely act for buyers and sellers all over England and Wales. What matters more is that the firm is on your lender's panel and responds quickly. Local knowledge can help with things like unusual local searches or properties affected by mining or flood risk, but a good conveyancer will know how to deal with those wherever the property is.

Lease extension work is mostly paperwork and negotiation with the freeholder's solicitor, supported by a valuation from a surveyor. The surveyor usually needs to know the local property market, but the solicitor can be based anywhere. If the matter goes to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber), hearings are often held remotely or at a regional venue.

Family solicitors often deal with the family court that covers the area where you or your children live. Many hearings now take place by video, but some are in person. Some people prefer a firm they can visit, especially during a difficult separation.

Motoring and licensing cases are the most local. Magistrates' courts, council licensing committees and local police all have their own ways of working. A firm that regularly appears before them can be a real help, and it may be able to attend a hearing at short notice.

Business debt recovery is usually handled through letters, negotiation and, if needed, the county court. Many money claims are issued and managed online, so the firm's location matters less than its experience with commercial debts.

Wales

England and Wales share one legal system, so firms in England can usually act for clients in Wales and the other way round. Some differences do apply in Wales, such as Land Transaction Tax instead of Stamp Duty Land Tax on property purchases, and some housing and planning rules. If you would like to deal with a firm in Welsh, mention it in the notes and the firm will tell you whether it can help.

Scotland and Northern Ireland

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own legal systems, so we do not cover matters there for conveyancing, family law, motoring offences, licensing, lease extensions or debt recovery. Immigration introductions are available across the whole of the UK.

Whether a firm is round the corner or at the other end of the country, it is worth checking it before you instruct it. This is easy to do and takes a few minutes.

Check the register

Solicitors and law firms in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can search for them on the SRA register. Licensed conveyancers are regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, which has its own register. Regulated immigration advisers can be checked with their regulator, such as the SRA or the Immigration Advice Authority.

Look for the regulatory information

Regulated law firms must show certain information on their websites, including who regulates them and their registration number. Many firms display the SRA's digital badge, which links to the firm's entry on the register. If a firm's website does not say who regulates it, ask.

Check the people, not just the firm

Ask who will handle your matter day to day. That might be a solicitor, a chartered legal executive, a licensed conveyancer or a paralegal working under supervision. All of these can be appropriate. It is reasonable to ask about their experience and who supervises them.

Read the paperwork

Before any work starts, the firm should send a client care letter and terms of business. These set out the fees, who will do the work, how complaints are handled and, if you were introduced by Leaflegal, the fee the firm pays for the introduction. Read them carefully and ask about anything you do not understand.

Every firm we introduce is regulated

Warning signs

Be cautious if anyone offering legal help will not say who regulates them, asks you to pay large sums before explaining their fees in writing, promises a particular result or pressures you to decide on the spot. Be especially careful with emails asking you to send money for a property purchase to new bank details. Always check bank details by phone using a number you already know before sending money to a firm.

Does a local firm cost more or less

Legal fees vary between firms, and location is only one factor. Firms in some large cities may have higher overheads, but that does not always mean higher prices for a particular piece of work. Fixed fee work, such as many house purchases, can be priced similarly across the country. The best way to understand the cost is to ask the firm for a written estimate or quote.

Travel and attendance

Where a solicitor needs to attend a court or hearing, the cost of travel and waiting time may be included in a fixed fee or charged separately. A local firm may charge less for travel to a local court. Ask how attendance is charged so you can compare like for like.

Disbursements

On top of the firm's own fees there may be disbursements, such as court fees, search fees, Land Registry fees, licensing application fees set by the council, or a barrister's fee. Official fees change from time to time, and GOV.UK lists many of them. The firm will tell you which apply.

Comparing quotes

If you speak to more than one firm, check that each quote covers the same work and includes the same items. A lower headline fee may leave out things that another firm includes. Ask each firm to confirm what is and is not included.

How long does a buy to let purchase take?
There is no fixed timescale. A simple purchase of a vacant freehold house with no chain can move quickly, while a leasehold flat with a tenant in place, a company buyer and a lender with strict conditions will usually take longer. Searches, the speed of the seller's side and the managing agent's response all play a part. Your conveyancer can give a better idea once they have seen the paperwork.
Can I buy through a limited company?
Yes. Many landlords now buy through a company set up to hold property. The company is the legal owner. Your conveyancer will need details of the company, its directors and its owners, and evidence that it can buy and borrow. Whether this route suits you is mainly a tax question for an accountant.
Will the lender want a personal guarantee?
Most lenders who lend to property companies want the directors to give personal guarantees. This means they can be personally liable for the company's mortgage debt. Lenders often require guarantors to have independent legal advice before signing, and this may have to come from a separate solicitor. Your conveyancer will explain the lender's requirements.
Does a company pay Stamp Duty?
Yes. A company buying residential property usually pays the higher rates of Stamp Duty Land Tax that apply to additional properties, and different rules can apply to higher value homes. Rates and reliefs change, so check GOV.UK or ask your conveyancer what applies to your purchase.
What documents will the firm need for a company purchase?
Typically the company's registration details, its articles of association, details of directors and people with significant control, identity documents for those people and evidence of where the purchase money is coming from. Some firms will also ask for board minutes approving the purchase and the mortgage. Having these ready at the start can save weeks.
Can I move my existing rental properties into a company?
It is possible, but the company is treated as buying the property, so it can trigger Stamp Duty Land Tax and capital gains tax, and you will usually need new mortgages. This is an area where specialist tax advice is essential before you start. Your conveyancer can then handle the legal transfers.
Can I buy through a company with a business partner?
Yes. Many property companies have two or more directors and shareholders. It is sensible for the owners to agree in writing how decisions are made, how profits are shared and what happens if one wants to leave, often in a shareholders' agreement. That is separate from the conveyancing, and a company or commercial solicitor usually prepares it. Your conveyancer will need identity checks on everyone involved.
Are prenups legally binding in England and Wales?
Not automatically. A court dealing with a divorce still decides the finances. However, the courts will usually give effect to a prenup that each person entered freely, with a full understanding of its effect, unless it would be unfair to hold them to it. A properly prepared agreement can carry a great deal of weight.
Do only wealthy people need a prenup?
No. Prenups are common where there is family wealth, but many couples with modest assets make one too. Someone who owns a flat, has savings from before the marriage or has children from a previous relationship may want clarity. The decision is a personal one. A solicitor can explain whether an agreement would make a real difference for you.
Is it unromantic to ask for a prenup?
Many people worry about this. Couples who go through the process often say the open conversation about money brought them closer. A solicitor can explain how to raise the subject and how the process works, so that it feels practical rather than adversarial. Raising it early, well before the wedding, usually makes the conversation easier for both of you.
Can civil partners make a similar agreement?
Yes. Couples entering a civil partnership can make a pre-registration agreement, which works in the same way as a prenup. Civil partners can also make an agreement after registration, similar to a postnuptial agreement. The same safeguards apply, including full disclosure, independent advice for each person and signing well before the registration date. The court approaches these agreements on dissolution in the same way as a prenup on divorce.
Can a prenup protect a future inheritance?
A prenup can say how an inheritance received during the marriage should be treated. Inherited assets are often treated differently from those built up together, but a court will still consider whether both people's needs are met. A solicitor can explain how an inheritance might be dealt with. Keeping an inheritance separate during the marriage can also help.
Can a prenup protect my business?
A prenup can set out how a business owned before the marriage, or a share in a family business, should be treated. This is one of the most common reasons people make one. A valuation of the business may be needed as part of disclosure. The agreement can also deal with any growth in the business during the marriage and whether that is shared.
What happens if we do not have a prenup?
If the marriage ends, the court decides how to divide the finances using a set of factors, including needs, contributions and the length of the marriage. Assets built up during the marriage are often shared. Assets brought in from outside may be treated differently, but there is no certainty. A prenup lets you set out your own view in advance.
Should I have a solicitor at my police interview?
Leaflegal cannot advise you on that. What we can say is that everyone interviewed under caution has a right to free legal advice, whatever their income. A solicitor can find out what the police allege before the interview and talk through how to approach it. The firm we introduce you to can explain this in more detail.
What does released under investigation mean?
It means the police have released you without charge and without bail conditions while they continue their enquiries. There is no fixed date for a decision. You may later be charged, interviewed again or told no further action will be taken. Your solicitor can contact the officer in the case for updates.
Will my case go to the Crown Court?
Not necessarily. Dangerous driving can be heard in either court. The magistrates decide whether the case is suitable for them, and you can choose a jury trial in the Crown Court if you plead not guilty. Magistrates can also send a case to the Crown Court for sentence. A solicitor will explain the pros and cons of each court for your case.
What happens at the first hearing?
The first hearing takes place at the magistrates' court. You confirm your details and indicate whether you intend to plead guilty or not guilty. The court then decides where the case should be heard. If the case stays in the magistrates' court and you plead guilty, sentence may happen that day or later after a report.
Will I need a barrister?
Crown Court cases usually involve a barrister, who represents you at hearings and trial, working with your solicitor. Some solicitors have higher rights of audience and can appear in the Crown Court themselves. In the magistrates' court, a solicitor normally represents you. Your firm will explain who will be doing what.
How long will the case take?
It depends on the length of the police investigation, whether you plead guilty, which court hears the case and how busy that court is. A guilty plea in the magistrates' court may be dealt with fairly quickly. Crown Court trials can take many months to come to court. Your solicitor can give you an idea once the case is underway.
Can I be banned before the case finishes?
In some circumstances the court can impose an interim disqualification, for example when a case is sent to the Crown Court or adjourned before sentence. If this happens, the time spent banned is usually taken into account when the final ban is decided. Your solicitor can explain whether this is likely to apply.
What is an operating schedule?
It is the part of the application that sets out what you will do, when you will do it and the steps you will take to promote the four licensing objectives. Those steps usually become conditions on your licence. A clear and realistic operating schedule makes it easier for the authorities to support the application and for you to comply later.
What must the plan show?
The plan must show the boundaries of the premises, exits and escape routes, fire safety equipment, any stage or raised area, the bar area, toilets and the areas used for licensable activities, using standard symbols or a key. Councils can reject applications with poor plans. Many operators use a specialist plan drawer to avoid problems.
Can I include an outside area?
Yes, outside areas such as beer gardens and pavement seating can be included on the plan. Whether to include them inside the licensed area affects where alcohol can be sold and consumed. Pavement seating on the public highway usually needs separate permission from the council. A solicitor can explain how to deal with outdoor space.
Who should be the DPS?
The designated premises supervisor must hold a personal licence and is usually the person in day to day charge of the premises. The police will treat them as the main contact. They need to be suitable, available and willing to take on the role. Your solicitor can explain what the role involves before you choose someone.
How long must the blue notice be displayed?
The notice must be displayed for a continuous period of 28 days, starting the day after the application is given to the council. It must be prominently placed where it can easily be read from outside. If it is missing for part of that time, the council may require the notice period to start again.
Do I have to advertise in a newspaper?
Yes, for a new premises licence a notice must be published in a local newspaper, or a local newsletter or circular if there is no local paper, within a short time after the application goes in. The wording follows a set format. Your solicitor will usually draft and arrange it for you.
Can I change the application after it goes in?
Minor amendments, especially agreeing extra conditions with the authorities, are often possible during the consultation. Bigger changes, such as adding activities or extending hours, may need a new application and new notices. Your solicitor will explain what can be changed without starting again.
Is claiming interest bad for customer relationships?
It can be, and some businesses choose not to claim with customers they value. Others find that knowing interest will be added encourages prompt payment. It is a commercial decision for your business. Your solicitor can tell you what is at stake so you can decide.
Do you help with consumer debts?
No. Leaflegal only introduces businesses that are owed money by other businesses in England and Wales. Late payment law does not apply to consumer contracts, and consumer debt collection is outside this service.
How is statutory interest calculated?
It is simple interest at 8% a year above the Bank of England base rate, calculated on the unpaid amount for each day the payment is late. The base rate used is fixed for six month periods. Your solicitor will calculate the total and the daily rate that continues until payment.
Which base rate is used?
For debts that become late between January and June, the base rate in force on the previous 31 December is used. For debts that become late between July and December, the rate on 30 June is used. That rate then applies to the debt even if the base rate changes later.
When does interest start to run?
If your contract sets a payment date, interest usually starts the day after that date. If no date was agreed, the law generally allows 30 days after the later of delivery and the customer receiving the invoice. Your solicitor will check the start date for each invoice.
Is it simple or compound interest?
Statutory interest is simple interest. It is calculated on the original unpaid amount and is not added to the debt and then charged interest itself. A contract can provide for a different arrangement, which your solicitor can explain.
Can I claim interest after the invoice has been paid?
Usually, yes. The right to statutory interest and compensation arises when the payment becomes late, so it can often be claimed even after the invoice itself has been paid late. Whether it is worth pursuing is a commercial decision for your business.
My flat is above a shop. Can I still extend?
Usually, yes. The right to extend is available to qualifying flat owners whether or not the building contains commercial units. The stricter rules about commercial space mainly affect buying the freehold of the whole building rather than extending an individual lease.
Can I extend if I own the share of freehold?
If you already own a share of the freehold, you may be able to grant yourself a longer lease by agreement with the other freeholders, which is often simpler than the statutory route. A solicitor will look at how your freehold is held and explain the options.
What if there are two landlords?
Some flats have a head lease between the freeholder and the flat owner, so there are two or more landlords. The statutory process deals with this by involving a competent landlord who runs the claim, with the premium split between the different landlords. Your solicitor will identify everyone involved.
What is in a section 42 notice?
The notice includes details of the leaseholder and landlord, a description of the flat, details of the lease, the premium the leaseholder proposes and any other amounts, the terms proposed for the new lease, and the date by which the landlord must reply. The details must be accurate, and your solicitor will check them against the lease and the title before serving it.
How long does the freeholder have to respond?
The notice states a date by which the freeholder must serve a counter notice. That date must be at least two months after the notice is given. In the meantime, the freeholder can ask for proof of title, ask for a deposit and arrange to inspect the flat for valuation.
What if the freeholder does not reply?
If no counter notice is served in time, the leaseholder can usually apply to the county court for an order that the new lease is granted on the terms in their notice. There is a deadline for this application too. Your solicitor will act quickly if the freeholder misses its date.
What happens if we cannot agree the price?
Either side can apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) once two months have passed after the counter notice. The application must be made within a fixed window, or the claim is treated as withdrawn. Most cases still settle before a hearing, but the application protects the claim while negotiations continue.
Should I just apply again?
Some people do make a new application after a refusal, and for others a different step is more suitable. Which approach fits your situation depends on the reasons for refusal and your circumstances. A regulated adviser can explain the options and the risks of each.
I do not understand the letter. Is that normal?
Very normal. Refusal letters are written in formal language and refer to rules and guidance that most people have never read. Advisers read these letters every day. They can explain what each part means and what the Home Office is saying.
Will a refusal affect future applications?
It may need to be mentioned on future forms, and the reasons for refusal can be relevant later. How much it matters depends on the decision and your circumstances. A regulated adviser can explain how your refusal might affect any future plans, including applications for family members.
How quickly can an adviser help?
Firms usually contact people within one working day of an enquiry, often sooner. When there is a deadline, we flag it to the firm. Many advisers can act quickly in urgent cases. If your deadline is very close, say so clearly when you enquire.
What will the adviser need from me?
In general, the refusal letter, a copy of the original application, the documents you sent, your passport and any other Home Office letters. They will also want to hear what has happened since. If something is missing, tell them and they will work with what you have.
Can I use the same adviser who did my original application?
You can, and some people prefer to. Others want a fresh pair of eyes after a refusal. Either way, the adviser handling things now will need the full file. Your previous adviser should be able to provide a copy if you ask.
Will I have to go to a hearing?
Some matters involve a hearing and others do not. If there is a hearing, it may be in person or by video, and your adviser will explain what to expect and help you prepare. Interpreters can be arranged if needed.
