Choosing a solicitor in Exeter

The right firm for you depends on the kind of matter you have, not just on being close by. A conveyancing firm on the high street in Exeter may not handle a contested licence review, and a family law specialist will not usually chase a business debt. That is why we ask what your matter is about before we introduce anyone.

It is worth asking any firm how it charges before work starts. Many matters are handled for a fixed fee, while others are charged by the hour. A good firm will explain the likely total, what could make it change and what is included, and confirm it in writing.

You can check that a solicitor is regulated on the Solicitors Regulation Authority register, and that a licensed conveyancer is regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Every firm we introduce in Exeter and Devon is regulated, and you are free to decide whether to instruct them.

It is natural to want a solicitor who is close by. For some kinds of matter that is useful. For others, the location of the firm makes very little difference to the service you receive.

When local knowledge can help

Some matters involve a particular court, council or local authority. A premises licence is applied for to the local council where the premises is, and the council's licensing policy and the views of local police and residents can matter. A family dispute may be heard at the local family court. In these cases a firm that knows the local procedures and people can be an advantage, and attending a hearing is easier if the firm is not far away.

When location matters less

Conveyancing, lease extensions and business debt recovery are now mostly handled by phone, email, post and online portals. Property searches and Land Registry applications are done electronically, county court claims for money are often issued online, and documents can be signed and returned without visiting an office. Many people use a firm in a different town and never meet in person, and are perfectly happy with the service.

Meeting in person

If meeting face to face matters to you, say so when you make your enquiry. Some people find it easier to discuss personal matters, such as a separation, in person. Others are more comfortable with video calls. Many firms offer both, and you can ask what the firm usually does on the first call.

Getting the balance right

In the end, experience in your type of matter, clear communication and fair fees usually matter more than the distance to the office. A nearby firm that rarely handles your kind of matter may not be the best choice, while a firm a few towns away that does it every day may suit you well. The firm we introduce handles your type of work and covers your area.

Area by area

Conveyancing firms routinely act for buyers and sellers all over England and Wales. What matters more is that the firm is on your lender's panel and responds quickly. Local knowledge can help with things like unusual local searches or properties affected by mining or flood risk, but a good conveyancer will know how to deal with those wherever the property is.

Lease extension work is mostly paperwork and negotiation with the freeholder's solicitor, supported by a valuation from a surveyor. The surveyor usually needs to know the local property market, but the solicitor can be based anywhere. If the matter goes to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber), hearings are often held remotely or at a regional venue.

Family solicitors often deal with the family court that covers the area where you or your children live. Many hearings now take place by video, but some are in person. Some people prefer a firm they can visit, especially during a difficult separation.

Licensing cases are the most local. Council licensing committees, local police and magistrates' courts all have their own ways of working. A firm that regularly appears before them can be a real help, and it may be able to attend a hearing at short notice.

Business debt recovery is usually handled through letters, negotiation and, if needed, the county court. Many money claims are issued and managed online, so the firm's location matters less than its experience with commercial debts.

Wales

England and Wales share one legal system, so firms in England can usually act for clients in Wales and the other way round. Some differences do apply in Wales, such as Land Transaction Tax instead of Stamp Duty Land Tax on property purchases, and some housing and planning rules. If you would like to deal with a firm in Welsh, mention it in the notes and the firm will tell you whether it can help.

Scotland and Northern Ireland

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own legal systems, so we do not cover matters there for conveyancing, family law, licensing, lease extensions or debt recovery. Immigration introductions are available across the whole of the UK.

Check the register

Solicitors and law firms in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can search for them on the SRA register. Licensed conveyancers are regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, which has its own register. Regulated immigration advisers can be checked with their regulator, such as the SRA or the Immigration Advice Authority.

Look for the regulatory information

Regulated law firms must show certain information on their websites, including who regulates them and their registration number. Many firms display the SRA's digital badge, which links to the firm's entry on the register. If a firm's website does not say who regulates it, ask.

Check the people, not just the firm

Ask who will handle your matter day to day. That might be a solicitor, a chartered legal executive, a licensed conveyancer or a paralegal working under supervision. All of these can be appropriate. It is reasonable to ask about their experience and who supervises them.

Read the paperwork

Before any work starts, the firm should send a client care letter and terms of business. These set out the fees, who will do the work, how complaints are handled and, if you were introduced by Leaflegal, the fee the firm pays for the introduction. Read them carefully and ask about anything you do not understand.

Warning signs

Be cautious if anyone offering legal help will not say who regulates them, asks you to pay large sums before explaining their fees in writing, promises a particular result or pressures you to decide on the spot. Be especially careful with emails asking you to send money for a property purchase to new bank details. Always check bank details by phone using a number you already know before sending money to a firm.

Does a local firm cost more or less

Legal fees vary between firms, and location is only one factor. Firms in some large cities may have higher overheads, but that does not always mean higher prices for a particular piece of work. Fixed fee work, such as many house purchases, can be priced similarly across the country. The best way to understand the cost is to ask the firm for a written estimate or quote.

Travel and attendance

Where a solicitor needs to attend a court or hearing, the cost of travel and waiting time may be included in a fixed fee or charged separately. A local firm may charge less for travel to a local court. Ask how attendance is charged so you can compare like for like.

Disbursements

On top of the firm's own fees there may be disbursements, such as court fees, search fees, Land Registry fees, licensing application fees set by the council, or a barrister's fee. Official fees change from time to time, and GOV.UK lists many of them. The firm will tell you which apply.

Comparing quotes

If you speak to more than one firm, check that each quote covers the same work and includes the same items. A lower headline fee may leave out things that another firm includes. Ask each firm to confirm what is and is not included.

Is buying a rental property different from buying a home?
The core legal work is the same. Your conveyancer checks the title, orders searches, raises enquiries and handles the money. The difference is in the extra checks around letting, such as lease restrictions, licensing, existing tenancies and the conditions in a buy to let mortgage. The firm will also need to know from the start whether you are buying personally or through a company.
Do I need a buy to let mortgage?
If you plan to let the property and are borrowing, you will usually need a mortgage designed for letting. A standard residential mortgage normally forbids letting without the lender's consent. Letting in breach of your mortgage terms can have serious consequences. Your mortgage broker can explain the options, and your conveyancer will check the offer conditions.
Can I let my old home instead of selling it?
Many people do, sometimes called being an accidental landlord. You will usually need your lender's consent to let, or a new buy to let mortgage. Buying your next home while keeping the old one can mean paying higher rates of Stamp Duty Land Tax. Your conveyancer can explain how the tax applies, and a tax adviser can help with the wider picture.
Can I buy a property at auction to let?
Yes, but auction purchases move quickly. When the hammer falls, contracts are usually exchanged and completion often follows within a short fixed period. That means the legal checks need to happen before the auction. A conveyancer can review the legal pack for you in advance, and you will need your finance ready in time.
Can I buy a house in multiple occupation?
Yes, but these properties carry extra rules. A large house in multiple occupation needs a mandatory licence, and smaller ones may need one under local schemes. Planning permission can also be needed. Many lenders have specific products for these properties. Your conveyancer will raise enquiries about licensing and planning, and it is wise to check with the council too.
Does the conveyancer check whether the property is a good investment?
No. Your conveyancer checks the legal title and raises questions about anything that might affect your ownership or your plans. Whether the rent, price and location make sense as an investment is a commercial decision for you. Many landlords speak to letting agents, mortgage brokers and accountants alongside their conveyancer.
How long does a buy to let purchase take?
There is no fixed timescale. A simple purchase of a vacant freehold house with no chain can move quickly, while a leasehold flat with a tenant in place, a company buyer and a lender with strict conditions will usually take longer. Searches, the speed of the seller's side and the managing agent's response all play a part. Your conveyancer can give a better idea once they have seen the paperwork.
Can the 20 week period be shortened?
Only in very limited circumstances. The court can shorten the 20 week period if the applicant shows a good reason, but this is rare and is decided case by case. In most divorces the full 20 weeks applies. A solicitor can explain whether there is any realistic basis for asking.
Does the divorce decide who gets the house?
No. The divorce ends the marriage but does not divide property. That is dealt with through a financial agreement or financial court proceedings. Until then, ownership stays as it is on the title deeds, whatever happens with the divorce. If you do not own the home but live there, you may be able to protect your right to stay. A solicitor can explain how.
Can I be made to leave the family home?
Getting divorced does not by itself mean you have to leave. Both spouses usually have a right to live in the family home until the final order, or until the court decides otherwise. If there are concerns about safety, the court can make orders about who lives there. A solicitor can explain your position.
Do I need a financial order if we have no assets?
Even couples with little money sometimes choose a consent order with a clean break. This stops either person making a claim later if their circumstances change, for example after an inheritance or a pay rise. A solicitor can explain whether it is worth considering in your situation. The cost is often modest compared with the protection it gives.
What happens to my will?
Divorce does not cancel a will, but your former spouse is usually treated as if they had died. Gifts to them and their appointment as executor may no longer take effect. Many people make a new will when they separate, as the old one may not reflect their wishes. Divorce does not affect a will in the same way as marriage.
Does divorce affect arrangements for our children?
The divorce itself does not decide where children live or how they spend time with each parent. Parents usually agree this between themselves, with help from mediation or solicitors if needed. Both parents keep parental responsibility after divorce. If you cannot agree, a solicitor can explain options including mediation and, as a last resort, an application to court.
Can I go back to my previous surname?
Yes. Many people start using their previous surname again during or after a divorce. Banks and other organisations often accept your marriage certificate with your birth certificate, or a final order. Some may ask for a deed poll. You do not need a court order to change your name back.
What is a provisional statement?
It is an application made where premises are being built, extended or changed. If granted, it gives a strong indication that a licence will follow once the work is finished in line with the plans. It can be useful for investors and lenders. A solicitor can explain whether it is worth applying for one in your case.
Is there a right to work check?
Yes. If an individual applies for a premises licence, the council must check that they have the right to work in the UK. Copies of documents usually go in with the application. Companies are not checked in the same way. Your solicitor will tell you what documents are needed.
What is an operating schedule?
It is the part of the application that sets out what you will do, when you will do it and the steps you will take to promote the four licensing objectives. Those steps usually become conditions on your licence. A clear and realistic operating schedule makes it easier for the authorities to support the application and for you to comply later.
What must the plan show?
The plan must show the boundaries of the premises, exits and escape routes, fire safety equipment, any stage or raised area, the bar area, toilets and the areas used for licensable activities, using standard symbols or a key. Councils can reject applications with poor plans. Many operators use a specialist plan drawer to avoid problems.
Can I include an outside area?
Yes, outside areas such as beer gardens and pavement seating can be included on the plan. Whether to include them inside the licensed area affects where alcohol can be sold and consumed. Pavement seating on the public highway usually needs separate permission from the council. A solicitor can explain how to deal with outdoor space.
Who should be the DPS?
The designated premises supervisor must hold a personal licence and is usually the person in day to day charge of the premises. The police will treat them as the main contact. They need to be suitable, available and willing to take on the role. Your solicitor can explain what the role involves before you choose someone.
How long must the blue notice be displayed?
The notice must be displayed for a continuous period of 28 days, starting the day after the application is given to the council. It must be prominently placed where it can easily be read from outside. If it is missing for part of that time, the council may require the notice period to start again.
Is a statutory demand the same as a letter before action?
No. A letter before action is a step before an ordinary court claim and gives the debtor a chance to respond. A statutory demand is an insolvency tool that can lead to winding up or bankruptcy. Some creditors send a letter before action first, and then consider a statutory demand if the debt is undisputed and remains unpaid.
Can a statutory demand be used on a debt owed by a sole trader?
Yes. A sole trader is an individual, so a statutory demand served on them can lead to a bankruptcy petition if the debt meets the bankruptcy threshold and is not paid. Different rules on service and challenges apply to individuals. Your solicitor will explain how this works and whether it is proportionate.
How is a statutory demand served on a company?
It is usually served by leaving it at the company's registered office, as shown on the public register. Your solicitor will check the address, arrange delivery, often through a process server, and keep evidence of when and how it was served. That evidence is needed if a petition follows.
How is a statutory demand served on a sole trader?
For an individual, the demand should usually be handed to them personally where possible. If that cannot be done after reasonable attempts, the rules allow other methods, but the creditor must be able to show the court what efforts were made. Your solicitor will arrange this carefully.
Can I email a statutory demand?
Email alone is not usually enough. The rules on service are strict, and a petition based on a demand that was not properly served can fail. Your solicitor will use a method of service that the court accepts and will keep a clear record of it.
What must the demand say?
It must follow a prescribed form and include details of the creditor and debtor, the amount owed and how it arose, any interest or charges and how they were calculated, and information about how the debtor can respond. Your solicitor will prepare the demand so that it meets these requirements.
Can I include interest in the demand?
Yes, if the interest is properly due, for example under your contract or under late payment law for debts between businesses. It must be shown separately with an explanation of how it was calculated. Including sums that are not due can give the debtor grounds to challenge the demand.
Why is 80 years such an important figure?
Under the long standing valuation rules, marriage value only applies once a lease has 80 years or fewer remaining. Crossing that line can add a large amount to the premium. The 2024 Act removes marriage value, but this is part of the reforms still being brought in. Your surveyor can explain whether 80 years matters for you.
Can I negotiate the premium?
Yes. Most statutory claims settle by negotiation between the two surveyors. Your surveyor will use market evidence and their experience to argue for a fair figure. If agreement is not possible, the tribunal can decide.
Does my ground rent affect the price?
Yes. The freeholder is giving up its future ground rent, so the higher it is, and the faster it rises, the more the freeholder can claim. Ground rents that double regularly can have a noticeable effect on the premium.
Will I pay my freeholder's costs?
Under the long standing rules, the leaseholder pays certain reasonable legal and valuation costs of the freeholder, but not tribunal costs. The 2024 Act moves towards each side paying its own costs, with exceptions. Your solicitor will confirm the position that applies on the date your claim starts.
Is the premium paid all at once?
Yes, the premium is normally paid in full on completion of the new lease, together with any costs payable. If you are borrowing to pay it, your lender will need to be involved early so the money is ready in time.
How does the 2024 Act change statutory extensions?
Among other things, it provides for a standard extension of 990 years for flats, removes marriage value, sets some valuation rates by regulation, removed the two year ownership rule from 31 January 2025 and generally makes each side pay its own costs. Some of these are already in force and others depend on further regulations. A solicitor will confirm what applies now.
Will I get 90 years or 990 years?
Under the long standing rules, a statutory extension adds 90 years. The 2024 Act provides for 990 years instead. Which applies to you depends on when the relevant part of the Act comes into force and on the transitional rules. Your solicitor will tell you which length applies when your notice is served.
Is my application public?
Yes. Once filed, application details including the owner's name and address appear on the public register. Firms often act as the address for service so that official letters go to them. Be wary of unsolicited invoices that look official after you file.
What if I already have an EU trademark?
EU trademarks registered before the end of the Brexit transition period were cloned into comparable UK marks automatically. Applications filed after that date need a separate UK application to protect the brand in the UK. A firm can check what you already hold.
Can a firm act for me if I am outside the UK?
Yes. A UK address for service is needed for UK trademark matters, and a UK firm can provide it. Overseas businesses often use a UK solicitor or attorney to file and look after their UK marks.
What happens if the examiner objects?
The UK IPO sends an examination report explaining the objection and setting a deadline to reply. The firm can argue that the mark is acceptable, provide evidence that it has become distinctive through use, narrow the specification or suggest changes. Many objections are resolved this way.
What is a notice of threatened opposition?
During the first two months after publication, someone considering an opposition can file a notice of threatened opposition, which extends their deadline to three months. It is often a sign that the other side wants to negotiate. The firm can contact them to understand their concerns.
Can an opposition be settled?
Yes, and many are. Common outcomes include narrowing the goods and services, agreeing how each side will use its mark, or a coexistence agreement. A cooling off period can sometimes be agreed to give time for talks before formal steps are taken.
What if I lose an opposition?
The application may be refused in whole or in part, and you may have to pay a contribution to the other side's costs, which at the UK IPO is usually set on a published scale. You may still be able to use the brand in some circumstances, but the firm should advise on the risk first.
What is a partner visa?
It is permission for someone to live in the UK with their husband, wife, civil partner, fiancé, fiancée or long term partner. People often call it a spouse visa. A regulated adviser can explain what a partner application would involve for you and whether it may be an option in your circumstances.
Can Leaflegal tell us if we qualify?
No. Leaflegal is not regulated to give immigration advice and never does. We do not ask about your immigration history or assess your chances. We introduce you to a firm or adviser who is regulated, and they will look at your situation and explain your options.
Is a spouse visa the same as a partner visa?
People use the two terms in different ways, and the Home Office treats different kinds of relationship in different ways. A regulated adviser will explain how your relationship would be treated and what that means for any application. Mention whether you are married, in a civil partnership, engaged or living together when you enquire, as it helps the adviser prepare for the first conversation.
My partner is overseas. Can you help?
Yes. We introduce couples to regulated advisers who handle applications made from outside the UK. Many advisers work by phone, video and email, so it does not matter where each of you is. Tell us where your partner is when you make your enquiry.
We are already in the UK together. Is this page for us?
It can be. Many couples who are already living here look for advice about their next step, such as a change of visa or a renewal. Tell us a little about your situation when you enquire, and the adviser will explain how they can help.
Can I enquire on behalf of my partner?
Yes, either partner can make the enquiry. The adviser will usually want to speak to both of you at some point, because they will need information from each of you. Let us know the best way to reach you both. If your partner is in a different time zone, tell us the best times to reach each of you.
Why do partner rules change so often?
Immigration policy is reviewed regularly by the government, and the rules for partners have been changed several times over the years. Fees, guidance and requirements can all move. That is why it is risky to rely on what worked for friends or family in the past. A regulated adviser explains things based on the rules that apply when you apply.
