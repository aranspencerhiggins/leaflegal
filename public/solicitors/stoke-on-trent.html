It is natural to want a solicitor who is close by. For some kinds of matter that is useful. For others, the location of the firm makes very little difference to the service you receive.

When local knowledge can help

Some matters involve a particular court, council or local authority. A motoring offence is usually heard at a magistrates' court in the area where the offence happened. A premises licence is applied for to the local council where the premises is, and the council's licensing policy and the views of local police and residents can matter. A family dispute may be heard at the local family court. In these cases a firm that knows the local procedures and people can be an advantage, and attending a hearing is easier if the firm is not far away.

When location matters less

Conveyancing, lease extensions and business debt recovery are now mostly handled by phone, email, post and online portals. Property searches and Land Registry applications are done electronically, county court claims for money are often issued online, and documents can be signed and returned without visiting an office. Many people use a firm in a different town and never meet in person, and are perfectly happy with the service.

Meeting in person

If meeting face to face matters to you, say so when you make your enquiry. Some people find it easier to discuss personal matters, such as a separation, in person. Others are more comfortable with video calls. Many firms offer both, and you can ask what the firm usually does on the first call.

Getting the balance right

In the end, experience in your type of matter, clear communication and fair fees usually matter more than the distance to the office. A nearby firm that rarely handles your kind of matter may not be the best choice, while a firm a few towns away that does it every day may suit you well. The firm we introduce handles your type of work and covers your area.

Area by area

Conveyancing firms routinely act for buyers and sellers all over England and Wales. What matters more is that the firm is on your lender's panel and responds quickly. Local knowledge can help with things like unusual local searches or properties affected by mining or flood risk, but a good conveyancer will know how to deal with those wherever the property is.

Lease extension work is mostly paperwork and negotiation with the freeholder's solicitor, supported by a valuation from a surveyor. The surveyor usually needs to know the local property market, but the solicitor can be based anywhere. If the matter goes to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber), hearings are often held remotely or at a regional venue.

Family solicitors often deal with the family court that covers the area where you or your children live. Many hearings now take place by video, but some are in person. Some people prefer a firm they can visit, especially during a difficult separation.

Motoring and licensing cases are the most local. Magistrates' courts, council licensing committees and local police all have their own ways of working. A firm that regularly appears before them can be a real help, and it may be able to attend a hearing at short notice.

Business debt recovery is usually handled through letters, negotiation and, if needed, the county court. Many money claims are issued and managed online, so the firm's location matters less than its experience with commercial debts.

England and Wales share one legal system, so firms in England can usually act for clients in Wales and the other way round. Some differences do apply in Wales, such as Land Transaction Tax instead of Stamp Duty Land Tax on property purchases, and some housing and planning rules. If you would like to deal with a firm in Welsh, mention it in the notes and the firm will tell you whether it can help.

Check the register

Solicitors and law firms in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can search for them on the SRA register. Licensed conveyancers are regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, which has its own register. Regulated immigration advisers can be checked with their regulator, such as the SRA or the Immigration Advice Authority.

Look for the regulatory information

Regulated law firms must show certain information on their websites, including who regulates them and their registration number. Many firms display the SRA's digital badge, which links to the firm's entry on the register. If a firm's website does not say who regulates it, ask.

Check the people, not just the firm

Ask who will handle your matter day to day. That might be a solicitor, a chartered legal executive, a licensed conveyancer or a paralegal working under supervision. All of these can be appropriate. It is reasonable to ask about their experience and who supervises them.

Read the paperwork

Before any work starts, the firm should send a client care letter and terms of business. These set out the fees, who will do the work, how complaints are handled and, if you were introduced by Leaflegal, the fee the firm pays for the introduction. Read them carefully and ask about anything you do not understand.

Does a local firm cost more or less

Legal fees vary between firms, and location is only one factor. Firms in some large cities may have higher overheads, but that does not always mean higher prices for a particular piece of work. Fixed fee work, such as many house purchases, can be priced similarly across the country. The best way to understand the cost is to ask the firm for a written estimate or quote.

Travel and attendance

Where a solicitor needs to attend a court or hearing, the cost of travel and waiting time may be included in a fixed fee or charged separately. A local firm may charge less for travel to a local court. Ask how attendance is charged so you can compare like for like.

Disbursements

On top of the firm's own fees there may be disbursements, such as court fees, search fees, Land Registry fees, licensing application fees set by the council, or a barrister's fee. Official fees change from time to time, and GOV.UK lists many of them. The firm will tell you which apply.

Comparing quotes

If you speak to more than one firm, check that each quote covers the same work and includes the same items. A lower headline fee may leave out things that another firm includes. Ask each firm to confirm what is and is not included.

Can I remortgage a property I own outright?
Yes. If you own your home without a mortgage and want to borrow against it, the process is similar but there is no existing lender to pay off. Your conveyancer checks the title, completes the new mortgage and registers it, then sends you the money.
Will I get the extra money on the day of completion?
If you are borrowing more than you owe, your conveyancer receives the full advance from the new lender, pays off the old mortgage and any debts the lender requires to be cleared, deducts agreed fees and sends you the rest. This usually happens on completion day or the next working day, to an account you have confirmed by phone.
How long does a remortgage take?
A straightforward remortgage often takes a few weeks from the mortgage offer to completion. It can take longer if there are title problems, consents to obtain, a leasehold notice process or an equity loan involved. Starting well before your current deal ends gives room for any delays.
When should I start looking for a new deal?
Many lenders let you secure a new rate several months before your current deal ends. Speaking to a broker or lender early gives time to compare options and complete the legal work. Your conveyancer can usually agree a completion date that matches the end of your current deal.
What happens if my remortgage is not ready in time?
If your current deal ends before the remortgage completes, your current lender will usually move you onto its standard variable rate until the new mortgage is in place. That can mean higher payments for a short period. You will not normally lose your home or be in breach, but it can cost more.
Can I choose the date my remortgage completes?
To some extent. Your conveyancer and the lender agree a completion date once the legal work is done and the funds are ready. Many people aim to complete the day after their current deal ends to avoid early repayment charges. Your conveyancer can help line this up.
What slows a remortgage down?
Common causes include delays in the lender's own checks, a low valuation, missing information about your current mortgage, consent needed from a landlord, housing provider or equity loan administrator, and problems on the title. Returning paperwork quickly and telling your conveyancer about anything unusual early helps.
Can I claim money for our children?
Yes. Child maintenance is usually arranged through the Child Maintenance Service or by agreement. In addition, a parent can sometimes apply to the court for further orders for the child's benefit, such as housing or a lump sum, under Schedule 1 to the Children Act 1989. These claims sit alongside any property dispute.
Can I stay in the home because of the children?
The court can sometimes order that a home is provided for a child until they finish their education, after which it may return to the other parent. This is for the child's benefit rather than the parent's. A solicitor can explain whether it is realistic in your case. Each case depends on the family's circumstances.
Does an unmarried father have parental responsibility?
An unmarried father usually has parental responsibility if he is named on the birth certificate of a child registered after 1 December 2003. Otherwise he can gain it by agreement with the mother or by court order. Parental responsibility is separate from questions about money. A solicitor can explain how it can be obtained if needed.
Can I claim maintenance for myself?
Generally no. Unlike married couples, cohabiting partners cannot usually claim maintenance for themselves when they separate. Any claim for money is normally made for the benefit of children. Your solicitor can explain what might be available in your situation. In some cases, orders for the children may indirectly help the parent who cares for them, for example by providing a home. A solicitor can explain how this might apply to you.
How are arrangements for the children decided?
Most parents agree arrangements themselves or with the help of mediation. If that is not possible, a parent can apply for a child arrangements order, usually after attending a mediation information meeting. The court's main concern is the child's welfare. Most parents agree without a court ever being involved. A solicitor can explain the process if agreement is not possible.
Does it matter if the children are not both of ours?
Claims under Schedule 1 to the Children Act 1989 are usually made against a child's legal parent. If a child is not your partner's child, claims against them for that child are more limited. Every family is different, so a solicitor will want to understand who is in your household.
What is a cohabitation agreement?
It is a written agreement between people who live together. It can record who owns what, how costs are shared and what will happen if you separate. Each person should give full information and take independent advice so that the agreement is more likely to be respected. It can be reviewed as life changes.
How do I check my points?
You can check your record on the GOV.UK view driving licence service using your licence number, National Insurance number and postcode. It shows your endorsements, offence dates and previous bans. It is a good idea to check before replying to a notice or speaking to a solicitor.
Does a speed awareness course add points?
No. A speed awareness course is offered instead of prosecution, so completing it means no conviction and no points. It does not count towards totting up. Whether a course is offered is a decision for the police, and it is not usually available once a case has gone to court or if you have done a similar course recently.
Do points from a fixed penalty count?
Yes. Points accepted through a fixed penalty are endorsed on your licence in the same way as points imposed by a court, and they count towards totting up. That is why a fixed penalty is not usually offered if it would take you to 12 points. In that situation, the case goes to court so that a ban can be considered.
How long is a totting up ban?
The minimum is six months. It rises to at least one year if you have had one ban of 56 days or more in the three years before the new offence, and at least two years if you have had two or more. The court can impose a longer ban, but usually imposes the minimum unless there are aggravating features.
Is a ban automatic once I reach 12 points?
Not automatic, but close to it. The law says the court must disqualify unless there are mitigating circumstances, which in practice means exceptional hardship. The court cannot take into account ordinary hardship or the fact that the new offence was minor. A solicitor can explain whether an exceptional hardship argument might be available.
Do I have to retake my test after a totting up ban?
No, not normally. At the end of the ban you apply to the DVLA to get your licence back. Retests are ordered for some serious offences, and new drivers who reach six points within two years face revocation and must pass again, but a standard totting up ban does not require a new test.
Can I drive while I wait for court?
In most cases, yes. A ban starts on the day the court imposes it. If the case is adjourned, the court may sometimes impose an interim ban before sentence. Until then, you can usually drive as long as your licence is valid and you are insured.
What is a summary review?
It is a fast track review that the police can apply for where a premises licensed to sell alcohol is associated with serious crime or serious disorder. A senior officer signs a certificate, and the council must consider interim steps within 48 hours. It is designed for urgent situations where the police believe immediate action is needed.
What interim steps can the council take?
The council can change the conditions, exclude the sale of alcohol, remove the DPS or suspend the licence while the full review is pending. These steps can take effect immediately. The council can make this decision without hearing from the licence holder first.
Can I challenge interim steps?
Yes. The licence holder can make representations against the interim steps, and the council must then hold a hearing within a short time to consider them. This can be an opportunity to propose alternative measures that address the police concerns while allowing some trading to continue.
What happens at the full summary review hearing?
The full hearing must take place within 28 days of the council receiving the police application. The committee can take any of the steps available on an ordinary review and must also decide what to do about the interim steps. Thorough preparation in a short period is essential.
Is a closure order the same as a summary review?
No. Closure notices and orders are powers under separate anti-social behaviour laws, used by the police or council to close premises quickly, often with a court hearing within days. A summary review is a licensing process before the council. They can happen together, and a closure order can itself trigger a review. Both need urgent help.
Can my licence be revoked?
Yes. Revocation is the most serious outcome and is available on any review. Committees must decide what is appropriate to promote the licensing objectives, and many reviews end with conditions or management changes rather than revocation. National guidance treats some criminal activity, such as drug dealing or knowingly employing illegal workers, particularly seriously.
How long can a licence be suspended?
A committee can suspend a licence for up to three months. During a suspension, the premises cannot carry on the licensable activities covered by the suspension. Suspension is sometimes used as a deterrent or to give the operator time to put changes in place.
Can I stop supplying the customer until they pay?
That depends on your contract. Some terms allow a supplier to suspend deliveries or work while invoices are overdue, while others do not. Stopping supply without a right to do so could cause problems. A solicitor can check your terms before you decide.
What if the customer promises to pay but never does?
Repeated promises without payment are common. It is worth keeping a note of each promise and asking for it in writing. If a deadline set in writing passes without payment, a formal letter from a solicitor is often the next step.
Do I need to send a letter before issuing a claim?
Courts expect parties to exchange information and try to settle before a claim is issued. Skipping this step can lead to costs penalties, even if you win. A solicitor will usually send a letter before action that meets these expectations, and will explain the deadline that applies to your customer.
What if the customer says the work was not good enough?
A complaint about quality does not end your claim, but it does need to be taken seriously. A solicitor will look at the contract, any acceptance or inspection terms and the evidence, and explain how strong the complaint appears and which routes are still sensible.
Can I still recover the part that is not disputed?
Often, yes. If the customer only disputes part of an invoice, the undisputed amount may be recoverable separately and more quickly. A solicitor can help identify what is genuinely in dispute and pursue the rest.
The customer says it is owed money by us. Can it deduct that?
This is called set off. Whether the customer can deduct money depends on your contract and the nature of its own claim. Some business terms exclude set off. A solicitor will look at your terms and the customer's claim and explain the position.
The customer raised a complaint only after I chased. Does that matter?
It can be relevant. A complaint that appears for the first time months after delivery, and only when payment is requested, may carry less weight than one raised promptly. However, it still needs to be looked at properly. A solicitor will consider the timing along with the other evidence.
My freeholder has offered an extension. Should I accept?
Leaflegal cannot tell you that. A solicitor can review the offer, compare it with the statutory route and point out any terms that might cause problems later, such as a ground rent that rises sharply. A surveyor can also tell you how the price compares. You can then decide whether to accept, negotiate or take another route.
Can my neighbours and I buy the freehold instead?
If the building and enough owners qualify, you may be able to buy the freehold together through collective enfranchisement. You could then grant yourselves long leases. This takes longer to organise and needs enough owners to take part, but it can suit buildings where several leases are getting short.
Can I extend if I have only just bought the flat?
The old requirement to have owned the flat for two years before using the statutory right was removed by the 2024 Act from 31 January 2025. This means a recent buyer may be able to start an extension straight away. Your solicitor will confirm this before serving a notice.
Is there an option to just remove the ground rent?
The 2024 Act includes provisions allowing qualifying leaseholders to pay to reduce their ground rent to a peppercorn without extending the term. This depends on commencement and supporting regulations. For a short lease, extending is often the main concern. Your solicitor will explain whether any ground rent option is available and relevant.
What if my freeholder cannot be found?
Some short lease flats have a freeholder that cannot be traced, for example because a company has been dissolved or an individual owner has died. There is a separate court process that can allow a lease extension to go ahead in that situation, with the price usually decided by the tribunal and paid into court. A solicitor can explain how long it might take.
Can I sell a flat with a short lease?
Yes, but it can be harder. Buyers who need a mortgage may not be able to get one, and others may offer less to reflect the cost of extending. Some owners start a statutory extension and pass the notice to the buyer, which can make the flat more attractive. A solicitor can explain how this would work for your sale.
How does passing a notice to a buyer work?
Once a statutory notice has been served, its benefit can usually be assigned to a buyer at the same time as the flat is transferred. The buyer then takes over the claim and completes the extension. The assignment must be done correctly and at the right time. Both solicitors will agree the details, including who pays which costs.
Can I transfer only some goods and services?
Yes. A registration can be partly assigned, so that different owners hold different goods or services. This needs care, because splitting a mark between owners can create a risk of confusion. The firm will advise on whether it is sensible.
Should I record a licence?
Recording a licence of a registered mark is not compulsory but can strengthen the licensee's position, including in enforcement. Licensees and lenders often ask for licences and security interests to be recorded.
How do I update my address on the register?
You can ask the UK IPO to record a change of name or address. It is a simple step but important, because official letters go to the address on the register. A firm can make the changes for all your marks at once.
What if the previous owner has been dissolved?
If a company that owned a mark has been dissolved, its property may have passed to the Crown, and getting a valid assignment can be complicated. The firm can look at the options, which may include restoring the company or applying for a new registration.
Do I need to tell anyone else when a mark changes hands?
Besides the UK IPO, it is worth updating online marketplace brand programmes, domain name registrations and any licensees or distributors. The firm can give you a checklist so nothing is missed.
What happens to trademarks if my business is sold?
They usually pass to the buyer as part of the deal, under the sale agreement and a separate assignment. The assignment should then be recorded at the UK IPO. Before a sale, it is worth checking that the marks are in the seller's name and in force.
Can a registered trademark be cancelled for non use?
Yes. Anyone can apply to revoke a mark that hasn't been genuinely used in the UK for the registered goods or services for a continuous five year period. Keeping dated evidence of use helps you defend the registration if challenged.
Is the first consultation free?
It depends on the firm. Some offer a short free call first, and others charge for the first consultation. The firm should tell you before the consultation whether there is a fee and how much it is, so that there are no surprises.
What should we have ready?
It helps to have passports, details of any current visas, any Home Office letters and key family documents to hand. A simple family tree with names, dates of birth and where everyone lives can be very useful. The adviser will tell you what else they need afterwards.
Should we mention past problems?
Yes. Advisers can only help properly if they know the whole family's history, including past refusals or problems with earlier visas. Leaving things out can cause bigger difficulties later. What you tell a regulated adviser is confidential under the rules of their regulator.
Can we have the consultation in our own language?
Some firms have advisers who speak other languages, and some can arrange interpreters. Mention your family's preferred language when you enquire. It is important that everyone involved understands what is being explained.
What happens after the consultation?
Most firms follow up in writing with a summary of what was discussed, what they can do for each family member and what it would cost. Read it carefully and share it with anyone else involved. If you decide to go ahead, the firm will send terms of business to agree before work starts.
Our matter involves a child. Is there anything special to know?
Matters involving children often need extra care. Tell your adviser about the child's whole history, including who has looked after them and any court orders. Some children's matters also involve family law, and your adviser may suggest speaking to a family lawyer as well. Have the child's birth certificate, passport and any court documents to hand for the first conversation.
Our relative is elderly and unwell. Can an adviser help?
We can introduce you to a regulated adviser who handles family matters involving older relatives. They will listen to your family's situation and explain honestly what may be possible. It helps to have a general picture of your relative's health and care arrangements when you speak to them. Families in this position are often under a lot of strain, so it can help to agree within the family who will lead on contact with the adviser.
