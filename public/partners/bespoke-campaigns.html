Marketing built around your firm

Some firms want more than a steady flow of shared demand. You might be launching a new office, pushing a practice area that is quiet, or competing in a town where three other firms already buy every search term. A bespoke campaign is planned around that goal.

We plan the audience, write the ads and landing pages, set up tracking and run the campaign day to day. You approve everything that goes out and the enquiries come straight to your team.

Two ways to work with us

Both models are built so your firm stays the regulated provider. The difference is whose name the client sees first.

Under your brand

We run the campaign as your marketing team. The ads, landing pages and follow up emails carry your firm’s name, and enquiries go straight to you.

  • Your firm is the advertiser and the regulated provider
  • Ads run in your own ad accounts, so you keep the data and history
  • Landing pages show your SRA number, digital badge, prices and complaints details
  • You sign off every ad and page before it goes live
  • We act as your data processor under a written agreement

Best for firms that want to build their own brand and keep everything in house.

On your behalf

We run the campaign under the Leaflegal name and pass every enquiry to your firm alone. Clients are told who you are and how we work together before they are introduced.

  • Clients are told in writing about our arrangement with your firm
  • Every enquiry goes to your firm only, never shared or resold
  • You never need to set up ad accounts or landing pages
  • Our intake asks questions only and never gives legal or immigration advice
  • Consent wording and time are recorded with each enquiry

Best for firms that want results without running a marketing operation.

Built around the rules for regulated legal services

Marketing for law firms comes with rules that most agencies never think about. We plan around them from day one.

You stay the regulated firm

Your firm does the legal work and gives the advice. We provide marketing services only, so reserved legal activities, immigration advice and client care stay with you and your regulator.

Publicity that meets the SRA Code

Ads and pages must be accurate and not misleading, including on price. The SRA holds firms responsible for marketing that third parties run for them, so you see and approve all of it and we keep a record of what ran.

No unsolicited approaches

We never cold call, door knock or send unsolicited texts or emails to members of the public. People come to the campaign, not the other way round.

Referral rules when we act for you

When we run a campaign on your behalf the SRA treats it as an introduction. Clients are told about the arrangement in writing and we never introduce clients facing criminal proceedings.

A fee for marketing, not for referrals

You pay a fixed setup fee and a monthly management fee agreed up front. Ad spend is paid at cost, ideally straight from your own account. Our fee never goes up or down with the number of enquiries or instructions.

Data handled properly

Under your brand we act as your processor under a written data processing agreement. On your behalf we tell each person who will receive their details before they submit, and sensitive matter details are never sent to ad platforms.

Work we can and can’t promote

Some areas of law cannot be marketed this way at all, so we stay out of them.

We can run campaigns for

  • Family law, divorce and child arrangements
  • Immigration, through SRA, CILEx or IAA regulated firms
  • Business to business debt recovery
  • Licensing for pubs, bars and venues

  • Anything that relies on cold calling or unsolicited messages

How pricing works

Every campaign is quoted in writing before it starts. There is a one off setup fee for planning, copy, pages and tracking, then a fixed monthly fee for running it.

Ad spend is separate and paid at cost. Under your brand it is billed by Google or Meta straight to your card. On your behalf we pass it on at cost with receipts.

Our fee is for the marketing work and stays the same however many enquiries or instructions the campaign brings in. That keeps the arrangement clear of the rules on referral fees.

Questions about bespoke campaigns

What is the difference between under your brand and on your behalf?
Under your brand the ads and pages carry your firm’s name, you are the advertiser and we work as your marketing team. On your behalf the campaign runs as Leaflegal and each enquiry is introduced to your firm alone, so the SRA referral rules apply and clients are told about the arrangement in writing.
Will you give advice to potential clients?
Never. Our ads, pages and intake questions explain what your firm does and gather the facts. All legal and immigration advice comes from your firm.
Who owns the ad accounts and the data?
Under your brand you own the ad accounts, landing pages, tracking and the enquiry data, and we work in them as your processor. On your behalf the enquiries are passed to you and you become responsible for them from that point.
Which regulators do you work with?
We work with firms regulated by the SRA, CILEx Regulation and the CLC, IPReg regulated trade mark attorneys and, for immigration, IAA regulated advisers.
Is it allowed for a marketing company to run campaigns for a law firm?
Yes. Firms regulated by the SRA can use outside marketing suppliers. The firm stays responsible for its publicity, so it must check that ads are accurate, include clear pricing where needed and are not run through cold calls or unsolicited messages to the public. We build each campaign so you can do that.
Can you run campaigns for personal injury or claims work?
No. Paying for personal injury and clinical negligence referrals is banned by the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012, and generating leads for claims such as housing disrepair or mis-sold finance needs FCA claims management authorisation. We do not work in those areas.
Do you check the copy against the CAP Code?
Yes. Every ad and page is checked against the CAP Code and the SRA Code before we send it to you for sign off, and we keep a copy of everything that ran.
Do you charge per lead for bespoke campaigns?
No. Bespoke campaigns have a setup fee and a fixed monthly fee, plus ad spend at cost. The fee does not depend on how many enquiries or instructions you receive. If you would rather pay per lead, our standard exclusive leads do that.
How long does a campaign run?
Most campaigns run for at least three months so there is time to learn and improve. After that you can carry on monthly and end it with 30 days’ notice.

More questions people ask

What is a lease extension lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a flat owner in England and Wales who wants to pay a solicitor to help extend their lease or buy their freehold. They have told us roughly how many years are left, which route they are considering, whether a sale or remortgage depends on it and where the flat is, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold. We do not send the same leaseholder to several firms at once. Because lease extension work is specialist and many clients are unsure who to approach, an exclusive introduction gives you a fair chance to explain your service and win the instruction.
Will I know how long is left on the lease?
Each lead includes the client's own estimate of how many years are left. Many leaseholders know the figure roughly but not exactly, so it is worth confirming it from the lease or the title once you are instructed. The estimate is still very useful for deciding which enquiries to call first and how to frame the conversation.
Do you send collective enfranchisement enquiries?
Yes, if you want them. Enfranchisement enquiries usually come from one leaseholder acting for a group, or exploring the idea on behalf of neighbours. Each lead includes the number of flats in the building, which helps you judge the size of the matter. These enquiries can take time to turn into instructions while the group organises itself.
Do clients know which route they want?
Some do, but many are unsure. We ask whether they are interested in a statutory extension, an informal extension or buying the freehold, and some answer that they do not know. Their answer gives you a starting point, and explaining the general differences on the first call is often where a firm earns the client's trust.
Will I know if a sale depends on the extension?
Yes. Each lead says whether a sale or remortgage depends on the outcome. Those enquiries are usually the most time sensitive, because buyers, agents and lenders are waiting. Knowing this before you call lets you route the matter to someone who can act quickly and talk the client through the general options.
Do you send enquiries from freeholders?
Our lease extension enquiries come from leaseholders who want to extend their lease or buy the freehold, along with some lease variation and dispute matters raised by leaseholders. If you also act for freeholders and want to discuss that work, raise it on your call. Your conflict checks remain important where a building or freeholder is already known to you.
Do you send enquiries about flats with very short leases?
Yes. Each lead includes the client's estimate of the years left, and some leases will be very short. These clients are often worried that their flat is hard to sell or mortgage. Their matters can be more complex and more valuable to a firm, so many partners ask for them. If you prefer to avoid them, tell us and we will take that into account in your matching.
How many leads will I receive?
That depends on the matter types and areas you choose and the enquiries our campaigns are generating at the time. The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that, you set the number your team can handle and we match to it. We will give you an honest view of what is realistic on your call, but we cannot promise a fixed number.
Can I choose the areas I cover?
Yes. You can choose particular towns, cities, counties or regions, or the whole of England and Wales. Much lease extension work can be done remotely, so many firms take national coverage, while others prefer areas where they know the local valuers and freeholders. Wider coverage usually means more matches. We will talk through the balance on your call.
Can I choose only statutory work?
Yes. Each matter type can be switched on or off. If your firm concentrates on statutory extensions and enfranchisement, you can leave out informal extensions and variations. If you prefer smaller, quicker matters, you can choose those instead. You can change the mix whenever you like by letting us know.
Can I set a monthly cap?
Yes. You set the number of leads you want each month, as long as it is at least the minimum of 10. When you reach your cap, we stop matching enquiries to you until the next month. You can change your cap whenever your capacity changes, so you never receive more enquiries than your team can call back promptly.
Can I pause leads?
Yes. If your team is busy with tribunal work, someone is on leave or you are changing systems, tell us and we will pause matching until you are ready. Pausing does not end your partner agreement. When you want to restart, let us know and matching begins again as new enquiries come in.
Do you send enquiries from Scotland or Northern Ireland?
No. Lease extension enquiries relate to flats in England and Wales. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different property law and systems of tenure, so we do not send those enquiries. Within England and Wales, how many leads you receive depends on the areas and matter types you choose and the volume of enquiries at the time.
Can leads go to different people in my team?
Yes. We can route by matter type or area, so enfranchisement enquiries go to one fee earner and single flat extensions to another. We can also send everything to a shared intake inbox or your CRM, where you assign each lead yourself. We set up whatever suits the way your leasehold team works.
Do I need to disclose the referral fee?
Yes. Under the SRA Code of Conduct, you must make sure clients are told in writing about any financial arrangement with an introducer, and that it does not affect your independence or advice. CLC regulated practices have similar rules. Our consent wording tells every client that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send it with each lead.
Does Leaflegal advise leaseholders?
No. Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm. We do not tell leaseholders which route to take, what premium they might pay or whether they qualify for a statutory extension. We make this clear to every client before they submit. All advice comes from your firm and the valuer you work with.
Who can receive lease extension leads?
SRA regulated firms, CILEx regulated practices and CLC regulated practices authorised for the work. You remain responsible for your own regulatory obligations, conflict checks and client identification. You must tell us if your authorisation changes, so we can update or pause your matching. We ask about your regulation and the leasehold work you handle when you apply, so your matching reflects what you are able to do.
How do you deal with the changes to leasehold law?
Leasehold law is being reformed, and parts of the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 are coming into force in stages. Our campaigns and form avoid statements about eligibility, premiums or how new rules apply. Explaining the current position to each client is for your firm, and the lead gives you the basic facts you need to start that conversation.
How is client consent recorded?
Each client ticks one box confirming they have read our terms and privacy policy and understand that Leaflegal is an introduction service, and a second box agreeing to their details being shared with one firm that may contact them by phone, email or text. We record the exact wording and the time, and the record arrives with the lead.
What about conflicts in the same building?
Run your usual conflict checks before taking on a matter. Conflicts can arise if you already act for the freeholder, the managing agent or other leaseholders in the same block, particularly on enfranchisement. If you find a conflict, do not act and let us know, and we will treat the lead as your partner agreement sets out.
How much does a lease extension lead cost?
There is no published price list. You pay per lead at a price agreed with you before you start, based on the matter types and areas you choose and the volume you want. We put a proposal together after your onboarding call, so you know the cost of every lead before the first one is delivered.
Is there a written agreement?
Yes. Your partner agreement covers pricing, billing, volumes, delivery and the rules on invalid leads, and it is in place before your first lead. Both sides know from the start what is charged, how leads are counted and how any problem with a lead is handled. We go through it with you and answer any questions before you sign.
What counts as an invalid lead?
Your partner agreement sets this out. It usually covers things like wrong contact details or an enquiry outside your agreed criteria. If you think a lead is invalid, tell us within the review period in your agreement and we will deal with it as the agreement says. A leaseholder deciding not to go ahead does not by itself make a lead invalid.
What if the client decides to wait?
Some leaseholders enquire early and then decide to wait, perhaps to see how the reforms develop or to save for the premium. A lead is an introduction rather than a guaranteed instruction. A clear first conversation and a helpful follow up email often bring clients back when they are ready, especially if a sale or remortgage later makes the extension urgent.
How do I become a partner firm?
Apply to become a partner firm and book a 30 minute call with our team. We go through the leasehold work you handle, the areas you cover, the valuers you work with and your volume. Then we agree your criteria and price, show you a sample lead and put your partner agreement in place before any leads are sent.
Can I see a sample lead?
Yes. On your call we show you a sample lease extension lead with every field, the format it arrives in and the consent record. You can see how the years remaining, the route, any sale or remortgage and the number of flats are presented, so you know exactly what your team would receive.
Is there a portal or login?
No. There is no platform to learn. Leads arrive by email, to a shared intake inbox, or straight into your CRM or case management system using a lead email address or a webhook. Your team works them in the systems they already use, so nothing gets lost in a separate tool.
What should we set up before our first lead?
Decide who calls new leads and how quickly, especially where a sale depends on the extension. Route leads to a shared inbox or CRM. Prepare a clear summary of your fees for each route, agree how you will introduce a valuer, and check that your client care letter covers the referral arrangement.
Can we change our criteria later?
Yes. Your matching is not fixed. If you build up your enfranchisement practice, want to add informal extensions or decide to focus on certain areas, tell us and we update your criteria. Feedback on which leads instruct and which do not helps us send you more of the leasehold work that suits your team and your valuers.
What is a legal lead?
It is an enquiry from a person or business who wants to speak to a solicitor about their own matter and has agreed to be contacted by a firm. A good legal lead includes their contact details, the type of matter, how urgent it is, where they are and a record of their consent. It is an opportunity to win an instruction, not an instruction in itself.
Are legal leads the same as referrals?
They are similar in that someone introduces a client to your firm. The difference is that a traditional referral usually comes from a professional contact who knows the client, while a lead comes from a client who responded to marketing and asked to be introduced. In both cases, the SRA Code requires you to tell clients about any financial arrangement with the introducer.
Are legal leads worth buying?
For firms that call quickly and follow up properly, they can be a reliable source of new private clients. For firms that leave leads for a day or two, results tend to be disappointing whoever the supplier is. We will give you an honest view of likely volumes for your criteria before you start, and the minimum of 10 a month is enough to judge quality.
Why does freshness matter so much?
People who need a solicitor usually act on that need straight away. Within hours they may have asked a friend, found another firm or decided to wait. A lead that arrives as soon as it is checked and is called within minutes catches the client while they are focused on their matter and expecting a call.
What is the difference between a lead and a cold contact?
A lead is someone who asked to be contacted about their own matter. A cold contact is someone whose details were collected for another purpose, or bought from a list, and who is not expecting to hear from you. Leaflegal only produces leads. We never buy data or pass on cold contacts.
Do you sell legal leads in Scotland or Northern Ireland?
Not at the moment. Our leads in six practice areas are for matters in England and Wales. Immigration introductions can come from anywhere in the UK, because immigration law applies UK wide. If you are a firm outside England and Wales with an interest in immigration work, mention it when you apply and we will tell you honestly whether the service could work for you.
Will every legal lead become a client?
No. Some people are comparing firms, some decide not to proceed and some cannot afford the fees once they understand them. A lead is an opportunity, not a guaranteed instruction. Firms that call quickly, use the information in the lead and are clear about their fees tend to win the most work from the leads they receive.
