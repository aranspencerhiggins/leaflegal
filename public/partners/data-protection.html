What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

Every Leaflegal lead contains personal data. Some leads also contain sensitive information, because people looking for help with a separation, an immigration matter or a driving offence often tell us something about their personal life or about an alleged offence. Handling that information properly protects the client, protects your firm and protects Leaflegal.

This page explains, in general terms, how Leaflegal approaches data protection and consent for legal leads, and what firms are usually responsible for once a lead is delivered. It is general information, not legal advice. Your firm's own data protection lead, or a data protection specialist, is the right person to advise on your firm's position under UK data protection law.

Our approach in brief

  • We collect only what a firm needs to make first contact about the client's matter
  • Every client gives consent by ticking two separate boxes, and we record the wording and the time
  • Each lead goes to one firm only and is never resold
  • We never share matter details with advertising platforms
  • We do not sell or rent personal data to anyone
  • Once a lead is delivered, your firm is the controller of its copy

The law in outline

In the UK, personal data is governed mainly by the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Electronic marketing, such as marketing emails and texts, is also governed by the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, usually called PECR. The Information Commissioner's Office, the ICO, regulates all three and publishes detailed guidance. The rules change from time to time, so it is worth checking the ICO's current guidance rather than relying on older summaries.

Why it matters for law firms

Law firms already handle sensitive information every day and usually have good data protection practices. Leads add a slightly different situation, because the data comes to you from a third party rather than directly from the client. That affects what the client needs to be told and when, and it is worth making sure your procedures cover it.

Data minimisation in practice

One of the principles of UK data protection law is that organisations should collect only the data they need for their purpose. For Leaflegal, the purpose is introducing a client to a firm that can help with their matter. That is why our forms are short and why we do not ask for documents, dates of birth, financial details or other information at the enquiry stage. Your firm collects what it needs for its own purposes once the client has chosen to speak to you.

Understanding who is responsible for the data at each stage makes everything else simpler.

Leaflegal as controller

Vertical Brands Ltd, trading as Leaflegal, is the controller for the information collected through our enquiry forms. That means we decide what information is collected, why it is collected and how it is used up to the point of introduction. Our privacy policy explains this to clients, and they confirm they have read it before submitting an enquiry.

Your firm as controller

Once a lead is delivered to your firm, your firm becomes the controller of its copy. From that point, you decide how the information is used, stored and protected, and you are responsible for handling it in line with UK data protection law and your own privacy notice. In practice, this is the same position you are in with any other new enquiry.

Not a processor arrangement

Leaflegal does not process data on your firm's behalf, and your firm does not process data on Leaflegal's behalf. Each of us handles the data for our own purposes. Your firm's data protection lead may want to confirm how your firm records this relationship.

One copy per firm

Each lead is delivered to one firm only. The copy your firm receives is the only copy any law firm receives from Leaflegal for that enquiry. We do not resell leads or pass the same details to another firm later.

Working together

Because both Leaflegal and your firm hold information about the same enquiry, there are times when we need to work together. A client may ask either of us how their data was used, a lead may be raised as invalid or a client may ask to stop being contacted. Telling each other promptly in these situations makes it much easier for both of us to deal with the client properly.

What Leaflegal keeps

Leaflegal keeps its own record of each enquiry, including the consent record, for the purposes explained in our privacy policy, such as dealing with queries, handling invalid lead reports and meeting our own legal obligations. We do not use matter details for advertising.

Consent is central to how Leaflegal introduces clients to firms. Every client makes an active, recorded choice to be contacted by a firm about their matter.

Two separate boxes

Every enquiry form ends with two consent boxes, neither of them ticked in advance. In the first, the client confirms they have read our terms and privacy policy and understand that Leaflegal is an introduction service rather than a law firm. In the second, they agree that their details can be passed to a firm we match them with, and that the firm may phone, email or text them about their matter. The enquiry cannot be submitted until both are ticked.

Recorded and sent with each lead

We record the exact wording the client agreed to and the time they agreed to it. That record is sent with every lead. If a client later asks how your firm got their details, or does not remember enquiring, you can explain what they agreed to and when.

What the consent covers

The consent covers contact from your firm about the client's enquiry. It does not cover adding the client to a newsletter, sending general marketing or passing their details to anyone else. If you want to send a client marketing, you need a separate basis for doing so, which for electronic marketing usually means their separate consent.

Your own lawful basis

Once a lead is with your firm, you are responsible for identifying your own lawful basis for each use you make of the client's data. If the client instructs you, much of what you do will relate to the retainer. Your data protection lead can advise on how your firm documents this.

Withdrawing consent

Clients can change their minds. If a client asks you to stop contacting them, stop, and let us know so that we have a record too. Handling this politely protects your firm's reputation as well as meeting your obligations.

Some legal matters naturally involve sensitive information. UK data protection law gives extra protection to special category data, such as information about health, sexual life, religious beliefs or ethnic origin, and to criminal offence data, such as information about alleged offences.

Where it can arise

Family enquiries can touch on relationships, health or the welfare of children. Immigration enquiries can involve nationality and, sometimes, information that reveals ethnic origin or religious belief. Motoring enquiries involve alleged offences, which count as criminal offence data. Clients may also mention health issues in any area when they describe their situation.

Keeping questions to a minimum

Because of this, our forms ask only what a firm needs to make first contact. We ask about the type of matter, urgency, location and whether the client wants paid help, plus a short description in their own words. We do not ask probing questions about health, relationships or offences beyond what is needed to match the enquiry to the right kind of firm.

What clients choose to tell us

Clients sometimes include sensitive details in their free text description. We pass that description to your firm as the client wrote it, because it helps you understand the matter. Treat it with the care you would give any sensitive information received from a new client.

Never shared with advertising platforms

We never pass matter details to advertising platforms. Where we use advertising tools to measure our own campaigns, they do not receive information about the client's legal matter.

Handling it inside your firm

Consider who in your firm needs to see a lead. In a family or immigration team, it may be sensible for leads to go to a restricted team inbox rather than a general firm address. The fewer people who see sensitive details unnecessarily, the lower the risk.

Immigration

Our immigration forms collect only the type of matter, urgency and contact details. Leaflegal does not ask questions that would amount to assessing a person's immigration position and never gives immigration advice.

Once a lead reaches your firm, your normal data protection duties apply. The points below are general reminders, not a complete list and not legal advice.

Most of these duties will already be covered by your firm's existing procedures for new enquiries. The main point is to make sure those procedures also apply to leads, so that nobody treats a lead as somehow outside the normal rules because it arrived from an introducer.

Telling the client

When personal data is obtained from someone other than the person it is about, UK data protection law generally expects that person to be given privacy information. Where the data is used to contact them, this is usually expected at the latest when you first contact them. Many firms send a link to their privacy notice in the first email after a call.

Using the lead for its purpose

Use the lead to contact the client about their enquiry. Do not use it for unrelated purposes, add it to marketing lists without separate consent or share it outside your firm except as needed to deal with the client's matter.

Keeping it secure

Store leads in your usual secure systems, such as your case management system, CRM or a secure inbox. Avoid forwarding leads to personal email accounts or saving them on unsecured devices. Limit access to the people who need it.

Retention

Decide how long you keep leads that do not become instructions, and set this out in your retention policy. Many firms keep unconverted enquiries for a shorter period than client files. Leads that become instructions fall under your normal file retention arrangements.

Requests from clients

Clients have rights over their data, such as the right to access it and, in some circumstances, to have it erased. Handle requests about your copy of a lead under your own procedures. If a request also concerns Leaflegal's records, let us know.

Breaches

If a lead is lost, sent to the wrong person or accessed without authority, follow your firm's breach procedure. If the breach involves Leaflegal's systems or delivery, tell us straight away.

Marketing is where most data protection mistakes with leads happen. The rule of thumb is simple. The consent a client gives through Leaflegal covers contact about their enquiry, not marketing.

Following up is not marketing

Calling, emailing and texting a client about the enquiry they made is what they agreed to. A reasonable follow up sequence, such as a call, a voicemail, an email and a text on the first day and another call the next day, falls within that.

Newsletters and promotions

Adding a client to a newsletter, sending promotional emails about other services or texting them about offers is marketing. Under PECR, electronic marketing to individuals usually needs their consent, subject to some limited exceptions. Ask for marketing consent separately, record it and give the client an easy way to opt out.

Clients who instruct you

Once a client instructs your firm, your relationship with them is governed by your own terms and privacy notice. Whether you can send them marketing depends on what they have agreed to and the rules that apply. Your data protection lead can advise.

Clients who do not instruct

If a client decides not to instruct you, stop contacting them about the enquiry after a reasonable follow up. Do not add them to marketing lists or keep contacting them about other services unless they have separately agreed.

Our own marketing

Leaflegal does not use matter details for advertising, does not sell or rent personal data and does not pass leads to other lead sellers.

In your usual secure systems, such as your case management system, CRM or a secure shared inbox. More questions people ask

What is pay per lead for law firms?
It is a way of paying for marketing where the firm pays for each enquiry delivered to it, rather than for clicks, impressions or campaign management. With Leaflegal, each lead is an exclusive enquiry that matches your agreed practice areas, matter types and locations, at a price agreed before you start.
When do I pay for a lead?
You pay for leads delivered to your firm that match your agreed criteria. How and when you are billed is set out in your partner agreement, which you see and agree before your first lead. There is no charge for enquiries that do not reach you. Keep your own record of leads received so you can check bills easily.
Do I pay for leads that do not convert?
Yes, as long as they are valid. A lead stays valid if the client chooses another firm, decides not to proceed or does not accept your fees. Pay per lead covers the cost of producing a qualified, exclusive enquiry. It is not a guarantee of instructions, and no honest lead provider can offer one.
Is pay per lead the same as pay per instruction?
No. Pay per lead means paying for each qualified enquiry, whether or not it becomes an instruction. Leaflegal charges per lead. Firms should take their own view on how any payment arrangement with an introducer sits with their regulator's rules. Your price per lead is agreed in advance and does not depend on whether the client instructs you or on the fees you earn from the matter.
Do I need to manage any campaigns?
No. Leaflegal runs the campaigns, the enquiry forms and the checks. You agree your criteria and price, then receive leads as they come in. You do not need to choose keywords, write adverts or watch advertising budgets. Your part is calling leads quickly and telling us which ones turn into instructions, so we can refine your criteria over time.
Is there a login or dashboard?
No. There is no online platform. Leads arrive by email or in your CRM, and changes to your criteria, cap or pause status are made by contacting our team. Many firms prefer it, because there is nothing new for their team to learn. Our team handles changes for you, usually by email or phone.
Can I use pay per lead alongside my own marketing?
Yes, and most firms do. Your website, reputation and referrers usually bring in some work on their own. Pay per lead adds a source of enquiries you can turn up or down as your capacity changes. Because each lead has a known cost, it is easy to compare with what your other channels cost you.
