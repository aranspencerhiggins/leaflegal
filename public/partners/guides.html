Pick a topic to read more.

These guides are for solicitors, conveyancers, immigration advisers and the intake and practice management teams who work with them. They cover the practical side of winning private clients, from the moment a new enquiry arrives to the day the client signs your engagement letter, along with marketing advice for specific practice areas and an honest comparison of the main ways firms find new work.

They are written from what we see working with law firms that take enquiries from Leaflegal, and from the questions firms ask us most often. They are not legal advice, and they do not tell you how to run your practice. Your regulator's rules, your own professional judgement and your firm's policies always come first. What the guides offer is a clear, practical starting point that you can adapt to your own team.

Why we publish them

Leaflegal introduces people and businesses who need paid legal help to one regulated firm at a time. We run the campaigns, ask the screening questions, verify contact details, record consent and send each enquiry to a single firm as soon as it is checked. What happens next is up to the firm. We have learned that the firms getting the most from their leads tend to do the same few things well, and we would rather share that openly than keep it to ourselves.

Useful whatever your source of work

Nearly everything in these guides applies to enquiries from any source, including your own website, paid search, referrals and directories. Responding quickly, explaining fees clearly and following up politely will help you convert more of the enquiries you already have, whether or not you ever buy a lead.

No invented numbers

You will not find conversion rates, industry benchmarks or promises of results here. Outcomes vary too much between firms, practice areas and locations for general figures to be useful, and we do not want to give you numbers we cannot stand behind. Instead, the guides explain how to measure your own results and use them to improve.

How the guides fit together

Think of a new client's journey in four parts. First, the enquiry reaches you and someone needs to speak to the client quickly. Second, that first conversation needs to be clear, warm and useful. Third, the client needs a consultation, quote or proposal and an easy way to say yes. Fourth, people who are not ready yet need a polite, planned follow-up. The intake guides cover each of those parts in turn.

The marketing guides sit around that journey. They look at how enquiries reach you in the first place, and how the way you work on each matter, from quoting to updates to reviews, brings in the next client. Read together, they give a complete picture of how a private client practice can grow steadily without relying on any single source of work.

Four guides cover the journey from a new enquiry to an instruction. They work best read in order, but each one stands on its own.

Speed to lead

How quickly you speak to a new enquiry has a large effect on whether they instruct. This guide explains why, then covers how to route leads to a team rather than one person, set up alerts, agree who calls at each part of the day, handle out of hours enquiries and measure response times. Read speed to lead for law firms.

First call script

The first call is where a stranger decides whether to trust your firm. This guide sets out a simple six stage structure, with example wording, for introducing yourself, showing you have read the enquiry, asking good questions, explaining how you can help, talking about fees and agreeing a next step. It also covers difficult moments and how the call differs by practice area. Read a first call script for legal enquiries.

Converting enquiries

Conversion is about every step between an enquiry and a signed engagement letter. This guide looks at where clients usually drop out, how to run consultations that lead somewhere, how to present fees and proposals, how to remove friction from onboarding and how to measure the whole process. Read converting legal enquiries into instructions.

Following up

Many people do not instruct on the first contact. This guide gives a simple follow-up sequence, example messages, advice on tone for each practice area, the consent and data points to keep in mind and how to handle matters that take months to decide. Read following up enquiries that go quiet.

The guides stand alone, but they are written with Leaflegal's way of working in mind. Here is a short summary of how that works.

What you receive

Each lead includes the client's name, a verified UK mobile and email, the practice area and matter type, a short description in their own words, urgency and any known deadline, location, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the consent record. Every client has confirmed they want paid legal help rather than legal aid or free advice.

Exclusive to your firm

Each enquiry is matched to one firm only and is never resold. You choose your practice areas, matter types and locations, and leads are delivered by email or into your CRM as soon as they are checked. There is no online platform or login.

Volume and price

You pay per lead at a price agreed with you individually before you start. The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that you set your own cap, and you can change it or pause when you need to. Pricing, billing, delivery and invalid lead rules are set out in a written agreement.

Getting started

Onboarding starts with a 30-minute call with our team. We talk through your practice areas, matter types, locations, regulation and volume, show you a sample lead and agree a price. Once your agreement is in place, leads start as soon as matching enquiries come in.

Compliance

Leaflegal is an introducer, not a law firm, and does not give legal advice. Under the SRA Code of Conduct, you must make sure clients are told about the referral arrangement in writing. We send our consent wording with each lead so your disclosure can match it. We do not work in personal injury or clinical negligence, where referral fees are banned. Read more about the SRA referral rules.

What happens after a lead arrives

Once a lead is delivered, the relationship is between your firm and the client. We do not chase clients on your behalf or contact them about whether they instructed you. Your firm becomes the data controller for your copy of the lead and is responsible for handling it in line with UK data protection law and your own privacy notice. If a lead is outside your agreed criteria or has genuinely invalid contact details, tell us within the review period in your partner agreement.

What we do not cover

We do not generate personal injury or clinical negligence leads, because paying referral fees for them is prohibited under section 56 of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012. We also do not handle housing disrepair or financial mis-selling claims. Our legal leads are for matters in England and Wales, except immigration introductions, which can come from anywhere in the UK.

Questions and answers

Who are these guides for?
Solicitors, conveyancers, immigration advisers and the intake teams and practice managers who support them. They are aimed at firms that handle privately funded work and want to win more of it, whether their enquiries come from Leaflegal, their own marketing or referrals. Most of the advice applies to firms of any size, from sole practitioners to large multi-office practices.
Are the guides legal advice?
No. They are practical guidance on intake, conversion and marketing. They do not replace your regulator's rules, your own professional judgement or advice from your compliance team. Where the guides mention regulatory points, such as disclosing referral arrangements, they do so in general terms. Always check the current rules that apply to your firm and your practice areas.
Do I need to be a Leaflegal partner to use them?
No. The guides are free to read and most of the advice applies to enquiries from any source. Responding quickly, being clear about fees and following up politely help with every enquiry. Where a guide explains how something works with Leaflegal leads, it says so clearly, so you can skip those parts if they do not apply to you.
Why don't the guides include conversion rates or benchmarks?
Because outcomes vary so much between firms, practice areas, fee levels and locations that general figures would be misleading. We also do not want to quote numbers we cannot stand behind. Instead, the guides explain how to measure your own results consistently, which is a much better guide to what is working for your firm.
How often are the guides updated?
We review them as we learn more from the firms we work with and as rules and practices change. If you spot something that looks out of date, or you think something important is missing, please tell our team. We would rather hear about it than have firms rely on guidance that no longer fits.
Can we share the guides with our team?
Yes. Many firms share them with intake staff, trainees and new fee earners as part of training. They work well as a starting point for a team discussion about how your firm handles new enquiries. You may want to adapt the examples and wording to match your own firm's voice, policies and practice areas.
Which guide should we read first?
If you are about to start receiving leads, read speed to lead and then the first call script. If you already receive enquiries but few instruct, start with converting enquiries, which will help you find where people drop out. If you are deciding whether to buy leads at all, start with the comparison of buying leads and SEO.
What is the single most useful change most firms can make?
For many firms it is calling new enquiries faster, with a clear rota so leads are never waiting for one person. For others it is following up people who do not answer or who say not yet. The converting enquiries guide helps you track where your own firm loses most clients, so you can focus on the change that will help you most.
Do the intake guides apply to all practice areas?
Yes. The basic principles of fast contact, a structured first call, clear fees, a booked next step and polite follow-up apply across conveyancing, family, immigration, motoring, licensing, lease extensions and debt recovery. The guides point out where tone, timing or emphasis should change for particular areas, such as discretion in family matters or deadlines in immigration.
Do we need a CRM to follow the guides?
No, although a CRM makes tracking and follow-up easier. Many firms start with a shared inbox, a call rota and a simple spreadsheet. The important things are that leads are visible to more than one person, that follow-up steps are planned and recorded, and that you track a few basic numbers. You can add software later if it solves a specific problem.
How should we train new intake staff?
Many firms use the first call guide as a training framework, with new staff listening to experienced colleagues, practising with example enquiries and then making calls with support. Give them fee notes for each practice area, a clear escalation route for urgent or sensitive matters and regular feedback. The following up guide helps them understand the full sequence, not just the first call.
How do we measure whether intake is improving?
Track when each enquiry arrives, when it is first called, when you first speak to the client, whether a consultation is booked and whether they instruct. Review these numbers monthly and look for patterns, such as slow responses at certain times or drop-off after consultations. Change one thing at a time so you can see what made a difference.
Will there be guides for other practice areas?
We have started with conveyancing, family law and immigration because they are the areas firms ask us about most. We may add guides for debt recovery, motoring offences, licensing and lease extensions over time. If you would find a guide for a particular area useful, let our team know, because we add guides based on demand.
Should we buy leads or invest in our own marketing?
Most firms benefit from both. Your own website, reviews, SEO and referrals build long term value, while bought leads add predictable volume you control. The buying leads vs SEO guide sets out the trade-offs honestly, including where buying leads may not be the right fit. The right mix depends on your budget, timescales and the work you want to grow.
How do we compare different marketing channels fairly?
Look at the total cost per instruction for each channel, including agency fees, software and your team's time, over the same period and for the same practice area. Also consider the value of the work each channel brings and how quickly it produces results. Comparing cost per click or cost per enquiry on their own can be misleading.
Do the marketing guides cover advertising rules?
They mention key points in general terms, such as not promising outcomes, being clear about fees and following your regulator's rules on publicity. They are not a complete guide to advertising regulation. Check the current rules from your regulator, and for immigration make sure any content that could amount to advice is prepared or checked by an authorised person.
How important are reviews?
Very important in most private client areas. Clients who cannot judge legal quality rely on what others say about responsiveness, clarity and how they were treated. Reviews also help convert enquiries from every channel, including bought leads. Ask happy clients at a natural point, make it easy, never pressure anyone and respond politely to every review you receive.
Is it lawful to pay for legal leads?
In the practice areas Leaflegal covers, firms can pay introducers, provided they meet their regulator's rules. Under the SRA Code of Conduct, clients must be told about the financial arrangement in writing, and it must not affect your independence or advice. Referral fees are banned for personal injury and clinical negligence under LASPO, which is why Leaflegal does not cover those areas.
What consent do Leaflegal clients give?
Before submitting an enquiry, each person ticks two separate boxes. The first confirms they have read our terms and privacy policy and understand Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm. The second agrees to their details being shared with a firm we match them with and to that firm contacting them by phone, email or text about their matter. We record the wording and time.
Can we add leads to our marketing list?
Only if the client separately agrees. The consent given with each Leaflegal lead covers contact about the client's enquiry, not newsletters, offers or general marketing. If you would like to send marketing, ask for consent clearly and separately and keep a record. The following up guide explains the difference between follow-up and marketing in more detail.
Who is responsible for lead data once we receive it?
Your firm becomes the data controller for your copy of the lead and must handle it in line with UK data protection law and your own privacy notice. Some leads include sensitive information, especially in family, immigration and motoring matters, so keep them in secure systems and limit who can see them. Use the data only to contact the client about their enquiry.
Can non-solicitor immigration advisers receive leads?
Yes, if they are registered with the Immigration Advice Authority, formerly the OISC, at the right level for the work. We also send immigration enquiries to firms regulated by the SRA or CILEx Regulation. Leaflegal never gives immigration advice. Our form asks only for the type of matter, urgency and contact details, and all advice comes from the firm or adviser.
What should we do if we find a conflict?
Run your normal conflict checks before taking on any matter. If you find a conflict with a Leaflegal lead, do not act and let us know, and we will deal with the lead as your partner agreement sets out. Running a quick check before the first call, where your systems allow it, avoids difficult conversations after you have spoken to the client.
What is Leaflegal?
Leaflegal is an introduction service run by Vertical Brands Ltd. We run campaigns for people and businesses who need legal help, ask them a short set of screening questions and, with their consent, introduce each one to a single partner firm that handles their kind of matter. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice.
Are Leaflegal leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only and is never resold. We do not send the same lead to several firms. The client may still contact other firms themselves, which is why the intake guides focus so much on fast, helpful first contact and polite follow-up, even with exclusive leads.
How are leads delivered?
By email to one or more addresses, to a shared intake inbox or straight into your CRM or case management system by webhook. Leads are sent as soon as each enquiry is checked, with every answer, the source, a timestamp and the consent record. There is no online platform or login to manage.
How much do leads cost?
You pay per lead at a price agreed with you individually before you start. It depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose. We do not publish prices because they vary by firm. The minimum is 10 leads a month, and pricing, billing, delivery and invalid lead rules are set out in a written agreement.
Do we need to disclose the referral to clients?
Yes. The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement with an introducer, in writing, and that it does not affect your independence or advice. Leaflegal's consent wording tells clients we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send that wording with each lead so your client care letter can match it.
How do we become a partner firm?
Apply through the partner application and book a 30-minute call with our team. We will talk through your practice areas, matter types, locations, regulation and monthly volume, show you a sample lead and agree a price per lead. Once your written agreement is in place, leads start as soon as matching enquiries come in.
Can we pause or change our leads later?
Yes. You can pause when your team is at capacity or a fee earner is away, change your monthly cap above the minimum of 10, and update your practice areas, matter types and locations as your firm's priorities change. Feedback on which leads instruct helps us adjust your criteria so you receive more of the work you convert best.

More questions people ask

Who can become a partner firm?
SRA regulated solicitors' firms, CILEx regulated practices, CLC regulated conveyancers and, for immigration, advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level for the work. You must be authorised for every kind of work you ask to receive. We ask about your regulation when you apply and only match enquiries in areas where you can act. If you are unsure whether your firm fits, mention it on your application.
Which practice areas do you cover?
Conveyancing, family law, immigration, debt recovery for businesses, motoring offences, licensing and lease extensions. You choose which areas you want, and the matter types and locations within each. We do not cover personal injury, clinical negligence or claims management work. Each area has its own page with more detail. Choose any combination.
Do you cover Scotland and Northern Ireland?
Not at the moment. Leads in six areas are for matters in England and Wales. Trademark and immigration introductions can come from anywhere in the UK, because one UK trademark register and one set of immigration rules apply across the UK. If you are a firm outside England and Wales, mention it when you apply and we will tell you honestly whether the service fits.
Is there an online platform?
No. There is no platform, portal or login. Leads arrive by email or in your CRM, and any changes to your criteria, volume or delivery are made by talking to our team. Many firms prefer this, because there is nothing new for their staff to learn and no extra system to check during a busy day.
How do I apply?
Complete the partner application, which takes a couple of minutes. It asks about your practice areas, how you are regulated, the areas you cover and how many leads you want. We then book a 30-minute call with our team and show you a sample lead. There is no obligation until you sign your partner agreement.
Can I see a sample lead before committing?
Yes. On your 30-minute call we show you a sample lead in the format you would receive it, including every field, the client's description and the consent record. It shows exactly what your intake team would be working with, so you can decide whether it suits your firm before you agree anything.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We never send the same lead to several firms and never resell a lead that has been delivered. When you call, you are the only firm Leaflegal has introduced the client to. Every partner firm receives leads on this basis. It is not an optional extra and it does not depend on your price or volume.
Where do your leads come from?
From people completing our own enquiry forms, usually after seeing one of our campaigns on social media or finding us through search. We do not buy data, rent lists, scrape directories or pass on cold contacts. Every lead is someone who asked to be contacted about their own matter and ticked a separate box agreeing to be introduced to a firm.
Do you send legal aid enquiries?
No. Every client confirms they are looking for paid legal help before their enquiry is submitted. People who only want legal aid or free advice are told we cannot help and pointed elsewhere. This does not mean every client will accept your fees, but it does mean everyone who reaches you expects a solicitor to charge for their work. Being clear about fees early helps.
Can I choose the matter types I receive?
Yes. Within each practice area, tell us what you want and what you do not. A family firm might take contested finances but not simple divorce. A conveyancing team might take purchases but not remortgages. We only send enquiries that fit. You can change your choices at any time by telling our team.
How do you decide which firm gets a lead?
Each lead goes to one firm whose practice areas, matter types and locations it fits, taking account of each firm's monthly cap. Where several firms fit, we balance distribution fairly between them. There is no auction or bidding. Your price is agreed in advance and does not move with other firms' bids. Paused firms are left out of matching until they are ready again.
Will every lead become an instruction?
No. Some clients compare firms, some decide not to proceed and some do not accept the fees. We provide qualified, exclusive enquiries, not guaranteed instructions. Calling quickly and being clear about fees gives you the best chance. A lead stays valid if the client chooses another firm or decides not to proceed. Your own records will show how leads work for your firm over time.
What information comes with each lead?
The client's name, a verified UK mobile number and email address, the practice area and matter type, a description in their own words, urgency and any deadline, their location, confirmation of paid help, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the consent record. Some areas include extra fields, such as rough property value or years left on a lease.
How much does each lead cost?
Pricing is agreed with each firm before it starts and depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose and the volume you want. We do not publish prices because they vary widely. We send a written proposal after your call. Each practice area you take has its own price.
Is there a minimum?
Yes. The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that, you set a monthly cap that your team can comfortably call back, and you can change it at any time. Ten a month is enough to judge the quality of the leads and see how they fit with your intake process. Many firms start near the minimum and raise their cap once their routine is working.
Can I pause leads?
Yes. If your team is at capacity, a fee earner is away or your office is closed, ask us to pause. You receive no leads while paused, and your criteria and price stay the same for when you return. Pausing is a normal part of how firms use the service and does not need explaining.
Do you guarantee volumes?
We cannot guarantee exact volumes, because they depend on how many people enquire and how closely they match your criteria. We give you an honest view of likely volumes when we agree your criteria, and tell you if your criteria are likely to limit them. Wider coverage usually helps. Narrow criteria can limit matches.
What if a lead is invalid?
Your partner agreement defines an invalid lead, such as one with wrong contact details or outside your agreed criteria, and sets a review period for raising it. Genuinely invalid leads are dealt with as your agreement sets out. A lead stays valid if the client chooses another firm or decides not to go ahead. Reporting promptly helps us fix any cause.
Do clients pay Leaflegal?
No. Clients do not pay Leaflegal anything. Your firm pays for the introduction and the client agrees fees directly with you. Every client is told that Leaflegal is paid by the firms it introduces. Our arrangement with your firm does not change what the client pays you. The client is free to decide whether to instruct you after your first conversation.
Can I change my criteria after starting?
Yes. Add or remove practice areas or matter types, widen or narrow your locations and raise or lower your cap whenever your firm's needs change. Tell our team and we will update your matching. Most firms adjust something in their first few months. Changes apply to new leads. Leads already delivered remain yours.
