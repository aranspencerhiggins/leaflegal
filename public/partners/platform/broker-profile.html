Your public profile on Loanleaf Platform is the logo, description and contact buttons shown to a business owner in the "you've been matched" email they receive when you buy their lead. It is their first impression of your firm, so a clear logo, a short honest description and working contact buttons make your first call easier.

Where the profile appears

When you buy a lead, Loanleaf emails the business owner to tell them they have been matched with a broker. That email shows your public profile: your logo, a short description of your firm and buttons they can use to contact you.

The business owner asked to be introduced to a broker, but they do not yet know you. The profile is how they decide that you look like a real, relevant firm before your call comes in, and it gives them a way to get in touch first if they would rather.

What goes in it

  • Logo: your firm's logo, so the email looks like it is from a business they can recognise
  • Description: a few lines about what you do and who you help
  • Contact buttons: the quick ways for the business owner to reach you

You fill this in during onboarding, as step four of setting up, and you can change it later from Public profile in the platform menu.

Writing a description that works

Keep it short and plain. The reader is a business owner who has just filled in a form about funding. They want to know who you are, what you arrange, and whether you are the right kind of firm for their ask.

A good description covers three things: the finance you arrange (for example asset finance, working capital or commercial mortgages), the sort of businesses you work with, and how you work (for example, "a specialist adviser will call you the same day"). Avoid jargon and avoid claims you cannot support.

Compare these two. Weak: "Leading provider of bespoke funding solutions." Better: "We arrange asset finance and working capital for UK limited companies. A named adviser will call you, explain your options and tell you our fees before you decide anything."

If you work with several finance types, lead with the one that brings you most leads and mention the others briefly. A profile that tries to cover everything tends to read as vague, and a business owner with a clear need wants to see their need named.

Logo tips

Use a clear version of your logo that reads well at small size on both light and dark backgrounds. A wide logo with tiny text will be hard to read in an email, so use a simplified or stacked version if you have one.

Check the profile as the business owner will see it before your first purchase. If it looks cramped or blurry, replace the image.

Contact buttons

The contact buttons give the business owner an easy route to you. Make sure each one goes somewhere that is checked all day, such as a direct line or a shared inbox, not a personal mobile that may be switched off.

If a business owner clicks to call and nobody answers, the lead has gone cold. Whoever is working the lead should know the profile exists, and what number it shows.

A short checklist before you buy

  • Is the logo sharp and readable at small size?
  • Does the description say what you arrange, in plain English?
  • Do the contact buttons reach someone who will answer today?
  • Does everyone who takes calls know what the profile promises?
  • Does your firm name match what you will say on the phone?

A few minutes spent on these checks costs nothing, and you cannot get the first impression back once the matched email has gone out. It also helps to ask a colleague outside the sales team to read the profile cold, and say what they think your firm does.

Making the profile work with your call

The profile and your first call should say the same thing. If your description promises a same-day call, make it a same-day call. Say on the call that you are the broker named in the email they received, as that is the connection that tells them the call is expected.

Treat the business fairly: explain your fees clearly, respect any request not to be contacted, and do not pressure anyone into a decision. Loanleaf asks partners to contact leads promptly and courteously. For how to work leads once bought, see lead management.

Keeping it current

Review the profile whenever your branding, contact routes or services change. Out-of-date contact details waste leads you have already paid for.

Questions

Does the business owner always see my profile?
Yes. When you buy a lead, they are sent a "you've been matched" email that shows your public profile with your logo and contact buttons.
Can I change my profile after onboarding?
Yes. It is under Public profile in the platform, and you can update it whenever your firm's details change.
How long should my description be?
A few short lines are enough. Say what finance you arrange, who you help and how you will contact them, then stop.
Is the profile a promise to the business owner?
In practice it sets expectations. Do what it says, call promptly and be clear about fees. You are responsible for your own regulatory position.
Do I have to complete it before buying?
The public profile is part of onboarding, along with your company details, criteria, plan, partner agreement and payment. You cannot buy until approval, the signed agreement and credits are in place.
