What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

A legal lead is worth most in the first few minutes after it arrives. The client has just described their matter, is expecting a call and is still focused on finding help. That is why Leaflegal sends each lead to your firm as soon as the enquiry has been checked, straight to wherever your intake team already works.

Delivery is deliberately simple. There is no portal to log into, no dashboard to refresh and no app to install. Leads arrive by email, to a shared intake inbox, or directly into your CRM or case management system. You choose the route when you set up with us, and you can change it whenever your firm's way of working changes.

Fast delivery only helps if someone acts on it. The rest of this page explains the delivery options in more detail, how firms route leads to the right people and how to handle leads that arrive outside office hours. Getting these details right before your first lead is one of the simplest ways to win more of the enquiries you pay for.

The delivery options

  • Email to one person or to several addresses
  • A shared intake inbox that your whole team can see
  • Your CRM or case management system, using a lead email address
  • Your CRM or case management system, using a webhook
  • Different routes for different practice areas or locations

No batching

We do not hold leads back and send them in a daily or weekly file. Each lead is sent on its own, as soon as it passes our checks. A lead that arrives at ten past two is in your inbox or CRM at ten past two, not the next morning.

One firm, one copy

Each lead is delivered to one firm only. The copy your firm receives is the only copy any law firm receives from Leaflegal, and it is never resold. Once it is delivered, your firm becomes the controller of that copy and is responsible for handling it securely under UK data protection law and your own privacy notice.

Setting it up

We agree your delivery route on your onboarding call, alongside your practice areas, matter types, locations and volume. If you want to test it first, we can talk about how to check that leads are reaching the right place before your first real enquiry arrives.

Email is the simplest way to receive leads and works with any intake process. Most firms start with email, even if they later move leads into a CRM.

Email also has the advantage that it needs no setup on your side beyond choosing an address. If your firm is trying Leaflegal for the first time, starting with email lets you see how leads fit your intake before deciding whether to connect them to your CRM.

One address or several

Leads can go to a single address or to several at once. A small firm might send them to the partner who handles new business and to a practice manager. A larger firm might send them to a team inbox and copy a supervising partner.

A shared intake inbox

Many firms set up a dedicated address for new enquiries, which several people can see. This is usually better than sending leads to one fee earner's personal inbox. If that person is in court, on leave or simply busy, a lead sent only to them can sit unread. A shared inbox means someone is always able to pick it up.

Notifications

Leads are only as fast as the person who sees them. Make sure whoever handles new leads has notifications switched on, on their phone as well as their desktop. Some firms set up a rule that flags Leaflegal leads so they stand out from general email.

Readable on a phone

Each lead email is formatted to be easy to read on a phone. The most important information, such as the client's name, number, matter type and urgency, is near the top, so the person calling can see what they need at a glance.

Keeping a record

If leads arrive by email, decide where you will record what happened to each one. A simple spreadsheet or a note in your case management system is enough. Without a record, it is hard to tell later which leads were called, which instructed and which need following up.

Avoiding spam filters

Make sure your email system treats lead emails as legitimate. When your delivery is set up, check that the first lead reaches the inbox rather than a junk or quarantine folder. If your firm uses strict filtering, your IT provider may need to allow our sending address.

If your firm uses a CRM or case management system to manage new enquiries, leads can be sent straight into it. This saves your team copying details from an email and means every lead is recorded in the same place as your other enquiries.

Delivery into a CRM also makes it easier to measure results, because the lead, every call and the eventual outcome sit together in one record. That makes it far simpler to see which practice areas and matter types bring in the most work from your leads.

Using a lead email address

Many CRMs and case management systems give each firm a special email address that turns incoming emails into new enquiries or records. If your system has one, we can send leads to it. The lead appears in your system just as an enquiry from your own website would.

Using a webhook

Some systems accept new records through a webhook, which is a way of sending information directly from one system to another over the web. If your system accepts webhooks, we can send each lead to it as structured data, so that fields such as name, phone, email and matter type land in the right places.

Which to choose

If you are not sure which your system supports, tell us what you use and ask your supplier or IT provider. A lead email address is often the quickest to set up. A webhook can give a neater result where the system supports it. Either way, the content of the lead is the same.

Email and CRM together

Some firms send leads into their CRM and also to an email address, so that the intake team gets an instant alert while the record is created in the background. Tell us if you would like this.

If something breaks

Systems change. A supplier updates its software, a firm changes CRM or an email address is retired. If leads stop arriving where you expect, tell us straight away. It is also worth keeping an email route in place as a backup when you rely on a CRM.

Not every lead needs to go to the same place. Firms with several teams or offices often route leads so they reach the right people first time.

By practice area

If you take leads in more than one area, each area can go to a different address or team. Family leads can go to your family team, conveyancing leads to your property team and debt recovery leads to your commercial team.

By location

If you have more than one office, leads can be routed by the client's location so that each office receives the enquiries closest to it. This can help when clients want to meet in person.

Into your CRM for assignment

Some firms prefer to send every lead into one place and assign them internally. A CRM is well suited to this, because it can show who has picked up each lead and when.

Avoiding double calls

If more than one person can see a lead, agree how you will mark it as taken. Two people calling the same client within minutes can be confusing. A simple reply to the team, a flag in the inbox or an assignment in your CRM is enough.

Cover for absences

Routing to a named person works well until that person is away. If you route leads to individuals, make sure someone else can see them during holidays and sickness, or switch the route temporarily. Tell us if you need to change it.

Keeping it simple

Routing can become complicated if every practice area, office and fee earner has its own rule. The more rules there are, the more likely it is that a lead goes somewhere nobody is watching when people change roles or go on leave. Many firms find the best approach is one shared inbox or one CRM route for all leads, with internal assignment from there. Start simple and add routing only where it clearly helps.

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. Nothing is added from other sources.

  • The client's name, verified UK mobile number and email address
  • The practice area and type of matter
  • A short description of the matter in the client's own words
  • How urgent it is, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about

  • Confirmation that they want paid legal help
  • The best time to call, where they gave one

Extra fields by practice area

Some areas include extra information. Conveyancing leads show the transaction type, rough property value, tenure and whether there is a mortgage. Lease extension leads show roughly how many years are left. Motoring leads show the type of offence, any court date and current points. Licensing leads show the licence type, the application or review involved and the council area. Debt recovery leads show the rough amount owed, the age of the debt and whether it is disputed.

What does not arrive

Leads do not include anything the client did not tell us. We do not add data from other sources, and we do not include any assessment of the merits of the matter, because Leaflegal does not give legal advice.

Reading a lead quickly

Most people who handle leads read them in a particular order. First the urgency and any deadline, to decide whether to call immediately. Then the matter type, to check it is something they handle. Then the client's own description, which gives them a natural way to open the call. The consent record is there if the client does not remember enquiring or asks how you got their details. With practice, this takes less than a minute.

People fill in enquiry forms when they have time, which is often in the evening, late at night or at the weekend. Leads arrive whenever enquiries are checked, so it helps to have a plan for those that come in outside office hours.

First thing in the morning

Many firms ask one person to check the intake inbox first thing each working day and call overnight leads before the day gets busy. A client who enquired at eleven at night and hears from you at nine the next morning is usually pleased with the response.

Evening cover for urgent areas

Some areas are more time sensitive than others. Motoring clients may have a court date within days, and immigration clients may be working to a deadline. Some firms arrange evening cover for these areas, or ask a fee earner to send a short text or email to acknowledge the enquiry and promise a call in the morning.

Weekends

Weekend leads can wait until Monday morning, but a short acknowledgement by text or email on Saturday or Sunday can stop the client looking elsewhere in the meantime. Decide what suits your firm and stick to it.

Holidays

Over bank holidays and the Christmas period, leads can build up. If your firm closes, consider pausing leads or lowering your cap so that you do not return to a backlog of enquiries that have gone cold.

A short acknowledgement

A brief text or email that says you have received the enquiry, gives your name and promises a call at a particular time can be sent in seconds from a phone. It reassures the client that a real person has seen their enquiry and makes them less likely to keep searching for another firm overnight.

More questions people ask

What is a licensing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a business or individual in England and Wales who wants to pay a solicitor to help with a licence. They have told us the licence type, whether it is a new application, a variation, a transfer or a review, any hearing or opening date, the council area and the business name, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm.
Are licensing leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold. We do not send the same venue or driver to several firms at once. That gives you the chance to have a proper first conversation, which matters in licensing because many clients come back for further work once they trust a firm.
Do you send enquiries from objectors?
No. Our licensing enquiries come from licence holders, applicants, operators and drivers who want help with their own licence or business. We do not send enquiries from residents or groups who want to object to a licence. That keeps the leads consistent with acting for the licensed trade and avoids an obvious source of conflict.
Which licence types can I receive?
You can choose premises licence work, personal licences, and taxi and private hire licensing, and within those you can choose new applications, variations, transfers and changes of designated premises supervisor, and reviews and hearings. You can take all of them or only the kinds of work your team wants, and change your choice later.
Will I know if there is a hearing date?
Yes. Each lead includes any hearing date or planned opening date the client knows about. A venue facing a review next week and a business planning to open in six months need very different first calls, and the lead tells you which you are dealing with before you pick up the phone.
Why do you include the business name?
The business name lets you run conflict checks before you call and look at publicly available information, such as the existing licence on the council's register. That way you can open the conversation already knowing something about the premises, which helps show the client you have taken their enquiry seriously.
Do you send taxi and private hire enquiries?
Yes, if you want them. Taxi and private hire enquiries come from drivers, operators and vehicle proprietors dealing with applications, renewals, suspensions, revocations and appeals. Drivers whose licence has been suspended are often under real financial pressure and want help quickly. You can switch taxi work on or off separately from premises work.
Do you send enquiries about temporary event notices?
Event organisers and venues sometimes enquire about one off events, often alongside a wider question about their premises licence. If you want this work, tell us and we will include it in your matching where it fits. Many firms find that a business asking about a single event turns into a longer relationship once it sees how a solicitor can help with its licence more generally.
