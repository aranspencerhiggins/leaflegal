Exclusive design rights enquiries from people who have asked to speak to a solicitor. Design enquiries come from product businesses protecting something they have invested in, often alongside trademark work.

What the client is looking for

Protecting the look of a product, and acting on copies.

Design rights protect the appearance of a product, such as its shape, pattern, contours or decoration. A registered design gives the strongest protection, for up to 25 years in five year periods, and is applied for at the UK IPO. Unregistered design rights also arise automatically but are shorter and harder to enforce.

What the client expects from you

  • Registered design applications
  • Unregistered UK design right and supplementary rights
  • Dealing with copies and lookalike products
  • Licensing and selling designs
  • Defending design claims

Where they are in the process

Some clients are at the idea stage with a shortlist of names. Others have filed themselves and hit an objection. Some have a registered mark and a problem with a copier, and some are on the receiving end of a complaint. The form records which, so you know before you call whether you are talking about a filing, a deadline or a dispute.

What clients care about

Most trademark clients want to understand quickly whether they have a problem, what it will cost to deal with and how long it will take. Clear fixed fees for searches and filings, a plain explanation of official fees and a realistic view of disputes go a long way. Many become long term portfolio clients if the first matter goes well.

How clients describe their problem

Most clients don't use technical language. They say things like someone has copied my brand on Amazon, I got a letter saying my name is too similar, or I want to protect my logo. The form turns that into a matter type and a set of tags, and the description in their own words often adds useful colour about the business and what is at stake. It is worth reading before you call.

Individuals and creators

Not every client is a company. Photographers, illustrators, writers and musicians enquire about copied work, and individuals sometimes receive image claims for personal or side business websites. These matters are often smaller but quick to resolve, and you can choose whether you want them.

Deadlines

Where a lead mentions a deadline, such as an opposition period or a reply date, prioritise it. If you can't help in time, tell the client quickly so they can look elsewhere, and let us know.

Explaining fees

Trademark clients are often surprised that there are two sets of fees, the official UK IPO fees and the professional fees. A short, clear explanation on the first call, followed by a written quote that separates the two, builds confidence and avoids misunderstandings later. Fixed fees for common work, such as searches, filings and simple replies to examination reports, tend to convert well.

If an enquiry turns out to need work you don't do, such as a patent or a court claim outside your jurisdiction, tell the client promptly and, if you can, point them to someone who can help. Following up

Not every client instructs on the first call. Some need to talk to a business partner, a designer or an investor first. A short follow up a few days later, with a reminder of any deadline and a clear next step, often makes the difference. Keep notes of what the client told you so the follow up feels personal.

What clients ask

What does a design right protect?
The appearance of a product or part of a product, such as its shape, contours, lines, colours, texture, materials or decoration. It does not protect how a product works, which may be a matter for patents, or a brand name, which is a matter for trademarks.
Do I need to register a design?
Not always, because some protection is automatic. Registration gives clearer and longer protection and doesn't require proof of copying, which makes it much easier to act against lookalikes. Many businesses register their most important designs.
How long does a registered design last?
Up to 25 years, in five year periods. It must be renewed every five years by paying a renewal fee. If you stop renewing, protection ends.
How long do unregistered design rights last?
It depends on the right. UK unregistered design right for shape generally lasts up to 15 years from creation or 10 years from first sale, whichever is earlier. Supplementary unregistered design protects appearance for three years from first disclosure in the UK.
What makes a design new?
A design is new if no identical design, or one differing only in immaterial details, has been made available to the public before the filing date, subject to the 12 month grace period for the designer's own disclosures.
What is individual character?
A design has individual character if the overall impression it gives an informed user differs from the overall impression of earlier designs. The informed user is someone familiar with the products in that sector, more observant than an average consumer.
Can I protect part of a product?
Yes. A registered design can cover part of a product, such as the handle of a kettle or the sole of a shoe. The images show which part is protected, often by drawing other parts in dotted lines.
Is a design right the same as a trademark?
No. A design right protects the look of a product. A trademark protects a sign that tells customers who the product comes from, such as a name or logo. Some shapes can be both, but the rules and tests are different.
Can I register a design I have already shown publicly?
Often yes, if you apply within 12 months of first disclosing it. After that, your own disclosure can stop the design being registered. If you plan to show a product, it is best to speak to a firm first.
What images do I need?
Clear images showing the design from enough angles to make it understood, often several views. Line drawings, photographs or renderings can be used. The images define the protection, so they need to be prepared carefully.
Does the UK IPO check whether my design is new?
Not in detail. The UK IPO mainly checks formal requirements and does not search earlier designs before registering. A registered design can be challenged later if it was not new, so it is worth being confident about your design.
Can I register several designs together?
Yes. You can file multiple designs in one application, which can reduce costs. Each design is treated separately for protection and renewal.
Can I delay publication of my registered design?
Yes. You can ask for publication to be deferred, which can help if you want to keep the design confidential until launch. The firm can explain the time limits.
Do I need protection abroad?
A UK registered design only covers the UK. You can register in other countries, including through the Hague system for international designs. If you manufacture or sell abroad, consider where copies are most likely.
Who owns a design created by an external designer?
Ownership rules for designs can differ from copyright and from the position for employees. Get a written agreement that assigns the design rights to your business, so there is no doubt when you register or enforce.
How long does registration take?
If the application meets the formal requirements, registration often takes a few weeks. Deferring publication will delay when the design appears on the register. The firm can tell you the likely timing for your application.
Can a design be registered if it is functional?
Features dictated solely by the product's technical function can't be protected by a registered design. Most products combine function with aesthetic choices, and those aesthetic features can be protected. The firm will help decide what to include in the images.
A competitor has copied my product. What can I do?
Gather evidence of your design and the copy, check which rights you have and get advice. Options include platform takedowns, a carefully worded letter and a court claim. A registered design makes action much easier, because copying doesn't need to be proved.
Do I have to prove copying?
For a registered design, no. If the other design gives the same overall impression, it can infringe even if it was created independently. For unregistered rights, you do need to show copying, which can be harder.
Is copying a registered design a crime?
Intentionally copying a registered design in the course of business, so as to make a product exactly like it or with features that differ only immaterially, can be a criminal offence in the UK. Civil remedies are also available.
I've been accused of copying a design. What should I do?
Don't ignore the letter and don't reply in anger. Gather evidence of how and when you created your design. The firm will compare the designs, check the other side's rights and help you respond.
Can I challenge a registered design?
Yes. A registered design can be declared invalid, for example if it was not new or lacked individual character when filed. A successful challenge removes the registration. Evidence of earlier designs is key.
What is a must fit or must match exclusion?
UK unregistered design right doesn't protect features that must fit another product to work, or that must match the appearance of another article of which the product is part, such as some spare parts. The details are technical, and the firm will advise.
Can I stop copies being imported?
Customs can be asked to watch for goods that infringe registered designs and other IP rights entering the UK. An application to customs needs details of your rights and how to identify copies. The firm can help prepare it.
What if the copy is slightly different?
For a registered design, the question is whether the other product gives the informed user a different overall impression. Small changes may not be enough to avoid infringement. For unregistered rights, the question is whether the design was copied exactly or substantially. The firm will compare the products.
How long do I have to bring a claim?
Claims for damages are generally subject to a six year limitation period, but delay can weaken your case and make an injunction harder to obtain. Unregistered rights may also expire if you wait too long, so it is best to act promptly.
Can I license my design?
Yes. A licence lets a manufacturer or another brand use your design on agreed terms, often in return for a royalty. Licences of registered designs should be in writing. The firm can prepare or review the agreement.
Can I sell my design rights?
Yes, by assignment. An assignment of a registered design should be in writing and recorded at the UK IPO so the register shows the new owner.
Should I use the word registered on my product?
You can mark products with the words registered design and the registration number. Marking can make it harder for an infringer to claim they didn't know about your rights, which can affect damages. Never mark a product as registered if it isn't.
How do I protect a product design and its brand together?
Many product businesses register both the design of the product and the brand name as a trademark. The firm can plan both together and file them at the same time.
Should my manufacturer sign anything about designs?
Yes, it is sensible. An agreement with your manufacturer should confirm that you own the design, that they can only make the product for you and that they keep your designs confidential. This helps prevent the manufacturer selling copies to others.
Does a registered design help with investors?
It can. Registered designs are clear, recorded assets that investors and buyers can check. Together with trademarks, they show that a product business has protected what makes its products distinctive.
How does Leaflegal help with design rights?
You tell us whether you want to register a design or deal with a copy or a claim. We pass your enquiry to one regulated firm that handles designs, and they contact you to discuss it and explain their fees.
Does it cost anything to use Leaflegal?
No. Leaflegal is free for you. The firm pays us a fee for the introduction, which doesn't change what you pay them, and they will tell you about it in writing.
Do you help with patents?
No. Patents protect how an invention works and are handled by patent attorneys, which is outside the work we introduce. If your product has a new technical feature, the Chartered Institute of Patent Attorneys has a directory.

Usually within one working day. If you have a launch date or a deadline, mention it in the form.
