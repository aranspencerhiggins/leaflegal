What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

Leaflegal charges law firms per lead. Each firm's price is agreed with it before it starts, based on the practice areas, matter types and locations it chooses and the volume it wants. The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that, the firm sets its own monthly cap and can change it or pause at any time.

We do not publish a price list. That is not because pricing is secret or complicated. It is because the value of a legal enquiry varies so much between areas of law and parts of the country that a single published figure would be wrong for almost every firm that read it. A price agreed for your criteria is fairer and more useful.

The pricing principles

  • You pay per lead, not per click, impression or hour of campaign management
  • Your price is agreed with you in writing before your first lead
  • Each practice area you take has its own price
  • The minimum is 10 leads a month, and above that you set a monthly cap
  • You can change your cap or pause at any time
  • There is no auction, so your price does not move with other firms' bids
  • Any costs beyond the price per lead are explained in your proposal before you commit

What is included

The price per lead covers everything that goes into producing an exclusive, checked enquiry. That includes our campaigns, our enquiry forms, the checks on contact details, the paid help question, the consent record and delivery to your inbox or CRM. You do not pay separately for advertising spend or campaign management.

What clients pay

Clients do not pay Leaflegal anything. We tell every client that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to. The client agrees fees directly with your firm, and our arrangement with you does not change what they pay you.

Pricing and your regulator

Your arrangement with Leaflegal is a payment to an introducer, so it sits alongside your regulatory duties. Under the SRA Code of Conduct, you must make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement with an introducer. Our consent wording tells every client that we are paid by the firms we introduce. Your compliance officer may want to see how the arrangement is set out in your partner agreement before you sign, and we are happy to explain it.

On your onboarding call we talk through the choices that shape your price. Understanding them helps you set up criteria that fit both your practice and your budget.

None of these factors is hidden. We will tell you how each of your choices affects your price, and you can try different combinations before deciding. If it helps, we can set out two versions in your proposal, one with broader criteria and one with narrower, so you can compare.

Practice area

Different areas of law produce enquiries of very different value. A matter that typically leads to substantial work over many months is worth more to a firm than one that is quick and low cost. Some areas are also harder and more expensive to generate enquiries in. Each area you choose is priced separately.

Matter type

Within an area, the matter types you choose make a difference. In family law, contested finances and simple divorce are very different propositions. In conveyancing, a sale and purchase together is different from a remortgage. Choosing narrower, higher value matter types usually means a higher price per lead but fewer enquiries you would not want.

Location

Coverage affects both the price and the volume. A firm covering one town will match fewer enquiries than a firm covering a region or working nationally. Filling narrow criteria can take more campaign work. Wider coverage tends to produce more matches and can make volumes more predictable.

Volume

The number of leads you want each month is part of the picture when we plan campaigns for your criteria. Volume is discussed on your call and reflected in your proposal.

What does not set your price

Your price does not depend on what another firm is willing to pay on a given day. There is no bidding and no auction. It does not rise during busy periods without agreement. Once agreed, it applies to every lead that matches your criteria until you and we agree something different.

Whatever your criteria, every lead you pay for is exclusive to your firm, checked and delivered with the client's consent record. Those things are not extras. They are part of every lead at every price.

After your call, we send a written proposal. It is the clearest way to see what working with Leaflegal would cost your firm.

The proposal is written in plain language. It should be possible for a partner who was not on the call to read it and understand exactly what the firm would receive and what it would pay. If any part of it is unclear, ask us to rewrite it.

What it sets out

  • The practice areas and matter types you will receive
  • The locations you will cover
  • Your monthly volume and cap
  • Your price per lead for each practice area
  • Any other costs, so nothing comes as a surprise
  • An honest view of likely volumes for your criteria

Adjusting it

Proposals often change once firms see them. A firm might decide to drop a lower value matter type, add a neighbouring county or start with a lower cap. Tell us what you would like to change and we will update it. It is better to get the proposal right than to sign something that does not quite fit.

Comparing it

When you compare our proposal with other ways of finding clients, look beyond the price per lead. Think about the cost of winning an instruction, the time your team spends chasing unsuitable enquiries and the management time other channels need. A proposal is easier to judge when you have a rough idea of what an instruction is currently costing you through other routes.

From proposal to agreement

Once you are happy with the proposal, its terms are written into your partner agreement. That agreement sets out pricing, billing, volumes, delivery and how invalid leads are handled. Leads start only once it is signed.

How and when you are billed is set out in your partner agreement before your first lead. The points below explain the general approach so that you know what to expect.

The aim is that nothing about billing should surprise you. Everything that affects what you pay is written down before you start, and any later change is agreed with you first.

You pay for delivered leads

Billing is based on the leads delivered to your firm that match your agreed criteria. If no matching enquiry comes in, there is nothing to bill for. If you pause, you receive no leads and pay nothing for leads while paused.

Your cap is your ceiling

Because you only pay for leads delivered and we stop matching once you reach your monthly cap, your cap also sets the most you will spend on leads in a month. Raise it, lower it or pause as your firm's needs change.

Invalid leads

Your agreement sets out what counts as an invalid lead, the review period for raising one and how it is dealt with. Typical examples are wrong contact details or a lead outside your agreed criteria. A lead remains valid if the client chooses another firm or decides not to proceed.

Records

Keep your own record of the leads you receive, when you called them and what happened. It makes it easy to check your bill against what arrived and to see which practice areas are working best.

Changes during the month

If you raise or lower your cap, or pause part way through a month, billing follows the leads actually delivered to you. You do not pay for leads you did not receive. Any change to your agreed price, for example because you add a new practice area, is agreed with you in writing before it applies.

Questions about a bill

If something on a bill does not look right, contact our team. Because there is no platform or login, queries are handled directly by people who know your account.

Pay per lead makes budgeting simpler than most marketing, because the cost of each enquiry is fixed and you control how many you receive. A little planning helps you get the most from your budget.

It also helps to decide in advance what success would look like. A firm that wants to fill one fee earner's diary needs a different budget from a firm that wants to open a new office. Having a clear goal makes it much easier to decide when to raise your cap and when to hold steady.

Start from capacity

The right budget is usually set by your team's capacity rather than by a number picked in advance. Work out how many new enquiries the people who will call leads could handle within minutes each month, and how many new matters they could take on. Your cap and your budget follow from that.

Start a little low

It is better to begin near the minimum and increase your cap once you are confident in your intake than to start high and leave leads waiting. Leads that are not called quickly are the most expensive leads of all.

Plan for seasons

Many firms have busy and quiet periods. Conveyancing teams may want more leads at some times of year than others. Family teams may see changes around holidays. Adjust your cap to match, and pause over closures.

Split by practice area

If you take leads in several areas, think about each area's budget separately. Each has its own price, its own conversion pattern and its own value to your firm.

Review regularly

Once a month, compare what you spent with the instructions and fees each practice area brought in. Allow time for slower matters to instruct. Use what you learn to shift your cap and criteria towards the work that pays best.

Think about lifetime value

Some clients bring more than one matter. A conveyancing client may later need a will or a lease extension. A business that instructs you on a debt may come back with others. A family client may refer a friend. When you judge the value of leads, it can help to think about what a good client is worth to your firm over time, not only the fee on the first matter. Track repeat work where you can.

Firms sometimes ask why we do not simply publish our prices. It is a fair question, and there are good reasons.

The range is too wide

The value of an enquiry varies enormously between practice areas, matter types and locations. A single figure would be too high for some firms and too low for others. A range would be so wide that it would tell you very little.

Your criteria change the answer

Two firms in the same town taking the same practice area can end up with different prices because one wants every matter type and the other wants only the most complex work, or because one covers a single town and the other covers a whole county.

Prices should be agreed, not assumed

We would rather talk to you, understand what you want and give you a price you can rely on than publish a figure that turns out not to apply. Agreeing a price in writing before you start means there are no surprises.

How to find out

Being honest about volume

A published price can also hide the more important question of volume. A low price is no use if your criteria will only ever produce two matching enquiries a month. When we put your proposal together, we give you our honest view of likely volume alongside the price, so you can judge the two together.

More questions people ask

What is a legal lead?
It is an enquiry from a person or business who wants to speak to a solicitor about their own matter and has agreed to be contacted by a firm. A good legal lead includes their contact details, the type of matter, how urgent it is, where they are and a record of their consent. It is an opportunity to win an instruction, not an instruction in itself.
Are legal leads the same as referrals?
They are similar in that someone introduces a client to your firm. The difference is that a traditional referral usually comes from a professional contact who knows the client, while a lead comes from a client who responded to marketing and asked to be introduced. In both cases, the SRA Code requires you to tell clients about any financial arrangement with the introducer.
Are legal leads worth buying?
For firms that call quickly and follow up properly, they can be a reliable source of new private clients. For firms that leave leads for a day or two, results tend to be disappointing whoever the supplier is. We will give you an honest view of likely volumes for your criteria before you start, and the minimum of 10 a month is enough to judge quality.
Why does freshness matter so much?
People who need a solicitor usually act on that need straight away. Within hours they may have asked a friend, found another firm or decided to wait. A lead that arrives as soon as it is checked and is called within minutes catches the client while they are focused on their matter and expecting a call.
What is the difference between a lead and a cold contact?
A lead is someone who asked to be contacted about their own matter. A cold contact is someone whose details were collected for another purpose, or bought from a list, and who is not expecting to hear from you. Leaflegal only produces leads. We never buy data or pass on cold contacts.
Do you sell legal leads in Scotland or Northern Ireland?
Not at the moment. Our leads in six practice areas are for matters in England and Wales. Immigration introductions can come from anywhere in the UK, because immigration law applies UK wide. If you are a firm outside England and Wales with an interest in immigration work, mention it when you apply and we will tell you honestly whether the service could work for you.
Will every legal lead become a client?
No. Some people are comparing firms, some decide not to proceed and some cannot afford the fees once they understand them. A lead is an opportunity, not a guaranteed instruction. Firms that call quickly, use the information in the lead and are clear about their fees tend to win the most work from the leads they receive.
