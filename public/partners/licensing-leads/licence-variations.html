Why the council area matters

Licensing is run by local authorities, and each one has its own statement of licensing policy, its own committee practices and, in some places, special policies for particular areas. Knowing the council before you call lets you speak to the client about what you know of that authority, in general terms, and decide whether the matter fits your coverage.

Taxi and private hire licensing is also run by councils, and each sets its own policies on matters such as driver standards and vehicle requirements. For drivers and operators, the council area tells you which policy and which appeal route are likely to be relevant.

Why the business name helps

Having the business name lets you run conflict checks before the first call and look at publicly available information, such as the existing licence on the council's register. That means you can open the conversation already understanding something about the premises.

What clients ask

What is the difference between a minor and a full variation?
A minor variation is a quicker process for small changes that could not adversely affect the licensing objectives. It has a shorter notice period, no hearing and a lower fee. A full variation is needed for bigger changes such as later alcohol hours. It involves a 28 day consultation, newspaper and blue notices and the possibility of a hearing.
Can I extend my alcohol hours with a minor variation?
No. A minor variation cannot be used to extend the hours during which alcohol is sold or supplied, or to change alcohol sales between 11pm and 7am. Those changes need a full variation. A minor variation can, however, be used to reduce hours if you no longer need them.
Can I add alcohol sales to my licence?
If your licence does not currently permit alcohol, adding it needs a full variation, not a minor one. You will also need a designated premises supervisor who holds a personal licence. A solicitor can explain whether a full variation or a new application is more suitable.
When do I need a new licence instead of a variation?
A variation cannot be used to vary the premises substantially. If a building is being completely rebuilt, extended into a different property or changed so much that it is effectively new premises, a fresh application may be required. Where the line falls is a matter of judgement, and a solicitor can advise on your project.
Can I change the DPS through a variation?
Changing the designated premises supervisor uses a separate, specific application, not a minor or full variation. It is usually quicker and can take immediate effect. Our page on transfers and DPS changes explains more about it.
Do I need to vary my licence after a refurbishment?
If the refurbishment changes the layout shown on your licence plan, such as moving the bar, changing exits or adding a new area, the plan will need updating. Small changes may be dealt with by a minor variation. Bigger changes may need a full variation. Trading on an out of date plan can cause problems.
How long does a minor variation take?
The council must make a decision within a set number of working days from receiving the application, and the white notice must be displayed for a shorter period than a full variation. In practice a minor variation is usually much quicker than a full variation. If the council does not decide in time, the application is treated as refused.
How long does a full variation take?
There is a 28 day consultation period. If there are no unresolved representations, the variation is usually granted shortly after. If a hearing is needed, it is normally held within 20 working days of the end of the consultation. Allow time beforehand for preparing the application and any plans.
What notice do I need to display?
For a minor variation, a white notice is displayed at the premises for a set period. For a full variation, a blue notice is displayed for 28 consecutive days, and a notice must also be published in a local newspaper. Each has set requirements for size, wording and position.
Can I trade under the new terms while I wait?
No. Until the variation is granted, you must trade in line with your existing licence. For a one off event, a temporary event notice may sometimes help, within the limits that apply. A solicitor can explain the options if you have a fixed date in mind.
Who is consulted on a minor variation?
The council decides which responsible authorities to consult, depending on what is being changed. Other people can also make representations during the notice period. The council considers what is said and decides whether the change could adversely affect the licensing objectives.
Do I need a new plan?
Only if the variation changes the layout or the areas used for licensable activities. If it does, the plan must meet the same requirements as for a new licence, including showing exits, fire safety equipment and the licensed area. Many operators use a specialist to redraw the plan.
What if the council does not decide a minor variation in time?
If the council does not make a decision within the time allowed, the application is treated as refused and the fee is not usually returned. In that situation the applicant can apply again, either as a minor variation or as a full variation. A solicitor can explain why the deadline was missed and what to do next.
Can I get later hours just for weekends or special days?
Yes, a variation can ask for different hours on different days, or for later hours on particular occasions such as bank holidays or New Year's Eve. These can be easier to justify than later hours every night. Temporary event notices are another way to cover occasional late nights, within the limits that apply.
Can I add a beer garden to my licence?
Usually yes, by including the outside area on the plan through a variation. Whether a minor or full variation is suitable depends on the possible impact. Conditions limiting the times the area is used, the number of people or the playing of music are common. Pavement seating on the highway usually needs separate permission.
Can I remove conditions that do not make sense?
Often yes. Unclear, out of date or unenforceable conditions can sometimes be removed by a minor variation. Conditions imposed after a hearing or review may need a full variation and a clear explanation. Your solicitor will look at the history of each condition before suggesting an approach.
Can I add deliveries or off sales?
If your licence only allows alcohol for consumption on the premises, adding sales for consumption off the premises, such as deliveries or takeaway drinks, usually needs a full variation. Conditions about age checks at the point of delivery are common. A solicitor can explain what the council will usually expect.
Do I need a variation to add live music?
Not always. Some live and recorded music in licensed premises has been deregulated within certain hours and audience sizes. Outside those limits, or for other types of entertainment, a variation may be needed. A solicitor can check whether your plans are already covered.
Can I change the times for late night refreshment only?
Yes. Late night refreshment is a separate licensable activity from alcohol, so the hours for hot food and drink can be varied on their own. Extending them still needs the right kind of variation and may attract objections about noise or people gathering. A solicitor can explain how your council usually treats these requests.
Can residents object to a variation?
Yes. Anyone can make a representation about a full variation, and people can also comment on a minor variation. For a full variation, representations must relate to the effect of the changes on the licensing objectives. Complaints about the existing licence as a whole are not normally relevant to a variation.
What happens if the police object?
Police representations are given significant weight because they are the main source of advice on crime and disorder. Many police objections are resolved by agreeing conditions or adjusting the hours requested. If not, the matter goes to a hearing. A solicitor can talk to the police licensing team on your behalf.
Can the council take away my existing hours at a variation hearing?
Generally the committee deals only with the changes applied for and cannot use a variation to take away existing rights. However, it can attach conditions to the varied part of the licence. Serious concerns raised at the hearing could lead someone to apply for a review separately.
Can I appeal a refused full variation?
Yes. An applicant can appeal to the magistrates' court within 21 days of being told of the decision. There is no right of appeal against a refused minor variation, but you can apply again as a full variation. Your solicitor will explain the merits and costs risks before you decide.
Can I reapply if my variation is refused?
You can apply again, but a council can treat a repeat application that is essentially the same as one recently refused as repetitive. Changing the proposal meaningfully, such as asking for shorter hours or offering stronger conditions, gives a better basis for a fresh application.
How much does a variation cost?
The council fee for a minor variation is a flat amount, while a full variation fee usually depends on the rateable value of the premises. Solicitor fees vary with the complexity of the change and whether there is a hearing. Check current council fees with the council or on GOV.UK, and ask the firm for a written quote.
Can a solicitor review my whole licence?
Yes. Many operators ask a solicitor to review the licence before a variation, checking the plan, hours and conditions against how the premises actually trades. This can identify other changes worth making at the same time and reduce the risk of accidental breaches.
Does Leaflegal charge me anything?
No. Leaflegal is free for businesses to use. The licensing firm we introduce you to pays us a fee for the introduction, and that does not change what you pay them. You are under no obligation to instruct the firm.
What should I have ready when the firm calls?
A copy of your current licence with its plan and conditions is the most useful thing. It also helps to explain what you want to change and why, any deadline such as a reopening date, and any history of complaints or contact with the police or environmental health about the premises.
Can the firm help with several premises at once?
Many licensing firms act for operators with several sites and can prepare variations across different councils. Each council applies its own policy, so the approach may differ from one area to another. Ask the firm how they manage multi site work and whether they offer a combined fee for related applications.
