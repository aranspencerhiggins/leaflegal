What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

Working with Leaflegal follows four steps. You book a call with our team. We agree your criteria and your price per lead, and show you a sample lead. You sign a partner agreement that sets out how everything works. Then matching enquiries are sent to you as soon as they are checked.

There is no software to install, no platform to learn and no login to remember. Leads arrive in the inbox or CRM your team already uses. When you want to change something, you tell our team and we change it.

Behind those four steps, a lot happens on our side that you never need to see. We run campaigns on social media, search and content aimed at people who need a solicitor now. We ask each person a short set of questions about their matter. We check their mobile number and email address, confirm they want paid help and record their consent to be contacted by a firm. We filter out anyone we cannot help. Only then is an enquiry matched to one firm.

The four steps at a glance

  • Book a call. A 30-minute conversation about your practice, your team and what you want.
  • Agree your criteria and pricing. Practice areas, matter types, locations, monthly volume and your price per lead, with a sample lead to look at.
  • Sign your partner agreement. Pricing, billing, volumes, delivery and invalid lead rules, all in writing.
  • Receive leads. Exclusive enquiries by email or CRM, as soon as they are checked.

What stays the same

Some things are the same for every partner firm, whatever their size or practice area. Every lead is exclusive to one firm and never resold. Every lead comes with the client's consent record. Every client has been told that Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm, and that we are paid by the firms we introduce. Leaflegal never gives legal advice, and never gives immigration advice in particular.

What you decide

Almost everything else is your choice. Which practice areas and matter types. Where your clients are. How many leads you want each month. Where leads are delivered. Whether to pause. You can change any of these as your firm changes.

How long it takes

The time from first call to first lead depends mostly on your firm. Some firms know exactly what they want, sign their agreement within days and start receiving leads as soon as matching enquiries come in. Others take longer because a partner or compliance officer wants to review the agreement, or because the firm is setting up a shared inbox and deciding who will call new leads. Neither approach is wrong. It is better to start ready than to start quickly and leave the first leads waiting.

Everything starts with a conversation. You can apply through our partner form, which takes a couple of minutes, and we will arrange a 30-minute call with our team at a time that suits you.

What we will ask about

  • The practice areas you handle and the matter types you want most
  • Matter types you would rather not receive
  • Where your clients usually are, and whether you work remotely
  • How you are regulated, and your registration details
  • Who will call new leads, and how quickly they can usually do it
  • How many new enquiries your team could take on each month
  • How you would like leads delivered

What you can ask us

Ask anything. Firms usually want to know where our leads come from, what the consent wording says, how we decide which firm receives a lead, how pricing works and what happens if a lead has wrong details. We will answer plainly. If we do not think the service is a good fit for your firm, we will say so.

Seeing a sample lead

On the call we show you a sample lead in the format you would receive it. You can see every field, how the client's description reads, what the consent record looks like and how it all appears on a phone screen. Many firms find this the most useful part of the conversation, because it shows exactly what their intake team would be working with.

No obligation

The call does not commit you to anything. It is a chance for both sides to understand whether working together makes sense.

After the call

Within a short time of the call, we send your proposal. It sets out the criteria we discussed, your price per lead for each practice area and our honest view of likely volumes. If something in it does not match what you expected, tell us. It is common for firms to adjust a matter type or a location at this stage before moving on to the agreement.

After the call, we set up your matching criteria and put together a proposal. Your criteria decide which enquiries reach you. Your price per lead depends on those criteria.

Criteria are written in plain words rather than codes, so you can read them and know exactly what you will receive. If you are not sure whether a particular kind of matter would be included, ask us before you sign.

Practice areas

Choose from conveyancing, family law, immigration, debt recovery for businesses, motoring offences, licensing and lease extensions. You can take one area or several, and each is set up separately.

Matter types

Within each area, choose the matters you want. A family firm might take contested finances and children work but not uncontested divorce. A conveyancing team might take purchases and sales but not remortgages. A commercial firm might take debt recovery above a rough amount owed.

Locations

Tell us the towns, counties or regions you cover, or that you work nationally. Matters in six of our areas are in England and Wales. Immigration enquiries can come from anywhere in the UK.

Volume

The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that you set a monthly cap based on how many enquiries your team can call back quickly.

Your proposal

Your proposal sets out your criteria, your price per lead for each practice area and any other costs, so you can see exactly what you would be agreeing to. We will also give you an honest view of likely volumes. If your criteria are narrow, we will tell you and suggest options.

A worked example

Imagine a firm with offices in two neighbouring towns. It might choose family law, taking contested finances and child arrangements but not simple divorce, plus conveyancing purchases and sales but not remortgages. It might cover its two towns and the surrounding county for family work, and only its two towns for conveyancing. It might set a monthly cap that reflects how many enquiries its two teams can call back quickly between them. Each area would have its own price. This is only an illustration, but it shows how much of the arrangement is in your hands.

Before your first lead, you sign a partner agreement. It sets out how the arrangement works in writing, so that your firm and Leaflegal both know where they stand.

What it covers

  • Your price per lead for each practice area
  • How and when you are billed
  • Your monthly volume and how to change it or pause
  • How leads are delivered
  • What counts as an invalid lead, the review period for raising one and how it is dealt with

Why it matters

Lead buying goes wrong when expectations are unclear. A firm thinks it will receive one thing and gets another, or a disagreement about a lead turns into a dispute about money. Writing everything down at the start avoids that. Your team knows what to expect and what to report, and so do we.

Taking a look before you sign

Take the time you need to read it. Many firms ask their compliance officer or practice manager to look at it too, especially the sections about referral disclosure and data. If something is unclear, ask us and we will explain.

Regulatory points

Your agreement sits alongside your own regulatory duties. You remain responsible for being authorised for the work you take on, for telling clients about the referral arrangement as the SRA Code of Conduct requires, for conflict checks and for handling client data under UK data protection law.

When things change

Your agreement records the arrangement as it stands when you sign. Criteria, caps and delivery details often change afterwards as your firm learns what works. Day to day changes such as a new matter type, a different cap or a new delivery address are made by telling our team. Bigger changes, such as a new price for a new practice area, are confirmed with you in writing so the record stays accurate.

Once your agreement is in place, matching enquiries are sent to you as soon as they have been checked. There is no batching and no waiting for a daily file.

Each lead goes to your firm alone. It is never sent to another firm at the same time and never resold later, so the client is expecting one call, from you.

How they arrive

Leads can be sent by email to one or more addresses, to a shared intake inbox, or into your CRM or case management system using a lead email address or a webhook. Each lead is formatted to be easy to read on a phone.

What each lead includes

The client's name, verified UK mobile number and email address. The practice area and matter type. A description in their own words. How urgent it is and any deadline or hearing date. Their location. Confirmation they want paid help. The best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the consent wording they agreed to.

What to do next

Call as soon as you can. Open with the matter and any deadline. Explain that they made an enquiry through Leaflegal and asked to speak to a solicitor. Be clear about your fees and agree a next step. If they do not answer, leave a voicemail and send an email and a text, then try again.

If something is wrong

If a lead has wrong contact details or falls outside your criteria, tell us within the review period in your agreement. If you cannot act because of a conflict, let us know too.

Your first week

The first few leads are a good chance to test your intake routine. Check that leads are landing where you expect, that notifications are reaching the right people and that whoever calls them knows how to explain the introduction. If anything does not work as planned, such as leads going to the wrong inbox, tell us and we will change the delivery. Small fixes in the first week save a lot of missed calls later.

Getting started is only the beginning. The firms that do best keep talking to us as they learn what works.

Feedback

Tell us which leads turned into instructions and which did not, and why if you know. We use that to adjust your criteria and to improve the campaigns that produce enquiries for firms like yours.

Adjusting your criteria

Most firms change something in the first few months. They add a matter type that is working well, drop one that is not, widen their locations or raise their cap. Tell our team what you want to change and we will update your matching.

Pausing

If your team is at capacity, a fee earner is away or you are moving offices, ask us to pause. You receive no leads while paused. When you are ready, we start matching to you again on the same terms.

Reviewing results

Training new staff

When someone new joins your intake team, show them a recent lead and walk them through the first call. Make sure they know to mention the Leaflegal enquiry, to explain the referral arrangement in the way your firm does and to record the outcome. A short, shared note on how your firm handles leads keeps the approach consistent as your team changes.

Keep a simple record of each lead and its outcome. Looking at it by practice area once a month shows you where your leads work best and helps you decide where to grow.

A clear idea of the practice areas and matter types you want, the locations you cover, how you are regulated and roughly how many new enquiries your team could call back each month. It also helps to know how you would like leads delivered, such as to a shared inbox or into your CRM.

It depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose and the volume you want. As soon as each enquiry has been checked. There is no batching or delay. That means you can call while the client is still focused on their matter and expecting to hear from a solicitor. More questions people ask

What does converting a legal enquiry mean?
It means turning someone who has asked about their legal problem into a client who has accepted your terms and instructed your firm. It usually involves several steps, such as the first call, a consultation or quote, a written proposal and onboarding. Clients can drop out at any of them, so conversion is about making each step clear, quick and easy.
What is a good conversion rate for legal enquiries?
There is no single figure that applies to every firm. It depends heavily on the practice area, your fees, your location and how enquiries reach you. A conveyancing quote and a contested family matter convert very differently. The most useful benchmark is your own rate, tracked consistently over time and broken down by area, so you can see whether changes you make are working.
Why do enquiries not turn into instructions?
Common reasons include slow first contact, unclear fees, no specific next step, delays in sending terms, difficult onboarding and no follow-up when the client goes quiet. Some reasons are outside your control, such as a client changing their mind or deciding they cannot afford it. Recording the reasons you hear helps you see which ones you can do something about.
Which matters convert most quickly?
Matters with a fixed date tend to move fastest, such as motoring offences with a court date, licence reviews, immigration deadlines and conveyancing with an agreed sale. Clients facing those dates need to act. Matters without a deadline, such as prenuptial agreements or some lease extensions, can take longer because the client has time to think and compare.
Is conversion mainly about sales skills?
Not really. Most improvements come from simple, consistent habits rather than persuasion. Calling quickly, explaining fees clearly, booking a next step, sending terms promptly and following up politely make a bigger difference than any sales technique. Clients choosing a solicitor are looking for competence, clarity and trust, and those come across through how your firm behaves.
Will buying more leads fix low instructions?
Not on its own. If your firm is losing clients at a particular stage, more enquiries will usually mean more people lost at the same stage. It is worth finding and fixing your biggest leak first. Once your process converts well, additional enquiries become more valuable, and you can scale up with more confidence.
