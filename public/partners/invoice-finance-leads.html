What Loanleaf offers invoice finance brokers

Invoice finance is a cash flow product. A business has sold to customers on credit terms, the money is tied up in unpaid invoices, and the owner wants to release it sooner. The people who need it tend to say so when they ask for finance, which makes the right enquiry source valuable for anyone who sells factoring or discounting.

Loanleaf is an introducer. Business owners find us through search, our own content and paid online advertising, including Meta, then complete an enquiry form on our website. We do not buy in data, scrape lists or pass on cold contacts. Every lead is a person who asked for finance, in their own words, on the day they submit.

This page explains how to pick invoice finance leads out of the Loanleaf feed, how pricing and auto-buy work, and what you will still need to establish on the call. It is deliberately open about the limits, so you can judge whether our leads fit the way you work.

Where the leads come from and who qualifies

Every enquiry is filtered on the form before it is submitted. Businesses that do not meet the basics are turned away at that point, so they never reach the platform and you never pay for them.

  • UK limited companies and LLPs only. Sole traders, ordinary partnerships and businesses not yet trading are turned away.
  • At least 6 months trading and annual revenue of £50,000 or more.
  • A loan amount chosen from £5,000 to £2m+.
  • A valid UK mobile number and an email domain that can receive email, both checked on the form.
  • No paying twice: a repeat enquiry within 7 days goes back to the broker who bought it at no extra cost, and after that you never receive the same lead again.

Before submitting, the business owner ticks one box confirming they agree to our terms and privacy policy, understand that Loanleaf is an introducer, not a lender or broker, and understand their details will be shared with a broker we match them with, who may contact them by phone, email or text about business finance. The exact wording and the time they agreed are recorded and sent with the lead.

What to look for in factoring and invoice discounting leads

Loanleaf does not run a separate invoice finance form. Instead, each lead carries enough detail for you to pick out enquiries that look like factoring leads or invoice discounting leads. Brokers who place these facilities tend to read four things together.

  • Purpose: Cash flow. This is the closest match to why most businesses use invoice finance. Growth and expansion can also fit where a business is growing faster than its customers pay.
  • Revenue band. Facilities are usually sized against a sales ledger, so annual revenue matters. The bands run from £50k to £100k up to £5m+, and you see the band before you buy.
  • Time trading. The bands are 6 to 12 months, 1 to 2 years, 2 to 5 years and 5+ years. Many funders want a track record, so this helps you sort quickly.
  • Existing finance. Each lead shows whether the business already borrows and, if so, bands for the amount left to repay and the monthly repayments. That tells you whether there may be an existing facility to replace or work around.

Factoring, where a funder takes on some of the credit control, and invoice discounting, where the business keeps control of collections, suit different owners. The lead cannot tell you which. That is a conversation, and a good reason to ask early how the business prefers to deal with its customers.

The honest limits, as the form does not ask about invoices

The Loanleaf form does not ask about the sales ledger, who the customers are, payment terms, how many invoices are outstanding or whether any single customer makes up most of the revenue. It also does not ask about sector, director names or bank statements.

That is deliberate. The form is built to qualify a business for commercial lending in general, and to keep it short enough that owners finish it. The cost of that approach is that invoice finance suitability is something you confirm on the first call.

Some cash flow enquiries will turn out to sell to consumers or take payment at the point of sale, which rarely suits invoice finance. If you or your panel can place those as a working capital loan or another product, the lead still has value. If you only write invoice finance, price a share of non-fits into how you buy and use your criteria to narrow the feed.

Setting up auto-buy for invoice finance leads

Loanleaf Platform lets you buy leads one at a time or switch on auto-buy, which purchases matching leads the moment they arrive. If several brokers match the same lead, a fair rotation decides who gets it. Each lead is sold to one broker only.

A starting point for invoice finance might look like this, adjusted to what your funders accept:

  • Purposes: Cash flow, plus Growth and expansion if you work with growing businesses.
  • Revenue bands: the minimum your funders will look at, so you skip businesses too small for a facility.
  • Time trading: the shortest track record your panel accepts.
  • Loan size range: the facility sizes you actually place, rather than the full £5,000 to £2m+.
  • Clean credit only: switch it on if your funders do not accept missed payments, defaults or CCJs, and leave it off if they do.
  • Limits: leads per day, credits per day, credits per month and a maximum credits per lead, so spend stays predictable.

If a lead matches your criteria but costs more than your per-lead maximum, it is not bought automatically. It stays available for manual buying and you get an email alert. Brokers who prefer manual mode can also get an email when a matching lead arrives.

What it costs

One credit is worth £1. Each lead costs between £20 and £300 in credits, and you see the price before you buy. The price comes from a scoring model that looks at loan size, revenue, time trading, credit history and affordability, which means whether the amount asked for looks realistic for the size of the business.

A sensible ask from an established business costs more. A long shot costs the minimum. For example, a £25k request from a business with £100k to £250k revenue and 1 to 2 years trading is 40 credits, while £250k from a business with £1m to £5m revenue and 5+ years trading is 165 credits.

You pay through a monthly plan, with prices excluding VAT. Starter is £2,500 a month for 2,500 credits, Growth is £5,000 for 5,000 credits, and Custom starts from £6,000 in £1,000 steps. One credit is £1 on every plan. Unused credits roll over while you stay subscribed, and you can cancel at any time. See pricing for the full detail.

Loanleaf is a marketplace, so lead prices can change without notice, including for auto-buy. Setting a maximum price per lead means auto-buy will never pay more than you choose.

What you see before you buy and what you get after

Before buying, you see a summary of each live lead: amount, purpose, timeline, revenue, time trading, credit history, existing finance and a quality score from 0 to 100. Contact details are masked, for example a***@b****.co.uk and +447*******90, with checks showing a valid UK mobile and a verified email domain.

When you buy, the business name and full contact details unlock. The lead arrives instantly on the platform, by email and in your CRM by webhook (JSON), which works with Zapier, Make, HubSpot, Pipedrive and any CRM that accepts webhooks. The platform also includes a simple pipeline with New, Contacted, Application, Funded and Lost stages, notes and CSV export.

If a phone number is not in service or belongs to someone else, or an email address does not exist, you can ask for a refund within 72 hours of purchase. Refunds are not given for any other reason.

Qualifying an invoice finance lead on the first call

The lead already tells you the amount, purpose, timeline, revenue band, time trading and credit history, so your first call can start with the business rather than with who you are. Many brokers then cover the invoice finance questions the form leaves out.

  • Who the business sells to, whether those customers are other businesses, and on what payment terms.
  • Roughly how many invoices are outstanding at any time, and whether sales are steady or seasonal.
  • Whether one customer accounts for a large share of the ledger.
  • Whether the owner wants a funder to chase payments or would rather keep collections in-house.
  • Any existing facility, charge or lender, building on the existing finance bands you already have.
  • Sector and trading address, which the form does not collect.

If invoice finance is not the right answer, the conversation can move on rather than stop. You may still be able to help with a working capital product, and you can look at our business loan leads and commercial finance leads pages to see how the wider feed works.

Questions

Are these leads pre-screened for invoice finance?
They are pre-screened for commercial lending, not specifically for invoice finance. Every lead is a UK limited company or LLP with at least 6 months trading and £50,000 or more in annual revenue. The form does not ask about invoices or customers, so you confirm suitability on the first call.
Which leads should I look at for factoring?
Most brokers start with the Cash flow purpose and then read revenue band, time trading and existing finance. Auto-buy lets you set purposes, revenue bands and time trading so matching leads are bought for you. Whether a business wants factoring or invoice discounting is something you discuss on the call.
How much do invoice finance leads cost?
Each lead costs between £20 and £300 in credits, where one credit is £1. The price depends on loan size, revenue, time trading, credit history and affordability. You always see the price before you buy.
Is each lead sold to more than one broker?
No. Each lead is sold to one broker only. Once you buy it, it disappears for everyone else and is never resold.
Does the form collect customer or debtor details?
No. The form does not ask about customers, the sales ledger or payment terms. It also does not ask about sector, director names or bank statements, so those are first call questions.
What if the contact details are wrong?
You can ask for a refund within 72 hours of purchase if the phone number is not in service or belongs to someone else, or if the email address does not exist. Refunds are not available for any other reason.
Can leads go straight into my CRM?
Yes. Bought leads are delivered instantly by webhook in JSON, which works with Zapier, Make, HubSpot, Pipedrive and any CRM that accepts webhooks. They also arrive on the platform and by email.
Do I have to take a minimum number of leads?
There is no minimum number of leads. You subscribe to a monthly plan, from £2,500 a month excluding VAT, and spend the credits on the leads you want. You can cancel at any time.
