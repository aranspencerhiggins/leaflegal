What Loanleaf offers working capital brokers

Working capital finance covers the everyday gap between money going out and money coming in: paying suppliers, covering wages, buying stock or smoothing a slow month. It is a broad need, which is why it shows up across a wide range of products, from short-term loans to revolving facilities.

Loanleaf is an introducer. Business owners find us through search, our own content and paid online advertising, including Meta, and complete an enquiry form on our website. We do not buy in data, scrape lists or pass on cold contacts. Every lead is someone who asked for finance, in their own words, on the day they enquire.

This page explains how to find working capital leads within the Loanleaf feed, how pricing and auto-buy work, and what to confirm on the first call. We say clearly what the form does not ask, so you can judge whether it fits the way you work.

Where the leads come from and who qualifies

Every enquiry is filtered on the form before it is submitted. Businesses that do not meet the basics are turned away at that point, so they never reach the platform and you never pay for them.

  • UK limited companies and LLPs only. Sole traders, ordinary partnerships and businesses not yet trading are turned away.
  • At least 6 months trading and annual revenue of £50,000 or more.
  • A loan amount chosen from £5,000 to £2m+.
  • A valid UK mobile number and an email domain that can receive email, both checked on the form.
  • No paying twice: a repeat enquiry within 7 days goes back to the broker who bought it at no extra cost, and after that you never receive the same lead again.

Before submitting, the business owner ticks one box confirming they agree to our terms and privacy policy, understand that Loanleaf is an introducer, not a lender or broker, and understand their details will be shared with a broker we match them with, who may contact them by phone, email or text about business finance. The exact wording and the time they agreed are recorded and sent with the lead.

How working capital shows up in the Loanleaf feed

The form does not have a purpose called working capital. Owners choose from Growth and expansion, Cash flow, Equipment or vehicles, Stock and inventory, Commercial property (buy, build or refurbish), Refinance existing debt, Tax or VAT bill and Something else. Working capital enquiries therefore appear under a handful of them.

  • Cash flow. The closest match, and the first purpose most brokers look at.
  • Stock and inventory. Businesses buying stock ahead of sales often need working capital rather than equipment finance.
  • Tax or VAT bill. A large bill can create a short-term gap that working capital lending can bridge.
  • Growth and expansion. Some growth plans need money for wages, marketing or materials before revenue arrives.
  • Something else. Worth a look if you work flexibly, though you will learn more on the call.

Equipment or vehicles, Commercial property and Refinance existing debt usually point to other products, though a conversation can change that. You choose which purposes to include.

Reading a working capital lead before you buy

Each lead carries a set of answers that help you judge fit without making a call. Working capital lenders often look at these in particular.

  • Timeline. As soon as possible, Within 2 weeks, Within a month or Just exploring options. Working capital is often needed quickly, so urgency is a useful signal.
  • Revenue band and time trading. Revenue runs from £50k to £100k up to £5m+, and trading from 6 to 12 months to 5+ years.
  • Loan amount. Smaller asks tend to cost fewer credits under our pricing model, and sit closer to typical short-term requests.
  • Credit history. Whether there have been missed payments, defaults or CCJs in the last 6 years: No, Yes or Not sure.
  • Existing finance. Whether the business already borrows, with bands for the amount left to repay and the monthly repayments. That helps you see whether it is stretched or looking to replace something.

The quality score, from 0 to 100, combines credit history, time trading, revenue, urgency and existing debt. Use it to sort, then read the detail.

The honest limits of a working capital lead

The form does not ask about card takings or card machine turnover, so it cannot tell you how the business is paid. It does not ask about sector, property, director names or bank statements either. Whether a cash flow enquiry suits a short-term loan, an overdraft style facility, invoice finance or something else is for you to establish on the call.

Not every enquiry will convert. Some owners are comparing options, some will not meet a lender's criteria and some will decide not to borrow. Loanleaf provides qualified enquiries, not guaranteed deals, and a lead stays valid even if the business later chooses another provider or is declined.

If you place a range of products, that breadth is helpful. A working capital lead that is not right for one product may be right for another, such as those covered on our merchant cash advance leads page.

Setting up auto-buy for working capital leads

Loanleaf Platform lets you buy one lead at a time or switch on auto-buy, which purchases matching leads the moment they arrive. If several brokers match the same lead, a fair rotation decides who gets it. Each lead is sold to one broker only.

A starting setup for working capital might be:

  • Purposes: Cash flow, plus Stock and inventory, Tax or VAT bill or Growth and expansion if they suit your lenders.
  • Loan size range: the amounts you actually place, rather than the whole £5,000 to £2m+ range.
  • Revenue bands and time trading: the minimums your panel works to.
  • Clean credit only: on if your lenders decline adverse history, off if they do not.
  • Limits: leads per day, credits per day, credits per month and a maximum credits per lead.

Working capital is often time sensitive, so many brokers prefer auto-buy for the fastest route to a call. Leads that match but cost more than your per-lead maximum are left for manual buying and you get an email alert. Manual-mode brokers can also receive an email when a matching lead arrives. See the auto-buy page for each setting.

What working capital leads cost

One credit is worth £1. Each lead costs between £20 and £300, set by a scoring model based on loan size, revenue, time trading, credit history and affordability. Affordability means whether the amount asked for is realistic for the business's revenue. You see the price before you buy.

A sensible ask from an established business costs more, and a long shot costs the minimum. A £25k request from a business with £100k to £250k revenue and 1 to 2 years trading is 40 credits. A £100k request from a business with £250k to £500k revenue and 2 to 5 years trading is 95.

Monthly plans exclude VAT: Starter is £2,500 for 2,500 credits, Growth is £5,000 for 5,000 credits, and Custom starts from £6,000 in £1,000 steps. Unused credits roll over while you are subscribed, and you can cancel at any time. Prices can change without notice, so set a maximum price per lead. The pricing page has the full detail.

What you get after you buy, and working the lead

On purchase, the business name and full contact details unlock. The lead is delivered instantly on the platform, by email and to your CRM by webhook (JSON), which works with Zapier, Make, HubSpot, Pipedrive and any CRM that accepts webhooks. The platform also has a simple pipeline with New, Contacted, Application, Funded and Lost stages, notes and CSV export.

On the first call, many brokers confirm the amount and the reason for it, then ask what the business needs to fund and how soon. They also ask about how it is paid, current repayments and whether recent statements are available, along with sector and trading address, since the form does not collect them.

If the phone number is not in service or belongs to someone else, or the email address does not exist, you can ask for a refund within 72 hours of purchase. Refunds are not given for any other reason.

Questions

Is there a working capital option on the form?
No. Owners choose from purposes such as Cash flow, Stock and inventory and Tax or VAT bill. Most brokers who want working capital leads start with Cash flow and then look at the others that suit their lenders.
Are these leads pre-screened?
They are pre-screened for commercial lending. Every lead is a UK limited company or LLP with at least 6 months trading and £50,000 or more in annual revenue, with a valid UK mobile and a working email domain. The form does not ask about card takings or bank statements.
How much do working capital leads cost?
Each lead costs between £20 and £300 in credits, where one credit is £1. The price depends on loan size, revenue, time trading, credit history and affordability. You see the price before buying.
Can I get leads as soon as they arrive?
Yes. With auto-buy, matching leads are bought the moment they arrive and delivered instantly to the platform, by email and to your CRM by webhook. If several brokers match, a fair rotation decides.
Is each lead exclusive?
Yes. Each lead is sold to one broker only. Once bought, it disappears for everyone else and is never resold.
Can I cap what I spend?
Yes. Auto-buy lets you set leads per day, credits per day, credits per month and a maximum credits per lead. Leads above your per-lead maximum are left for manual buying and you get an email alert.
What if the contact details are wrong?
You can ask for a refund within 72 hours of purchase if the phone number is not in service or belongs to someone else, or the email address does not exist. Refunds are not given for any other reason.
Do I have to commit to a minimum number of leads?
No. You subscribe to a monthly plan from £2,500 a month excluding VAT and spend your credits on the leads you want. You can cancel at any time.
