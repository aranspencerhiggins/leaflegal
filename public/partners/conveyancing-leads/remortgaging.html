What the client is looking for

Legal work for switching your mortgage to a new lender.

When you remortgage to a new lender, a conveyancer acts for the lender to make sure the new mortgage is properly secured on your property. They check the title, pay off your existing mortgage and register the new charge at HM Land Registry.

What the client expects from you

  • Checking the title for the new lender
  • Reporting on the mortgage offer conditions
  • Redeeming your existing mortgage
  • Registering the new mortgage
  • Dealing with a transfer of equity at the same time, if needed

  • Rough property value

Leaflegal sends conveyancing enquiries from people who are buying, selling or remortgaging a home in England and Wales and want a firm to handle the legal work. Each enquiry goes to one firm only. It is never shared with a panel of other firms and it is never resold once it has been delivered to you.

Conveyancing clients usually contact a firm at a clear point of decision. An offer has been accepted, a buyer has been found, a fixed rate is coming to an end or a lender has asked for a solicitor to be named. At that moment they want a quote, a sense of how long things will take and someone who will pick up the phone. Our enquiries are built around that moment.

We run campaigns aimed at people at these points in a move, ask them a short set of questions about the transaction and confirm that they want to instruct a firm for paid conveyancing. When an enquiry matches the matter types and locations you have chosen, we check it and send it to you straight away, by email or into your CRM.

Why firms add bought leads to their pipeline

Most conveyancing firms rely on estate agent referrals, repeat clients and their own website. Those channels matter, but agent referrals can come with conditions, and paid search for conveyancing terms is crowded and costly to manage. Exclusive leads give you a source of direct enquiries where you decide the volume and the type of work.

Direct clients also tend to have chosen you rather than been handed to you. That can make the relationship simpler, because the client is not looking back to an agent for updates and you can set expectations from the first call.

What we do not do

We are not a comparison site and we do not run a quote engine that sends a buyer five competing prices. We do not tell a client which firm is cheapest or best. We introduce them to one firm that has chosen to receive their kind of matter, and the rest of the relationship is between you and the client.

Leaflegal is not a law firm, is not regulated by the SRA or the CLC and does not give legal advice. You are responsible for being authorised to carry out reserved conveyancing work and for meeting the requirements of any lender you act for.

Conveyancing enquiries come from a wide mix of people, which is why we let you choose the matter types you want. A firm that only handles freehold purchases can receive just those, while a firm with a strong leasehold team can ask for more of that work.

Typical matter types

  • Purchases, including first time buyers
  • Sales of freehold and leasehold homes
  • A sale and purchase together, often in a chain
  • Remortgages, with or without a change of lender
  • Transfers of equity, for example when a partner is added or removed
  • Leasehold flat purchases and sales
  • New build purchases with a developer deadline
  • Buy to let purchases by individual landlords

Where they are in the process

Some people enquire the day an offer is accepted. Others are still viewing and want to line up a firm early so they can move quickly when they find the right property. Sellers often enquire when they put the house on the market, because agents increasingly ask them to instruct a solicitor before a buyer is found. Remortgage clients usually enquire a few months before their current deal ends.

Each lead tells you the stage the client has reached and their rough timescale, so you can decide who to call first and how to pitch your service.

What clients care about

In our experience of the questions people ask, conveyancing clients care about three things above all. They want a clear quote with no surprises later, they want regular updates without having to chase, and they want to know the firm will not hold up their chain. A first call that speaks to those three points usually lands well.

Many clients are anxious about the process, especially first time buyers, so a calm explanation of the main stages helps. They are rarely choosing on price alone, but they do compare quotes, so speed and clarity matter.

Every lead is the full record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and nothing is guessed. The aim is to give your quoting team enough to give a figure on the first call.

Delivery

Leads are sent as soon as they have been checked. You can receive them by email, to a shared intake inbox, or straight into your CRM or case management system using a lead email address or a webhook. There is no platform to log into.

How you handle the first contact makes a large difference to how many enquiries become instructions. The firms that do best treat each lead as a live quote request rather than a name to add to a list.

Call quickly

A conveyancing client who has just had an offer accepted often contacts more than one firm or is being pushed towards the agent's panel firm. Calling while they are still thinking about their move gives you the best chance of being the firm they choose. Set up alerts so the right person sees a lead the moment it arrives.

Quote on the first call if you can

The lead tells you the transaction type, the rough value, the tenure and whether there is a mortgage. That is usually enough for a provisional figure. Follow up the call with a written, itemised quote that sets out your fee, the likely disbursements and anything that could add cost, such as a leasehold supplement or a management pack.

Set expectations

Explain the main stages in plain terms and how you will keep them updated. Many clients have heard stories about slow conveyancing, so telling them how often they will hear from you, and how to reach their case handler, builds trust before they instruct.

Mention the introduction

The client agreed to be contacted by a firm after making an enquiry through Leaflegal. Say so when you introduce yourself. Your client care letter should then cover any referral arrangement you are required to disclose.

Follow up

Not every client instructs on the first call. Some are waiting for a mortgage offer or for the agent to confirm a sale. A short email the same day, a second call a few days later and a final check in after a week often turn a not yet into an instruction. Keep it helpful rather than pushy.

Tell us what works

Let us know which leads instruct and which do not, and why. If remortgages rarely convert for you but leasehold sales do, we can change your matching so you receive more of the work you want.

Firms that review their results every month or so, and share what they see with us, usually find it easier to shape the work they receive over time.

Conveyancing is a reserved legal activity, and buying leads does not change your professional duties. Leaflegal is set up to support those duties rather than work around them.

What clients ask

What is the difference between a remortgage and a product transfer?
A product transfer is when you take a new deal with your existing lender. The mortgage stays the same legal charge, so usually no conveyancer is involved. A remortgage is when you move to a new lender, or sometimes borrow more in a way that needs a new deed. That requires legal work to pay off the old loan and register the new one.
Who does the conveyancer act for in a remortgage?
The conveyancer acts for the new lender, making sure its mortgage is properly secured, and usually also for you. If the lender's own panel firm is doing free legal work, it may act mainly for the lender. If you choose your own firm, it acts for you and the lender, and must be on the lender's panel.
Do I need searches for a remortgage?
Not always. Many lenders accept a remortgage search insurance policy instead of full searches, which is quicker and cheaper. Some lenders, or some properties, still require searches. Your conveyancer will follow the lender's instructions and tell you if searches are needed.
Can I remortgage to release money from my home?
Yes, many people borrow more when they remortgage, sometimes called equity release in the general sense, though that term also describes specialist lifetime mortgages for older homeowners. Your lender will decide how much you can borrow. Your conveyancer handles the legal side and pays you the extra money on completion.
Is a lifetime mortgage the same as a remortgage?
No. A lifetime mortgage is a specialist form of equity release usually for older homeowners, with its own rules and protections. Lenders normally require you to have independent legal advice from a solicitor before it completes. It is quite different from a standard remortgage and needs specialist advice.
Can I remortgage a property I own outright?
Yes. If you own your home without a mortgage and want to borrow against it, the process is similar but there is no existing lender to pay off. Your conveyancer checks the title, completes the new mortgage and registers it, then sends you the money.
Will I get the extra money on the day of completion?
If you are borrowing more than you owe, your conveyancer receives the full advance from the new lender, pays off the old mortgage and any debts the lender requires to be cleared, deducts agreed fees and sends you the rest. This usually happens on completion day or the next working day, to an account you have confirmed by phone.
How long does a remortgage take?
A straightforward remortgage often takes a few weeks from the mortgage offer to completion. It can take longer if there are title problems, consents to obtain, a leasehold notice process or an equity loan involved. Starting well before your current deal ends gives room for any delays.
When should I start looking for a new deal?
Many lenders let you secure a new rate several months before your current deal ends. Speaking to a broker or lender early gives time to compare options and complete the legal work. Your conveyancer can usually agree a completion date that matches the end of your current deal.
What happens if my remortgage is not ready in time?
If your current deal ends before the remortgage completes, your current lender will usually move you onto its standard variable rate until the new mortgage is in place. That can mean higher payments for a short period. You will not normally lose your home or be in breach, but it can cost more.
Can I choose the date my remortgage completes?
To some extent. Your conveyancer and the lender agree a completion date once the legal work is done and the funds are ready. Many people aim to complete the day after their current deal ends to avoid early repayment charges. Your conveyancer can help line this up.
What slows a remortgage down?
Common causes include delays in the lender's own checks, a low valuation, missing information about your current mortgage, consent needed from a landlord, housing provider or equity loan administrator, and problems on the title. Returning paperwork quickly and telling your conveyancer about anything unusual early helps.
Can I remortgage soon after buying?
Yes, although some lenders have rules about lending on a property that was bought very recently, and an early repayment charge on your existing mortgage may make it expensive. If you want to remortgage soon after buying, perhaps to fund works, speak to a broker about the lender's criteria and the costs before you apply.
Do I have to stop paying my old mortgage while I remortgage?
No. Keep paying your existing mortgage as normal until completion. The redemption figure will take account of payments made, and any overpayment is usually refunded by the old lender afterwards. Stopping payments early can lead to arrears, fees and a mark on your credit record, which could affect the new lending.
What is a redemption statement?
It is a document from your current lender showing exactly how much you need to pay to clear the mortgage on a given date. It includes the outstanding balance, interest to that date and any charges. Your conveyancer uses it to make sure the old lender is paid off in full on completion.
What if there is an old charge on my title?
Sometimes a mortgage or loan that was paid off years ago is still shown on the register because it was never formally removed. Your conveyancer will ask the old lender for proof that it was repaid and apply to remove it. This is usually straightforward but can take a little time.
Why do occupiers have to sign a form?
If an adult who is not an owner lives in the property, such as a partner, parent or grown up child, the lender wants to make sure that person cannot claim rights that rank ahead of the mortgage. The occupier is usually asked to sign a consent form, sometimes after taking independent advice.
What if my name on the register is different?
If you have changed your name since you bought, your conveyancer may ask for evidence, such as a marriage certificate or deed poll, and update the register at the same time as registering the new mortgage. It is a routine step but needs the correct documents.
Do I need to tell my landlord about a remortgage?
If your home is leasehold, the lease often requires you to give notice of any new mortgage to the landlord or managing agent, usually with a fee. Your conveyancer will check the lease and send the notice after completion. Some leases also require the landlord's consent before the new mortgage.
What if my lease is short?
Lenders have rules on how many years must remain on a lease, and a short lease can lead to a lower valuation or a refusal to lend. If your lease is getting short, the lender may want it extended first. Your conveyancer can explain the options, and a lease extension is a separate legal process.
What happens if the lender finds a problem with my title?
Your conveyancer will explain the issue and what the lender needs to resolve it. Common fixes include providing evidence, removing an old entry, obtaining a consent or arranging an insurance policy. If the problem cannot be fixed in time, the lender may delay completion or, rarely, withdraw its offer.
Can I remove my ex-partner from the mortgage when I remortgage?
Yes, this is common after a separation. It involves a transfer of equity, where your ex-partner transfers their share in the property to you, as well as the new mortgage in your sole name. The new lender must be satisfied you can afford the loan alone. Your ex-partner usually needs separate advice.
Can I add my partner when I remortgage?
Yes. Adding someone to the ownership as part of a remortgage is done through a transfer of equity. The new lender will assess both of you and the new mortgage will be in joint names. Your conveyancer will explain the ways of owning jointly and any tax that might apply.
Can I remortgage a buy to let property?
Yes, but buy to let remortgages use different lenders and products. The lender may want details of the tenancy and the rent. If the property is owned through a limited company, the legal work is different and the lender usually wants personal guarantees from directors.
Can I remortgage with an equity loan?
Usually, but you need consent from the equity loan administrator, and the new lender must accept the equity loan's position on the title. The consent process can take weeks, so start it early. Your conveyancer will explain the forms and any fees involved.
Can I remortgage a property held in a trust or a company?
It is possible, but the legal work is more involved. The conveyancer must check that the trustees or the company have the power to borrow, that the right people sign, and that any restrictions on the title are met. Not every lender lends in these situations, so a broker can help find one that does.
