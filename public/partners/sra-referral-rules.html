Disclosure

The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement with an introducer. Our consent wording tells every client that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send you that wording with each lead.

Independence

The arrangement must not affect your advice or your duty to act in the client’s best interests. We never tell a client which firm to choose beyond the introduction, and we never give legal advice.

Prohibited referral fees

Paying for personal injury and clinical negligence referrals is prohibited under section 56 of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012. We don’t generate those enquiries. We also don’t handle financial claims, which are regulated claims management.

Immigration

Leaflegal is not regulated to give immigration advice and doesn’t. We only introduce immigration enquiries to regulated firms or to advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority.

Solicitors in England and Wales can pay introducers for client introductions in most areas of work, provided they follow the rules that apply to referral arrangements. Those rules come mainly from the SRA Standards and Regulations, including the SRA Principles and the Code of Conduct, and from the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012, often called LASPO, which bans referral fees in certain kinds of work.

This page explains, in general terms, how those rules usually apply to a firm that pays an introducer such as Leaflegal, and how our service is set up with them in mind. It is general information, not regulatory advice. Every firm's position is different, the rules and the SRA's guidance can change, and your compliance officer is the right person to take a view on any arrangement your firm enters into.

Leaflegal's position

Leaflegal is an introduction service run by Vertical Brands Ltd. It is not a law firm, is not regulated by the SRA and does not give legal advice. It runs campaigns, asks people a short set of questions about their matter and, with their consent, introduces each one to a single partner firm. Firms pay Leaflegal for each introduction at a price agreed in advance.

The main themes

  • Clients must be told about the financial arrangement between the firm and the introducer
  • The arrangement must not compromise the firm's independence or its duty to act in the client's best interests
  • Referral fees are banned in personal injury and clinical negligence work
  • There are specific restrictions involving clients in criminal proceedings and clients with public funding
  • Clients introduced to a firm should not have been obtained in a way that would breach the SRA's rules if the introducer were regulated
  • The firm remains responsible for its own conduct, including conflicts, client care and data protection

Why it matters

Referral arrangements are an area the SRA has looked at closely over the years. A firm that gets them wrong can face regulatory consequences, and clients who feel misled about how they were introduced can lose trust in the firm. Getting them right is not difficult, but it does need attention.

Transparency is at the heart of the rules on introducers. In general terms, the SRA Code of Conduct requires firms to make sure that clients are told about any financial or other interest that an introducer has in referring them, and about any fee sharing arrangement that is relevant to their matter. Fee sharing arrangements are required to be in writing.

How Leaflegal helps

Before anyone can submit an enquiry, they tick a box confirming they understand Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm. Our consent wording explains that Leaflegal is paid by the firms it introduces people to. We record the exact wording and the time the client agreed to it, and we send that record with every lead.

What firms usually do

Most firms mention the introduction on the first call, for example by saying the client made an enquiry through Leaflegal and asked to speak to a solicitor. Many then confirm the referral arrangement in writing, usually in the client care letter, so there is a clear record. The wording you use, and how much detail you give, is a matter for your firm and your compliance officer.

Matching what the client was told

Having our consent wording with each lead makes it easier for your own client care information to match what the client has already been told. A client who reads the same message from Leaflegal and from your firm is far less likely to be confused or concerned about how they came to you.

Timing

Firms generally aim to tell clients about a referral arrangement at the outset, before the client is committed. Mentioning it on the first call and confirming it at the point of engagement is a common approach. Your compliance officer can confirm what your firm expects.

Keeping a record

Whatever approach your firm takes, keep a record that the client was told. The consent record that comes with each Leaflegal lead shows what the client was told before they enquired. Your own file note of the first call and your client care letter show what your firm told them. Together they give a clear picture if anyone later asks how the client came to you.

The SRA Principles require solicitors to act with independence and in the best interests of each client. A referral arrangement must not get in the way of either.

What this means in practice

An introducer should not be able to influence the advice a firm gives, the way it runs a matter or the fees it charges. The client should be free to instruct the firm or not, and free to go elsewhere at any time. The firm's professional judgement must remain its own.

How Leaflegal is set up

Leaflegal makes the introduction and then steps back. We do not tell clients which firm to choose beyond the introduction itself. We do not comment on the merits of their matter, the likely outcome or what they should do. We have no say in the advice you give, the work you do or the fees you agree with the client. We do not receive any share of your fees. We are paid for the introduction at a price agreed in advance.

No pressure on outcomes

Your price per lead does not depend on whether the client instructs you or on the fees you earn from the matter. A lead stays valid if the client chooses another firm or decides not to proceed. That separation helps keep the introduction and the legal work apart.

Client choice

Exclusive introductions mean Leaflegal introduces each client to one firm. That does not limit the client's freedom. They can choose not to instruct your firm and can approach any other firm on their own. Firms should respect that choice and never pressure a client to instruct.

Due diligence on introducers

Many firms carry out their own checks on any introducer they work with, looking at how clients are found, what they are told and how their data is handled. We are happy to answer those questions and show you our consent wording before you sign anything.

Some referral fees are banned outright by law, regardless of how well an arrangement is disclosed.

Personal injury and clinical negligence

Section 56 of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 prohibits the payment and receipt of referral fees in relation to claims for damages for personal injury or death, which includes clinical negligence claims. Leaflegal does not generate personal injury or clinical negligence enquiries in any form. People with those matters are told we cannot help before they submit an enquiry.

Claims management

Some kinds of claim, such as financial mis-selling claims, involve regulated claims management activity, which is overseen by the Financial Conduct Authority. Leaflegal does not handle financial claims or housing disrepair claims. Our seven practice areas are conveyancing, family law, immigration, debt recovery for businesses, licensing, lease extensions and trademarks.

Disguised referral fees

The SRA has made clear over the years that it looks at the substance of an arrangement rather than its label. A payment described as a marketing fee or an administration charge can still be treated as a referral fee if that is what it really is. This matters most in areas where referral fees are banned, which is one reason Leaflegal stays out of those areas entirely.

If an enquiry slips through

Our form filters out personal injury and clinical negligence matters, but clients do not always describe their situation accurately. If a lead you receive turns out to involve a personal injury claim, do not treat it as a Leaflegal introduction for that work, and let us know straight away. Your partner agreement sets out how such a lead is handled.

Areas we chose to leave out

Leaflegal's choice of practice areas reflects these rules. We generate enquiries only in areas where paying an introducer is permitted, subject to the other rules on this page, and we have chosen to stay out of claims work altogether. If you would like us to cover an area we do not currently offer, mention it on your call, but we will not add any area where referral fees are banned.

Beyond disclosure and the LASPO ban, the SRA Code contains further restrictions that firms need to consider when using any introducer.

Criminal proceedings

The Code restricts firms from making payments to an introducer in respect of clients who are the subject of criminal proceedings. For that reason Leaflegal does not generate enquiries from people facing criminal proceedings. If a criminal matter comes up during your first call with a client, deal with it under your own arrangements and do not treat it as part of the introduction.

Public funding

The Code also restricts payments to an introducer in respect of clients who have the benefit of public funding. Every Leaflegal client confirms they want paid legal help rather than legal aid or free advice before their enquiry is submitted. If a client you speak to turns out to be publicly funded for the matter, consider the position carefully and let us know.

How clients were obtained

Firms are expected to make sure that clients referred by an introducer were not obtained in a way that would breach the SRA's rules if the introducer were regulated by the SRA. That includes rules against unsolicited approaches to members of the public. Leaflegal does not cold call, buy data or scrape directories. Every client found us through our own campaigns and asked to be introduced.

Publicity

Firms are responsible for making sure publicity about their services is accurate and not misleading. Leaflegal runs its campaigns under its own name and does not advertise partner firms by name. We do not present ourselves as a law firm.

Immigration

Leaflegal is not regulated to give immigration advice and does not do so. Immigration enquiries go only to firms regulated by the SRA or CILEx Regulation, or to advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level for the work.
