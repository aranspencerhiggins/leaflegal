Leads that respect how solicitors work

Solicitors have professional duties that a generic lead seller doesn’t think about, like referral disclosure, conflicts, client care and data protection. Leaflegal is built around them. Every client is told we are an introducer paid by the firms we introduce, gives recorded consent to be contacted, and is told we don’t give legal advice.

  • Practice area, matter type and a description in their own words
  • How urgent it is and any known deadline
  • Their location
  • The consent wording they agreed to, and when

Leads arrive by email or straight into your case management system or CRM, as soon as the enquiry is checked.

Who it suits

High street firms looking for private client work, specialist family, licensing and immigration practices, conveyancing teams and commercial firms recovering business debts. You must be authorised for the work you take on.

Pricing and volume

You pay per lead at a price agreed with you, with a minimum of 10 leads a month. You set a monthly cap and can pause at any time.

What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

Leaflegal sends solicitors private client enquiries from people and businesses who have asked to speak to a solicitor about their own matter. Each enquiry is pre-screened, its contact details are checked and it goes to one firm only. You decide the practice areas, matter types and locations you want, and the number of enquiries your team can handle each month.

Solicitors are not like other businesses that buy leads. You have professional duties around referrals, independence, client care, conflicts and confidentiality. A generic lead seller has no reason to think about any of that. We built the service around those duties, so that an enquiry from Leaflegal fits into the way a regulated practice already works.

Most solicitors who use Leaflegal already have a healthy referral network and a website that brings in some work. What they want is a steady, controllable flow of new private clients on top, without employing a marketing team or spending evenings adjusting search adverts. That is the gap we fill.

The client's side of the introduction

Clients find Leaflegal through our campaigns, complete a short enquiry and agree to be introduced to a firm. They are told that Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm, that we do not give legal advice and that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to. Leaflegal does not charge the client anything. They agree fees directly with you.

Your side of the introduction

You receive the enquiry as soon as it has been checked, by email or into your CRM. It contains everything the client told us, the consent wording they agreed to and the time they agreed to it. From there the relationship is yours. You decide whether to act, how to advise and what to charge.

There is no portal to log into and no software to install. Your contact with us is through our team, who set up your criteria with you and adjust them when your firm's needs change.

Why solicitors look for leads

The reasons vary from firm to firm. A newly qualified solicitor may have capacity before their own network has built up. A firm may have opened a second office and want work in a town where it is not yet known. A partner may want to shift the practice towards contested or higher value matters. Or a team may simply be tired of paying for search adverts that bring in people looking for free help. In each case the aim is the same, which is more of the right enquiries, at a cost the firm can plan around.

An introduction arrangement has to sit comfortably with the SRA Standards and Regulations, or the equivalent rules of your regulator. This is general information about how Leaflegal supports those duties. It is not regulatory advice, and your compliance officer will want to look at the arrangement in the context of your own firm.

Telling the client about the arrangement

The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement you have with an introducer. Our consent wording explains to every client that Leaflegal is paid by the firms it introduces. We send you that wording with each lead. Most firms also confirm the arrangement in writing in their client care letter, so there is a clear record.

Independence and best interests

A referral arrangement must not affect your independence or your duty to act in the client's best interests. Leaflegal does not tell clients which firm to choose beyond making the introduction, does not comment on the merits of their matter and has no say in how you advise them or what you charge.

Prohibited referral fees

Referral fees are banned for personal injury and clinical negligence work under section 56 of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012. We do not generate those enquiries. We also stay out of claims management areas such as financial mis-selling and housing disrepair.

Conflicts and identity

You run your normal conflict and identity checks before taking on any matter. If a conflict means you cannot act, let us know. Your partner agreement sets out how that lead is treated.

Authorisation

You must be authorised for the work you take on, including reserved legal activities such as conveyancing and the conduct of litigation, and immigration advice. We ask how you are regulated when you apply and match enquiries only within the areas you are able to handle.

Extra detail by practice area

Some areas carry extra fields that help you prepare for the first call. A conveyancing enquiry tells you whether it is a purchase, a sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value and whether it is leasehold. A lease extension enquiry tells you roughly how many years are left. A debt recovery enquiry tells you the rough amount owed and whether it is disputed.

What you will not receive

You will not receive enquiries from people who only want legal aid or free advice, personal injury or clinical negligence claims, matters outside the areas and locations you chose, or contacts who have not agreed to be called. Those are filtered out before submission.

Reading a lead in practice

Most solicitors read a lead in under a minute. The matter type and urgency tell you whether it needs a call straight away or can wait for the next free slot. The client's own description tells you how they see the problem, which is often different from how a lawyer would describe it, and gives you a natural way to open the call. The consent record tells you when they asked to be contacted, so you can mention it if they have forgotten.

Leaflegal works best for solicitors who offer privately funded services in one or more of our seven practice areas and who can call a new enquiry back quickly.

It also helps if your firm offers clear pricing for private clients. People who enquire through Leaflegal have confirmed they want paid help, and many of them have never instructed a solicitor before. A fixed fee first meeting, a written quote or an honest estimate of the likely range gives them the confidence to take the next step. Firms that are vague about cost on the first call tend to lose enquiries to firms that are not.

High street practices

Multi-discipline firms often take enquiries in conveyancing, family and licensing, and set separate locations and caps for each team. A steady flow of private client work can help keep fee earners busy through quieter months.

Specialist practices

Specialist family, licensing and immigration firms often cover a wide area or work nationally. They tend to be selective about matter types, taking contested or complex work and leaving out simpler matters.

Conveyancing teams

SRA and CLC regulated conveyancing teams receive purchase, sale and remortgage enquiries from people who want a quote. They win the most when they quote quickly and clearly.

Commercial teams

Commercial litigation teams receive business to business debt recovery enquiries. Licensing teams receive enquiries from pubs, restaurants, late night venues and drivers, often with a hearing or opening date already fixed.

Sole practitioners

Sole practitioners are welcome if they are regulated for the work and can respond promptly. A clear plan for covering calls during court days and holidays makes a big difference.

Who it may not suit

Firms that mainly handle legally aided work, firms that cannot call enquiries back the same day and firms looking for personal injury work will find the service is not a good fit.

The quality of a lead matters, but so does what your firm does with it. Solicitors who get the most from Leaflegal tend to have a simple, reliable intake routine.

Decide who owns new leads

Send leads to a shared intake inbox or straight into your CRM rather than to one fee earner's personal email. Make it clear who picks up new enquiries at each point in the day, including when someone is in court, on leave or in a long meeting.

Call first, then write

Call as soon as you can. If there is no answer, leave a short voicemail and follow with an email and a text that mention the enquiry. Try again later the same day and the next morning.

Use the lead

Open with the matter type and any deadline the client gave. Mention that they enquired through Leaflegal and asked to speak to a solicitor. It reassures them that the call is expected.

Be clear about the next step

Explain what happens next and how your fees work, whether that is a fixed fee first meeting, a written quote or a call back from a partner. Confirm it by email the same day.

Record the outcome

Note whether you spoke to the client and whether they instructed. Sharing that with us helps us refine your criteria over time.

Cover the gaps

Enquiries arrive when people have time to fill in a form, which is often in the evening or at the weekend. Some firms have one person check the intake inbox first thing each morning and call any overnight leads before the working day gets busy. Others use evening cover for urgent areas such as immigration and licence reviews. Whatever you choose, agree it in advance so that leads are not left waiting because everyone assumed someone else would pick them up.

You pay per lead, at a price agreed with your firm before you start. The price depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose and the volume you want. We do not publish a price list, because an enquiry about contested family finances and a simple remortgage enquiry have very different value to a firm.

The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that you set a monthly cap. Once you reach it, we stop matching to you until the next month. You can raise or lower the cap and pause whenever your team is at capacity.

Your partner agreement sets out pricing, billing, volumes, delivery and how invalid leads are handled. You see and agree it before your first lead arrives.

Because the cost of each lead is fixed and agreed, it is easy to compare spend with the instructions and fees each practice area brings in. Many firms review this monthly with their own figures and adjust their cap or matter types as a result. We are happy to talk through those numbers with you and suggest changes to your criteria, but the decision on how much to spend is always yours.

To begin, book a 30-minute call with our team. We will talk through your practice, show you a sample lead and then send you a proposal. You can apply to become a partner firm in a couple of minutes.

Questions and answers

Do you work with sole practitioners?
Yes, if you are regulated for the work and can call enquiries back quickly.
Can I choose which matters I receive?
Yes. Within each area you choose the matter types you want and the ones you don’t.
How quickly do leads arrive?
As soon as the enquiry has been checked, so you can call while it is fresh.
Do you only work with solicitors?
No. We work with SRA regulated solicitors' firms, CILEx regulated practices, CLC regulated conveyancers and, for immigration, advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority. This page is written for solicitors, but the service works the same way for each. Whoever you are regulated by, you must be authorised for the work you take on.
Do you work with sole practitioners?
Yes, if you are regulated for the work and can call enquiries back quickly. Sole practitioners usually set a modest monthly cap and plan ahead for days in court or on leave. You can pause leads when you are away, so you are not paying for enquiries you cannot respond to.
We already get referrals. Why add leads?
Referrals are valuable but hard to increase on demand. Leads give you a flow of new private clients you can turn up or down. Many solicitors use them to build a newer practice area, support a fee earner who has capacity or keep work steady when referrers go quiet. They sit alongside your existing work rather than replacing it.
What if we only do certain types of family work?
Tell us exactly what you want. You might want contested financial remedy and children work but not uncontested divorce, or cohabitation disputes but not prenuptial agreements. We only send enquiries that fit the matter types you have chosen, and you can change your choices whenever you like. Being specific saves your team time.
Can a commercial firm use the service?
Yes. Commercial teams usually take business to business debt recovery enquiries, and some also take licensing work for pubs, restaurants and venues. Every debt recovery enquiry is a business creditor chasing a business debtor. We do not send consumer debts. Each lead tells you the rough amount owed, how old the debt is and whether it is disputed, so you can judge the likely work before you call.
Do you send legal aid enquiries?
No. Every client confirms they are looking for paid legal help before their enquiry is submitted. People who only want legal aid or free advice are told politely that we cannot help and are pointed in the right direction. They never become a lead. Every lead you receive is from someone expecting to pay for legal help.
Can we use leads to build up a new practice area?
Yes, and many firms do. If you have recruited a fee earner in a new area or want to grow a team that your referrers do not feed, leads give you a flow of enquiries you can measure. Start with a cap your team can handle, track what converts and adjust. We can give you an honest view of likely volumes for the area and locations you have in mind.
How do I disclose the referral arrangement?
The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement with an introducer. Our consent wording tells every client that Leaflegal is paid by the firms it introduces, and we send that wording with each lead. Many firms also set the arrangement out in their client care letter. Your compliance officer can confirm how your firm handles it.
Does the arrangement affect my independence?
It should not, and it is designed not to. Leaflegal makes the introduction and steps back. We do not tell clients which firm to choose, comment on their matter or have any say in your advice or your fees. Your duty to act in the client's best interests is unaffected by how they came to you.
Is Leaflegal regulated by the SRA?
No. Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm, and does not give legal advice. That is explained to every client before they submit an enquiry. The professional relationship, and everything that comes with it, is between the client and your firm. Your firm remains responsible for its own regulatory duties, including telling clients about the referral arrangement.
What about conflict checks?
Run your normal conflict checks before acting. Leaflegal does not carry them out for you. If you find a conflict and cannot act, let us know. Your partner agreement sets out how that lead is treated. The client can then be told you are unable to help. The same applies to identity and anti-money laundering checks.
Can I give advice on the first call?
That is a matter for your professional judgement. Some solicitors give a short outline and then book a proper consultation. Others prefer to gather information and confirm terms first. Whatever you decide, the client has agreed to be contacted about their matter, so the conversation is expected. Many firms confirm terms in writing before giving detailed advice.
Does Leaflegal see what happens after the introduction?
Only what you choose to tell us. Leaflegal is not involved in the retainer and does not see your advice, your file or your fees. We do ask firms to let us know which leads turned into instructions, because that helps us improve their criteria. Any feedback you share should be limited to what you are comfortable sharing under your duty of confidentiality.
What does a lead look like?
It is a short, readable summary. It shows the client's name, mobile and email, the practice area and matter type, their description, urgency and any deadline, their location, confirmation of paid help, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the consent record. We show you a real sample on your first call.
How quickly do leads arrive?
As soon as the enquiry has been checked. There is no batching and no delay while we wait to collect several leads. That means you can call while the client is still thinking about their matter and expecting to hear from a solicitor. Switch on notifications for whoever handles new leads so they see each one straight away.
Can leads go to different fee earners?
Yes. We can send leads for different practice areas or locations to different email addresses, or into your CRM where you can assign them. Many firms use a shared intake inbox so nothing is missed when someone is out of the office. Tell us how your firm is organised on your call and we will set up delivery to match.
Do I need to log into anything?
No. There is no online platform or login. Leads arrive by email or in your CRM. If you want to change your criteria, volume or delivery, or pause leads, you contact our team and we make the change for you. Many solicitors like this, because there is no new system for the team to learn or check.
Can I choose where my clients are?
Yes. Tell us the towns, counties or regions you cover, or that you work nationally and remotely. We only send enquiries from those areas. For immigration, enquiries can come from anywhere in the UK. If you cover different areas for different practice areas, tell us and we will set each one up separately. You can widen or narrow your coverage later.
What if a client says they did not enquire?
Each lead comes with the consent wording the client agreed to and the time they agreed to it, which you can refer to. Occasionally people forget, especially if they enquired late at night. If a lead appears not to be genuine, tell us within the review period in your partner agreement.
What if a lead arrives while we are in court?
The lead waits in your inbox or CRM until someone picks it up. Clients understand that solicitors are busy, but they also expect to hear back fairly soon. A short text or email to say you have received their enquiry and will call later that day keeps them engaged. Agreeing cover in advance avoids leads sitting unread.
How much does each lead cost?
The price is agreed with your firm before you start. It depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose and the volume you want. We do not publish prices because they vary widely between areas. After your call we will put a proposal together for you. There is no obligation.
Is there a minimum?
Yes, the minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that you set a monthly cap that your team can comfortably call back. You can change the cap at any time. Ten a month is enough to see the quality of the leads and how they fit with your intake. Many firms start near the minimum and raise their cap once their routine is working.
Can I pause?
Yes. If your team is at capacity, a fee earner is on leave or you are moving offices, tell us and we will pause matching until you are ready. Pausing is a normal part of how firms use the service. Your criteria and price stay the same while you are paused, so you can pick up where you left off.
What does the partner agreement cover?
It covers pricing, billing, volumes, delivery and how invalid leads are handled, including the review period for raising a problem. You see it and agree it before your first lead, so you know exactly how the arrangement works. Take the time you need to read it, and ask your compliance officer to look at it if you wish. If anything is unclear, ask us.
Do you guarantee instructions?
No. We provide qualified enquiries, not guaranteed instructions. Some clients compare firms, some decide not to proceed and some cannot afford the fees. What we can do is send you real enquiries, quickly and exclusively, so you have a fair chance to win the work. Calling quickly helps more than anything.
How do I get started?
Apply to become a partner firm and book a 30-minute call with our team. We will talk about your practice areas, locations, team and volume, show you a sample lead and then send a proposal. Once your agreement is signed, leads start as matching enquiries come in. The application takes a couple of minutes.
Can we change our criteria after we start?
Yes. Criteria are not fixed. You can add or remove practice areas, change matter types, widen or narrow your locations and raise or lower your cap. Most firms adjust things in the first few months as they learn which enquiries convert best. Just tell our team what you want to change.
Who controls the client's data once I have it?
Your firm becomes the controller of its copy of the lead once it is delivered. You are responsible for handling it under UK data protection law and your own privacy notice, keeping it secure and using it to contact the client about their enquiry. Vertical Brands Ltd, trading as Leaflegal, is the controller for what we collect.
Can I add clients to our newsletter?
Only if the client separately agrees. The consent they gave us covers contact from your firm about their enquiry. It does not cover general marketing. If they instruct you or ask to hear more, you can ask for marketing consent in the usual way. Record any marketing consent in the usual way.
Do clients pay Leaflegal anything?
No. Clients do not pay Leaflegal anything to make an enquiry or be introduced. Your firm pays for the introduction and the client agrees fees directly with you. Clients are told that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to. Our arrangement with your firm does not change what the client pays you.
How long can we keep a lead's details?
That depends on your own retention policy and your privacy notice. Once the lead is delivered, your firm is the controller of its copy and decides how long to keep it, in line with UK data protection law. Many firms keep enquiries that do not become instructions for a shorter period than client files. Your data protection lead can advise on what suits your firm.

More questions people ask

What is a lease extension lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a flat owner in England and Wales who wants to pay a solicitor to help extend their lease or buy their freehold. They have told us roughly how many years are left, which route they are considering, whether a sale or remortgage depends on it and where the flat is, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold. We do not send the same leaseholder to several firms at once. Because lease extension work is specialist and many clients are unsure who to approach, an exclusive introduction gives you a fair chance to explain your service and win the instruction.
Will I know how long is left on the lease?
Each lead includes the client's own estimate of how many years are left. Many leaseholders know the figure roughly but not exactly, so it is worth confirming it from the lease or the title once you are instructed. The estimate is still very useful for deciding which enquiries to call first and how to frame the conversation.
Do you send collective enfranchisement enquiries?
Yes, if you want them. Enfranchisement enquiries usually come from one leaseholder acting for a group, or exploring the idea on behalf of neighbours. Each lead includes the number of flats in the building, which helps you judge the size of the matter. These enquiries can take time to turn into instructions while the group organises itself.
Do clients know which route they want?
Some do, but many are unsure. We ask whether they are interested in a statutory extension, an informal extension or buying the freehold, and some answer that they do not know. Their answer gives you a starting point, and explaining the general differences on the first call is often where a firm earns the client's trust.
Will I know if a sale depends on the extension?
Yes. Each lead says whether a sale or remortgage depends on the outcome. Those enquiries are usually the most time sensitive, because buyers, agents and lenders are waiting. Knowing this before you call lets you route the matter to someone who can act quickly and talk the client through the general options.
Do you send enquiries from freeholders?
Our lease extension enquiries come from leaseholders who want to extend their lease or buy the freehold, along with some lease variation and dispute matters raised by leaseholders. If you also act for freeholders and want to discuss that work, raise it on your call. Your conflict checks remain important where a building or freeholder is already known to you.
Do you send enquiries about flats with very short leases?
Yes. Each lead includes the client's estimate of the years left, and some leases will be very short. These clients are often worried that their flat is hard to sell or mortgage. Their matters can be more complex and more valuable to a firm, so many partners ask for them. If you prefer to avoid them, tell us and we will take that into account in your matching.
