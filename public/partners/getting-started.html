What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

This page is for firms that have decided to look seriously at Leaflegal and want to know what getting started involves. In short, we need to know what you do, where you do it, how you are regulated and how many enquiries your team can call back quickly. You need a place for leads to land and someone ready to call them.

None of this is complicated, but a little preparation makes a big difference. The firms that get the most from their first month are usually the ones that decided in advance who would handle new leads, how quickly they would call and how they would record what happened. The firms that struggle are usually the ones that hoped leads would somehow fit around everything else.

The route in

  • Apply through the partner form, which takes a couple of minutes
  • Have a 30-minute call with our team and see a sample lead
  • Receive a proposal with your criteria, price per lead and an honest view of likely volumes
  • Review and sign your partner agreement
  • Set up delivery and your intake routine
  • Start receiving exclusive leads as matching enquiries come in

What it does not involve

There is no platform to learn, no software to install and no login to manage. You do not need to write adverts, approve campaigns or set budgets for media spend. You do not need a dedicated marketing person. Leads arrive by email or in your CRM, and changes are made by talking to our team.

Who to involve at your firm

In a small firm, one person might handle everything. In a larger firm, it helps to involve a partner who can make decisions about new business, a practice or intake manager who will run the day to day process and a compliance officer who can look at the referral arrangement and the data side. Getting them involved early avoids delays later.

A realistic starting point

It helps to be clear about what you want from the first few months. Some firms want to fill a new fee earner's diary. Some want to test demand in a town where they have just opened an office. Some want to move their practice towards a particular kind of matter. Telling us your aim on the call helps us suggest criteria and a cap that fit it, and gives you something specific to measure results against once leads start arriving.

Leaflegal works with firms and advisers who are regulated for the work they take on. We ask about your regulation when you apply and only match enquiries in areas where you can act.

Regulated firms

  • Solicitors' firms regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority
  • Practices regulated by CILEx Regulation
  • Licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers
  • For immigration, advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority, formerly the OISC, at the right level for the work

Authorised for the work

You must be authorised for every kind of work you ask to receive, including reserved legal activities such as conveyancing and the conduct of litigation, and immigration advice. If your authorisation is limited to certain areas or levels, tell us so that we can set your criteria to match.

Privately paid work

Every Leaflegal client has confirmed they want paid legal help rather than legal aid or free advice. Firms that mainly handle publicly funded work may find the service is not a good fit.

Areas we cover

We generate enquiries in conveyancing, family law, immigration, debt recovery for businesses, motoring offences, licensing and lease extensions. We do not generate personal injury or clinical negligence work, where referral fees are banned, or claims management work such as housing disrepair and financial mis-selling.

Where clients are

In six areas, enquiries are for matters in England and Wales. Trademark and immigration enquiries can come from anywhere in the UK. Your firm needs to be able to act for clients in the locations you choose.

If you are not sure

If you are unsure whether your firm fits, apply anyway and mention your question. We would rather talk it through on a call than have a firm rule itself out for no reason, or join and find out later that the service does not suit it.

When you apply and on your call, we will ask for the information we need to set up your matching and your proposal. Having it ready makes the process quicker.

None of this information is shared with other partner firms. We use it only to set up your matching, prepare your proposal and make sure enquiries reach the right people at your firm.

Your practice

Which of our eight practice areas you want to receive, and which matter types within each. Be as specific as you like. It is far better to tell us you only want contested financial remedy work than to receive enquiries you will not take.

Your regulation

Your regulator, your registration or authorisation details and any limits on the work you can do. For immigration advisers, this includes your Immigration Advice Authority level.

Your locations

The towns, counties or regions you cover, or confirmation that you work nationally and remotely. If you cover different areas for different practice areas, tell us.

Your capacity

How many new enquiries your team could call back quickly each month. This becomes your monthly cap. The minimum is 10 leads a month.

Your delivery

Where leads should go. This might be one email address, several addresses, a shared intake inbox, or your CRM or case management system using a lead email address or a webhook.

Your contacts

Who we should speak to about criteria, billing, invalid leads and any compliance questions. In many firms these are different people.

Your languages

For immigration and some family work, tell us the languages your team speaks. Many clients feel more comfortable when they can explain their situation in their first language, and it can help you understand their matter more quickly. It is useful information to have in mind when we set up your matching.

Before your first lead arrives, decide how your firm will handle it. A few simple choices make the difference between a lead that is called within minutes and one that sits unread until the next day.

None of these steps needs new software. Most firms can put them in place in an afternoon with the tools they already use. The important thing is to decide them before the first lead arrives rather than after the first one is missed.

Choose where leads land

A shared intake inbox or a CRM route is better than one fee earner's personal email. If that person is in court, on leave or off sick, a lead sent only to them can go unseen.

Switch on notifications

Make sure whoever handles new leads gets an alert on their phone or desktop when one arrives. Leads lose value quickly, and an email sitting unnoticed in a busy inbox is easy to miss.

Decide who calls

Agree who calls new leads at each point in the day, including lunchtime, late afternoon and holidays. If more than one person might pick up a lead, agree how they will mark it as taken so that two people do not call the same client.

Agree how quickly

Set a target, such as calling within minutes during working hours and first thing the next morning for overnight leads. The firms that do best call within minutes.

Prepare your opening

Agree how your team will introduce themselves, mention the Leaflegal enquiry and explain the referral arrangement. Prepare a short follow up email that confirms the next step and your fees.

Plan your record

Decide where you will note each lead's outcome. A field in your CRM or a simple spreadsheet is enough.

If you are not sure how to set any of this up, ask us on your call. We can explain how other firms usually organise their intake, without sharing any firm's details, and help you choose an approach that suits the size of your team.

The first month is a chance to test your process and learn how leads fit with your firm. It is not the time to draw final conclusions.

Think of the first month as a trial run of your intake routine as much as a test of the leads. Both matter, and the first month is when small changes to either have the biggest effect.

Check delivery early

When your first lead arrives, check that it reached the right place, that notifications worked and that the formatting is easy to read on a phone. If anything is wrong, tell us straight away and we will change it.

Watch your speed

Look at how quickly each lead was called. If some waited hours, find out why. It is often a simple gap, such as nobody covering lunchtime or leads going to an inbox no one checks on Fridays.

Listen to clients

Notice what clients say on the first call. Are they expecting you? Are they surprised by your fees? Are they asking for things you do not offer? This tells you whether your criteria and your opening need adjusting.

Report problems promptly

If a lead has wrong contact details or falls outside your criteria, tell us within the review period in your partner agreement. Reporting promptly also helps us find and fix the cause.

Give leads time

Some clients instruct on the first call. Others take weeks. Keep your record up to date and look back at the first month's leads after a little time has passed.

Talk to us

At the end of the first month, tell us what worked and what did not. Most firms adjust something, such as a matter type, a location or their cap.

Most problems in the early weeks come from a handful of avoidable mistakes. Knowing about them in advance makes them easy to avoid.

Setting the cap too high

It is tempting to ask for as many leads as possible. If your team cannot call them all back quickly, you pay for leads that go cold. Start with a number you can handle comfortably and raise it later.

Criteria that are too broad

If you would not take a particular matter type, leave it out. Receiving enquiries you will not act on wastes your team's time and your budget.

Criteria that are too narrow

The opposite problem is criteria so narrow that very few enquiries match. We will tell you if we think this is likely and suggest options, such as a neighbouring area or a related matter type.

Leads going to one person

A single fee earner's inbox is a single point of failure. Use a shared route.

Forgetting the referral disclosure

The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about the financial arrangement with an introducer. Build it into your opening and your client care letter from day one.

Judging too early

A handful of leads in the first week will not tell you much. Give it time, keep records and look at the results by practice area.

Not explaining fees

Clients who have confirmed they want paid help still want to know what it will cost. Firms that avoid the subject on the first call, or promise a quote that arrives days later, often lose the client to a firm that gave a clear answer. Decide in advance how your team will talk about fees for each kind of matter you receive.

Questions and answers

Who can become a partner?
SRA, CILEx Regulation and CLC regulated firms, and for immigration, advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority.
Can I see a sample lead?
Yes, on your call.
Who can become a partner firm?
SRA regulated solicitors' firms, CILEx regulated practices, CLC regulated conveyancers and, for immigration, advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level for the work. You must be authorised for the work you take on, including reserved legal activities and immigration advice. We ask about your regulation when you apply.
Is the service right for a new firm?
It can be. New firms often have capacity before their referral network has built up, and leads give them a source of enquiries they can control. You will still need to be regulated for the work and able to call leads back quickly. Start with a cap your team can comfortably handle.
Do I need a marketing team?
No. Leaflegal runs the campaigns, the enquiry forms and the checks. You need someone to receive and call leads, and someone to make decisions about your criteria and volume. In a small firm that may be the same person. Your role is to call leads quickly, tell us which ones work and let us know when your criteria or capacity change.
Should I talk to my compliance officer first?
Many firms do, and it is a good idea. Your compliance officer may want to look at our consent wording, how clients are told about the referral arrangement and how data is handled. We can explain all of these, and your partner agreement sets out how the arrangement works. Leaflegal cannot give your firm regulatory advice.
What if we only want one practice area?
That is fine. Many firms start with one area and add others later. Each area is set up separately, with its own matter types, locations and price, so adding another one later is straightforward. Starting with one area also makes it easier to set up your intake routine and judge results before you expand. There is no requirement to take more than one.
Can we join if we mainly do legal aid work?
Every Leaflegal client has confirmed they want paid help, so the service suits firms with a privately paying client base. If your firm offers both, you can take Leaflegal leads for your private client work. Firms that only do publicly funded work will find the service is not a good fit.
Can an immigration adviser who is not a solicitor join?
Yes, if they are registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level for the work they want to receive. We match immigration enquiries only to registered advisers and to SRA or CILEx regulated firms. Leaflegal never gives immigration advice itself. Tell us your level when you apply so we can match you only to suitable work.
How do I apply?
Complete the partner application. It takes a couple of minutes and asks about your practice areas, how you are regulated, the areas you cover and how many leads you want each month. We then contact you to book a 30-minute call with our team. There is no obligation at the application stage.
What happens on the call?
We talk about your practice areas and matter types, your locations, your team and how quickly you can call leads back. We show you a sample lead, explain how pricing works and answer your questions. There is no obligation to go ahead. It is also your chance to ask anything about where our leads come from, our consent wording and how matching works.
Can I see a sample lead?
Yes. We show you a sample lead on your call, including every field, the client's description and the consent record. It shows exactly what your intake team would be working with, so you can judge whether it suits your firm. Your compliance officer is welcome to look at it too, particularly the consent wording and how the referral arrangement is explained to clients.
What details do you need about our regulation?
Your regulator, your registration or authorisation details and any limits on the work you can do. For immigration, we need to know your Immigration Advice Authority level, or confirmation that you are an SRA or CILEx regulated firm. We only match enquiries in areas where you can act. Tell us if anything changes later.
How long before we receive a proposal?
After your call, we put together a proposal with your criteria, your price per lead for each practice area and our view of likely volumes. If anything in it does not match what you expected, tell us and we will talk it through before you move on to the agreement.
What if we are not a good fit?
We will tell you. If your firm mainly handles work we do not cover, cannot call leads back quickly or wants criteria that would produce very few matches, it is better for both of us to say so at the start rather than after you have spent money. There is no cost to finding out.
What do we need in place before the first lead?
A place for leads to land, such as a shared inbox or your CRM, notifications switched on for whoever handles them, a clear decision on who calls and how quickly, an agreed way to explain the introduction and referral arrangement, and somewhere to record outcomes. None of this needs new software. Most firms can set it up in an afternoon with the tools they already use.
Can leads go straight into our CRM?
If your CRM or case management system accepts leads by email or webhook, yes. Tell us what you use and we will set up delivery. If you prefer, leads can simply arrive by email to one or more addresses or a shared intake inbox. Many firms start with email and move to CRM delivery once they have seen how leads fit their intake.
Do our staff need training?
Not on any software, because there is none. It helps to brief whoever calls leads on how to open the call, mention the Leaflegal enquiry, explain the referral arrangement and record the outcome. A short shared note is usually enough. Make sure they also know what to do if a lead has wrong details or falls outside your criteria, so it can be reported within the review period.
How do we set our monthly cap?
Think about how many new enquiries the people handling leads could call back within minutes on a normal day, and how many new matters they could realistically open. Work back from there. The minimum is 10 a month. It is better to start a little low and increase later. You can change it later.
What should our first call sound like?
Introduce yourself and your firm. Say that they made an enquiry through Leaflegal and asked to speak to a solicitor about their matter. Refer to the matter type and any deadline from the lead. Explain how your fees work and agree a clear next step, then confirm it by email.
Can different offices receive their own leads?
Yes. If you have more than one office, we can route leads by location so that each office receives the enquiries closest to it. You can also route by practice area, so that family leads go to your family team and conveyancing leads go to your property team. Tell us how your firm is organised.
Should we tell clients about Leaflegal in writing?
The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement with an introducer. Many firms do this on the first call and again in writing in their client care letter. Our consent wording, which comes with every lead, tells clients that Leaflegal is paid by the firms it introduces. Your compliance officer can confirm your firm's approach.
How quickly will the first lead arrive?
Once your agreement is signed and delivery is set up, leads start as matching enquiries come in. How soon the first one arrives depends on your practice areas, matter types and locations. Wider coverage usually produces matches sooner than a narrow area. We will give you an honest view of likely volumes before you start, so you know roughly what to expect in your first month.
What if the first few leads do not instruct?
That is normal. Some clients compare firms, some take time to decide and some do not go ahead. A handful of leads is too few to judge. Keep calling quickly, keep records and look at results after a couple of months, by practice area. Look at how quickly each lead was called and how the first conversation went, as these often explain early results.
What if something is wrong with delivery?
Tell us straight away. If leads are going to the wrong address, arriving in a format that is hard to read or not reaching your CRM, we will fix the delivery. Checking the first lead carefully catches most of these issues. It is worth asking someone to check the first two or three leads carefully, from arrival through to the first call.
What if a lead has wrong details?
Tell us within the review period set out in your partner agreement. Where a lead is genuinely invalid, we deal with it as your agreement sets out. Reporting promptly also helps us find the cause. Ask whoever calls new leads to flag anything that looks wrong on the first call, and collect those flags in one place so they can be sent to us promptly.
When should we review our criteria?
Most firms find it useful to talk to us after their first month. By then you will have a feel for which matter types and locations suit you best. Adjusting criteria is simple and can be done at any time. You do not need to wait for a review to make a change. If a matter type clearly is not working, tell us straight away.
Should we follow up clients who do not answer?
Yes. Leave a short voicemail, send an email and a text that mention their enquiry, then try again later the same day and the next morning. Many clients are at work or wary of unknown numbers. A polite, planned follow up wins a surprising amount of work that would otherwise be lost.
Do you check in with new firms?
We are always happy to talk, and we find a conversation after the first month is useful for most firms. Tell us what is working and what is not, and we will suggest changes to your criteria, cap or delivery. There is no portal or report to log into, so a call or an email is the way to share feedback.
How much will it cost to get started?
You pay per lead, at a price agreed before you start, with a minimum of 10 leads a month. Your price depends on your practice areas, matter types, locations and volume. Any other costs are explained in your proposal before you commit. We do not publish prices because they vary widely.
Can we pause after starting?
Yes. If your team is at capacity, someone is away or you need a break, ask us to pause. You receive no leads while paused and your criteria and price stay the same when you return. Pausing is a normal part of how firms use the service, especially around holidays, and does not need to be explained.
Do clients pay Leaflegal anything?
No. Leaflegal does not charge clients anything. Your firm pays for the introduction and the client agrees fees directly with you. Every client is told that Leaflegal is paid by the firms it introduces. Our arrangement with your firm does not change what the client pays you. The client is free to decide whether to instruct you after your first conversation.
Is there a long contract?
The terms, including any notice period, are set out in your partner agreement, which you see and agree before your first lead. We would rather firms stay because the leads work for them than because a contract says so. If anything in the agreement is unclear, ask us before you sign. You can also pause at any time without ending the arrangement.

More questions people ask

What is a family law lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person in England and Wales who wants to pay a solicitor to help with a family matter, such as a divorce, finances on separation or arrangements for children. They have told us the type of matter, whether proceedings have started, how urgent it is and where they are, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm.
Are family leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold. We do not send the same person to several firms at once, which matters in family work because people at a difficult time can find several calls from competing firms overwhelming. You are the only firm that receives the client's details from us, so you have space for a proper first conversation.
Do family clients pay privately?
Yes. Every client confirms they want paid help rather than legal aid or free advice. Legal aid for most private family matters was withdrawn some years ago, so most people now pay privately. If someone tells us they only want legal aid, they are pointed elsewhere and do not become a lead. Your first call can therefore start from that shared understanding.
Which family matters can I receive?
You can choose from divorce and dissolution, financial settlements and consent orders, child arrangements, cohabitation disputes, prenuptial and postnuptial agreements and separation agreements. You can take all of them or only the work your team wants. Many firms focus on financial and children work and leave simpler matters out, while others welcome fixed fee divorce work.
Will I know if court proceedings have started?
Yes. Each lead says whether proceedings have started and whether there is a hearing date the client knows about. That helps you tell the difference between someone planning ahead and someone who has just received a court application, so you can route the urgent ones to a fee earner who can act quickly and plan the rest around your diary.
What does the best time to call mean for family leads?
Each client tells us when it is best to call. In family matters this can be important, because a client may still live with their partner or may not want to be overheard. Respecting that time, and leaving a short and discreet message if they do not answer, helps the client feel safe and makes it more likely they will speak openly.
Do you send enquiries from both parties to the same firm?
We match each enquiry on its own, and we cannot always know whether two enquiries relate to the same relationship. That is why your own conflict checks remain essential. If you find that you already act for the other party, do not take the matter on and let us know. We will treat the lead as your partner agreement sets out.
Do you send enquiries about domestic abuse or emergency protection?
Our family enquiries focus on privately funded separation, money and children matters. Some clients will mention difficult circumstances in their own words, and you should handle those leads with your usual care. Legal aid can be available for some people affected by domestic abuse, so if a client turns out to qualify and prefers that route, your normal approach to signposting applies.
