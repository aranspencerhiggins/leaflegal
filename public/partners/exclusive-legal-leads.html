Every Leaflegal enquiry is matched to one firm only. We never send the same lead to several firms and never resell a lead after it has been delivered.

Why exclusivity matters

When an enquiry goes to several firms, the client gets a flurry of calls and tends to choose whoever is cheapest or first. With an exclusive lead, you are the only firm calling. You can take the time to understand the matter, explain your fees and win the instruction on quality.

How we decide which firm gets a lead

Each lead goes to a firm whose practice areas, matter types and locations it fits, taking account of each firm’s monthly cap. Where more than one firm could take it, we balance distribution fairly.

What exclusivity doesn’t mean

The client is free to choose any firm, and some will compare quotes on their own. Exclusive means we only introduce them to you.

Leaflegal is a trading name of Vertical Brands Ltd and an introducer, not a law firm. It is not regulated by the SRA and does not give legal or immigration advice. Partner firms are responsible for their own regulatory position.

What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

An exclusive legal lead is an enquiry that is sent to one firm only. With Leaflegal, every lead works this way. When someone asks to speak to a solicitor about their matter, we match their enquiry to a single partner firm, send it as soon as it is checked and never pass it to anyone else, then or later.

That might sound like a small detail. In practice it changes almost everything about the first call. A client who has been introduced to one firm is expecting one call. They have time to listen, explain their situation and ask questions. A client whose details have gone to five firms gets five calls in quick succession, often from people who know nothing about them, and quickly starts to treat the whole thing as a price comparison.

Better for the client

People contacting a solicitor are often worried. They may be separating from a partner, facing a driving ban, chasing money their business badly needs or trying to sort out a lease before a sale falls through. A flood of calls from firms they have never heard of does not help. An exclusive introduction to one firm, which has the details of their matter in front of it, feels far more like getting help.

Better for your firm

When you are the only firm calling, you do not need to drop everything to win a race. You still want to call quickly, because the client's need is real and their attention will not last forever. But you can use the call to understand the matter, explain how you work and talk honestly about fees. You win the work because of the service you offer, not because you happened to dial first.

Better for the profession

Clients who feel pursued by several firms sometimes come away with a poor view of solicitors in general. An exclusive introduction treats them with more respect. It also fits more comfortably with the professional standards solicitors work to, because the conversation is about the client's needs rather than about beating competitors to the phone.

Exclusive from start to finish

Exclusivity is only meaningful if it runs through the whole process. Our campaigns run under the Leaflegal name, so clients are not responding to adverts for several firms at once. Our forms are our own, so their details are not being collected by anyone else at the same time. And once a lead is delivered, it is never offered to another firm. Each of those steps matters. A lead that is exclusive at the moment of delivery but was collected through a shared form, or resold a week later, is not really exclusive at all.

Every enquiry is matched to one firm using the criteria that firm has agreed with us. There is no auction and no bidding. A lead does not go to whoever is willing to pay the most on the day.

The criteria we use

  • Practice area, such as conveyancing, family law or licensing
  • Matter type within that area, such as contested finances or remortgages
  • Location, by town, county, region or national coverage
  • Your monthly cap and whether you have reached it
  • Whether your firm is active or paused

When one firm fits

If only one partner firm's criteria match an enquiry, it goes to that firm. This is common for specialist matter types and for locations where we work with a single firm.

When several firms fit

If more than one firm could take an enquiry, we still send it to one of them. We balance distribution fairly between the firms that fit, taking account of each firm's cap and how many leads it has already received that month. No firm is starved of leads because another firm is larger.

When no firm fits

If an enquiry does not match any partner firm's criteria, it is not forced on a firm that did not ask for it. A firm that wants only purchases in one county will not receive a remortgage from the other end of the country simply because nobody else wanted it.

Why caps matter

Caps protect your team as well as your budget. Once you reach your monthly number, we stop matching to you until the next month. That means you are never sent more enquiries than you can call back properly, and other firms have a fair chance to receive leads that fit them.

Pausing and matching

If your firm is paused, you are left out of matching until you tell us you are ready again. Pausing is useful when a fee earner is on leave, when your team is busy with existing work or when you are moving offices. It does not affect your criteria or your agreed price, and you return to matching on the same basis as before. Leads you have already received remain yours throughout.

Exclusivity has two parts. The first is that the enquiry goes to one firm. The second is that once it has gone to that firm, it stays there. Leaflegal never resells a delivered lead.

What happens after delivery

Once a lead reaches your firm, the client's relationship is with you. We do not send the same details to another firm a week later, add them to a list or offer them to anyone else. If the client does not instruct you, that is the end of our part in it.

If you cannot act

Sometimes a firm cannot take on a client, perhaps because of a conflict or because the matter turns out to be outside what it does. Tell us if that happens. Your partner agreement sets out how such a lead is treated. We do not quietly pass it on to another firm without the client's knowledge.

If the client enquires again

Occasionally a client comes back to Leaflegal later with a new matter. That is a new enquiry, with its own consent, and it is matched like any other. A person who enquired about a house purchase last year might enquire about a lease extension this year, and the matching will depend on the firms whose criteria fit at that time.

No data trading

We do not sell or rent personal data to anyone. Matter details are never passed to advertising platforms. This is part of how we keep exclusivity meaningful. A lead is only exclusive if the details in it are not available anywhere else.

What this means for your records

Because each lead is sent once, the copy your firm receives is the only copy any law firm has from Leaflegal. Your firm becomes the controller of that copy and handles it under UK data protection law and your own privacy notice. If the client asks you about how their details reached you, the consent record that comes with every lead shows what they agreed to and when.

Exclusive leads give you room to have a proper first conversation. Firms that make the most of that room tend to follow a similar pattern.

Call quickly anyway

Exclusive does not mean the client will wait forever. They asked for help because they need it, and they may look elsewhere on their own if nobody calls. Aim to call within minutes during working hours, and first thing the next morning for leads that arrive overnight.

Start with their matter

Use the lead. Mention the type of matter, any deadline they gave and something from their own description. It tells the client straight away that you are the firm they were introduced to and that you have listened.

Explain the introduction

Tell the client they made an enquiry through Leaflegal and asked to be put in touch with a solicitor. This reassures them that the call is expected and is also a natural moment to mention the referral arrangement, which you need to make sure clients are told about.

Talk about fees early

Every Leaflegal client has said they want paid help, but most will want to know how much before they commit. Explaining your fees clearly, or when they will receive a written quote, builds trust.

Agree a next step

End the call with something definite, such as a booked appointment, a list of documents to send or a quote to review by a certain date. Confirm it in writing the same day.

If you cannot get through

Clients do not always answer straight away. They may be at work, driving or simply wary of unknown numbers. Leave a short voicemail that mentions their enquiry, then send an email and a text with your name and direct number. Try again later the same day and the next morning. Because nobody else has been sent their details by Leaflegal, a polite, persistent approach is usually welcome rather than intrusive.

Firms sometimes ask whether they can have every Leaflegal lead for a particular town, county or matter type. This is different from lead exclusivity, which every partner firm receives.

What area exclusivity would mean

Area exclusivity would mean no other firm receives leads that match a given location or matter type. For a firm with a strong local brand this can be attractive, because it means competitors are not receiving Leaflegal introductions in its patch.

Why it is not automatic

Whether it is possible depends on the volume of enquiries in that area, how many leads the firm can realistically call back each month and the other firms we already work with. Granting one firm an area when it can only handle a fraction of the enquiries would mean clients go unhelped.

Talk to us

If area exclusivity matters to you, raise it on your call. We will be honest about what is realistic for your criteria. Anything agreed is written into your partner agreement.

Alternatives to area exclusivity

Some firms find that what they really want is not exclusivity but focus. Narrowing your matter types to the work you most want, setting your locations around the offices you can realistically serve and choosing a cap that keeps your team busy without overloading it often achieves the same result. You receive enquiries that fit your firm closely, each one exclusive to you, without needing a special arrangement.

Every partner firm already has the most important form of exclusivity, which is that each lead it receives goes to it alone.

Exclusive leads are priced per lead, at a price agreed with your firm before you start. We do not publish prices, because the value of a lead varies a great deal between practice areas, matter types and locations.

An exclusive lead usually costs more than a shared one. The useful comparison is not the price of a lead but the cost of winning an instruction. If a shared lead goes to several firms and only one can win it, each firm is paying for a fraction of a chance. With an exclusive lead, the whole chance is yours.

Exclusive leads also save time that is easy to overlook. With shared leads, intake staff spend a large part of their day calling people who have already been contacted by other firms, explaining who they are and competing on price. With exclusive leads that time goes into conversations about the client's matter. For a busy team, that difference can matter as much as the price of the lead itself.

The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that you set a monthly cap and can change it or pause at any time. Pricing, billing, volumes, delivery and the rules for invalid leads are set out in your partner agreement before your first lead.

Selling each lead several times brings in more money per enquiry, so shared leads are attractive to sellers. Exclusive leads cost more to produce, because every enquiry can only be sold once. Fair distribution means neither firm is consistently overlooked. Narrowing your matter types or locations to the work you most want can make your matching more focused, but overlap is normal and nothing to worry about.

More questions people ask

What is a conveyancing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person who is buying, selling or remortgaging a property in England and Wales and has asked to be contacted by a firm about paid conveyancing. They have answered a short set of questions about the transaction, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm. We send that enquiry to you alone, as soon as it has been checked.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We do not send the same enquiry to several firms at once and we never resell a lead after it has been delivered. If you are the firm we match, you are the only firm that receives that person's details from us. The client can still choose to speak to other firms on their own, as anyone can.
How do you check the leads?
Every client confirms they want paid legal help with a property in England and Wales. We verify their UK mobile number and their email address, and we filter out enquiries that fall outside our scope or your criteria. People we cannot help are told politely and pointed elsewhere, so they never become a lead. Each lead then arrives with the client's answers and the consent record.
What details will I get for each enquiry?
You get the client's name, mobile and email, whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, the tenure, whether there is a mortgage, the target timescale and the location. There is also a short description in the client's own words, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the exact consent wording they agreed to.
Can I quote from the lead alone?
Usually you can give a provisional figure on the first call, because the lead covers the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether a mortgage is involved. You will still want to confirm the details with the client and send a written quote. Things like a leasehold management pack, a new build or a gifted deposit may change your figure once you know more.
Do you send leasehold purchases and sales?
Yes, if you want them. Every lead says whether the property is freehold or leasehold, and you can choose to receive leasehold matters, leave them out or ask for them only in certain areas. Firms with an experienced leasehold team sometimes prefer this work, while others choose to focus on freehold transactions. We set your matching to suit you.
Are remortgage leads included?
Only if you ask for them. Remortgages, transfers of equity, purchases, sales and combined sale and purchase matters can each be switched on or off. Some firms take remortgages to keep a steady flow of shorter matters, while others prefer to concentrate on purchases. You can change the mix at any time by letting us know.
Do clients know a firm will call them?
Yes. Every client has agreed to be contacted by a firm about their matter, by phone, email or text, and most are expecting a call soon after they submit. It helps to say early in the call that you are getting in touch because of the enquiry they made through Leaflegal. That puts the client at ease and moves the conversation quickly on to their purchase, sale or remortgage.
