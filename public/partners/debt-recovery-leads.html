Leaflegal sends debt recovery enquiries from businesses that are owed money by other businesses and want a law firm to help recover it. Every enquiry is business to business. We do not send consumer debts, and we do not send enquiries from people or businesses who owe money and want help with their own debts.

Each enquiry goes to one firm only. It is never shared with a panel of firms or debt collection agencies and it is never resold after it has been delivered to you.

Business owners usually look for legal help with a debt after their own credit control has run out of road. Invoices have gone unpaid past their terms, reminders have been ignored or a customer has gone quiet. Some have a single large invoice that matters to their cash flow. Others have several smaller debts that have built up over months. Either way, they want a firm that acts quickly and tells them clearly what it will cost.

How the enquiries are generated

Our campaigns run on search, social media and helpful content aimed at business owners, finance managers and bookkeepers dealing with late payment. Each person answers a short set of questions about the debt, confirms that the debtor is a business and that they want paid help from a law firm, and gives their contact details. When an enquiry matches your criteria, we check it and send it to you straight away.

Why business debt work suits bought leads

Commercial debt recovery is often repeat work. A business that has a good experience with one unpaid invoice will usually come back when the next one goes overdue, and may pass its whole collections book to the firm it trusts. An exclusive introduction gives you the chance to build that relationship from the first call, without a competing firm calling at the same time.

What Leaflegal is not

Leaflegal is not a law firm, a debt collection agency or a credit reference service. We do not give legal advice, we do not assess the strength of a claim and we do not contact debtors. We introduce a business creditor to one firm, and the advice and recovery work come from you.

Debt recovery enquiries come from businesses of all sizes, but most are small and medium sized firms without an in house legal team. You choose the kinds of matter you want, so a firm that prefers larger commercial claims does not have to take very small invoices.

Typical clients

  • Trades and construction businesses chasing payment for completed work
  • Wholesalers, manufacturers and distributors owed for goods supplied
  • Agencies, consultants and professional services firms with unpaid fees
  • Logistics, haulage and transport businesses
  • Recruitment businesses owed placement or temporary worker fees
  • Landlords of commercial premises owed rent by business tenants
  • Technology and software businesses with unpaid subscriptions or project fees

Matter types you can choose

  • Letters before action
  • County Court and High Court claims
  • Statutory demands
  • Winding up and bankruptcy petitions against business debtors
  • Enforcement of judgments

Where they are in the process

Some clients enquire as soon as an invoice is a few weeks overdue and want a solicitor's letter to prompt payment. Others have chased for months, tried a collection agency and now want to issue a claim. A smaller group already hold a judgment that has not been paid and want help with enforcement. Each lead tells you the age of the debt and gives a description in the client's own words, so you can see which stage you are dealing with.

What clients care about

Business creditors want three things. They want to know whether it is worth pursuing, what it will cost and how quickly something will happen. Many have been put off by open ended hourly rates, so a fixed fee for a letter before action or a clear scale for issuing a claim tends to land well. They also want to know whether they can recover interest and costs from the debtor, which you can explain on the first call.

Some clients have already used a collection agency or tried the online money claim service themselves. Knowing what has been tried before helps you explain what a law firm can add, and the description in the client's own words often covers this.

Every debt recovery lead is the full record of what the client told us. The questions are short, but they give you the commercial basics you need to triage the enquiry before you pick up the phone.

Why the dispute question matters

Whether a debt is disputed changes the route a matter is likely to take. An undisputed debt may suit a letter before action followed quickly by a claim, while a disputed one may need a closer look at the contract, the correspondence and the evidence. Knowing the answer before you call helps you decide who in your team should speak to the client and how to frame the first conversation.

Age of the debt

The age of the debt helps you judge urgency and spot limitation issues early. In England and Wales, most claims for a contract debt must be brought within six years of the date the money became due, although a written acknowledgment or a part payment can restart the clock. Older debts may also be harder to collect in practice, which is worth exploring with the client.

Debt recovery enquiries are commercial and practical. Business owners respond well to a firm that is quick, organised and clear about money.

Call promptly

A business owner who has decided to involve a solicitor usually wants to see something happen that week. Calling soon after a lead arrives shows the kind of service they can expect. Set up alerts so a fee earner or credit control lawyer sees each new lead quickly.

Triage on the first call

The lead gives you the amount, the age of the debt and whether it is disputed. On the first call you can ask for the invoice, the terms and any correspondence, and check basic points such as who the debtor is and whether it is still trading. A short, structured conversation reassures the client that their matter is in capable hands.

Be clear about fees and recovery

Explain your fees for each stage, such as a letter before action, issuing a claim and enforcement, and what the client might be able to recover from the debtor. Many business creditors are not aware of statutory interest and compensation for late payment under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, or of the fixed costs that may apply on court claims. A clear explanation of how this works in general often helps them decide.

After the call, a short email confirming the next step, the fee for that step and the documents you need keeps momentum. Business owners are busy, and a clear written summary makes it easy for them to say yes.

Think about the wider relationship

A client with one overdue invoice may have others. Asking about their wider credit control, their terms of business and how they chase late payers can open the door to ongoing work, such as reviewing their terms and conditions or handling collections on a regular basis.

Mention the introduction

The client agreed to be contacted after making an enquiry through Leaflegal. Mention that when you call, and cover any referral arrangement in your client care letter as your regulator requires.

Tell us what works

Let us know which leads instruct and which do not. If disputed debts or very small invoices rarely convert for you, we can change your matching to send you more of the work that fits.

Sole traders and the pre-action protocol

Some business debtors are sole traders or partnerships rather than companies. Where the debtor is an individual, including a sole trader, the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims will usually apply, with its own requirements on the letter of claim and the time allowed for a response. The lead may not tell you the debtor's legal form, so it is worth checking on the first call.
