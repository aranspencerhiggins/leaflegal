Debt recovery enquiries come from businesses of all sizes, but most are small and medium sized firms without an in house legal team. You choose the kinds of matter you want, so a firm that prefers larger commercial claims does not have to take very small invoices.

Typical clients

  • Trades and construction businesses chasing payment for completed work
  • Wholesalers, manufacturers and distributors owed for goods supplied
  • Agencies, consultants and professional services firms with unpaid fees
  • Logistics, haulage and transport businesses
  • Recruitment businesses owed placement or temporary worker fees
  • Landlords of commercial premises owed rent by business tenants
  • Technology and software businesses with unpaid subscriptions or project fees

Matter types you can choose

  • Letters before action
  • County Court and High Court claims
  • Statutory demands
  • Winding up and bankruptcy petitions against business debtors
  • Enforcement of judgments

Where they are in the process

Some clients enquire as soon as an invoice is a few weeks overdue and want a solicitor's letter to prompt payment. Others have chased for months, tried a collection agency and now want to issue a claim. A smaller group already hold a judgment that has not been paid and want help with enforcement. Each lead tells you the age of the debt and gives a description in the client's own words, so you can see which stage you are dealing with.

What clients care about

Business creditors want three things. They want to know whether it is worth pursuing, what it will cost and how quickly something will happen. Many have been put off by open ended hourly rates, so a fixed fee for a letter before action or a clear scale for issuing a claim tends to land well. They also want to know whether they can recover interest and costs from the debtor, which you can explain on the first call.

Some clients have already used a collection agency or tried the online money claim service themselves. Knowing what has been tried before helps you explain what a law firm can add, and the description in the client's own words often covers this.

Every debt recovery lead is the full record of what the client told us. The questions are short, but they give you the commercial basics you need to triage the enquiry before you pick up the phone.

Why the dispute question matters

Whether a debt is disputed changes the route a matter is likely to take. An undisputed debt may suit a letter before action followed quickly by a claim, while a disputed one may need a closer look at the contract, the correspondence and the evidence. Knowing the answer before you call helps you decide who in your team should speak to the client and how to frame the first conversation.

Age of the debt

The age of the debt helps you judge urgency and spot limitation issues early. In England and Wales, most claims for a contract debt must be brought within six years of the date the money became due, although a written acknowledgment or a part payment can restart the clock. Older debts may also be harder to collect in practice, which is worth exploring with the client.

What clients ask

What is late payment interest?
It is interest that a business can claim when another business pays it late. Under late payment law, a creditor can usually claim statutory interest at 8% a year above the Bank of England base rate, plus a fixed sum of compensation for each late invoice, unless the contract provides its own substantial remedy for late payment.
Does late payment law apply to my business?
It applies to contracts for the supply of goods or services where both parties are acting in the course of a business. That covers most trade between businesses, including sales to public bodies. It does not apply to sales to consumers. Your solicitor will check whether your contract is covered.
Do I have to tell the customer in advance?
No. The right to statutory interest arises under the law, so you do not need to have warned the customer or included it in your terms. Many businesses do mention it in their terms or on invoices, which can encourage prompt payment and avoid surprises.
Does it apply to sole traders?
Yes, as long as the sole trader bought the goods or services for their business. A sole trader buying for personal use would be a consumer, and late payment law would not apply. Your solicitor will consider how your customer was acting when the contract was made.
Can I claim against a large company or public body?
Yes. Late payment law applies regardless of the size of the customer, and public authorities are covered too. There are also rules on how long public authorities should take to pay. Your solicitor will explain how these rules affect your invoices.
Is claiming interest bad for customer relationships?
It can be, and some businesses choose not to claim with customers they value. Others find that knowing interest will be added encourages prompt payment. It is a commercial decision for your business. Your solicitor can tell you what is at stake so you can decide.
Do you help with consumer debts?
No. Leaflegal only introduces businesses that are owed money by other businesses in England and Wales. Late payment law does not apply to consumer contracts, and consumer debt collection is outside this service.
How is statutory interest calculated?
It is simple interest at 8% a year above the Bank of England base rate, calculated on the unpaid amount for each day the payment is late. The base rate used is fixed for six month periods. Your solicitor will calculate the total and the daily rate that continues until payment.
Which base rate is used?
For debts that become late between January and June, the base rate in force on the previous 31 December is used. For debts that become late between July and December, the rate on 30 June is used. That rate then applies to the debt even if the base rate changes later.
When does interest start to run?
If your contract sets a payment date, interest usually starts the day after that date. If no date was agreed, the law generally allows 30 days after the later of delivery and the customer receiving the invoice. Your solicitor will check the start date for each invoice.
Is it simple or compound interest?
Statutory interest is simple interest. It is calculated on the original unpaid amount and is not added to the debt and then charged interest itself. A contract can provide for a different arrangement, which your solicitor can explain.
Can I claim interest after the invoice has been paid?
Usually, yes. The right to statutory interest and compensation arises when the payment becomes late, so it can often be claimed even after the invoice itself has been paid late. Whether it is worth pursuing is a commercial decision for your business.
Can I claim both contractual and statutory interest?
No. If your contract provides a substantial remedy for late payment, that remedy usually applies instead of statutory interest. If your contractual term is not a substantial remedy, it may be void and statutory interest may apply instead. Your solicitor will check your terms.
What if the customer paid part of the invoice?
Interest is usually calculated on whatever remains unpaid from time to time. Interest on the part paid stops on the date it was paid, while interest on the balance continues. A clear account history showing each payment makes the calculation straightforward. Your solicitor can prepare it for you.
What fixed compensation can I claim?
Late payment law sets a fixed sum for each late debt, depending on its size. The bands have been £40 for debts under £1,000, £70 for debts of £1,000 up to £10,000 and £100 for debts of £10,000 or more. Check GOV.UK or with your solicitor in case they change.
Is compensation per invoice or per customer?
It is usually per debt, which normally means each separate invoice. If a customer has paid several invoices late, a fixed sum can often be claimed for each one. Your solicitor will look at how the invoices were raised and what the contract says.
What are reasonable recovery costs?
If the actual cost of recovering a debt is more than the fixed sum, the creditor may be able to claim reasonable costs of recovery as well, less the fixed sum already claimed. This can include some external costs of chasing. Whether costs are reasonable depends on the circumstances.
Do I need to prove what chasing cost me?
Not for the fixed sum. It is set by law and does not depend on the actual cost of chasing. If you want to claim reasonable recovery costs above the fixed sum, you will need to show what those costs were and why they were reasonable.
Can I claim compensation if my contract sets its own interest rate?
It depends. Fixed compensation is tied to statutory interest, so if your contract replaces statutory interest with its own substantial remedy, the position may be different. Your solicitor will look at your terms and explain what can be claimed.
Can I claim compensation on a small invoice?
Yes. The fixed sum applies to qualifying late debts of any size, with the smallest band covering debts under £1,000. On a small invoice the compensation can be a noticeable proportion of the amount owed, which is one reason it can help make chasing smaller debts worthwhile. Your solicitor will confirm what applies.
Can my customer's terms exclude late payment interest?
Terms cannot simply exclude statutory interest. A term that tries to exclude or limit it without providing a substantial remedy for late payment can be void. Whether a remedy is substantial depends on factors such as the rate and the circumstances. Your solicitor will review whichever terms apply.
Whose terms apply, mine or the customer's?
It depends on how the contract was formed. Where both sides send their own terms, it can be unclear which set applies, and the answer often depends on the order of documents and what happened next. Your solicitor will look at the paperwork and explain which terms are likely to govern the contract.
Are long payment terms allowed?
Businesses can generally agree their own payment periods, but between businesses a period of more than 60 days is only allowed if it is not grossly unfair to the supplier. Public authorities are generally expected to pay within 30 days. Your solicitor can explain how these rules apply.
Should my terms mention late payment interest?
It is not required, because statutory interest applies anyway unless replaced. However, many businesses state their rights clearly in their terms or on invoices so that customers know what to expect. A solicitor can review your terms and suggest clear wording.
What if there is no written contract?
Late payment law still applies to contracts made orally or through emails and orders, as long as both parties are acting in the course of business. Without agreed payment terms, the default payment period set by the law will usually apply.
How do I claim statutory interest?
Usually by adding it to the amount demanded, either in a letter to the customer or in a court claim. The claim should show how the interest and compensation have been calculated. Many solicitors include a schedule listing each invoice, its due date and the amounts claimed.
Can I add interest in a court claim?
Yes. Statutory interest and compensation can be included in a claim, with the basis set out in the particulars of claim. You cannot usually claim statutory interest and the court's discretionary interest for the same period. Your solicitor will choose the right basis.
Can the court reduce the interest?
In some situations, yes. The court can reduce or remove statutory interest where the creditor's own conduct makes it fair to do so. Keeping accurate invoices and dealing fairly with the customer helps protect your position.
How long do I have to claim?
The usual time limit for claiming money due under a contract or under the law is six years. It is easier to claim while records are clear and the debt is recent. If you are thinking about claiming interest on older invoices, speak to a solicitor promptly.
Can I claim interest on a whole history of late payments?
Often, yes, within the usual six year time limit. A customer that has paid many invoices late over several years may owe interest and compensation on each of them, even if all the invoices are now paid. Gathering the dates for every invoice can take time. Your solicitor can help assemble the history and work out the total.
Will claiming interest make the customer dispute the debt?
Sometimes adding interest prompts a customer to look harder for reasons not to pay, and sometimes it prompts quicker payment. It depends on the customer. Your solicitor can talk through the likely reaction and whether to include interest in the first letter or hold it back as part of a negotiation.

No. Leaflegal is free for businesses to use. The firm we introduce you to pays us a fee for the introduction, which does not change what you pay them.
