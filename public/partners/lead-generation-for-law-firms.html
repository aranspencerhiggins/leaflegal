Leaflegal generates client enquiries for solicitors and law firms in England and Wales. We run the campaigns, ask the qualifying questions and send each enquiry to one firm only, while it is fresh.

Why law firms buy leads

Most firms win private clients through referrals, reputation and their own website. Those channels are valuable but hard to scale. Paid search for legal terms is expensive and needs constant management, directories send the same enquiry to several firms, and social media campaigns need creative, testing and a way to qualify the people who respond.

Lead generation hands that work to a specialist. You agree the practice areas, matter types and locations you want, and pay for each enquiry that matches. Your team spends its time on calls and instructions, not on running adverts.

How Leaflegal generates enquiries

Our campaigns run on social media, search and content, aimed at the moments people need a solicitor, like a house move, a separation, a visa deadline, a court date, an unpaid invoice, a licence review or a shortening lease.

Each person completes a short enquiry about their matter. We ask what your intake team would ask, covering the type of matter, how urgent it is, where they are and whether they are looking for paid help. People we can’t help are told politely and never become a lead.

  • Practice area and matter type
  • Urgency and any deadline or hearing date
  • Location, matched to the areas you cover
  • Paid legal help confirmed, not legal aid
  • Verified UK mobile and email
  • Recorded consent to be contacted

What makes a good legal lead

A good lead is a person with a real matter, who expects your call, in an area you handle. Exclusivity matters too, because an enquiry sent to five firms turns into a race on price. Every Leaflegal lead goes to one firm only.

Practice areas

We generate enquiries in seven areas where paying for introductions is permitted. They are conveyancing, family law, immigration (introductions to regulated advisers only), debt recovery for businesses, licensing, lease extensions and trademarks. We don’t generate personal injury, clinical negligence or claims management work.

What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

Lead generation for law firms means someone else does the work of finding people who need a solicitor, asking them the right questions and passing their details to your firm with their permission. You spend your time on the conversation and the instruction. The lead generator spends its time on adverts, forms, filtering and checks.

Leaflegal does this for firms in England and Wales across seven practice areas. We run campaigns aimed at people at the moment they need legal help, such as when an offer on a house is accepted, a relationship ends, a court letter arrives or a lease starts to get short. Each person answers a short set of questions about their matter. If they fit what your firm does, we send the enquiry to you and to no other firm.

Most firms already win work through referrals, reputation and their own website. Those channels matter and they are not going away. The trouble is that they are hard to grow on demand. A firm that wants ten more family finance matters next month cannot simply ask its referrers to send more. Lead generation gives you a source of new enquiries you can turn up, turn down or pause, alongside everything else you already do.

What you are actually buying

You are not buying clicks, impressions or a share of an advertising budget. You are buying finished enquiries. Each one is a named person or business with a real matter, a verified UK mobile number and email address, a description in their own words and a record of their consent to be contacted by a firm. What happens next depends on your intake team and the service you offer.

What it is not

Lead generation is not a guarantee of instructions. Some people are comparing options, some are not ready yet and some decide not to go ahead. A good lead generator is honest about that. What it can do is put the right kind of enquiry in front of you, quickly, so that your firm has a fair chance of winning the work on the strength of its service.

Leaflegal is not a law firm and does not give legal advice to the people who enquire. We tell every client that we are an introduction service and that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to.

Every firm that wants more private clients ends up choosing between a handful of channels. Each has a place. The useful question is how much time, money and management each one takes before it produces a conversation with a real client.

Your own website and search

Ranking well for legal searches is valuable because the enquiries feel like they came to you directly. It is also slow and competitive. Firms in larger towns often compete with national brands, comparison sites and directories for the same searches. Content has to be written, updated and kept accurate as the law changes. Results build over months and years rather than weeks.

Paid search

Paid search can produce enquiries quickly, but legal keywords are some of the most expensive to bid on. Someone has to choose keywords, write adverts, build landing pages, filter out people looking for free help and watch the budget every week. Without that management, spend can drift towards searches that never turn into instructions.

Directories and comparison sites

Directories are easy to join and can bring some enquiries. Many send the same enquiry to several firms at once, which turns the first call into a race and the conversation into a discussion about price.

Social media

Social campaigns reach people who are not yet searching, which can be powerful for matters like separation or a short lease. They need creative work, testing and a way to sort serious enquiries from casual ones. Running them well is a job in itself.

Buying exclusive leads

Buying leads moves the campaign work to a specialist. You agree what you want and pay for each matching enquiry. The cost of each enquiry is known in advance and you control the volume. It works best alongside your own channels rather than instead of them, and it suits firms that can call new enquiries back quickly.

Putting channels together

Many firms find the most reliable mix is their own website and reputation for steady, low cost enquiries, with bought leads added for volume they can plan around. Bought leads also help when a firm wants to test demand for a new practice area or a new office location before committing to a long term marketing budget. Because each lead has a known cost, it is straightforward to compare the work it brings in with what you spend.

We try to send each firm the kind of enquiry its own intake team would be pleased to receive. That shapes how we run campaigns, what we ask people and what we filter out before anything reaches you.

Campaigns built around real moments

Our adverts and content are aimed at the points in life when people realise they need a solicitor. A house purchase with a deadline. A partner moving out. A notice of intended prosecution. An invoice ninety days overdue. A licence review letter. A visa that is about to expire. People in these moments are looking for someone to help, not for general reading.

Questions your intake team would ask

Each person completes a short enquiry. We ask what kind of matter it is, how urgent it is, whether there is a deadline or hearing date, where they are and whether they want paid legal help. They describe the situation in their own words. Anyone we cannot help, such as someone who only wants legal aid, is told politely and pointed elsewhere. They never become a lead.

Checks before delivery

The UK mobile number and email address are checked when the enquiry is submitted. The client ticks one box to confirm they understand Leaflegal is an introduction service and not a law firm, and a separate box to agree that a firm can contact them about their matter. We record the exact wording and the time and send that record with the lead.

One firm, straight away

Once an enquiry passes our checks, we match it to one firm whose practice areas, matter types and locations it fits. It is sent by email or into your CRM as soon as it is checked. We never send the same enquiry to several firms and we never resell a lead once it has been delivered.

No platform to learn

There is no online portal or login. Leads arrive where your team already works. Changes to your criteria, volume or pause status are made by talking to us.

If something changes at your end, such as a fee earner joining or leaving, a new office opening or a matter type you no longer want, tell us and we will update your matching.

We generate enquiries in seven areas where paying an introducer is permitted. Within each one you choose the matter types you want and leave out the rest.

  • Conveyancing, covering purchases, sales, remortgages, transfers of equity and leasehold purchases
  • Family law, covering divorce, finances on separation, child arrangements, cohabitation disputes and nuptial agreements
  • Immigration, introductions only, to regulated firms and registered advisers
  • Debt recovery for businesses, covering unpaid invoices and commercial debts owed by other businesses
  • Licensing, covering premises licences, reviews, personal licences and taxi licences
  • Lease extensions, covering statutory and informal extensions and collective enfranchisement

What we leave out

We do not generate personal injury or clinical negligence enquiries, because paying referral fees for that work is prohibited under section 56 of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012. We also do not handle housing disrepair or financial mis-selling claims, which fall under regulated claims management. People looking only for legal aid or free advice are filtered out before submission.

Where clients are

Enquiries in five of the seven areas are for matters in England and Wales. Trademark and immigration introductions can come from anywhere in the UK, because one UK trademark register and one set of immigration rules apply across the UK. You tell us the towns, counties or regions you cover, or that you work nationally.

Each practice area has its own page explaining the fields we collect and the firms it suits. Start with conveyancing leads or family law leads.

Two firms can buy the same kind of lead and get very different results. The difference is almost always in what happens after the lead arrives. These habits are common to the firms that get the most from lead generation.

Call within minutes

Someone who has just described their legal problem is expecting a call and often feels anxious. The longer they wait, the more likely they are to try another route or lose momentum. Send leads to a shared inbox or CRM with notifications on, and agree who picks them up during lunch, late afternoon and holidays.

Open with what they told us

Every lead includes the matter type, any deadline and a description in the client's words. Referring to these straight away shows you have listened and saves them repeating a difficult story.

Explain the introduction and your fees

Tell the client how you came to be calling. Be clear about how your fees work and what the first step would cost. Private clients want to know where they stand before they commit.

Follow up properly

Not everyone answers the first call or instructs on the day. A voicemail, an email and a text, followed by another call the next day, often turns silence into a conversation.

Tell us what works

Let us know which enquiries became instructions and which did not. We use that to adjust your matter types and locations so that more of what you receive is the work you want.

Measure by practice area

Keep a simple record of each lead, when it was called, whether you spoke to the client and whether they instructed. Look at it by practice area and matter type rather than as one overall figure. A family team and a conveyancing team will see very different patterns, and the useful changes usually show up at that level. Sharing the picture with us means we can shift your criteria towards the enquiries that work best for you.

Questions and answers

How is lead generation priced for law firms?
Pay per lead, at a price agreed with you before you start. Pricing depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose.
Is there a minimum?
Yes, the minimum is 10 leads per month. Above that, you set your monthly volume.
Are the leads shared with other firms?
No. Each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold.
Which areas of law do you cover?
Conveyancing, family law, immigration, debt recovery, licensing, lease extensions and trademarks.
What does a lead generation company do for a law firm?
It finds people who need legal help, asks them the questions your intake team would ask and passes their details to your firm with their consent. Leaflegal runs the campaigns, the enquiry forms and the checks, then sends each matching enquiry to one firm. Your team picks up from the first call onwards. You do not have to manage adverts, landing pages or budgets yourself.
How is this different from running my own adverts?
When you run your own adverts you pay for clicks and impressions whether or not they produce a real enquiry, and someone has to manage the campaigns every week. With Leaflegal you pay for each enquiry that matches your agreed criteria. The campaign work, filtering and checks happen before the enquiry reaches you. Many firms do both, and that works well.
Who generates the enquiries?
Leaflegal does. People find us through our own campaigns on social media, search and content, and complete our own enquiry forms. We do not buy data from third parties, scrape directories or pass on cold contacts. Every lead is someone who asked to be contacted about their own matter and agreed to be introduced to a firm.
Is Leaflegal a law firm?
No. Leaflegal is an introduction service run by Vertical Brands Ltd. We do not give legal advice and we are not regulated by the SRA. We make this clear to every client before they submit an enquiry, and they tick a box to confirm they understand it. All advice comes from the firm we introduce them to.
Will lead generation replace my referrals?
It is not designed to. Referrals and reputation remain the backbone of most private client practices. Lead generation adds a source of new enquiries you can control, which is useful when you want to grow a team, fill quiet periods or build up a practice area that your existing referrers do not feed. Most partner firms use it alongside their other channels.
How quickly will I start receiving enquiries?
Once your criteria, pricing and delivery are agreed and your partner agreement is signed, leads start as soon as matching enquiries come in. How many arrive and how fast depends on your practice areas, matter types and locations. Wider coverage usually produces more matches than a narrow area around one office. We will give you an honest view before you start.
Why do law firms buy leads at all?
Mostly because the alternatives take time or management they do not have. Building search rankings is slow, paid search needs constant attention and directories often share enquiries between several firms. Buying exclusive leads gives a predictable cost per enquiry and a volume you control, without having to employ someone to run campaigns. It suits firms that want growth they can plan around.
Where do you advertise?
Our campaigns run on social media, search and content. They are aimed at the moments people need a solicitor, such as a house move, a separation, a visa deadline, an unpaid invoice, a licence review or a shortening lease. We adjust campaigns over time based on which enquiries firms tell us turn into instructions.
Do you mention my firm in your adverts?
No. Our campaigns are run under the Leaflegal name. The client enquires through Leaflegal and agrees to be introduced to a firm. When you call, you introduce your firm and explain that they made an enquiry through Leaflegal. This keeps the introduction clear and means you do not need to approve advertising copy.
What questions do clients answer?
They tell us the practice area and type of matter, how urgent it is, any deadline or hearing date, where they are and whether they want paid help. They describe the matter in their own words and give their name, UK mobile and email. Some areas include extra fields, such as rough property value for conveyancing or years left on a lease.
Do clients know they will be contacted by a firm?
Yes. Before submitting, every client ticks a separate box agreeing that their details can be shared with a firm we match them with, and that the firm can contact them by phone, email or text about their matter. We record the exact wording they saw and the time, and send that record with the lead.
What happens to people you cannot help?
Our enquiry flow filters them out before submission. Someone who only wants legal aid, has a personal injury claim or has a matter outside the areas we cover is told politely that we cannot help and, where possible, pointed in the right direction. They never become a lead and you never have to deal with them.
Can I suggest changes to the questions?
Tell us what your intake team needs to know. We keep the form short so that more people finish it, but feedback from firms helps us decide which questions matter most. We will not add questions that would mean collecting more personal or sensitive information than a firm needs to make first contact.
Which firms does this suit?
It suits firms that handle privately paid work in at least one of our seven areas and can call new enquiries back quickly. High street practices, specialist family firms, conveyancing teams, licensing and commercial teams and immigration practices all work with us. You must be authorised for the work you take on, including any reserved legal activities.
Do you work with small firms and sole practitioners?
Yes, as long as you are regulated for the work and can respond to enquiries promptly. Smaller firms often benefit from setting a modest monthly cap and a clear plan for who calls new leads. The minimum is 10 leads a month, which gives you enough enquiries to judge whether the service suits your practice.
Can I choose more than one practice area?
Yes. Many firms take enquiries in two or three areas, such as conveyancing and lease extensions, or family law and conveyancing. You can set different matter types and locations for each area. Pricing is agreed separately for each, because the value of an enquiry varies between areas. Each area is set up on its own terms.
Can I take only certain matter types?
Yes. Within each area you tell us the matters you want and those you do not. A family firm might want contested finances and children work but not simple divorce. A conveyancing team might want purchases and sales but not remortgages. We only send enquiries that fit what you have chosen.
Do you work with immigration advisers who are not solicitors?
Yes, if they are registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level for the work. We also work with SRA and CILEx regulated firms. Leaflegal never gives immigration advice. Our immigration form asks only for the type of matter, urgency and contact details, and everything about the client's options comes from you.
Can a firm outside England and Wales join?
Our enquiries in five areas are for matters in England and Wales, so firms need to be able to act on those. Trademark and immigration enquiries can come from anywhere in the UK. If you are unsure whether your firm fits, mention it when you apply and we will talk it through on your call.
How is lead generation for law firms priced?
You pay per lead, at a price agreed with your firm before you start. The price depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose and the volume you want. We do not publish a price list because the range between areas is wide. We will put a proposal together after your call.
Is there a minimum commitment?
The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that you set your own monthly cap. You can change your volume or pause when your team is at capacity or a fee earner is away. Your partner agreement sets out the detail, including billing and how invalid leads are handled. You will see it all before you sign.
Do I pay for advertising on top?
No. You pay for leads, not for our campaigns. We take on the cost and risk of advertising and only charge for enquiries we deliver that match your criteria. Any costs are explained in your proposal before you commit, so you know what you are agreeing to. Advertising is our cost, not yours.
What if a lead is not genuine?
Your partner agreement defines an invalid lead, such as one with wrong contact details or one outside your agreed criteria, and sets a review period for telling us. Where a lead is genuinely invalid, we deal with it as the agreement sets out. A lead stays valid if the client simply chooses another firm or decides not to proceed.
Can I stop at any time?
You can pause matching whenever you need to. The terms for ending the arrangement are set out in your partner agreement, which you will see and agree before your first lead. We would rather you stayed because the leads are worth it than because a contract says so. Ask us if anything is unclear.
Do clients pay Leaflegal anything?
No. Clients do not pay Leaflegal to make an enquiry or to be introduced. The firm pays for the introduction, and the client agrees fees directly with the firm. We tell clients that we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, so the arrangement is clear from the start.
Do I need to tell clients about the referral?
Yes. The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement you have with an introducer. Our consent wording tells every client that Leaflegal is paid by the firms it introduces. We send you that wording with each lead so your own client care information can match it.
Why don't you offer personal injury leads?
Paying referral fees for personal injury and clinical negligence work is prohibited under section 56 of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012. We do not generate those enquiries in any form. We also stay out of financial claims and housing disrepair, which are regulated claims management activities.
Who is responsible for the client's data?
Vertical Brands Ltd, trading as Leaflegal, is the controller for information collected through our forms. Once a lead is delivered, your firm becomes the controller of its copy and is responsible for handling it under UK data protection law and your own privacy notice. You should use it to contact the client about their enquiry.
Do I still need to run conflict checks?
Yes. Run your normal conflict and identity checks before taking on any matter. If you find a conflict, do not act, and let us know. We will treat the lead in the way your partner agreement sets out. Leaflegal does not carry out conflict checks on your behalf. The same goes for identity checks.
Do you give clients legal advice before passing them on?
No. Leaflegal does not give legal advice to anyone. Our forms ask factual questions about the matter, its urgency and the client's location, and the client is told clearly that we are an introduction service. Any view on the merits, options or likely costs of the matter comes from your firm after you make contact.

More questions people ask

What is pay per lead for law firms?
It is a way of paying for marketing where the firm pays for each enquiry delivered to it, rather than for clicks, impressions or campaign management. With Leaflegal, each lead is an exclusive enquiry that matches your agreed practice areas, matter types and locations, at a price agreed before you start.
When do I pay for a lead?
You pay for leads delivered to your firm that match your agreed criteria. How and when you are billed is set out in your partner agreement, which you see and agree before your first lead. There is no charge for enquiries that do not reach you. Keep your own record of leads received so you can check bills easily.
Do I pay for leads that do not convert?
Yes, as long as they are valid. A lead stays valid if the client chooses another firm, decides not to proceed or does not accept your fees. Pay per lead covers the cost of producing a qualified, exclusive enquiry. It is not a guarantee of instructions, and no honest lead provider can offer one.
Is pay per lead the same as pay per instruction?
No. Pay per lead means paying for each qualified enquiry, whether or not it becomes an instruction. Leaflegal charges per lead. Firms should take their own view on how any payment arrangement with an introducer sits with their regulator's rules. Your price per lead is agreed in advance and does not depend on whether the client instructs you or on the fees you earn from the matter.
Do I need to manage any campaigns?
No. Leaflegal runs the campaigns, the enquiry forms and the checks. You agree your criteria and price, then receive leads as they come in. You do not need to choose keywords, write adverts or watch advertising budgets. Your part is calling leads quickly and telling us which ones turn into instructions, so we can refine your criteria over time.
Is there a login or dashboard?
No. There is no online platform. Leads arrive by email or in your CRM, and changes to your criteria, cap or pause status are made by contacting our team. Many firms prefer it, because there is nothing new for their team to learn. Our team handles changes for you, usually by email or phone.
Can I use pay per lead alongside my own marketing?
Yes, and most firms do. Your website, reputation and referrers usually bring in some work on their own. Pay per lead adds a source of enquiries you can turn up or down as your capacity changes. Because each lead has a known cost, it is easy to compare with what your other channels cost you.
