Why current points matter

Current points help you see quickly whether a matter could lead to a totting up disqualification. Under the totting up rules, a driver who reaches 12 or more points within three years will usually face a disqualification unless the court accepts that exceptional hardship would result. A driver with nine points facing a speeding charge is in a very different position from one with a clean licence, and the lead lets you see that before you call.

Points also matter for newer drivers. A driver who reaches six or more points within two years of passing their test will usually have their licence revoked under the new driver rules. A new driver facing even a modest offence may therefore be very anxious, and the lead helps you spot that.

Whether a ban is likely

We ask the client whether they think a ban is likely. This is the client's own view, not an assessment by us, and it may not be accurate. It is still useful, because it tells you what the client is worried about and what they will want to talk about first.

What clients ask

What does careless driving actually mean?
It means driving that falls below the standard expected of a competent and careful driver. The court does not need to find that you were reckless or that you knew you were driving badly. It looks at what a careful driver would have done in the same situation and compares that with what you did. It is a summary offence, which means it is dealt with in the magistrates' court.
Is inconsiderate driving the same offence?
It is part of the same section of the law and carries the same penalties. Inconsiderate driving is where your driving inconvenienced other people, such as pedestrians, cyclists or other drivers. Typical examples include driving through a puddle and soaking people at a bus stop, or tailgating to force another driver to move over. The prosecution has to show that someone was actually inconvenienced.
Can I be charged if nobody was hurt?
Yes. Careless driving does not require anyone to be injured or any damage to be caused. A police officer who sees poor driving can report the driver even where nothing happened as a result. That said, the harm caused or risked is relevant to how serious the court considers the offence to be, which affects the penalty.
The other driver was also at fault. Does that help me?
It may be relevant. The question for the court is whether your driving fell below the standard, not whether someone else was more to blame. However, the other driver's actions can explain why an incident happened and may show that your driving was reasonable in the circumstances. A solicitor will look at the evidence of both drivers' actions.
What if I was charged with dangerous driving instead?
Dangerous driving is a more serious offence with a compulsory ban. If you have been charged with it, the court can convict you of careless driving instead if it decides the driving was poor but not far below the standard. A solicitor dealing with a dangerous driving charge will usually consider whether this is a realistic outcome.
How long do the police have to bring a case?
There are time limits for starting a prosecution for careless driving, and there are rules about warning the driver within 14 days unless an accident happened that the driver knew about. Whether these rules were followed depends on the facts. If you think the case has been brought late or you were never warned, ask a solicitor to check.
What is a notice of intended prosecution?
It is a formal warning that the police are considering prosecuting a driver for certain offences, including careless driving. It is usually sent to the registered keeper within 14 days of the incident, or given verbally at the scene. Where there was an accident that the driver was aware of, no notice is usually needed. The notice is not a charge, and receiving one does not mean a case will definitely follow.
Can the police offer a course instead of court?
Sometimes. For less serious careless driving, the police may offer a driver awareness course or a fixed penalty with points. These are offered at the discretion of the police and depend on the circumstances, your history and local policy. There is no right to be offered one. If you are offered a course, the paperwork will explain the conditions and the deadline for accepting it.
Do I have to go to court?
For careless driving you will usually be asked to attend the first hearing. If you are pleading not guilty, you will need to attend the trial. Your solicitor can tell you whether you need to be there for other hearings. If the court decides to disqualify you, it may want you present. Not attending when you are required to can lead to a warrant.
What happens at the first hearing?
You confirm your name and details and are asked to enter a plea. If you plead guilty, the court will hear the facts from the prosecution and any mitigation, then decide the sentence. If you plead not guilty, the court will fix a trial date and decide what evidence and witnesses are needed. A solicitor can attend with you or on your behalf for parts of the process.
Who decides the case?
Careless driving is decided in the magistrates' court. The case will be heard either by a bench of usually three lay magistrates, who are trained volunteers, or by a district judge, who is a legally qualified judge sitting alone. They are advised on the law by a legal adviser. There is no jury.
What evidence will the prosecution use?
It depends on the case. Common evidence includes statements from the other driver and witnesses, police officer accounts, photographs, dashcam or CCTV footage, collision investigation reports and anything you said in interview or in writing. The prosecution should provide initial details of its case before the first hearing, and your solicitor will review it.
Can I give my side of the story?
Yes. If you plead not guilty, you can give evidence at your trial and be questioned by the prosecution. You can also call your own witnesses. Your solicitor will advise you on whether giving evidence is likely to help and will prepare you for what to expect.
Can I appeal if I am convicted?
You can appeal to the Crown Court against your conviction, your sentence or both. The time limit for lodging an appeal is short, usually 21 days from sentence. In some cases you can also ask the magistrates to reopen the case. A solicitor can advise on whether an appeal has reasonable prospects.
What is mitigation?
Mitigation is information put before the court to explain the circumstances of the offence and your personal situation, with the aim of reducing the sentence. It might cover your previous driving record, why the incident happened, any remorse you have shown, and how a ban or high fine would affect you and others. A solicitor knows what courts tend to find relevant and how to present it clearly.
How many points will I get?
If you are not disqualified, the court must give between 3 and 9 points. The number depends on how serious the court considers the driving to be, following sentencing guidelines. Minor lapses tend to be at the lower end, while more prolonged or risky driving attracts more points. A solicitor can explain where your case might fall.
Could I be banned from driving?
Yes. Careless driving does not carry a compulsory ban, but the court can disqualify you if it thinks the driving was serious enough. You can also face a ban if the points you receive take you to 12 or more within three years. A solicitor can tell you how likely a ban is and what can be argued.
I passed my test recently. What happens?
If you are within two years of passing your test and your total reaches six or more points, the DVLA will revoke your licence. You would have to go back to a provisional licence and pass the theory and practical tests again. Points from before you passed can count too. This makes the number of points especially important for new drivers.
What is exceptional hardship?
If the new points would take you to 12 or more, the court must normally disqualify you for at least six months. You can ask the court not to do this because it would cause exceptional hardship. Ordinary inconvenience such as losing a job is often not enough on its own. The court looks closely at the effect on other people, such as employees, family members or people you care for.
How long will the points stay on my licence?
Points for careless driving usually stay on your driving record for four years from the date of the offence. They count towards a totting up ban for three years. Insurers often ask about convictions for several years, so the effect on your premiums can last a while.
Will I have to retake my test?
The court can order you to take an extended driving test if it disqualifies you, although this is not automatic for careless driving. New drivers whose licence is revoked under the new driver rules must also retake the standard tests. A solicitor can explain whether a retest is a realistic possibility in your case.
Can I get legal aid?
Advice at the police station is free for everyone. For court, legal aid depends on a means test and on whether the interests of justice require it. Careless driving rarely carries a risk of prison, so legal aid is often refused. Most people instructing a solicitor for careless driving pay privately. The firm can explain what funding might be available to you.
How do solicitors charge for careless driving cases?
Many firms offer fixed fees for separate stages, such as advising before plea, representing you on a guilty plea, preparing an exceptional hardship application or conducting a trial. Trials cost more because of the preparation, witness work and time at court. Ask for a written breakdown of what is included before you agree to anything.
Will I have to pay prosecution costs?
If you are convicted, the court will usually order you to contribute to the prosecution's costs and to pay a victim surcharge as well as any fine. The amounts vary from case to case. If you are acquitted, you will not pay prosecution costs, and you may be able to claim back some of your own legal costs.
Does my motor insurance cover legal costs?
Some motor policies include legal expenses cover that may help with defending a motoring prosecution, though many exclude it or limit it. Check your policy documents and any separate legal expenses policy. If you have cover, the insurer may have its own panel of firms, so read the terms carefully.
