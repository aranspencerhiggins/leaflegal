What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

Pay per lead is a simple idea. You pay for each enquiry that is delivered to your firm and matches the criteria you agreed. You do not pay for clicks on adverts, for impressions, for a share of a media budget or for someone's time managing campaigns. If no matching enquiry comes in, there is nothing to pay for.

The model suits law firms well because legal work is high value and enquiries are relatively rare. A firm might need only a handful of new instructions a month in a practice area to make a real difference to its income. Paying for those enquiries directly, rather than for the thousands of clicks and impressions it can take to produce them, puts the focus on the thing that actually matters to the firm.

With Leaflegal, the price of each lead is agreed with your firm before you start. It depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose and the volume you want. Once it is agreed, you know exactly what each lead costs, which makes it straightforward to plan a budget and to measure what you get back.

Who carries the risk

In most forms of legal marketing, the firm carries the risk that the money spent will not produce enquiries. A month of paid search can bring in a stream of people looking for free advice. A directory listing can produce nothing at all. With pay per lead, Leaflegal carries the cost and risk of running the campaigns. You pay only for enquiries that reach you and fit what you asked for.

What counts as a lead

A lead is an enquiry from a named person or business, in a practice area, matter type and location you have chosen, with a verified UK mobile number and email address, confirmation they want paid help and a record of their consent to be contacted by a firm. It is sent to your firm only and never resold.

What you are not paying for

You are not paying for an instruction. Some clients will compare firms, some will decide not to proceed and some will not accept your fees. A lead remains valid if the client chooses another firm or does not go ahead. What you are paying for is a fair chance to win the work, delivered quickly and exclusively.

No platform fee

There is no software to subscribe to and no login. Leads arrive by email or in your CRM. Any costs beyond the price per lead are explained in your proposal before you commit, so nothing comes as a surprise.

Law firms can pay for marketing in several ways. Each spreads cost and risk differently. It helps to understand them side by side.

Pay per click

With paid search and many social campaigns, you pay each time someone clicks on an advert. Legal keywords are among the most expensive to bid on, and many clicks never become enquiries. Someone has to choose keywords, write adverts, build landing pages, filter out unsuitable searches and watch spend closely. The firm carries all the risk that clicks do not turn into enquiries.

Retainers and agency fees

Many marketing agencies charge a monthly fee for managing campaigns, on top of the advertising spend itself. A good agency can be worth it, but the firm pays whether or not the campaigns work in a given month, and it can take time to see whether they do.

Directory subscriptions

Directories usually charge a fixed fee for a listing or a premium position. Enquiries may come, or they may not, and they are often shared between several firms on the same page.

Shared lead services

Some lead services charge per lead but send each enquiry to several firms. The price looks lower, but each firm is buying a share of a chance rather than the whole of one.

Exclusive pay per lead

With Leaflegal, you pay a known price for each exclusive enquiry that matches your criteria. You do not manage campaigns or pay for clicks. The trade off is that the price per lead is higher than the cost of a single click, because a lead is a finished enquiry rather than a visit to a web page.

There is also a hidden cost in every model that needs managing. Someone at the firm has to brief an agency, check reports, approve adverts or chase directory listings. With pay per lead, that time is mostly replaced by a short conversation about your criteria and the occasional update when your needs change.

Many firms use pay per lead alongside their own website, referrals and sometimes their own paid search. The value of pay per lead is that it adds volume you can predict and control.

We agree a price with each firm rather than publishing a list, because the value of a legal lead varies a great deal. Three things matter most.

Practice area and matter type

Some matters are worth more to a firm than others. A contested family finances enquiry usually leads to more work, over a longer period, than a simple remortgage. A licence review hearing for a busy venue is a different proposition from a personal licence application. Matters that are harder to generate, or that tend to bring in higher fees, are priced accordingly.

Location

A firm that covers the whole of England and Wales will match more enquiries than a firm that covers one town. Narrow coverage around a single office can be very valuable to the firm, but it produces fewer matches and can take more campaign work to fill. Coverage is reflected in the price.

Volume

The number of leads you want each month affects how we plan campaigns for your criteria. Volume is part of the conversation we have when putting your proposal together.

What does not affect the price

Your price does not change from day to day because another firm is willing to pay more. There is no auction and no bidding. Once your price is agreed, it applies to every lead that matches your criteria until you and we agree something different.

Different prices for different areas

If you take leads in more than one practice area, each area has its own price. A firm taking conveyancing and family leads will have a price for each, reflecting the difference in the work involved.

Talking it through

On your call we will ask about the work you want, where you want it and how many enquiries your team can handle. We will explain how those choices affect the price and the likely volume, and suggest where small changes might make a big difference. For example, adding a neighbouring county or a related matter type can sometimes make a narrow set of criteria far more workable. Your proposal sets out the result in writing.

Pay per lead only works well if you can control how many leads you receive. Too few and you cannot judge the results. Too many and your team cannot call them back quickly, which wastes both the leads and the money spent on them.

Minimum volume

The minimum is 10 leads a month. That is enough for a firm to see the quality of the enquiries and how they fit with its intake process. Below that, results can swing a great deal from month to month for reasons that have nothing to do with lead quality.

Your monthly cap

Above the minimum, you set a cap. Once you reach it, we stop matching to your firm until the next month. The cap is also your maximum spend for the month, because you only pay for leads delivered within it.

Changing your cap

You can raise or lower your cap whenever you need to. Firms often lower it when a fee earner is on leave and raise it when a new member of the team joins.

Pausing

If your team is at capacity, you can pause altogether. While you are paused, you receive no leads and pay nothing. When you are ready, tell us and we will start matching to you again.

Honest volume expectations

We cannot guarantee exact volumes, because they depend on how many people enquire and how closely they match your criteria. When we agree your criteria, we will give you an honest view of likely volumes. If your criteria are narrow, we will tell you.

Matching volume to your team

The right cap is the number your team can call back within minutes, on a normal working day, without other work suffering. A useful way to set it is to count the fee earners or intake staff who will handle leads, think about how many new matters each can realistically open in a month and work back from there. It is better to start a little low and raise your cap than to start high and leave leads waiting.

Paying per lead raises a fair question. What happens when a lead is not what it should be? Your partner agreement answers that before your first lead is delivered.

What usually counts as invalid

Your agreement defines an invalid lead. Typical examples are a lead with wrong contact details, so that the client cannot be reached on the number or email given, or a lead that falls outside the practice areas, matter types or locations agreed with you.

What does not count as invalid

A lead is still valid if the client chooses another firm, decides not to proceed, cannot afford your fees or does not answer your first call. These are normal parts of working with private client enquiries. Pay per lead means paying for a fair chance to win the work, not for a guaranteed instruction.

The review period

Your agreement sets a review period for raising an invalid lead. Tell us within that time, with a short note of what was wrong. Where a lead is genuinely invalid, we deal with it as your agreement sets out.

Why the rules are written down

Having the rules in writing from the start avoids disagreement later. Your team knows what to report, and we know what we have agreed to. It also helps us spot patterns. If a particular campaign or matter type keeps producing invalid leads, we want to know so we can fix it.

Keeping it practical

Most firms find that invalid leads are a small part of the picture, but it still pays to have a routine. Ask whoever handles new leads to flag anything that looks wrong on the first call, such as a number that does not connect or a matter clearly outside your criteria. Collect those flags in one place and send them to us within the review period. A short, regular note is easier for everyone than a long list at the end of the month.

One of the strengths of pay per lead is that the cost side of the calculation is simple. Each lead has an agreed price. What you need to add is a clear record of what happens to each lead after it arrives.

Track each lead

Keep a simple log with the date each lead arrived, when it was first called, whether you spoke to the client, whether they instructed and the fee the matter brought in. A spreadsheet or a field in your CRM is enough.

Look at it by practice area

Compare results by practice area and matter type rather than as one figure. A family team and a conveyancing team will see different patterns, and the useful decisions usually sit at that level.

Think about the cost per instruction

Divide what you spent on leads in a period by the number of instructions they produced. Compare that with the fees those instructions brought in and with what an instruction costs you through other channels. This gives a much clearer picture than the price per lead alone.

Allow for time

Some matters instruct on the first call. Others take weeks while clients gather documents, talk to family or save for fees. Give each month's leads time to mature before judging them.

Share the results

Tell us which leads converted and which did not. We use that feedback to adjust your criteria so that more of your spend goes on the enquiries that work best for you.

What is pay per lead for law firms?

Do I pay for leads that do not convert?

No. Pay per lead means paying for each qualified enquiry, whether or not it becomes an instruction. Leaflegal charges per lead. Can I use pay per lead alongside my own marketing?
Yes, and most firms do. Your website, reputation and referrers usually bring in some work on their own. Pay per lead adds a source of enquiries you can turn up or down as your capacity changes. Because each lead has a known cost, it is easy to compare with what your other channels cost you.

Pricing each area separately means you are not paying the same for very different kinds of work. We explain how on your call.

You only pay for the leads you receive. If volumes are lower than expected, talk to us. Can I raise an invalid lead after the review period?

More questions people ask

What is a conveyancing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person who is buying, selling or remortgaging a property in England and Wales and has asked to be contacted by a firm about paid conveyancing. They have answered a short set of questions about the transaction, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm. We send that enquiry to you alone, as soon as it has been checked.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We do not send the same enquiry to several firms at once and we never resell a lead after it has been delivered. If you are the firm we match, you are the only firm that receives that person's details from us. The client can still choose to speak to other firms on their own, as anyone can.
How do you check the leads?
Every client confirms they want paid legal help with a property in England and Wales. We verify their UK mobile number and their email address, and we filter out enquiries that fall outside our scope or your criteria. People we cannot help are told politely and pointed elsewhere, so they never become a lead. Each lead then arrives with the client's answers and the consent record.
What details will I get for each enquiry?
You get the client's name, mobile and email, whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, the tenure, whether there is a mortgage, the target timescale and the location. There is also a short description in the client's own words, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the exact consent wording they agreed to.
Can I quote from the lead alone?
Usually you can give a provisional figure on the first call, because the lead covers the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether a mortgage is involved. You will still want to confirm the details with the client and send a written quote. Things like a leasehold management pack, a new build or a gifted deposit may change your figure once you know more.
Do you send leasehold purchases and sales?
Yes, if you want them. Every lead says whether the property is freehold or leasehold, and you can choose to receive leasehold matters, leave them out or ask for them only in certain areas. Firms with an experienced leasehold team sometimes prefer this work, while others choose to focus on freehold transactions. We set your matching to suit you.
Are remortgage leads included?
Only if you ask for them. Remortgages, transfers of equity, purchases, sales and combined sale and purchase matters can each be switched on or off. Some firms take remortgages to keep a steady flow of shorter matters, while others prefer to concentrate on purchases. You can change the mix at any time by letting us know.
Do clients know a firm will call them?
Yes. Every client has agreed to be contacted by a firm about their matter, by phone, email or text, and most are expecting a call soon after they submit. It helps to say early in the call that you are getting in touch because of the enquiry they made through Leaflegal. That puts the client at ease and moves the conversation quickly on to their purchase, sale or remortgage.
