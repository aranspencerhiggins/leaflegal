Individuals and families

Many enquiries come from people who are already in the UK and want help with their next application, from people outside the UK planning to join family, and from people who have received a decision they do not understand. Some have handled earlier applications themselves and now want a professional because the stakes feel higher.

Employers

Business enquiries usually come from owners and HR managers who want to recruit from overseas, keep an existing worker or understand their duties as a sponsor. They often want a fixed price and a clear plan, and they may have more than one worker to discuss.

Urgency

Each lead says how soon the client needs help and whether they know of any deadline or decision date. Some enquiries are planning ahead by several months. Others have a date approaching quickly. That lets you send the most urgent leads to a senior adviser first.

Clients in the UK and clients abroad have different needs from a first call. Someone in the UK may want a face to face meeting, while someone overseas usually expects a video call at a time that suits their time zone. Knowing this from the lead helps you plan the first contact properly.

Funding

We focus on privately funded matters. Every client confirms they are looking for paid help rather than legal aid or free advice. If you also do legal aid work, talk to us about what you want to receive.

Immigration advice is one of the most tightly controlled areas of legal services. It is a criminal offence for an unqualified person to provide immigration advice or services in the UK in the course of a business. Leaflegal is set up around that rule.

Who can receive immigration leads

Immigration enquiries only go to SRA regulated firms, CILEx regulated practices, or advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at a level that covers the work. You are responsible for staying within your authorisation, including the level and categories of work your registration allows. We ask for your regulatory details when you apply and you must tell us if anything changes.

If your authorisation changes, for example because a registration lapses or the level changes, tell us straight away. We will pause or adjust your matching so that no enquiry reaches you that you are not permitted to handle. Keeping this up to date protects your clients, your practice and us.

Leaflegal never advises

Our form, our campaigns and our team do not give immigration advice. We do not comment on eligibility, routes, requirements or a client's prospects. We tell every client this before they submit an enquiry, and we keep that line on every page we publish about immigration.

Why immigration enquiries are different

People looking for immigration help are often under real pressure. A visa may be running out, a letter may have arrived from the Home Office, or a family is trying to plan a life together across borders. Many of them have already read conflicting information online and want to speak to someone qualified who can look at their own situation.

Most of these clients pay privately, and they are choosing someone they will trust with something that matters a great deal to them. Clear fees, a calm first conversation and a fast response tend to be what they remember.

What clients ask

What is a work visa?
It is permission from the Home Office to come to the UK, or stay in the UK, to work. There are several kinds of work visa, each for a different purpose, and they change over time. A regulated adviser can explain which, if any, might fit your situation, based on the rules as they stand today.
Can Leaflegal tell me which visa I need?
No. Leaflegal is not regulated to give immigration advice and does not do so. We do not ask about your immigration history or assess your chances. We introduce you to a firm or adviser who is regulated, and they will look at your situation and explain your options.
Can you find me a job?
No. Leaflegal is not a recruitment agency and cannot help you find work. We introduce you to a regulated immigration adviser who can help with the visa side of things. If you already have a job offer, mention it when you enquire so the adviser has the full picture. Be cautious of anyone who offers to find you a UK job in return for a payment.
I am already in the UK. Can you still help?
Yes. We introduce people both inside and outside the UK to regulated advisers. If you are already here, tell us when your current visa ends when you make your enquiry, so that the adviser knows how much time there is. Having the exact date to hand, and any letters you have received about your current visa, will help the first conversation go smoothly.
I am overseas. Can a UK adviser help me?
Yes. Many regulated advisers in the UK help people who are applying from abroad, working by phone, video and email. Tell us where you are when you enquire. The adviser will explain how they work with clients overseas and how they handle time differences.
Is it worth getting advice if my employer is handling things?
Some employers arrange advice for their workers, and some do not. Even when an employer is involved, some people like to have their own adviser to explain things from their point of view, particularly if family members are involved. A regulated adviser can explain how they would work alongside your employer.
Why do the rules keep changing?
Immigration policy is set by the government and reviewed regularly, often in response to the economy and the jobs market. Changes can affect fees, guidance and the kinds of visa available. This is why advice from a few years ago, or from someone whose situation was different, may not help you now. A regulated adviser works from the current rules.
What happens at the first consultation?
The adviser will ask about your background, your current situation, any job offer and your plans. They will explain how they could help and what it would cost. You do not need a full set of documents for this first conversation. It is also your chance to decide whether you want to work with them.
Is the first consultation free?
It depends on the firm. Some offer a short free call to understand what you need, and then charge for a full consultation. Others charge from the start. The firm should tell you before the consultation whether there is a fee and how much it is.
What should I have ready?
It helps to have your passport, details of any current visa, any job offer letter and any letters from the Home Office to hand. If you have made applications before, have their dates and outcomes in mind. The adviser will tell you what else they need afterwards.
Should I mention a past refusal?
Yes. Advisers can only help properly if they know your full history, including anything that worries you. A past refusal or problem does not stop an adviser from helping, but they need to know about it. What you tell them is confidential under the rules of their regulator.
Can my partner join the consultation?
Usually, yes. Many people like their partner or a family member to join the conversation, especially if the move affects the whole family. Let the adviser know in advance who will be attending so they can plan the meeting. If they are in a different country, a video call can often include everyone.
Can I have the consultation in my own language?
Some firms have advisers who speak other languages, and some can arrange interpreters. Mention your preferred language when you enquire, and ask the firm what it can offer. It is important that you understand everything that is explained to you.
What happens after the consultation?
Most advisers follow up in writing with a summary of what they discussed, what they can do and what it would cost. Read it carefully and ask about anything you do not understand. If you decide to go ahead, they will usually send terms of business for you to agree before work starts.
Will my employer talk to my adviser?
Often, yes. Where an employer is involved, advisers usually need information from them, and it helps if both sides can communicate. Ask your adviser how they normally work with employers, and let your employer know who is helping you. Keeping everyone on the same page reduces the risk of mistakes, such as details that do not match between documents.
My employer has its own adviser. Do I need one too?
Not necessarily. An employer's adviser acts for the employer, and some people are happy with that arrangement. Others want independent advice about their own position, especially for family matters. A regulated adviser can explain the difference and help you decide.
What if my job changes?
Changes to your job, employer or pay can matter for a work visa. If any change is proposed, speak to a regulated adviser before you agree to it. They can explain whether it affects your position and what, if anything, would need to happen. Keep copies of any letters about the change, such as a new contract or a letter confirming a pay rise or new job title.
What if I lose my job?
Losing your job can be worrying, and it can affect your immigration position. The timing can be important. Speak to a regulated adviser as soon as you know your job is ending, and keep copies of your contract, payslips and any letters from your employer.
Can my employer see what I tell my adviser?
If you instruct your own adviser, what you tell them is confidential under the rules of their regulator. If your employer has instructed the adviser, the position can be different, because the employer is the client. Ask at the start who the client is and how information will be shared, so that you understand the arrangement.
How much does a work visa cost?
There is usually an application fee and, for many people, the Immigration Health Surcharge, plus smaller costs such as translations. These amounts change from time to time. Check GOV.UK for the current figures, or ask your adviser for a full breakdown before you apply. If family members are applying too, the total can be much higher, so it is worth getting a full picture early.
How much will an adviser charge?
Many advisers charge a fixed fee for each application, with separate fees for family members. More complicated situations may be charged differently. Ask for the fee in writing, and check whether government fees are included, before you agree to anything.
Will my employer pay?
Some employers pay for visas and advisers, some pay part and some leave it to the worker. Ask your employer early what they will cover. Be wary of anyone who asks you to pay for a job itself, and check with a regulated adviser before paying.
Does Leaflegal charge me?
No. Leaflegal is free to use. The firm or adviser we introduce you to pays us a fee for the introduction, which does not change what you pay them. You agree any fees directly with the firm, and you are under no obligation to instruct them.
Can I pay in instalments?
Some firms allow fees to be paid in stages, for example part at the start and part when the application is submitted. Others ask for payment upfront. Government fees are usually paid when the application is made. Ask the firm what payment arrangements it offers before you commit.
Are cheaper advisers just as good?
Price is only one part of the picture. It is worth looking at what is included, who will handle your matter, how experienced they are and whether they are properly regulated. A clear written quote helps you compare firms fairly and avoid surprises later.
My visa ends soon. What should I do?
Leaflegal cannot advise on your situation, but we can introduce you to a regulated adviser quickly. Tell us the date your visa ends when you enquire. The sooner you speak to an adviser, the more time there is to understand your options and prepare.
I have received a letter from the Home Office.
Read it carefully and note any deadline, as some time limits are short. Contact a regulated adviser as soon as you can and have the letter ready to share. If you do not have an adviser yet, mention the deadline when you enquire so the firm knows it is urgent.
My application was refused. Can you help?
We can introduce you to a regulated adviser who can look at the refusal. There may be short time limits for some kinds of challenge, so tell us the date on the decision when you enquire. Have the refusal letter and a copy of your application ready for the adviser.
How quickly will an adviser contact me?
The firm usually gets in touch within one working day, often sooner. If you have an urgent date, such as a visa ending soon or a job starting, say so in your enquiry. Make sure you check the phone number and email you give us.
How do I check an adviser is regulated?
You can search the Solicitors Regulation Authority register for solicitors and the Immigration Advice Authority register for advisers. Both are free online. A genuine adviser will give you their registration details if you ask. Every firm we introduce is regulated. Advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority are registered at different levels, which show the kind of work they can do.
Do I have to go ahead after the consultation?
No. You are free to decide whether to instruct the firm, and there is no obligation. Nothing is agreed until you accept their terms. If you decide not to go ahead, you do not need to give a reason. We will not pass your enquiry to anyone else without asking you first.
