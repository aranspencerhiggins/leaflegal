Immigration advice is one of the most tightly controlled areas of legal services. It is a criminal offence for an unqualified person to provide immigration advice or services in the UK in the course of a business. Leaflegal is set up around that rule.

Who can receive immigration leads

Immigration enquiries only go to SRA regulated firms, CILEx regulated practices, or advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at a level that covers the work. You are responsible for staying within your authorisation, including the level and categories of work your registration allows. We ask for your regulatory details when you apply and you must tell us if anything changes.

If your authorisation changes, for example because a registration lapses or the level changes, tell us straight away. We will pause or adjust your matching so that no enquiry reaches you that you are not permitted to handle. Keeping this up to date protects your clients, your practice and us.

Leaflegal never advises

Our form, our campaigns and our team do not give immigration advice. We do not comment on eligibility, routes, requirements or a client's prospects. We tell every client this before they submit an enquiry, and we keep that line on every page we publish about immigration.

Referral disclosure

Leaflegal is an introducer. SRA regulated firms must make sure clients are told in writing about any financial arrangement with an introducer, and that it does not affect their independence or advice. CILEx Regulation and the Immigration Advice Authority have their own requirements on referrals and fees. Our consent wording tells every client we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send that wording with each lead.

Sensitive data

Immigration enquiries can involve special category data, such as information that reveals someone's nationality or ethnic origin. We keep our questions to the minimum needed for first contact and never pass matter details to advertising platforms. Leaflegal, a trading name of Vertical Brands Ltd, is the controller for data collected through our forms. Once a lead is delivered, you become controller of your copy.

Why immigration enquiries are different

People looking for immigration help are often under real pressure. A visa may be running out, a letter may have arrived from the Home Office, or a family is trying to plan a life together across borders. Many of them have already read conflicting information online and want to speak to someone qualified who can look at their own situation.

Most of these clients pay privately, and they are choosing someone they will trust with something that matters a great deal to them. Clear fees, a calm first conversation and a fast response tend to be what they remember.

Where the enquiries come from

Our campaigns run on social media, search and helpful content aimed at the moments people look for immigration help. Each person answers a short set of practical questions and confirms they want paid help from a regulated adviser. When an enquiry fits your criteria, we check it and send it to you straight away by email or into your CRM.

Because every enquiry is matched to one adviser, the client hears from you rather than from a queue of firms. That gives you room to have a proper first conversation, and it gives the client a clear next step at a stressful time.

What clients ask

I have just been refused. What should I do first?
Many people start by finding the date of the decision and any deadline in the letter, keeping every page and contacting a regulated adviser quickly. An adviser can read the decision and explain what it means. Leaflegal can introduce you to one, and we pass the deadline to the firm so they can prioritise your matter.
Can Leaflegal tell me if I can appeal?
No. Leaflegal is not regulated to give immigration advice and does not do so. We cannot tell you whether you can appeal or what your options are. We introduce you to a regulated adviser who can read your decision and explain what may be possible.
Is there a deadline?
Many refusals come with time limits, and some are short. The letter usually mentions any time limit and when it starts. Because the details matter, a regulated adviser can confirm exactly how long you have. When you enquire, tell us the date on your letter.
Does a refusal mean I did something wrong?
Not necessarily. Refusals happen for many reasons, and sometimes the Home Office itself makes mistakes. The reasons in the letter are the starting point. A regulated adviser can explain them in plain language and talk through what they mean for you.
Can I stay in the UK after a refusal?
That depends on your circumstances and the decision, and it is a question for a regulated adviser. It is one of the first things many people ask about, and an adviser can usually explain your position quickly once they have seen the letter and your immigration history.
Should I just apply again?
Some people do make a new application after a refusal, and for others a different step is more suitable. Which approach fits your situation depends on the reasons for refusal and your circumstances. A regulated adviser can explain the options and the risks of each.
I do not understand the letter. Is that normal?
Very normal. Refusal letters are written in formal language and refer to rules and guidance that most people have never read. Advisers read these letters every day. They can explain what each part means and what the Home Office is saying.
Will a refusal affect future applications?
It may need to be mentioned on future forms, and the reasons for refusal can be relevant later. How much it matters depends on the decision and your circumstances. A regulated adviser can explain how your refusal might affect any future plans, including applications for family members.
How quickly can an adviser help?
Firms usually contact people within one working day of an enquiry, often sooner. When there is a deadline, we flag it to the firm. Many advisers can act quickly in urgent cases. If your deadline is very close, say so clearly when you enquire.
What will the adviser need from me?
In general, the refusal letter, a copy of the original application, the documents you sent, your passport and any other Home Office letters. They will also want to hear what has happened since. If something is missing, tell them and they will work with what you have.
Can I use the same adviser who did my original application?
You can, and some people prefer to. Others want a fresh pair of eyes after a refusal. Either way, the adviser handling things now will need the full file. Your previous adviser should be able to provide a copy if you ask.
Will I have to go to a hearing?
Some matters involve a hearing and others do not. If there is a hearing, it may be in person or by video, and your adviser will explain what to expect and help you prepare. Interpreters can be arranged if needed.
Can a family member contact an adviser for me?
Yes. A relative or friend can make the enquiry, which can help if you are abroad, unwell or in detention. The adviser will need to speak to you directly before acting for you, and will explain how they can take your instructions.
What if I cannot afford an adviser?
Legal aid is available for some types of immigration matter but not most. Some advisers offer fixed fees for an initial review, which can help you understand your options before committing to more. A regulated adviser can tell you whether any funding might apply.
Can the adviser speak to the Home Office for me?
Once instructed, your adviser can usually act as your representative, so official letters may go to them as well as or instead of to you. They can deal with correspondence and keep you informed. Ask how they will let you know when anything arrives.
How do I know an adviser is genuine?
You can search the public registers kept by the SRA, CILEx Regulation and the Immigration Advice Authority. A genuine adviser will give you their registration details. Every firm or adviser Leaflegal introduces is regulated to give immigration advice.
Can any regulated adviser handle an appeal?
Not always. Advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority are authorised at different levels, and tribunal work usually needs a higher level. Solicitors and barristers have their own rules. It is reasonable to ask any adviser whether they are authorised and experienced in the work you need.
What if someone promises I will win?
No regulated adviser can promise the outcome of a Home Office or tribunal decision. A guarantee is a warning sign. A good adviser will explain the possibilities and the risks honestly, and give their view in writing if you ask.
Is it a problem if help is offered by someone in my community?
Informal support can be valuable, but only regulated people can lawfully give immigration advice. Unregulated advice can lead to missed deadlines or mistakes. You can check whether someone is regulated using the public registers.
What if I am unhappy with my adviser?
Regulated advisers must have a complaints procedure, which should be in your client care letter. If a complaint is not resolved, you may be able to go to the Legal Ombudsman or the Immigration Advice Authority, depending on who regulates the adviser.
Should I ask the adviser for their view in writing?
Many people find it helpful to have the adviser's main points confirmed by email or letter, especially deadlines, options and costs. It gives you something to refer back to and share with family. Most advisers will confirm the key points in writing after the first conversation.
How much does it cost to deal with a refusal?
It depends on the work involved. There may be official fees, which change and are listed on GOV.UK, and adviser fees. Many advisers offer a fixed fee to review the decision first. Ask for the full likely cost in writing before you agree to further work.
Will I need a barrister?
Some matters, particularly those with a hearing, may involve a barrister instructed by your adviser. Their fee may be separate. Your adviser will explain if a barrister is likely to be needed and what it might cost.
How long will it all take?
It varies widely. Some steps take days and others take months. Tribunal and court timescales depend on their workload. Your adviser can tell you what to expect at each stage and what might cause delay.
Is legal aid available?
Legal aid covers some immigration matters, including asylum and some detention cases, but not most others. A regulated adviser can tell you whether you might be eligible for legal aid or any other funding.
Does Leaflegal charge me?
No. Leaflegal is free to use. The firm or adviser we introduce you to pays us a fee for the introduction, and that does not change what you pay them.
Is there a charge for the first conversation?
Some advisers offer a short free conversation and charge for a full review of the decision, while others charge from the start. Any charge should be explained before the work begins. It is worth asking when the firm first contacts you.
I have been told I will be removed from the UK. Can anyone help?
This is very urgent. A regulated adviser can look at your situation and explain whether anything can be done. When you enquire, say clearly that removal has been arranged and give any dates you have, so the firm can act as quickly as possible.
Someone I know has been detained. What can I do?
A family member or friend can make an enquiry on their behalf. Have their full name, date of birth, Home Office reference if known and where they are held. Legal aid may be available for some detention matters, and an adviser can explain more.
I missed a deadline. Is it too late?
Not always. In some cases late steps can still be taken if there is a good reason for the delay, but the longer the delay, the harder it can be. A regulated adviser can look at what happened and explain what options remain.
Is my enquiry confidential?
Yes. Leaflegal only shares your details with the one regulated firm or adviser we introduce you to, with your consent, and never with advertising platforms or the Home Office. The adviser then has their own duty of confidentiality.
My visa has been cancelled or cut short. Is that the same as a refusal?
It is a different type of decision, but it can have similar effects and may come with its own deadlines. The letter should explain the reasons and dates. A regulated adviser can read the decision and explain what it means and what options may exist.
