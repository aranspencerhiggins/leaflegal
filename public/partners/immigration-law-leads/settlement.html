Leaflegal sends immigration enquiries from people and businesses who want paid help from a regulated immigration adviser. Each enquiry goes to one firm or adviser only. It is never sent to a list of firms at the same time and it is never resold after it has been delivered.

Immigration enquiries only go to firms and advisers who are allowed to give immigration advice. That means solicitors' firms regulated by the SRA, practices regulated by CILEx Regulation, or advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority, formerly the OISC, at the right level for the work. We check how you are regulated when you apply.

Leaflegal never gives immigration advice. Our form does not ask people about their eligibility, their history or which route might suit them, and we do not comment on their chances. We ask only the type of matter, how urgent it is, a few practical details and how to contact them. Everything about the law and the client's options comes from you.

Why immigration enquiries are different

People looking for immigration help are often under real pressure. A visa may be running out, a letter may have arrived from the Home Office, or a family is trying to plan a life together across borders. Many of them have already read conflicting information online and want to speak to someone qualified who can look at their own situation.

Most of these clients pay privately, and they are choosing someone they will trust with something that matters a great deal to them. Clear fees, a calm first conversation and a fast response tend to be what they remember.

Where the enquiries come from

Our campaigns run on social media, search and helpful content aimed at the moments people look for immigration help. Each person answers a short set of practical questions and confirms they want paid help from a regulated adviser. When an enquiry fits your criteria, we check it and send it to you straight away by email or into your CRM.

Because every enquiry is matched to one adviser, the client hears from you rather than from a queue of firms. That gives you room to have a proper first conversation, and it gives the client a clear next step at a stressful time.

Immigration enquiries come from individuals, families and employers. You choose the matter types you want, so a firm focused on family work does not receive business immigration enquiries unless it asks for them.

Individuals and families

Many enquiries come from people who are already in the UK and want help with their next application, from people outside the UK planning to join family, and from people who have received a decision they do not understand. Some have handled earlier applications themselves and now want a professional because the stakes feel higher.

Employers

Business enquiries usually come from owners and HR managers who want to recruit from overseas, keep an existing worker or understand their duties as a sponsor. They often want a fixed price and a clear plan, and they may have more than one worker to discuss.

Urgency

Each lead says how soon the client needs help and whether they know of any deadline or decision date. Some enquiries are planning ahead by several months. Others have a date approaching quickly. That lets you send the most urgent leads to a senior adviser first.

Clients in the UK and clients abroad have different needs from a first call. Someone in the UK may want a face to face meeting, while someone overseas usually expects a video call at a time that suits their time zone. Knowing this from the lead helps you plan the first contact properly.

Funding

We focus on privately funded matters. Every client confirms they are looking for paid help rather than legal aid or free advice. If you also do legal aid work, talk to us about what you want to receive.

We do not ask about a client's immigration history, their eligibility for any route, their finances, their relationships in detail or anything else that would amount to assessing their case. We do not tell them whether they are likely to succeed. That keeps Leaflegal on the right side of the rules on immigration advice and keeps the advice where it belongs, with you.

Leads are sent as soon as they are checked, by email, to a shared inbox or into your CRM or case management system using a lead email address or a webhook. There is no portal or login.

Immigration clients often contact more than one adviser, and those with a deadline will usually instruct whoever helps them first. A well run first call matters more here than in most practice areas.

Respond quickly

Aim to call soon after a lead arrives, especially where the client has flagged a deadline. Set up alerts so the right adviser knows straight away. If a lead comes in out of hours, a short holding email that tells the client when you will call can make a real difference.

Open with what they told us

The lead tells you the type of matter, the urgency and whether the client is in the UK. Opening with that shows you have read their enquiry and lets them talk about their situation rather than repeating basic details.

Be clear about the first step

Many firms offer a paid initial consultation, either fixed fee or deducted from later work. Explaining clearly what the client gets from the first meeting, what it costs and what they should bring helps people say yes. Clients who are unsure often want to know how fees are structured for the whole matter, including any government fees that are separate from yours.

It also helps to be clear about what the client should have ready for the first meeting, in general terms. People often worry about bringing the wrong thing, and a short list sent by email after the call takes that worry away.

Follow up with care

Some clients need time to gather documents or talk to family before they instruct. A short follow up email and a second call a few days later is often welcome, as long as the tone is helpful rather than pushy.

You are responsible for staying within your authorisation, including the level and categories of work your registration allows. If your authorisation changes, for example because a registration lapses or the level changes, tell us straight away. We will pause or adjust your matching so that no enquiry reaches you that you are not permitted to handle. Keeping this up to date protects your clients, your practice and us.

CILEx Regulation and the Immigration Advice Authority have their own requirements on referrals and fees. Our consent wording tells every client we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send that wording with each lead.

What clients ask

What does indefinite leave to remain mean?
Indefinite leave to remain, often called ILR, is permission to stay in the UK without a time limit. It is the most common form of settlement. Whether it is relevant to you, and how it might apply to your situation, is something a regulated adviser can explain after looking at your history.
Is settlement the same as British citizenship?
No. Settlement is a type of immigration status, while British citizenship is a nationality. They are separate and involve separate applications. Many people hold settlement for years without applying for citizenship, and some never do. A regulated adviser can explain the differences and what each means in practice for you.
Can Leaflegal tell me if I can apply?
No. Leaflegal is not regulated to give immigration advice and does not do so. We cannot tell you whether you can apply or when. We introduce you to a regulated adviser who can look at your circumstances and explain your options.
Why does everyone say the rules keep changing?
Settlement policy has changed many times over the years, and the government has announced further changes. This means information from friends, older websites or social media may be out of date. A regulated adviser works with the current rules every day and can explain how they apply to you.
What is an eVisa?
An eVisa is a digital record of a person's immigration status, linked to a UK Visas and Immigration account. The Home Office has moved away from physical cards towards eVisas. It is used to prove status to employers, landlords and others. Your adviser can explain how this works if you are granted permission.
Does settlement affect my family?
It can, because family members often have their own immigration status and their own applications to think about. How your application relates to theirs depends on your circumstances. A regulated adviser can look at the whole family's position and explain how applications might be planned.
What happens after I make an enquiry?
Leaflegal passes your enquiry to one regulated firm or adviser. They usually contact you within one working day, often sooner, to arrange a first conversation. They will ask some questions about your situation and explain how they can help and what it might cost.
Will I have to meet the adviser in person?
Not necessarily. Many advisers work by phone, video call and email, and some also offer face to face meetings at their offices. Ask the adviser how they usually work. Many people find remote meetings convenient, especially if they work long hours or have caring responsibilities.
How involved will I need to be?
You will need to provide documents, answer questions and check the application carefully before it is submitted, because you are responsible for what it says. The adviser does the legal work, but they rely on you for accurate information. Most advisers will give you a clear list of what they need.
Can I speak to the adviser in my own language?
Some advisers speak other languages, and some firms can arrange interpreters. It is worth mentioning this when you enquire so the firm can tell you what is available. Being able to explain your history clearly can make the process easier and less stressful.
What if I am unhappy with the service?
Regulated advisers must have a complaints procedure, which should be explained in your client care letter. If a complaint is not resolved, you may be able to take it further, for example to the Legal Ombudsman or the Immigration Advice Authority, depending on who regulates the adviser.
Can the adviser deal with the Home Office for me?
Yes. Once instructed, your adviser can usually act as your representative, which means the Home Office may write to them about your application. They can respond to requests for information and keep you updated. Ask how they will let you know when something arrives.
How do I know the adviser is regulated?
You can search the public registers kept by the SRA, CILEx Regulation and the Immigration Advice Authority. A regulated adviser will give you their registration details if you ask. Every firm or adviser Leaflegal introduces is regulated to give immigration advice.
What should I bring to the first consultation?
In general, it helps to have your passport, details of your current visa, any Home Office letters and a rough timeline of your time in the UK. A note of trips abroad and any worries you have is also useful. The adviser will tell you if they need anything else.
I cannot find some old documents. Is that a problem?
Many people have gaps in their paperwork, especially from years ago. Advisers are used to this and can explain what other records might help. Sometimes old records can be requested from employers, banks or government departments. Mention any missing documents at the start so there is time to deal with them.
Should I tell the adviser about something I am worried about?
Advisers can only work with what they know. What you tell a regulated adviser is confidential, and they deal with difficult histories regularly. Raising a concern early gives them the chance to explain how it might be relevant and avoids surprises later.
How early should I start?
There is no fixed answer, and it depends on your situation. Many people find it helps to make contact well before their current permission ends, so there is time to gather documents and ask questions. If your visa is due to end soon, say so when you enquire so the firm knows it is urgent.
Can I prepare my own application and ask an adviser to check it?
Some advisers offer a checking service for applications people have prepared themselves, usually for a lower fee than full representation. Others prefer to handle the whole matter. Ask the adviser what they offer and what a check would cover.
Do I need to keep copies of everything?
It is sensible to keep copies of every document you send and every letter you receive. Your adviser will also keep a file. Having your own copies can help in future, for example if you later apply for citizenship or need to prove your history.
Do I need to have everything organised before I call?
No. Many people make contact with documents in a box or spread across emails. The adviser can help you work out what is needed and how to organise it. Having your passport and current visa details to hand for the first call is usually enough to get started.
How much will a settlement application cost?
There are Home Office fees, which change regularly and are listed on GOV.UK, and separate adviser fees. Many advisers offer fixed fees for settlement work. Ask for the full cost in writing before you agree, and check whether Home Office fees are included.
Why do advisers charge different amounts?
Fees vary depending on the adviser's experience, location, how complex the case is and what the fee includes. A higher fee may include more support, such as dealing with follow up requests. Comparing what is included, not just the headline price, gives a clearer picture.
How long will it take to get a decision?
Processing times vary and change over time. The Home Office publishes its current service standards on GOV.UK, and some applications may have a faster service available. Your adviser can tell you how long things are currently taking and what might affect the timing in your case.
Is legal aid available?
Legal aid is not usually available for most settlement applications, although it can be available in some limited circumstances. A regulated adviser can tell you whether any form of funding might apply to you.
Does Leaflegal charge me anything?
No. Leaflegal is free to use. The firm or adviser we introduce you to pays us a fee for the introduction, and that does not change what you pay them.
Will I be charged for the first consultation?
Some advisers offer a short free first conversation and others charge a fixed fee for a full consultation. If there is a charge, it should be explained before the meeting. It is worth asking when the firm first contacts you so there are no surprises.
I have received a letter from the Home Office. What do I do?
Read it carefully and note the date it was sent and any deadline it mentions. If you already have an adviser, send it to them straight away. If not, many people contact a regulated adviser quickly, because some letters have short time limits.
My visa ends soon. Can someone help quickly?
Many advisers can act quickly where there is a deadline. When you make an enquiry, tell us the date your visa ends so the firm can see it is urgent. They will explain what they can do in the time available.
What if the Home Office asks for more documents?
Requests for further information usually have a deadline. Missing it can affect the outcome. If your adviser is representing you, they will usually receive the request and help you respond. If you receive it directly, it helps to pass it to them as soon as possible.
Is my enquiry kept private?
Yes. Leaflegal only shares your details with the regulated firm or adviser we introduce you to, and only with your consent. We never share them with advertising platforms. Once you speak to the adviser, they have their own professional duties of confidentiality.
Can I travel while my application is being decided?
Travel while an application is pending can affect some applications, and the position depends on your circumstances. This is a question to raise with your adviser before you book anything, so they can explain how it might affect you.
