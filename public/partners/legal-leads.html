A legal lead is an enquiry from a person or business who has asked to speak to a solicitor about their own matter, and who has agreed that a firm can contact them. It is the starting point of a possible instruction. It is not an instruction, and it is not a guarantee of one.

The term covers a wide range of things in the market. At one end are fresh, exclusive enquiries where someone has described their matter minutes ago and is waiting for a call. At the other end are lists of names and numbers that have been bought, sold and passed around several times, sometimes without the person having any idea who will call them. Both get called legal leads. They are not the same product.

Leaflegal only produces the first kind. Every lead comes from someone who completed one of our own enquiry forms, about their own matter, in one of eight practice areas. They confirmed they want paid help, gave a UK mobile number and email address that we checked, and ticked a separate box agreeing to be contacted by a firm. The lead goes to one firm, as soon as it is checked.

The parts of a legal lead

A useful lead answers the questions an intake team would otherwise spend the first few minutes asking. Who is this person. What kind of matter do they have. How urgent is it. Where are they. Are they looking for paid help. How do I reach them. Did they agree to hear from me. If any of those answers are missing, the first call becomes a fact finding exercise rather than a conversation about how you can help.

A lead is a moment in time

The value of a legal lead falls quickly. Someone who has just received a court summons, had an offer accepted on a house or been told their visa application was refused is thinking about little else. A day later they may have spoken to a friend's solicitor, found another firm online or simply lost momentum. That is why freshness matters as much as the information in the lead itself.

Leads and your own enquiries

Firms sometimes ask how a bought lead compares with an enquiry that comes through their own website. The honest answer is that a website enquiry often arrives with some trust already built, because the client chose your firm by name. A lead from Leaflegal arrives with more structure, because the client has already answered questions about their matter, urgency, location and willingness to pay. Both are worth calling quickly. The difference is that you can decide how many leads you receive, which is much harder to do with your own enquiries.

Every Leaflegal lead starts with our own marketing. We do not buy data from third parties, scrape directories or reuse old enquiries.

Our campaigns

Our campaigns run on social media, search and content. They are written for people at the point they need a solicitor, not for people browsing general legal information. Someone searching how to extend a lease with a sale coming up is in a very different place from someone reading about leasehold law in general, and our campaigns aim at the former.

Our enquiry forms

People who respond to our campaigns complete a short enquiry form on Leaflegal. The questions depend on the practice area. They always cover the type of matter, urgency, location and whether the person wants paid help, plus a short description in their own words. The forms are designed to be finished on a phone in a couple of minutes.

Filtering before submission

The form filters people before they become a lead. Someone who only wants legal aid, has a personal injury claim, has a matter outside England and Wales in one of our non-immigration areas, or does not want to be contacted is told politely that we cannot help. Where we can, we point them to a better place to go. They never reach your inbox.

What we never do

  • Buy lists of names or phone numbers from anyone else
  • Scrape solicitor directories or review sites
  • Resell a lead once it has been delivered to a firm
  • Send the same enquiry to several firms at once
  • Pass matter details to advertising platforms

Why we keep the form short

Every extra question on a form means some people give up before they finish. Every question left off means your first call has more ground to cover. We try to strike a balance by asking only what your intake team needs to decide how quickly to call and how to open the conversation. Detailed facts, documents and identity checks are left to your firm, which is where they belong once the client has chosen to speak to you.

The biggest difference between legal lead suppliers is whether a lead is sold to one firm or several. It changes how the client experiences the first call and how firms compete for the work.

Shared leads

With shared leads, the same enquiry goes to several firms. The price per lead can look lower, but the client receives several calls in quick succession, often from firms that know nothing more than a name and a phone number. The first firm through tends to win, and the conversation often turns quickly to who is cheapest. Firms that cannot call within minutes rarely get a look in.

Exclusive leads

With exclusive leads, one firm receives the enquiry. The client hears from one solicitor about their matter. You can take the time to understand the problem, explain your service and talk about fees on your own terms. Every Leaflegal lead is exclusive. We never send the same lead to more than one firm and never resell it.

What exclusive does not mean

Why we chose exclusive

We chose to send each lead to one firm because we think it is better for clients and for firms. Clients going through a separation, a court case or a house move do not want five phone calls in ten minutes. Firms do not want to spend their time chasing people who have already been called by four competitors. Exclusive leads cost more to produce, but they make the first conversation far more useful for everyone involved.

Exclusive describes what Leaflegal does with the enquiry. It does not stop the client from looking elsewhere. Some people will speak to more than one firm on their own initiative, especially for higher value matters. A quick, helpful first call is still the best way to win the work.

Each legal lead passes the same set of checks before it is delivered. They are there so that your team spends its time on real matters with real people.

Contact details

The mobile number is checked as a valid UK mobile number and the email address is checked for a real domain when the form is submitted. This catches most typing errors and made up details before a lead is created.

Scope and suitability

We check that the matter is in a practice area and matter type you have chosen, and in a location you cover. Personal injury, clinical negligence and claims management matters are filtered out completely.

Paid help

Every client confirms they are looking for paid legal help rather than legal aid or free advice. This does not mean they have agreed to a particular fee. It means they understand that a solicitor will charge for their work.

Consent

The client ticks one box to confirm they understand Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm, and a separate box to agree that a firm can contact them by phone, email or text about their matter. We record the exact wording and the time, and send it with the lead.

What checks cannot do

No set of checks can tell you whether a client will instruct you, whether their matter has merit or whether they can afford your fees. Those are questions for your first conversation. We do not call clients before sending a lead, because we would rather you were the first person they speak to.

Feedback makes the checks better

When firms tell us which leads turned into instructions and which did not, we look for patterns. If a particular matter type, location or campaign keeps producing enquiries that do not suit a firm, we can change what that firm receives or change how we reach people in the first place. The checks at submission are fixed, but the overall quality of what reaches you improves as we learn from the firms we work with.

Within each area you choose the matter types you want and leave out the rest. A firm that only wants contested work or only wants purchases can say so, and we match accordingly.

Legal leads behave differently in each area of law. The fields we collect, how urgent the matter usually is and what wins the instruction all vary.

Conveyancing

Purchase, sale and remortgage enquiries come from people at a clear decision point. They usually want a quote quickly. We tell you the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether there is a mortgage.

Family law

Separation, finance and children enquiries often come from people who are anxious and want to speak to someone the same day. Most private family clients pay privately. We tell you the type of matter and whether proceedings have started.

Immigration

Immigration enquiries are often deadline driven. Leaflegal never gives immigration advice. We collect only the type of matter, urgency and contact details, and introduce clients only to regulated firms and registered advisers.

Debt recovery

Business owners chasing unpaid invoices want action and clear charges. Every enquiry is business to business. We tell you the rough amount, the age of the debt and whether it is disputed.

Motoring offences

Drivers facing a ban are highly motivated and usually have a fixed court date. We tell you the type of offence, any court date and their current points.

Licensing

Pubs, restaurants, venues and drivers often have a hearing or opening date already fixed. We tell you the licence type, the application or review involved and the council area.

Lease extensions

Flat owners with a shortening lease, often prompted by a sale or remortgage, want to understand the process and cost. We tell you roughly how many years are left.

If you are comparing legal lead suppliers, a few direct questions will tell you most of what you need to know. These are the questions we would ask in your position, and the answers we give.

  • Where do the leads come from? Ours come from our own campaigns and forms only.
  • Is each lead sent to one firm or several? Ours go to one firm and are never resold.
  • Has the client agreed to be contacted by a firm, and can you show the wording? Ours have, and the wording and time come with every lead.
  • How quickly are leads delivered? Ours are sent as soon as they are checked.
  • Are legal aid and free advice enquiries filtered out? Ours are.
  • Do you avoid personal injury and other prohibited referral areas? We do.
  • What happens if a lead has wrong details? Our partner agreement sets out a review period and how invalid leads are handled.
  • Can I see a sample before committing? Yes, on your first call.

Be wary of any supplier who cannot explain where its leads come from or will not show you the consent wording. A cheap lead that the client does not remember asking for can cost far more in wasted time than it saves.

It is also worth asking how the supplier talks about itself to clients. A lead seller that presents itself as a law firm, gives clients legal opinions or hides the fact that it is paid by firms can create problems for the firm that receives the lead. Leaflegal tells every client clearly that it is an introduction service, that it does not give legal advice and that it is paid by the firms it introduces.

Questions and answers

Are legal leads worth it?
For firms that call quickly and follow up properly, yes. We will give you an honest view of likely volumes before you start.
Can I try a small volume first?
The minimum is 10 leads a month, which is enough to judge quality.
Do you offer personal injury leads?
No. Referral fees for personal injury are prohibited under LASPO.
What is a legal lead?
It is an enquiry from a person or business who wants to speak to a solicitor about their own matter and has agreed to be contacted by a firm. A good legal lead includes their contact details, the type of matter, how urgent it is, where they are and a record of their consent. It is an opportunity to win an instruction, not an instruction in itself.
Are legal leads the same as referrals?
They are similar in that someone introduces a client to your firm. The difference is that a traditional referral usually comes from a professional contact who knows the client, while a lead comes from a client who responded to marketing and asked to be introduced. In both cases, the SRA Code requires you to tell clients about any financial arrangement with the introducer.
Are legal leads worth buying?
For firms that call quickly and follow up properly, they can be a reliable source of new private clients. For firms that leave leads for a day or two, results tend to be disappointing whoever the supplier is. We will give you an honest view of likely volumes for your criteria before you start, and the minimum of 10 a month is enough to judge quality.
Why does freshness matter so much?
People who need a solicitor usually act on that need straight away. Within hours they may have asked a friend, found another firm or decided to wait. A lead that arrives as soon as it is checked and is called within minutes catches the client while they are focused on their matter and expecting a call.
What is the difference between a lead and a cold contact?
A lead is someone who asked to be contacted about their own matter. A cold contact is someone whose details were collected for another purpose, or bought from a list, and who is not expecting to hear from you. Leaflegal only produces leads. We never buy data or pass on cold contacts.
Do you sell legal leads in Scotland or Northern Ireland?
Not at the moment. Our leads in six practice areas are for matters in England and Wales. Immigration introductions can come from anywhere in the UK, because immigration law applies UK wide. If you are a firm outside England and Wales with an interest in immigration work, mention it when you apply and we will tell you honestly whether the service could work for you.
Will every legal lead become a client?
No. Some people are comparing firms, some decide not to proceed and some cannot afford the fees once they understand them. A lead is an opportunity, not a guaranteed instruction. Firms that call quickly, use the information in the lead and are clear about their fees tend to win the most work from the leads they receive.
How do people find Leaflegal?
Through our campaigns on social media, search and content. Our adverts and pages are aimed at people who have a legal need now, such as a house move, a separation, a court date or a shortening lease. They complete a short enquiry form on our site and agree to be introduced to a firm.
Do you buy data from other companies?
No. Every Leaflegal lead comes from our own enquiry forms. We do not buy data, rent lists, scrape directories or take enquiries from other lead sellers. That is the only way we can be sure every client knows who we are and what they agreed to. It also keeps every lead exclusive.
How do you check contact details?
The mobile number is checked as a valid UK mobile number and the email address is checked for a real domain at the point of submission. This catches most mistakes and invented details. If a lead still turns out to have wrong details, tell us within the review period in your partner agreement.
Do you speak to clients before sending leads?
No. Leads are sent as soon as they are checked, so you are the first person to speak to the client about their matter. Calling them first would slow things down and mean the client had to explain their situation twice. Our checks run while the client fills in the form, so they do not hold the lead back.
How do you stop people who want free advice?
Every client confirms they are looking for paid legal help before their enquiry is submitted. People who only want legal aid or free advice are told we cannot help and pointed to other sources of support. This does not mean every client will accept your fees, but it does mean they expect to pay.
What information do clients give about their matter?
They choose the practice area and type of matter, say how urgent it is and whether there is a deadline or hearing, and write a short description in their own words. Some areas have extra questions, such as rough property value, years left on a lease or the amount of an unpaid debt.
Can I see the consent wording before I start?
Yes. We show you a sample lead on your first call, including the consent record, so you can see exactly what clients agree to and how it is presented. Your compliance officer is welcome to review it. The same wording and the time it was agreed are sent with every lead you receive.
Are your legal leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We never send the same lead to several firms and never resell a lead that has been delivered. When you call, you are the only firm Leaflegal has introduced the client to. Exclusivity is not an optional extra. It is how every Leaflegal lead is handled, for every partner firm.
Why are shared leads cheaper?
Because the supplier sells the same enquiry several times. The cost per lead looks lower, but the client gets several calls, the first firm through usually wins and conversations tend to focus on price. Many firms find the real cost of an instruction from shared leads is higher once wasted calls are counted.
Can a client still contact other firms?
Yes. Exclusive means Leaflegal only introduces the client to you. It does not stop them from looking at other firms themselves. Calling quickly, listening carefully and being clear about fees gives you the best chance of being the firm they choose. Most people are glad to deal with the first firm that listens properly and explains things clearly.
How do you decide which firm gets each lead?
Each lead goes to a firm whose practice areas, matter types and locations it fits, taking account of each firm's monthly cap. Where more than one firm could take it, we balance distribution fairly between them. There is no auction or bidding, so a lead never goes to whoever pays most on the day.
Do exclusive leads convert better than shared leads?
We do not publish conversion figures, and results depend heavily on each firm's intake. What exclusivity does change is the first call. You are not racing other firms to reach the client, and the conversation can focus on their matter rather than on price. Many firms find that a calmer first call suits the way they want to work.
Which practice areas do you generate leads for?
Conveyancing, family law, immigration, debt recovery for businesses, motoring offences, licensing and lease extensions. Within each area you choose the matter types you want and the locations you cover. We do not generate personal injury, clinical negligence or claims management work. Each area has its own page explaining the fields we collect and the firms it suits.
Do you offer personal injury leads?
No. Paying referral fees for personal injury and clinical negligence work is prohibited under section 56 of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012. We do not generate those enquiries in any form. People with personal injury or clinical negligence matters are told we cannot help before they submit, so they never become leads.
Do you generate housing disrepair or mis-selling leads?
No. Those are claims management activities, and we have chosen to stay out of them. Our focus is on privately paid work in our eight practice areas. Financial claims involve regulated claims management activity, and we do not generate them. People with those matters are told we cannot help before they submit an enquiry.
Do you send employment or wills leads?
Not at the moment. We only generate enquiries in our eight current practice areas. If there is an area you would like us to cover in future, mention it on your call. We cannot promise to add it, but it helps us understand what firms want. Any new area would only be added where paying an introducer is permitted.
Are immigration leads handled differently?
Yes. Leaflegal never gives immigration advice. Our immigration form asks only for the type of matter, urgency and contact details, and enquiries go only to firms regulated by the SRA or CILEx Regulation, or to advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level for the work. All advice comes from the firm or adviser.
Can I take leads in more than one area?
Yes. Many firms take leads in two or three areas, such as conveyancing and lease extensions or family law and conveyancing. Each area has its own matter types, locations and price, so you can set them up in a way that suits each of your teams. You can add an area later.
How much do legal leads cost?
Leaflegal charges per lead at a price agreed with your firm before you start. The price depends on the practice area, matter types, locations and volume. We do not publish prices because they vary widely between areas. We will send you a proposal after your call. There is no obligation to go ahead.
Can I try a small number first?
The minimum is 10 leads a month, which is enough to see the quality and judge how the leads work for your team. Above that you set a monthly cap and can change it or pause at any time. Starting near the minimum lets you test your intake routine before increasing volume. Pausing does not affect your criteria or price.
What if a lead is invalid?
Your partner agreement defines an invalid lead, such as one with wrong contact details or one outside your agreed criteria, and sets a review period for raising it. Genuinely invalid leads are dealt with as the agreement sets out. A lead stays valid if the client chooses another firm or decides not to go ahead.
How do I start buying leads from Leaflegal?
Apply to become a partner firm and book a 30-minute call with our team. We will talk through your practice areas, locations and volume, show you a sample lead and then send a proposal. Leads start once your partner agreement is signed. The application takes a couple of minutes, and there is no obligation until you sign.
Is there a login or dashboard?
No. There is no online platform or login. Leads arrive by email or in your CRM, and any changes to your criteria, volume or delivery are made by contacting our team. Many firms prefer this because there is nothing new for their staff to learn. Our team handles every change for you.

More questions people ask

What is a licensing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a business or individual in England and Wales who wants to pay a solicitor to help with a licence. They have told us the licence type, whether it is a new application, a variation, a transfer or a review, any hearing or opening date, the council area and the business name, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm.
Are licensing leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold. We do not send the same venue or driver to several firms at once. That gives you the chance to have a proper first conversation, which matters in licensing because many clients come back for further work once they trust a firm.
Do you send enquiries from objectors?
No. Our licensing enquiries come from licence holders, applicants, operators and drivers who want help with their own licence or business. We do not send enquiries from residents or groups who want to object to a licence. That keeps the leads consistent with acting for the licensed trade and avoids an obvious source of conflict.
Which licence types can I receive?
You can choose premises licence work, personal licences, and taxi and private hire licensing, and within those you can choose new applications, variations, transfers and changes of designated premises supervisor, and reviews and hearings. You can take all of them or only the kinds of work your team wants, and change your choice later.
Will I know if there is a hearing date?
Yes. Each lead includes any hearing date or planned opening date the client knows about. A venue facing a review next week and a business planning to open in six months need very different first calls, and the lead tells you which you are dealing with before you pick up the phone.
Why do you include the business name?
The business name lets you run conflict checks before you call and look at publicly available information, such as the existing licence on the council's register. That way you can open the conversation already knowing something about the premises, which helps show the client you have taken their enquiry seriously.
Do you send taxi and private hire enquiries?
Yes, if you want them. Taxi and private hire enquiries come from drivers, operators and vehicle proprietors dealing with applications, renewals, suspensions, revocations and appeals. Drivers whose licence has been suspended are often under real financial pressure and want help quickly. You can switch taxi work on or off separately from premises work.
Do you send enquiries about temporary event notices?
Event organisers and venues sometimes enquire about one off events, often alongside a wider question about their premises licence. If you want this work, tell us and we will include it in your matching where it fits. Many firms find that a business asking about a single event turns into a longer relationship once it sees how a solicitor can help with its licence more generally.
