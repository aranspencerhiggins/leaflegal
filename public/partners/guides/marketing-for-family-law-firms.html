Family law clients come to a solicitor at some of the most difficult moments of their lives. A marriage or relationship has ended, there are worries about children, money or the family home, and often there is fear about what the other person will do next. They are not just buying a legal service. They are choosing someone to guide them through a period of real uncertainty, and they want to feel safe in that choice.

That makes family law marketing different from most other areas. Clever advertising matters far less than trust, empathy, discretion and a quick, human response. The firms that win the most private family work tend to be the ones that make a frightened person feel understood within minutes of getting in touch, and then explain clearly and honestly what will happen and what it will cost.

This guide is for family solicitors and firms handling divorce and dissolution, financial settlements, child arrangements, cohabitation disputes and prenuptial agreements. It looks at how family clients choose a firm, how to talk about fees, which marketing channels tend to work, the particular sensitivities of family marketing and how buying leads fits in.

A private paying market

Since legal aid for most private family matters was withdrawn, the great majority of clients pay privately. Many have never instructed a solicitor before and have no idea what family law costs. That creates both a challenge and an opportunity. Clients are nervous about cost, so firms that explain fees clearly and offer a manageable first step stand out.

Different clients, different needs

A family practice may handle a simple uncontested divorce one day and a complex financial remedy case with business assets the next. These clients find you in different ways, value different things and are worth very different amounts to the firm. Good marketing recognises that and does not treat all family enquiries the same.

Your reputation does the heavy lifting

Family clients talk to friends, family and colleagues, and many will ask someone they trust for a recommendation before they search online. Every client you help well can lead to several more. Every client who feels let down can put people off. In family law, the quality of your client care is inseparable from your marketing.

Where to start

If you are unsure where to begin, look first at what happens when a new family enquiry arrives. How quickly is it called, who calls, how is cost explained and what is the first step offered? For most family firms, improving those few things wins more clients from existing enquiries before any money is spent on new marketing.

Understanding what goes through a family client's mind when they look for a solicitor is the starting point for marketing that works.

They are often in crisis

Many family enquiries come at a moment of acute stress. A partner has just left, divorce papers have arrived, there has been an argument about the children or the other person has instructed a solicitor. The client wants to talk to someone soon, often the same day. They are less likely to compare five firms carefully and more likely to go with the first one that makes them feel safe.

What they look for

  • Someone who listens and does not judge
  • A clear explanation of what happens next
  • Honesty about cost, ideally with a fixed first step
  • Experience with situations like theirs
  • A calm, measured approach rather than aggression
  • Discretion about how and when they are contacted
  • Reviews or recommendations from people in a similar position

Tone matters

Some clients want a firm that will fight hard. Many more want a firm that will help them reach a fair outcome without making things worse, especially where children are involved. Marketing that sounds aggressive can put off the larger group. Many firms describe a constructive approach and explain that they will go to court when it is needed. Membership of professional bodies that promote a non-confrontational approach, such as Resolution, can reassure some clients.

Specialism reassures

Clients facing a complex financial case, a business, a pension, an international element or a dispute about relocating children want to know the firm has handled similar matters. Showing the range of work your team does, without breaching confidentiality, helps those clients feel confident.

Timing varies

Some clients need help immediately. Others are thinking about separating and want to understand their options before they say anything to their partner. Both are valuable, but they need different approaches. The first needs a fast call. The second may need a discreet, patient follow-up over weeks.

Cost is one of the biggest worries for family clients, and how you handle it on the first call often decides whether they instruct.

Offer a clear first step

A fixed fee initial consultation is one of the most effective tools a family firm has. It gives the client a defined, affordable way to get proper advice on their situation, with no open-ended commitment. It also sets expectations that your time has value. Some firms offer a short free call first and then a fixed fee consultation.

Be honest about what follows

Family matters can be hard to price because so much depends on the other party. Clients understand that, but they still want an idea. Explain how you charge, give estimates for each stage and say what could make costs rise, such as court proceedings or a lack of cooperation. Confirm it in writing.

Fixed fees where the work allows

Many firms offer fixed fees for uncontested divorce, drafting consent orders, prenuptial agreements and some stages of financial or children matters. These are easy for clients to understand and compare, and they can bring in clients who later need further help.

Options for clients on a budget

  • Staged fees, so the client pays for one stage at a time
  • Limited scope or unbundled help, such as advice and document review while the client represents themselves
  • Payment plans for some matters, if your cash flow allows
  • Information about other ways of funding legal fees, where appropriate

If you offer any of these, explain them clearly and in writing. Every Leaflegal family client has confirmed they want paid help rather than legal aid, but budgets vary widely.

The referral disclosure

Where a client is introduced by Leaflegal, the SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure they are told about the financial arrangement, in writing, and that it does not affect your independence. Most firms include this in the client care letter. We send our consent wording with each lead so your disclosure can match it.

Family firms usually win work through a mix of channels. The right mix depends on your size, location and the kind of work you want.

Recommendations and past clients

Word of mouth remains one of the strongest sources of family work. Clients who felt well looked after recommend their solicitor to friends going through the same thing. You cannot control this directly, but excellent client care and a simple way for past clients to pass on your details both help.

Professional referrers

Financial advisers, accountants, mediators, counsellors and other professionals often meet people before they see a solicitor. Building relationships with trusted local professionals can bring well suited referrals. Any financial arrangement with a referrer must be disclosed to the client in writing and must not affect your advice.

Your website

Most family clients look at a firm's website before getting in touch, often late at night. A calm, clear site that explains how you work, what things cost and how to book a first conversation helps. Plain English guides to common questions can build trust and bring search traffic over time.

Search

Family law search terms are competitive, both organically and in paid search. Local searches and specific questions can be valuable. Paid search can bring enquiries quickly but needs careful management because clicks can be expensive and many searches come from people looking for free information.

Reviews

Reviews matter in family law, but many clients are reluctant to leave them publicly because of the personal nature of their case. Make it easy and optional, never pressure anyone, and respect clients who prefer not to. Even a modest number of genuine reviews can reassure new clients.

Buying exclusive leads

Buying leads gives you enquiries from people actively looking for paid family help, without running campaigns yourself. With Leaflegal, each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold. You choose the matter types you want. Our family law leads page explains what each lead includes.

Family marketing and intake carry responsibilities that most other areas do not. Getting these right protects clients and your firm's reputation.

Discretion in contact

The client may share a phone, email account or home with the person they are separating from. Never mention the matter in a text, voicemail or email subject line. If someone else answers, ask for the client by name and do not say why you are calling. Use the best time to call on each lead, and if in doubt, ask the client how they prefer to be contacted.

Safety concerns

Some enquiries involve domestic abuse or fears for a child's safety. Your intake team should know how to recognise these situations, how to respond safely and when to escalate to a senior fee earner. They should also know how to point the client to emergency or specialist support where needed.

Advertising standards

Family law advertising must follow your regulator's rules on publicity and must not be misleading. Be careful about promising outcomes, suggesting a particular approach will always save money or using language that could inflame a dispute. Testimonials need the client's consent and must not identify them or their family without clear agreement.

Emotional pacing

A client who is crying on the phone is not ready to discuss hourly rates. Let them talk first. Marketing and intake that feel rushed or transactional often lose family clients who would have instructed if they had been given a little more time.

Look after your team

Intake staff and fee earners deal with distressing stories every day. Supporting them, through training and the chance to talk things through, helps them stay calm and empathetic on calls, which in turn helps conversion.

Buying leads works well for family firms that can respond quickly and handle enquiries with care.

What each lead tells you

A Leaflegal family law lead tells you whether the client needs help with divorce, finances, children or something else, whether proceedings have started, the urgency and any hearing date, and that the client has confirmed they want paid help. It also includes a description in the client's own words, verified UK mobile and email, location, the best time to call and the consent record.

Choose your matters

You choose the matter types you want. Some firms take contested financial and children matters and leave out simple uncontested divorce. Others take everything because a straightforward divorce often leads to further work. You can also choose your locations or take national enquiries if you work remotely.

Respond the same day

Family clients often want to speak to someone the same day. Fast, warm first calls make a big difference. Our speed to lead guide covers how to set this up.

Volume and price

The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that you set your own cap, and you can change it or pause at any time. You pay per lead at a price agreed with you before you start, which reflects the matter types and locations you choose. A contested finances enquiry is priced differently from a simple divorce. Everything is set out in a written agreement.

Questions and answers

Do you send legal aid enquiries?
No. Every lead has confirmed they want paid help.
Can I choose financial matters only?
Yes.
What makes family law marketing different?
Family clients are often in distress and are choosing someone to guide them through a very personal crisis. Trust, empathy, discretion and a fast human response matter more than clever advertising. Marketing that sounds aggressive or transactional can put people off. The firms that do best usually make clients feel understood quickly and are clear and honest about cost.
How do family clients choose a solicitor?
Many ask friends or family for a recommendation first, then look at websites and reviews. In a crisis, they often choose the first firm that responds quickly and makes them feel safe. They look for someone who listens, explains clearly, is honest about cost and has experience with situations like theirs. A calm, constructive approach reassures many clients.
Should we market ourselves as aggressive or constructive?
Most family clients want a fair outcome without making things worse, especially where children are involved. Marketing that sounds aggressive may attract a small group of clients but can put off many more. Many firms describe a constructive approach while making clear they will go to court when it is needed. It also helps to be consistent with how you actually work.
Which family matters are most valuable?
Contested financial remedy cases, complex children disputes and matters involving businesses, pensions or international elements usually generate the most work. But simple matters such as uncontested divorce can lead to further instructions and recommendations. It depends on your firm's capacity and expertise. Many firms choose a mix, and with Leaflegal you can select the matter types you want.
How important is specialism in family law marketing?
Quite important for complex work. Clients with a business, significant pensions, overseas assets or a relocation dispute want to know the firm has handled similar matters. You can show this through clear descriptions of the work your team does, articles and guides, and accreditations, while always respecting client confidentiality. Simpler matters benefit too, because clients like to know the firm handles their kind of case every week.
Should a small firm try to compete with larger family practices?
Yes. Many clients prefer a smaller firm where they deal directly with an experienced solicitor and feel personally looked after. Smaller firms can often respond faster and offer more continuity. Focus on what you do well, be clear about fees and make sure your intake is quick and warm. Choosing the matter types and locations that suit you also helps.
How long does family law marketing take to work?
It depends on the channel. Bought leads and paid search can bring enquiries within days of starting. Referral relationships and reputation build over months. SEO and content usually take longer still. Many firms combine faster channels with slower ones so that they have steady enquiries now while building long term sources that cost less over time.
Should we offer a free first consultation?
Some firms do and some do not. A free first call can attract more enquiries, but sometimes from people mainly looking for free information. A fixed fee initial consultation is easy to say yes to, sets expectations and values your time, and many family firms find it works well. You could also offer a short free call followed by a fixed fee consultation.
How should we talk about fees with family clients?
Clearly, early and kindly. Explain how you charge, give estimates for each stage and be honest about what could make costs rise, such as court proceedings. Confirm it in writing. Many clients have never paid a solicitor and are anxious about cost. Clear information often reassures them enough to take the first step.
Should we offer fixed fees in family law?
Where the work allows, fixed fees are popular with clients. They are common for uncontested divorce, consent orders and prenuptial agreements, and some firms offer fixed fees for defined stages of financial or children matters. For less predictable work, staged estimates give some certainty. Fixed fees can also bring in clients who later need further help.
What can we offer clients with a limited budget?
Options include staged fees, limited scope or unbundled help where the client represents themselves with your support, and payment plans for some matters. Some firms also explain other ways of funding fees where appropriate. Whatever you offer, explain the terms clearly in writing and make sure the client understands what is and is not included.
Do Leaflegal family leads include legal aid enquiries?
No. Every family lead has confirmed they are looking for paid legal help rather than legal aid or free advice. People who only want legal aid are pointed elsewhere before their enquiry is submitted. Budgets still vary, so being clear about cost on the first call remains important. Some clients will ask about staged fees or payment options, so it helps to have an answer ready.
Do we need to tell family clients about the referral fee?
Yes. The SRA Code of Conduct requires you to make sure clients are told about any financial arrangement with an introducer, in writing, and that it does not affect your independence or advice. Most firms include it in the client care letter. Leaflegal's consent wording tells clients we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send it with each lead.
Which marketing channels work best for family law?
Most firms rely on a mix of recommendations, professional referrers, their website, search, reviews and, increasingly, bought leads. The right mix depends on your size, location and the work you want. Word of mouth tends to be strong for family firms with good client care, while leads and search can add volume you control.
Is SEO worth it for family law firms?
It can be, especially for local searches and specific questions people ask when separating. But family law search terms are competitive and results take time. Helpful, plain English content about common questions tends to work best. SEO is usually a long term investment that works alongside faster sources of enquiries such as leads or paid search.
Is paid search worth it for family law?
It can bring enquiries quickly, but family keywords can be expensive and many searches come from people looking for free information. Campaigns need careful targeting and regular management. If you use paid search, track the cost of each instruction, not each click, and compare it with your other channels. Negative keywords and careful location targeting can reduce wasted spend.
How can we build referrals from other professionals?
Get to know trusted financial advisers, accountants, mediators and counsellors in your area. Explain how you work and the kinds of clients you help. Refer clients to them where appropriate too. Any financial arrangement with a referrer must be disclosed to the client in writing and must not affect your independence.
How do we get reviews when family matters are so private?
Make it easy and entirely optional. Some clients are happy to leave a review once their matter is resolved, especially if they can do so without giving details. Never pressure anyone and respect those who decline. Even a small number of genuine reviews helps reassure new clients who are nervous about choosing a firm.
Should family firms use social media?
It can help keep your firm visible and show your approach, through short, helpful posts about common questions. Avoid anything that could identify clients or inflame disputes. Paid social advertising can reach people at the right moment but must follow your regulator's publicity rules and be handled sensitively given the subject matter.
How should we contact family clients discreetly?
Never mention the matter in a text, voicemail or email subject line. If someone else answers, ask for the client by name without saying why you are calling. Use the best time to call on each lead. If in doubt, ask the client on the first call how they prefer to be contacted, and record it so the whole team follows it.
What if an enquiry involves domestic abuse?
Your intake team should be trained to recognise signs of abuse, respond safely and escalate to a senior fee earner. They should know how to point clients to emergency or specialist support where needed and be extra careful about how and when they make contact. Safety must always come before marketing or conversion.
Can we use client testimonials in family law?
Only with the client's clear consent, and they must not identify the client or their family without explicit agreement. Testimonials must be genuine and must not be misleading or promise outcomes. Many family clients prefer not to be quoted at all, so respect their choice and rely on anonymous or general feedback where appropriate.
How do we handle very distressed callers?
Slow down, listen and acknowledge how they feel before moving to practical matters. Do not rush through your fee information while they are upset. If they cannot continue, offer to call back at a better time. A calm, patient first call is often what persuades a family client to instruct a firm.
Should our marketing mention children?
Carefully. Many family clients are most worried about their children, so explaining that you help with child arrangements and that you take a child focused approach can reassure them. Avoid emotive images or language that could upset people or seem to exploit their situation. Keep the focus on how you help families find workable arrangements and what the process usually involves.
What if the other party contacts us after seeing our marketing?
Run your conflict checks as normal before discussing anything. If you already act or have been approached by the other party, you will usually be unable to act for both. Handle the contact politely and promptly. If a Leaflegal lead turns out to be a conflict, do not act and let us know so we can deal with it under your partner agreement.

What clients ask

What is a conveyancing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person who is buying, selling or remortgaging a property in England and Wales and has asked to be contacted by a firm about paid conveyancing. They have answered a short set of questions about the transaction, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm. We send that enquiry to you alone, as soon as it has been checked.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We do not send the same enquiry to several firms at once and we never resell a lead after it has been delivered. If you are the firm we match, you are the only firm that receives that person's details from us. The client can still choose to speak to other firms on their own, as anyone can.
How do you check the leads?
Every client confirms they want paid legal help with a property in England and Wales. We verify their UK mobile number and their email address, and we filter out enquiries that fall outside our scope or your criteria. People we cannot help are told politely and pointed elsewhere, so they never become a lead. Each lead then arrives with the client's answers and the consent record.
What details will I get for each enquiry?
You get the client's name, mobile and email, whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, the tenure, whether there is a mortgage, the target timescale and the location. There is also a short description in the client's own words, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the exact consent wording they agreed to.
Can I quote from the lead alone?
Usually you can give a provisional figure on the first call, because the lead covers the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether a mortgage is involved. You will still want to confirm the details with the client and send a written quote. Things like a leasehold management pack, a new build or a gifted deposit may change your figure once you know more.
Do you send leasehold purchases and sales?
Yes, if you want them. Every lead says whether the property is freehold or leasehold, and you can choose to receive leasehold matters, leave them out or ask for them only in certain areas. Firms with an experienced leasehold team sometimes prefer this work, while others choose to focus on freehold transactions. We set your matching to suit you.
Are remortgage leads included?
Only if you ask for them. Remortgages, transfers of equity, purchases, sales and combined sale and purchase matters can each be switched on or off. Some firms take remortgages to keep a steady flow of shorter matters, while others prefer to concentrate on purchases. You can change the mix at any time by letting us know.
Do clients know a firm will call them?
Yes. Every client has agreed to be contacted by a firm about their matter, by phone, email or text, and most are expecting a call soon after they submit. It helps to say early in the call that you are getting in touch because of the enquiry they made through Leaflegal. That puts the client at ease and moves the conversation quickly on to their purchase, sale or remortgage.
