Conveyancing is one of the most competitive areas of private client legal work. Most buyers and sellers need a conveyancer at a fixed point in their move, usually when an offer is accepted or a sale is agreed, and most of them will compare a few quotes before choosing. The work is regulated and the core process is broadly similar from firm to firm, so clients often struggle to tell firms apart. That makes marketing harder, but it also means firms that communicate well and quote clearly can stand out quickly.

This guide is for SRA regulated firms and CLC licensed conveyancers handling residential purchases, sales, remortgages and transfers of equity. It looks at how clients choose a conveyancer, how to quote so you win more work, which marketing channels firms commonly use, why service quality is your most powerful marketing, and how buying leads fits alongside your own efforts.

What clients are really buying

Most clients cannot judge the legal quality of conveyancing work. What they can judge is how quickly you respond, how clearly you explain costs, how often you update them and whether their move happens on time. Those are the things they talk about in reviews, mention to estate agents and tell friends who are moving next. Good conveyancing marketing is built around proving you do those things well.

A market shaped by timing

Conveyancing enquiries are heavily driven by the property market. Busy periods bring more enquiries but stretch teams. Quiet periods leave fee earners with capacity and fewer new files. Firms that plan their marketing around these cycles, and that can adjust volume up and down, tend to keep a steadier pipeline than those that only market when things are quiet.

Regulation and panels

You must be authorised to carry out reserved conveyancing work, and for most mortgaged transactions you need to be on the relevant lender panels. Some firms also hold quality accreditations that lenders or clients look for. These are not marketing in themselves, but they affect which clients you can take on, so check them before you plan any campaign.

Where to start

If you are not sure where to focus, start with the quote. Look at how quickly your firm responds to new enquiries, how clear your quote emails are and how often you follow them up. For most conveyancing firms, improving those three things brings more instructions from the enquiries they already have, before spending anything on new channels.

Understanding how buyers and sellers decide between firms is the starting point for any conveyancing marketing.

The moment of choice

Most clients start looking for a conveyancer when an offer is accepted or a sale is agreed. Some start earlier, often first time buyers who want everything ready. Either way, they usually want to instruct someone within a few days. That short window rewards firms that respond and quote quickly.

What clients compare

  • The total price, including disbursements and any supplements
  • What is included and what might cost extra
  • How quickly the firm responded to the enquiry
  • Whether the firm explained things clearly and without jargon
  • Reviews and recommendations from people they trust
  • Whether the firm is on their lender's panel
  • How the firm will keep them updated during the transaction

Price matters, but not on its own

Many clients start by looking for the lowest quote, but plenty end up choosing a firm that was not the cheapest. A clear, complete quote from a firm that answered quickly often beats a lower headline figure that leaves the client unsure what they will really pay. Clients who have moved before and had a poor experience are often willing to pay more for better communication.

The role of the estate agent

Estate agents and mortgage brokers often recommend conveyancers, and some clients simply take the recommendation. Others are wary of recommended firms, particularly if they suspect a referral arrangement, and prefer to choose for themselves. Both groups are worth reaching, but they need different approaches.

First time buyers and experienced movers

First time buyers usually need more explanation and reassurance. They value a firm that explains each stage and is patient with questions. Experienced movers and investors usually want speed and efficiency. Tailoring your quote emails and first calls to each group can make a noticeable difference.

In conveyancing, the quote is often the single most important piece of marketing you produce. It is usually the client's first real impression of your firm.

Quote fast

Clients typically ask several firms at once. A clear quote sent within the hour, ideally after a short call, usually beats a cheaper quote that arrives the next day. Speed tells the client how quickly you will move on their file.

Quote clearly

Itemise your legal fee, VAT, likely disbursements such as searches, Land Registry fees and any identity check costs, and any supplements for leasehold, new build, help to buy, shared ownership or a mortgage. Explain which figures are fixed and which could change. Point clients to GOV.UK for Stamp Duty Land Tax, since rates and reliefs change, rather than quoting rates yourself.

Explain what is not included

Clients are rarely angry about a cost they were told about at the start. They are often angry about one they were not. Listing likely extras, such as leasehold management packs or indemnity policies, protects your reputation and reduces disputes later.

Call, then send

A quote email on its own competes with every other email the client receives. A short call to understand the transaction, followed by the quote, lets you answer questions, spot complications and build a relationship. Our first call guide has a simple structure.

Follow up the quote

Many clients do not reply straight away because they are waiting for other quotes. A short check-in a day or two later, offering to answer questions and confirming how quickly you can start, wins a surprising amount of work. Our following up guide sets out a sequence.

Make instructing easy

Once the client says yes, send the client care pack, ID check and payment details promptly and in one place. Electronic ID checks and e-signature make it much easier for busy buyers and sellers to get started.

Conveyancing firms usually win work through a mix of channels. Each has strengths and drawbacks.

Estate agent and broker referrals

These can bring steady volume, especially in a local market. But they often come with referral fees or expectations about service and fees, and the client may feel the firm works for the agent rather than for them. Any referral arrangement needs to be disclosed to the client in writing under the SRA rules, or the equivalent rules for CLC firms. Relying on one or two agents also leaves you exposed if the relationship ends.

Your own website

A clear website with an instant or quick quote option, honest information about your fees and an easy way to get in touch can bring direct enquiries. It works best when it is fast, easy to use on a phone and backed by good reviews.

Search and maps

Many people search for conveyancers near them or for conveyancing quotes. Organic search takes time to build and conveyancing terms are competitive. A complete and active local business profile with genuine reviews is often one of the most valuable things a local firm can have.

Paid search and social

Paid search can bring enquiries quickly, but conveyancing keywords can be expensive and campaigns need regular management to avoid wasted spend. Social media advertising can reach people around the time they are moving, but needs good targeting and creative work.

Comparison and quote sites

Some firms use quote comparison sites. These can bring volume, but the same enquiry is often sent to several firms, which pushes competition towards price.

Buying exclusive leads

Buying leads gives you enquiries without running campaigns yourself. With Leaflegal, each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold, so you are not competing with other firms on the same lead. It adds volume you control alongside your other channels. Our conveyancing leads page explains what each lead includes.

In conveyancing, the way you run each file is your most effective marketing. Happy clients leave reviews, recommend you to friends and come back when they next move. Unhappy ones do the opposite, loudly.

Communicate before clients chase

The most common complaint about conveyancers is not hearing anything. Regular updates, even when there is nothing new, prevent anxious calls and build trust. Many firms send updates at set points, such as when searches are ordered, when enquiries are raised and when a completion date is close.

Give clients a named contact

Clients want to know who is handling their file and how to reach them. A named fee earner with a direct line or email makes a big difference, as does cover when that person is away.

Use case tracking where it helps

Some firms offer online case tracking so clients can check progress themselves. It can reduce calls, but it does not replace personal updates at key moments. Clients still want to hear from a person when something important happens.

Ask for reviews at the right time

The best time to ask for a review is shortly after completion, when the client is happy and the move is fresh in their mind. Make it easy with a direct link. Respond politely to all reviews, including critical ones.

Look after the next move

Many clients will move again or remortgage in a few years. A short, consented check-in around the time a fixed rate is likely to end, or a simple way to get in touch again, can bring repeat business. Make sure any marketing you send has the right consent.

Look after agents too

Estate agents remember which firms keep chains moving and which hold them up. Even if you do not pay referral fees, good communication with agents can lead to recommendations.

Buying leads works best for conveyancing firms that can quote quickly and handle the volume they take on. These points help you get the most from them.

What each lead tells you

A Leaflegal conveyancing lead tells you whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, whether it is freehold or leasehold, whether there is a mortgage and the client's target timescale. It also includes verified UK mobile and email, location, the best time to call and the consent record. That is usually enough to prepare a quote before you call.

Choose what you want

You choose your matter types and locations. Some firms take purchases and sales only, some include remortgages and transfers of equity, and some leave out leasehold or new build. You can cover a few towns or the whole of England and Wales if you work remotely.

Match volume to capacity

The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that, you set your own cap and can change it or pause when your team is busy. In a busy market, taking fewer leads and quoting each one quickly usually works better than taking more than you can handle.

Price per lead

You pay for each lead at a price agreed with you before you start, based on the matter types and locations you choose. There are no shared or recycled enquiries. Pricing, billing, delivery and invalid lead rules are set out in a written agreement. You can read more about how pricing works.

Disclose the referral

Leaflegal is an introducer. You need to make sure each client is told about the financial arrangement in writing, in line with your regulator's rules. Our consent wording tells clients we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send that wording with each lead.

What is the best way to market a conveyancing firm?
There is no single best channel. Most successful firms combine fast, clear quoting, excellent communication during transactions, strong reviews and a mix of enquiry sources, such as referrals, their own website, local search and bought leads. The common thread is proving to clients that you will be quick, clear about cost and easy to deal with, because that is what most of them are choosing on.
Should conveyancing firms compete on price?
Price matters, but competing on price alone is hard to sustain and tends to attract clients who will leave for a cheaper quote next time. Many firms do better by being competitive but clear, explaining what is included and showing how their service is better. Clients who have had a poor experience before are often willing to pay more for good communication.
How important are reviews for conveyancing firms?
Very. Most clients cannot judge the legal work, so they rely on what other people say about speed, communication and whether the move happened smoothly. Genuine reviews on your local business profile and independent review sites can make a big difference. Ask happy clients shortly after completion, make it easy with a direct link and respond politely to every review.
How do we plan for busy and quiet periods?
Plan your marketing and capacity together. In busy periods, make sure you can quote and update clients quickly without overloading fee earners. In quieter periods, increase activity to keep files coming in. Buying leads with an adjustable monthly cap helps, because you can raise volume when you have capacity and lower it or pause when you are stretched.
Is it worth targeting first time buyers?
Many firms find first time buyers a good market. They often need more explanation, so firms that are patient and clear can win their trust. They also tend to move again and to recommend firms to friends. Tailor your quote email and first call to explain each stage simply, and be clear about schemes or lender requirements that may affect the cost.
Should we focus on one area or work nationally?
Both can work. A local firm can build strong agent relationships and local reputation. A firm that works remotely can take clients from anywhere in England and Wales, but competes with more firms and relies more on online reviews and fast communication. With Leaflegal you choose your locations, from a few towns to national coverage.
How quickly should we send a conveyancing quote?
As quickly as you can, ideally within the hour during working hours. Clients usually ask several firms at once and often instruct the firm that responds first with a clear quote. A short call before sending the quote lets you understand the transaction and answer questions, which makes the quote more accurate and more persuasive.
What should a conveyancing quote include?
Your legal fee and VAT, likely disbursements such as searches and Land Registry fees, any identity check costs and any supplements for leasehold, new build, shared ownership, help to buy or a mortgage. Say which figures are fixed and which may change. For Stamp Duty Land Tax, refer clients to GOV.UK or explain that it depends on their circumstances, because rates and reliefs change.
Should we include possible extras in the quote?
Yes. Listing likely extras, such as leasehold management pack fees or indemnity policies, protects you from complaints later. Clients are much more forgiving of costs they were warned about at the start. A quote that looks slightly higher but is complete often wins against a lower one that leaves the client wondering what else they will be charged.
Should we call before sending a quote?
Usually, yes. A short call lets you check the details, spot complications such as a leasehold or new build, answer questions and start a relationship. It also means the client has spoken to a person at your firm rather than simply receiving another email. If you cannot reach them, send the quote anyway and follow up with a call.
How do we follow up a quote without being pushy?
Send a short message a day or two after the quote, offering to answer questions and confirming how quickly you can start. If the client is waiting for other quotes, ask when they expect to decide and check in then. Keep it helpful and brief. Many clients appreciate a reminder because they have so much else to deal with during a move.
Do online instant quote tools help?
They can, because they let clients get a figure straight away at any time. But they work best when backed by a quick personal follow-up, because an automated quote alone does not build much trust. Make sure the tool shows a complete picture of likely costs, and keep it up to date as disbursements and your fees change.
Should we pay estate agents for referrals?
That is a business decision for your firm. Agent referrals can bring steady volume but often come with fees, expectations about service and some loss of independence in the client's eyes. Any arrangement must be disclosed to the client in writing under your regulator's rules and must not affect your advice. Many firms combine some agent work with direct enquiries they control.
How do we build relationships with estate agents?
Keep chains moving, respond quickly to agent queries and keep the agent informed about progress where the client agrees. Agents remember which conveyancers help sales complete and which slow them down. Being reliable and easy to work with often earns recommendations even without a formal referral arrangement. Simple habits help, such as returning agent calls the same day and flagging delays early rather than letting them discover problems themselves.
When should we ask clients for reviews?
Shortly after completion, when the client is happy and the move is fresh in their mind. Send a short message thanking them and including a direct link to your review page. Do not offer incentives for positive reviews. Respond politely to all reviews, including critical ones, because prospective clients read your responses as well as the reviews themselves.
How can we get repeat business from past clients?
Give a great service first, because that is what clients remember. Then keep in touch in a way they have agreed to, such as a short message around the time a remortgage might be due. Make it easy for them to find you again. Many people move or remortgage several times, and a firm that handled the last move well is often the first one they call.
Does good communication really affect marketing?
Yes. Poor communication is one of the most common reasons clients complain about conveyancers and leave negative reviews. Regular updates prevent anxious calls, reduce complaints and lead to better reviews and recommendations. In conveyancing, the experience of the last client is often the most powerful marketing you have for the next one.
Should we offer case tracking?
It can help, especially for clients who like to check progress themselves at any time. It may also reduce routine calls. But it does not replace personal updates at key moments, such as when searches come back, when a completion date is agreed or when something goes wrong. Clients still want to hear from a person.
Do conveyancing firms need a good website?
Yes, because most clients will look at your website before instructing, even if they were referred. It should load quickly on a phone, explain your fees honestly, show genuine reviews and make it easy to get a quote or call you. It does not need to be elaborate. A clear, simple site that answers the main questions clients have is usually enough.
Is SEO worth it for a conveyancing firm?
It can be, particularly for local searches, but it takes time and conveyancing terms are competitive. Useful content that answers real client questions, a complete local business profile and genuine reviews tend to matter most. SEO works best as a long term investment alongside faster sources of enquiries. Our guide to buying leads versus SEO looks at the trade-offs in more detail.
Should we run paid search for conveyancing?
Paid search can bring enquiries quickly and lets you target specific areas. But conveyancing keywords can be expensive, and campaigns need regular management to avoid wasted spend on searches that never become instructions. If you run paid search, track cost per instruction rather than cost per click, and compare it honestly with your other channels.
How should we use social media?
Social media can help keep your firm visible to past clients and local people, and paid social advertising can reach people around the time they are moving. Short, helpful posts about the buying and selling process tend to work better than general promotion. Make sure any advertising complies with your regulator's rules on publicity, including how you describe your fees.
What should our local business profile include?
Accurate opening hours, address, phone number and website, a clear description of the services you offer, photos of your office and team where appropriate and regular responses to reviews. Many people choose a local conveyancer from a map search, so a complete and active profile can bring enquiries at little cost. Keep it up to date when anything changes.
Should we publish our conveyancing fees online?
Many firms do, and clients increasingly expect to see pricing information before they get in touch. Your regulator also has rules on price and service transparency that apply to residential conveyancing. Publishing clear fee information, including typical disbursements, can build trust and attract clients who value transparency. Check your regulator's current requirements for exactly what you must show.

What clients ask

What is a conveyancing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person who is buying, selling or remortgaging a property in England and Wales and has asked to be contacted by a firm about paid conveyancing. They have answered a short set of questions about the transaction, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm. We send that enquiry to you alone, as soon as it has been checked.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We do not send the same enquiry to several firms at once and we never resell a lead after it has been delivered. If you are the firm we match, you are the only firm that receives that person's details from us. The client can still choose to speak to other firms on their own, as anyone can.
How do you check the leads?
Every client confirms they want paid legal help with a property in England and Wales. We verify their UK mobile number and their email address, and we filter out enquiries that fall outside our scope or your criteria. People we cannot help are told politely and pointed elsewhere, so they never become a lead. Each lead then arrives with the client's answers and the consent record.
What details will I get for each enquiry?
You get the client's name, mobile and email, whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, the tenure, whether there is a mortgage, the target timescale and the location. There is also a short description in the client's own words, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the exact consent wording they agreed to.
Can I quote from the lead alone?
Usually you can give a provisional figure on the first call, because the lead covers the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether a mortgage is involved. You will still want to confirm the details with the client and send a written quote. Things like a leasehold management pack, a new build or a gifted deposit may change your figure once you know more.
Do you send leasehold purchases and sales?
Yes, if you want them. Every lead says whether the property is freehold or leasehold, and you can choose to receive leasehold matters, leave them out or ask for them only in certain areas. Firms with an experienced leasehold team sometimes prefer this work, while others choose to focus on freehold transactions. We set your matching to suit you.
Are remortgage leads included?
Only if you ask for them. Remortgages, transfers of equity, purchases, sales and combined sale and purchase matters can each be switched on or off. Some firms take remortgages to keep a steady flow of shorter matters, while others prefer to concentrate on purchases. You can change the mix at any time by letting us know.
Do clients know a firm will call them?
Yes. Every client has agreed to be contacted by a firm about their matter, by phone, email or text, and most are expecting a call soon after they submit. It helps to say early in the call that you are getting in touch because of the enquiry they made through Leaflegal. That puts the client at ease and moves the conversation quickly on to their purchase, sale or remortgage.
