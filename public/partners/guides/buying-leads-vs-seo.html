Every law firm that wants private clients has to decide how to find them. The main options are building your own search visibility through SEO, paying for clicks through paid search, advertising on social media, listing in directories, relying on referrals or buying leads from a lead generation business. Most firms end up using several of these. The question is not which one is best in general, but which mix suits your firm, your practice areas, your budget and how quickly you need new work.

This guide compares buying leads with SEO and the other common channels in plain terms. It looks at how each one works, what it really costs, how long it takes to produce enquiries, how much control you have and how much management it needs. It also explains how to compare channels fairly using your own numbers, and how buying leads can sit alongside your own marketing rather than replacing it.

A note on fairness

Leaflegal sells leads, so we have an interest in this question. We have tried to be balanced. SEO and paid search can work very well for many firms, and we do not suggest that any firm should stop its own marketing. Buying leads is one tool among several, and it suits some firms and some situations better than others.

The questions that matter

  • How quickly do you need new enquiries?
  • How predictable do you need your costs to be?
  • How much time can you or your team spend managing marketing?
  • Do you want to build a long term asset or buy results as you need them?
  • Which practice areas and locations do you want to grow?
  • How well does your firm convert the enquiries it already gets?

Your answers to these questions will usually point you towards the right mix more reliably than any general rule.

Start with your intake

Before comparing channels, look at what happens to the enquiries you already receive. If enquiries wait hours for a call, fees are explained vaguely or nobody follows up, every channel will look worse than it should. Improving intake first gives you a fair basis for comparison and makes whichever channels you choose work harder. Our guide to converting legal enquiries covers this in detail.

Search engine optimisation means improving your website and online presence so that it appears higher in unpaid search results when people look for legal help. For law firms it usually covers local search, practice area pages, helpful content and the technical health of the site.

How it works

Search engines try to show the most useful and trustworthy results for each search. Firms improve their chances with clear pages for each service and location, genuinely helpful content that answers real questions, a fast and secure website, consistent business details across the web, reviews and links from other reputable sites. A complete local business profile is often especially important for searches that include a town or 'near me'.

Strengths

  • Enquiries from organic search do not cost anything per click
  • A strong position can bring enquiries for years
  • It builds your brand and credibility as well as enquiries
  • Helpful content can answer client questions before they call
  • You own the website and the content

Drawbacks

  • It usually takes months to see meaningful results
  • Legal search terms are highly competitive, especially in cities
  • Results can change when search engines update how they rank sites
  • Good content and technical work cost time or money, often both
  • You cannot easily turn volume up or down to match capacity
  • It is hard to target a specific matter type or location precisely

Who it suits

SEO suits firms that can invest steadily over a long period, want to build a lasting asset and do not need enquiries urgently. It works particularly well for firms with a clear local presence or a well defined specialism. Firms that are growing fast or entering a new area often find it too slow on its own.

Paid search means paying to appear in sponsored results when people search for particular terms. You usually pay each time someone clicks your advert, and the price is set by an auction against other advertisers.

How it works

You choose keywords, write adverts, set a budget and decide where and when your adverts appear. People who click go to a page on your website, where you hope they will call or fill in a form. The cost per click depends on how many other firms are bidding for the same terms. Legal keywords are known to be among the more expensive in paid search, and prices change over time.

Strengths

  • It can bring enquiries within days of starting
  • You can target specific locations, times and search terms
  • You can increase or reduce spend quickly
  • Results are measurable if tracking is set up properly

Drawbacks

  • You pay for clicks, not enquiries, and many clicks never become enquiries
  • Legal keywords can be expensive and costs can rise without warning
  • Campaigns need regular, skilled management to avoid wasted spend
  • Many searches come from people wanting free information or forms
  • You still need landing pages and intake that convert visitors
  • Enquiries stop as soon as you stop paying

Who it suits

Paid search suits firms that have the budget to test, the skills in house or through an agency to manage campaigns closely and good tracking to measure cost per instruction. Without careful management, it can become expensive quickly.

Buying leads means paying a lead generation business for each enquiry it delivers, rather than paying for clicks or building your own visibility. The lead generator runs the marketing, collects enquiries and passes them to firms.

How it works with Leaflegal

Leaflegal runs campaigns on social media, search and content aimed at people who need legal help. Each person answers a short set of questions about their matter, urgency and location, and confirms they want paid help rather than legal aid or free advice. Their UK mobile and email are verified and they give recorded consent to be contacted by a firm. We match each enquiry to one firm only, based on its practice areas, matter types and locations, and send it by email or into its CRM as soon as it is checked. You can read more about how it works.

Strengths

  • A known cost for each enquiry, agreed before you start
  • No campaigns, keywords or landing pages to manage
  • Enquiries can start soon after onboarding
  • You choose the matter types and locations you want
  • You set a monthly volume and can pause when busy
  • With exclusive leads, the enquiry is not sent to other firms

Drawbacks

  • You are paying for enquiries, not guaranteed instructions
  • You do not build a lasting asset of your own
  • Volume depends on how many people enquire in your areas
  • Quality varies widely between lead providers, so you need to choose carefully
  • You still need fast, effective intake to convert leads

Exclusive and shared leads

Some lead providers send the same enquiry to several firms. That can make each lead cheaper but turns every enquiry into a race, often decided by price. Exclusive leads go to one firm only, which gives you a fair chance to have a proper conversation. Leaflegal leads are exclusive and are never resold.

Who it suits

Buying leads suits firms that want predictable volume without managing campaigns, that are entering a new practice area or location, that have capacity to fill quickly or that want to add steady volume alongside slower channels like SEO.

What each lead contains

Every Leaflegal lead includes the client's name, verified UK mobile and email, the practice area and matter type, a short description in their own words, urgency and any known deadline, location and the best time to call. It also includes the source, a timestamp and the consent record, so your first call can be about the client's matter rather than about who you are.

Most firms use more than SEO, paid search and leads. These other channels each have a place.

Referrals

Referrals from past clients, other professionals, estate agents or other firms are often among the best quality enquiries a firm receives. They come with built-in trust. But they are hard to scale quickly, and some come with referral fees or expectations that need managing. Any financial arrangement must be disclosed to the client in writing under the SRA rules or your regulator's equivalent.

Social media advertising

Social platforms let firms reach people based on interests, life events and location. They can be effective for some practice areas but need good creative work, careful targeting and compliance with publicity rules. Running them well takes skill and time.

Directories and comparison sites

Legal directories and comparison sites can bring visibility and some enquiries. Many charge a listing fee whether or not enquiries arrive, and some send the same enquiry to several firms. They can be worth testing, but measure results carefully.

Your own reputation

Reviews, recommendations and the way you treat every client underpin every other channel. A firm with strong reviews converts more of its SEO traffic, more of its paid clicks and more of its bought leads. Investing in client care is investing in marketing.

The only fair way to compare channels is to look at what each one costs you per instruction, not per click or per enquiry, and to include your own time.

Cost per instruction

For each channel over a set period, add up everything you spent, including agency fees, software, content and the value of your team's time spent managing it. Then divide by the number of instructions that channel produced. Do this separately for each practice area if you can. Two channels with very different costs per enquiry can end up much closer once conversion is taken into account.

Value per instruction

Some channels bring higher value work than others. A channel that brings fewer but larger matters can be better than one that brings many small ones. Track the fee value of instructions, not just the count.

Time to results

SEO may cost less per instruction in the long run but take months to start working. Paid search and leads can work within days but stop when you stop paying. Think about when you need the work, not only how much it costs.

Use your own numbers

General figures from other firms or other industries are rarely useful, because so much depends on your practice areas, fees, location and intake. Leaflegal does not publish conversion rates for this reason. Track your own results consistently and compare channels within your firm.

Combining channels

Most firms do best with a mix. A common pattern is to build SEO and reputation steadily as a long term base, use referrals where they come naturally, and use bought leads or paid search to add predictable volume, enter new areas or fill capacity when needed. Each channel has a different job. Buying leads does not replace your own marketing. It adds volume alongside it.

Review regularly

Markets change, costs rise and fall, and your firm's priorities shift. Review your channel mix every few months using your own numbers. Move budget towards the channels producing instructions at a cost and value that suit you, and away from those that are not. With leads, you can adjust volume, matter types and locations as you learn what works.

Questions and answers

Should we stop our own marketing?
No. Leads add volume alongside your own channels.
Can we start small?
The minimum is 10 leads a month.
Is it better to buy leads or invest in SEO?
Neither is better in every case. SEO can bring lower cost enquiries over the long term but takes months to work and is competitive in legal search. Buying leads brings predictable volume quickly without campaign management, but you pay for each enquiry. Many firms use both, with SEO building a long term base and leads adding volume they control.
How long does SEO take to work for a law firm?
It varies, but meaningful results usually take months rather than weeks, and competitive terms in large cities can take longer. Local searches and specific questions can sometimes improve more quickly. Because results build gradually and depend on many factors, SEO works best as a steady long term investment rather than a way to fill capacity quickly.
How quickly can bought leads start?
With Leaflegal, once your criteria, pricing and delivery are agreed and your partner agreement is in place, leads start as soon as matching enquiries come in. Onboarding begins with a 30-minute call with our team and a sample lead. Volumes depend on your practice areas and locations, and we will give you an honest view of likely volume when we agree your criteria.
Should we stop our own marketing if we buy leads?
No. Leads work best alongside your own channels, not instead of them. Your website, reviews and reputation help convert every enquiry, including bought ones, and SEO and referrals build long term value. Buying leads adds predictable volume you control, which can be especially useful while slower channels build up. It also means you are not dependent on any single source of work.
Is paid search better than buying leads?
It depends on your budget, skills and time. Paid search gives you direct control and can be very effective, but you pay for clicks rather than enquiries and campaigns need close management. Buying leads gives you a known cost per enquiry with no campaign work. Some firms run both and compare cost per instruction over time.
Can a small firm afford to buy leads?
Many small firms buy leads because they do not have the time or budget to run their own campaigns. With Leaflegal, you pay per lead at a price agreed before you start, and the minimum is 10 leads a month. You choose your matter types and locations, which helps you focus spending on the work you most want.
What does SEO for a law firm involve?
Usually clear pages for each service and location, helpful content that answers real client questions, a fast and secure website, a complete local business profile, consistent business details across the web, genuine reviews and links from reputable sites. It also needs regular updates as services, fees and rules change. Many firms use an agency, but the firm still needs to supply expertise and review content.
Why are legal keywords so expensive in paid search?
Because many firms compete for the same searches and each client can be worth a lot to the firm that wins them. In paid search, prices are set by auction, so competition pushes costs up. Prices vary by practice area, location and time and change often. Careful targeting and management can reduce waste but rarely makes legal keywords cheap.
Can we do SEO ourselves?
Some firms do, particularly for local search and content. Keeping your local business profile complete, asking for reviews and writing helpful pages about the questions clients ask are all things a firm can do in house. Technical work and competitive campaigns often need specialist help. Whoever does it, content should be accurate and checked by someone who knows the law.
What are the risks of relying on SEO?
Rankings can change when search engines update how they rank sites, and competitors can overtake you. Results take time to build and are hard to switch up or down to match capacity. If most of your work depends on one or two search positions, a change can hit enquiries suddenly. Spreading your sources reduces that risk.
What are the risks of relying on paid search?
Costs can rise when competitors bid more, and poor management can waste a lot of money on clicks that never become enquiries. Enquiries stop as soon as you stop paying. Tracking can be hard to set up properly, so some firms do not really know what each instruction costs. Regular review and clear tracking help manage these risks.
Do we still need a website if we buy leads?
Yes. Most clients will look at your website before instructing, even if they came through a lead. A clear, trustworthy site with your regulation details, fee information and reviews helps convert every enquiry. It does not need to rank highly in search to do that job, but it does need to reassure people that you are a firm they can trust.
What is the difference between exclusive and shared leads?
Shared leads are sent to several firms at once, so each one becomes a race and often a price competition. Exclusive leads go to one firm only. Leaflegal leads are exclusive and are never resold, so you are not competing with other firms we have sent the same enquiry to. The client may still contact other firms themselves, so fast intake still matters.
How do we judge the quality of a lead provider?
Ask where the leads come from, whether they are exclusive, what checks are made, whether contact details are verified, how consent is recorded and what happens if a lead is invalid. Ask to see a sample lead. Check the provider does not deal in areas where referral fees are banned. Good providers will answer these questions clearly and put the terms in writing.
Where do Leaflegal leads come from?
From people completing our own enquiry forms, usually after seeing one of our campaigns on social media or finding us through search. We do not buy in data, scrape directories or pass on cold contacts. Each person answers questions about their matter, urgency and location, confirms they want paid help and gives recorded consent to be contacted.
Will every lead become an instruction?
No. Some people are comparing options, some decide not to proceed and some find the fees more than they expected. Leaflegal provides qualified enquiries, not guaranteed instructions. How many become clients depends a great deal on how quickly and well your firm responds. Fast first calls, clear fees and polite follow-up make the biggest difference.
How much do leads cost?
With Leaflegal, you pay per lead at a price agreed with you before you start. It depends on the practice areas, matter types and locations you choose. We do not publish a price because it varies by firm. Pricing, billing, delivery and invalid lead rules are set out in a written agreement before your first lead arrives.
Can we control how many leads we receive?
Yes. The minimum is 10 leads a month. Above that, you set your own cap and we stop matching to you once you reach it. You can change the cap or pause at any time, for example when your team is busy or a fee earner is away. That control is one of the main differences from SEO, where volume is hard to adjust.
Which practice areas can we buy leads for?
Leaflegal covers conveyancing, family law, debt recovery for businesses, licensing, lease extensions, trademarks and immigration. We do not generate personal injury or clinical negligence leads, where referral fees are banned, or financial mis-selling claims. You choose the areas and matter types you want within the areas we cover. Most of our leads are for matters in England and Wales, while immigration introductions can come from anywhere in the UK.
How should we compare different marketing channels?
Look at the total cost per instruction for each channel, including agency fees, software and your team's time, over the same period and for the same practice area. Also look at the value of instructions and how long each channel takes to produce results. Comparing cost per click or cost per enquiry alone can be misleading.
What should we count as the cost of SEO?
Everything you spend on it, including agency or freelancer fees, content writing, website development, software and the time your own team spends reviewing content and managing the work. Because SEO results build over time, it often makes sense to look at costs and results over a longer period than you would for paid search or leads.
Can we trust general figures about conversion rates?
Be cautious. Conversion varies so much by practice area, fee level, location, client type and intake process that general figures rarely apply to a particular firm. Leaflegal does not publish conversion rates for that reason. Your own numbers, tracked consistently over time, are a far better guide to which channels work for you.
How do we know if bought leads are working for us?
Track how many leads you reach, how many book a consultation and how many instruct, along with the fee value of those instructions. Compare the total cost of leads with the value of the work won over a reasonable period. Share your results with us, so we can adjust your matter types, locations and volume to match the work that converts best.
Should we spread our budget across channels?
Most firms benefit from a mix, because each channel has different strengths, timescales and risks. Relying entirely on one source leaves you exposed if it changes. A common approach is a steady long term investment in SEO and reputation, plus a more flexible channel such as leads or paid search that you can turn up or down as capacity changes.
Does reputation affect all channels?
Yes. Strong reviews and a good reputation help convert enquiries from every channel, whether they come from search, paid adverts, referrals or bought leads. A firm with excellent client care often gets more value from the same marketing spend than one without. Investing in how you treat clients is one of the most effective forms of marketing.

No. There is no online platform or login. Leads are sent by email to one or more addresses, to a shared intake inbox or straight into your CRM or case management system by webhook. Can we try leads in one practice area first?
Yes. Many firms start with one practice area or a small set of matter types and locations, then expand once they have seen how leads convert. Starting focused also makes it easier to measure results and compare leads with your other channels. The minimum is 10 leads a month across the criteria you choose.

What clients ask

What is a conveyancing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person who is buying, selling or remortgaging a property in England and Wales and has asked to be contacted by a firm about paid conveyancing. They have answered a short set of questions about the transaction, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm. We send that enquiry to you alone, as soon as it has been checked.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We do not send the same enquiry to several firms at once and we never resell a lead after it has been delivered. If you are the firm we match, you are the only firm that receives that person's details from us. The client can still choose to speak to other firms on their own, as anyone can.
How do you check the leads?
Every client confirms they want paid legal help with a property in England and Wales. We verify their UK mobile number and their email address, and we filter out enquiries that fall outside our scope or your criteria. People we cannot help are told politely and pointed elsewhere, so they never become a lead. Each lead then arrives with the client's answers and the consent record.
What details will I get for each enquiry?
You get the client's name, mobile and email, whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, the tenure, whether there is a mortgage, the target timescale and the location. There is also a short description in the client's own words, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the exact consent wording they agreed to.
Can I quote from the lead alone?
Usually you can give a provisional figure on the first call, because the lead covers the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether a mortgage is involved. You will still want to confirm the details with the client and send a written quote. Things like a leasehold management pack, a new build or a gifted deposit may change your figure once you know more.
Do you send leasehold purchases and sales?
Yes, if you want them. Every lead says whether the property is freehold or leasehold, and you can choose to receive leasehold matters, leave them out or ask for them only in certain areas. Firms with an experienced leasehold team sometimes prefer this work, while others choose to focus on freehold transactions. We set your matching to suit you.
Are remortgage leads included?
Only if you ask for them. Remortgages, transfers of equity, purchases, sales and combined sale and purchase matters can each be switched on or off. Some firms take remortgages to keep a steady flow of shorter matters, while others prefer to concentrate on purchases. You can change the mix at any time by letting us know.
Do clients know a firm will call them?
Yes. Every client has agreed to be contacted by a firm about their matter, by phone, email or text, and most are expecting a call soon after they submit. It helps to say early in the call that you are getting in touch because of the enquiry they made through Leaflegal. That puts the client at ease and moves the conversation quickly on to their purchase, sale or remortgage.
