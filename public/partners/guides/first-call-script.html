A first call script for business finance leads

A good first call uses what the business owner has already told you, then asks only what the form did not.

A good first call uses what the business owner has already told you, then asks only what the form did not. This guide gives a structure you can adapt, using the real Loanleaf lead fields, and the questions that qualify what the form leaves out.

What you already know before you dial

Before you call, read the whole lead. You already have the loan amount, purpose, how soon they need it, whether they are a Ltd or LLP, the revenue band, the time trading band, their answer on missed payments, defaults or CCJs, and whether they have existing finance with the amount left and monthly repayments.

You also have their name, business name, UK mobile and email, the source page, the timestamp and the consent record. They have agreed to be contacted by a broker by phone, email or text.

The most common mistake is to ask for all of this again. Owners notice, and it signals that you did not read their enquiry. Open with what you know and ask only for what is missing.

The opening, who you are and why you are calling

State who you are, your firm and why the call is expected, in one or two sentences.

For example: "Hello, is that Sam? It's Alex from Example Finance. You asked about a business loan of around £150,000 for new equipment through Loanleaf, so I'm calling to see how I can help. Have you got a few minutes?"

The owner knows they asked, so the call feels expected. Mention Loanleaf, because that is where they made the request. Ask whether it is a good time. If it is not, agree a specific time to call back and note it.

Confirm, do not interrogate

Spend the first minute playing their answers back. This shows you have read the enquiry and gives them a chance to correct it.

Use the lead fields directly. "You mentioned the funds are for equipment, and you would like them within two weeks. Is that still right?" Then the amount, then the company, trading time and turnover in a single sentence.

If something has changed, such as a larger amount or a different purpose, update your notes before moving on. Enquiries are a snapshot, and a short call often shifts the figures.

Questions the form does not answer

The form does not ask for card takings or card machine turnover, property value or loan to value, the address of the property, whether a property is residential or commercial investment, planning status, sector or industry, director names or bank statements. This is the real work of the first call. Choose the questions that fit the purpose.

  • All enquiries: what does the business do, who are the directors, and who is the main decision maker?
  • Cash flow or working capital: what is the gap, and how long does it last? Do you take card payments, and what are the monthly card takings?
  • Equipment or vehicles: what is being bought, from whom, and what is the cost? New or used?
  • Commercial property: what is the property, its address and value, and how much deposit is there? Is it owner-occupied or an investment? Is there any planning or development involved?
  • Refinance: what is the current lender, balance and rate? Is there an early repayment charge?
  • Tax or VAT: how much is owed, and when is it due?

Keep each question short and listen to the answer before moving on.

Testing the credit and existing finance answers

The credit answer is Yes, No or Not sure. Treat all three as a starting point. "Not sure" is common, and a gentle question helps: "Have you had any late payments, defaults or CCJs on the company or its directors in the last six years?"

For "Yes", ask what happened, when, and whether it is settled. Lenders often care about the detail more than the headline, so write it down.

If the lead has existing finance, check the amount left to repay and the monthly repayments against revenue. Ask whether the new borrowing is additional or replaces it. This is where you find out whether the ask is affordable, which our guide to qualifying enquiries covers in more detail.

Being clear about fees and next steps

Before you finish, tell the owner how you work and what it might cost. Business owners are wary of broker fees they did not expect, so explain any fee, when it is payable and what it covers.

Then explain what happens next: the documents you need, such as bank statements, filed accounts or management accounts; how soon you can go to lenders; and a realistic idea of timescales. Do not promise an outcome. You are responsible for your own regulatory position, including how you communicate and any fees you charge.

Agree the next step with a date. "I'll email you a list of documents today and call you on Thursday at 10" is better than "I'll be in touch".

After the call

Record the outcome straight away. On the platform, move the lead through the pipeline stages (New, Contacted, Application, Funded or Lost) and add notes, so the conversation can be picked up by anyone in your team.

Send a short email or text with your name, number and the document list. If the owner did not pick up, move to a follow-up routine from following up quiet leads.

Once you have worked a few leads, review which questions led to the best conversations and refine your script. If you are new to the platform, apply to become a partner to see the fields on live leads.

Questions

How long should a first call take?
Long enough to confirm the enquiry, ask the questions the form did not, explain your fees and agree next steps. Many are shorter than people expect because you already hold the main answers.
Should I mention Loanleaf on the call?
Yes. The owner submitted their enquiry through Loanleaf and agreed to be contacted by a broker, so naming it explains why you are calling.
What if they say they did not make an enquiry?
Apologise, stop the call and make a note. The consent record supplied with the lead shows the wording and timestamp. If the details are wrong, check whether you can request a refund within 72 hours, which is only for invalid contact details.
What if the amount has changed?
Update your notes and continue. Enquiries are a snapshot, and many owners refine the figures once they talk to a broker.
Do I need to read from a script?
No. Use the structure, not the words. Opening, confirming, filling gaps, fees and next steps is the pattern, and your own phrasing will sound more natural.
