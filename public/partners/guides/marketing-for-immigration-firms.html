Everything you need to know

Immigration clients are often making some of the most important decisions of their lives. Whether they can stay with a partner, keep a job, bring their children to the UK, become a British citizen or challenge a refusal can shape their whole future. Many are working to a deadline set by a visa expiry date or a decision letter. Many have heard stories about unregulated advisers who took money and did nothing. They are looking for someone they can trust, who will respond quickly and who will be honest with them about cost and process.

This guide is for immigration solicitors, CILEx regulated practices and advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority, formerly the OISC, who handle privately funded work. It looks at how immigration clients choose a firm, how to market within the strict rules that govern immigration advice, which channels tend to work, how to handle urgency and language, and how buying leads fits alongside your own marketing.

Regulation shapes everything

In the UK, giving immigration advice or services is restricted to people who are regulated to do so, such as solicitors regulated by the SRA, CILEx regulated professionals and advisers registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level for the work. That affects marketing as well as advice. Anything that could amount to advice, including detailed website guides and social media posts, should be prepared or checked by someone authorised to give it. Unregulated businesses, including lead generators, must not advise.

A market built on trust

Immigration clients are often wary, and with good reason. Many know someone who paid an unregulated adviser or an agent who promised a guaranteed visa. Showing clearly that you are regulated, who regulates you and how a client can check, is one of the most persuasive things you can do. It turns a legal obligation into a marketing strength.

Private clients and businesses

Immigration firms often serve two quite different groups. Individuals and families come with personal applications, settlement, citizenship and refusals. Employers come with sponsor licences and work visas for staff. They find firms in different ways and value different things, so it helps to market to each separately.

Where to start

If you are not sure where to focus first, look at what happens when a new enquiry arrives. How quickly is it checked for a deadline, who calls, can they speak the client's language, and how clearly are fees explained? Improving those few steps usually wins more instructions from the enquiries you already receive, before you spend anything on new channels.

It is also worth checking that your website and profiles show your regulation clearly and that any guidance on them is current. Out of date content in immigration can mislead clients and damage trust quickly, so a regular review by an authorised person is good practice as well as good marketing.

Understanding what immigration clients are looking for helps you focus your marketing on what actually wins instructions.

Deadlines drive decisions

Many immigration enquiries have a date attached, such as permission ending, a refusal with a short window to act, an appointment or a job start date. Clients with a deadline want to speak to someone quickly and often instruct the first credible firm that responds. Fast, organised intake matters a great deal in this area.

What clients look for

  • Clear proof that the firm or adviser is regulated
  • Experience with their kind of matter
  • Honest, written information about fees
  • Someone who will explain things simply and patiently
  • The ability to speak their language, or clear use of interpreters
  • A realistic view rather than guarantees
  • Reviews or recommendations from people in their community

Language and understanding

Many clients speak English as a second language, or are helping a family member who does not speak English well. A firm that can speak to them in their own language, or that explains things carefully in plain English, often wins their trust quickly. If your team speaks other languages, say so prominently.

Family and community influence

Immigration decisions are often made with family members, sometimes in other countries. Recommendations from friends and community groups carry great weight. Clients may want a partner or relative on the call. Making room for that, while respecting confidentiality, helps.

Employers think differently

Business clients seeking sponsor licences or work visas are usually focused on timescales, compliance risk and cost over the long term. They value firms that understand their sector, explain duties clearly and can support them after the licence is granted. They often compare firms more methodically than individual clients do.

Marketing immigration services means working within a framework that is stricter than in most other areas of law. Getting it right protects clients and your practice.

Only authorised people advise

Make sure every piece of content that could amount to immigration advice, such as website guides, blog posts, videos and social media answers, is prepared or approved by someone authorised to give it at the right level. Marketing staff and agencies should not be writing advice on their own.

No guarantees

Never suggest that a visa or outcome is guaranteed. The Home Office decides applications, and claims of guaranteed success mislead clients and breach professional standards. Words like 'experienced', 'thorough' and 'clear' are safer and more honest than 'guaranteed' or 'certain'.

Show your regulation

Display your regulator, registration or authorisation details clearly on your website and in your materials, along with how clients can check them on the relevant public register. This is often required and it is also persuasive, because it answers one of the client's biggest worries.

Be clear about fees

Separate your professional fees from Home Office fees and charges. Home Office fees, the Immigration Health Surcharge and other charges change from time to time, so point clients to GOV.UK or give current figures at the time of quoting rather than publishing fixed amounts that may go out of date.

Introducers and referral fees

If you receive enquiries through an introducer such as Leaflegal, you need to make sure clients are told about the financial arrangement in writing, in line with your regulator's rules, and that it does not affect your advice. Leaflegal does not give immigration advice. Our form asks only for the type of matter, urgency and contact details, and every enquiry goes to a firm or adviser regulated to give immigration advice. Everything about eligibility and routes comes from you.

Data and sensitivity

Immigration enquiries can include sensitive personal information. Collect only what you need, keep it secure and handle it in line with UK data protection law and your privacy notice. Be discreet in how you contact clients, who may share devices or living space.

Immigration firms usually build their client base through a mix of channels. The best mix depends on whether you focus on individuals, businesses or both.

Community and word of mouth

Recommendations within communities are often the strongest source of personal immigration work. Clients who feel well treated tell family and friends. Some firms build links with community organisations, faith groups and cultural associations, always making sure any events or talks stay within the rules on giving advice.

Your website

Many clients research firms online, sometimes from abroad. A clear website that shows your regulation, explains the kinds of matters you handle, sets out how fees work and makes it easy to book a consultation helps. Pages in the languages your team speaks can make a big difference. Keep guidance general and make sure it is checked by an authorised person.

Search

People search for help with specific matters and for advisers in particular languages or locations. Organic search takes time and immigration terms can be competitive. Paid search brings enquiries faster but needs careful management, because many searches come from people looking for free information or Home Office forms.

Social media

Social media can reach communities effectively, including in other languages. It needs care, because posts and replies can easily stray into advice. Many firms share general updates and invite people to book a consultation rather than answering individual questions publicly.

Business networks

For sponsor licence and corporate work, links with accountants, HR consultants, recruitment agencies and business groups can bring steady referrals. Short guides on employer duties, checked by an authorised person, can position the firm as a trusted source.

Buying exclusive leads

Buying leads gives you enquiries from people actively seeking paid help, without running campaigns yourself. With Leaflegal, each enquiry goes to one firm only and is never resold. Our immigration leads page explains what each lead includes.

Two practical things separate the immigration firms that win the most private work from those that struggle. How fast they respond and how well they communicate.

Triage by deadline

Every immigration enquiry should be checked for a date as soon as it arrives. Refusals, expiry dates and appointments should go to the top of the list and, where needed, to a senior adviser straight away. Each Leaflegal immigration lead includes urgency and any deadline or refusal date, so your team can sort leads in the right order.

Answer quickly

A client facing a deadline may contact several firms. The first credible response often wins. Mobile alerts for new leads, a shared inbox and a clear rota help. Our speed to lead guide covers how to set this up.

Use the client's language

Each Leaflegal immigration lead tells you the languages the client speaks. If someone on your team speaks the same language, ask them to make the first call. If not, think about how you will use interpreters, and make sure the client understands their options for language support before the consultation.

Account for location

Leads also tell you whether the client is in the UK. Clients outside the UK may be in a different time zone and may prefer video calls or messaging. Check the best time to call and be flexible where you can.

Explain the consultation

Many immigration clients have never used a lawyer before. Explain clearly what happens at a consultation, how long it lasts, what it costs and what general documents to bring, such as passports and any letters they have received. A client who knows what to expect is more likely to attend and to instruct afterwards.

Buying leads works well for immigration firms that respond quickly, triage by urgency and can communicate with a diverse client base.

What makes immigration marketing different?
Immigration advice is restricted to regulated people, so marketing has to stay within strict rules about who can advise and what can be claimed. Clients are often working to deadlines, may speak English as a second language and are wary of unregulated advisers. Trust, speed, clear fees and visible regulation tend to matter more than anything else in winning private immigration clients.
How do immigration clients choose a firm?
Many rely on recommendations from family, friends and community groups, then check websites and reviews. Those facing a deadline often instruct the first credible firm that responds. They look for clear proof of regulation, experience with their type of matter, honest fees, patient explanations and, where possible, someone who speaks their language. Realistic advice matters more to them than bold promises.
Should we market to individuals and businesses separately?
Usually, yes. Individuals and families come with personal applications and refusals, and often find firms through community networks and search. Employers want sponsor licences and work visas for staff, and often find firms through business networks and referrals. Their concerns, language and decision making differ, so separate messages and channels tend to work better than one general approach.
How important are languages in immigration marketing?
Very important. Many clients feel more comfortable explaining their situation in their own language, and a firm that can do that often wins their trust quickly. If your team speaks other languages, say so clearly on your website and in your materials. Tell us your languages too, so we can take that into account when matching enquiries.
How long does immigration marketing take to work?
Bought leads and paid search can bring enquiries within days. Community reputation, referral relationships and search rankings take months to build. Many firms combine faster channels with slower ones so they have steady enquiries now while building long term sources that cost less over time. Track the cost of each instruction from each channel to see what is working.
Can smaller immigration practices compete with large firms?
Yes. Many clients prefer a smaller practice where they deal directly with an experienced adviser who knows their case. Smaller practices can often respond faster, offer more continuity and build strong community reputations. Focus on speed, clarity about fees, visible regulation and the languages and matter types you handle best.
Who can write immigration content for our website?
Anything that could amount to immigration advice should be prepared or approved by someone authorised to give it at the right level. Marketing staff and agencies can help with structure and design, but the substance should be checked by an authorised person. Keep general content accurate and up to date, because immigration rules and fees change often.
Can we say we have a high success rate?
Be very careful. Claims about success rates can easily mislead, especially if the basis is unclear or the figures are not comparable. Never suggest that a visa or outcome is guaranteed. The Home Office makes the decision. Describing your experience, your thoroughness and how you work is safer and more honest, and it still reassures clients.
Should we display our regulation details?
Yes. Show your regulator, registration or authorisation details clearly, along with how clients can check them on the relevant public register. This is often required and it is also one of the most persuasive things you can show, because many clients are worried about unregulated advisers. It helps them feel safe choosing you.
How should we present Home Office fees?
Separately from your own professional fees. Home Office fees, the Immigration Health Surcharge and other charges change from time to time, so it is safer to point clients to GOV.UK or to confirm current figures when you quote, rather than publishing fixed amounts that may go out of date. Make clear which costs are yours and which are paid to the Home Office.
Does Leaflegal give immigration advice?
No. Leaflegal is not regulated to give immigration advice and does not do so. Our form asks only for the type of matter, urgency, whether the client is in the UK, languages and contact details. We do not ask about eligibility or assess anyone's case. Every immigration enquiry goes to a regulated firm or registered adviser, and all advice comes from you.
Do we need to disclose the referral to immigration clients?
Yes. If you receive a client through an introducer, your regulator's rules require you to make sure the client is told about the financial arrangement in writing, and that it does not affect your advice. Leaflegal's consent wording tells clients we are paid by the firms we introduce them to, and we send it with each lead so your client care letter can match it.
Can we answer immigration questions on social media?
Be careful. Answering individual questions publicly can amount to giving advice without the full facts, and other people may rely on answers that do not apply to them. Many firms share general updates and invite people to book a consultation instead. Any content that could be advice should be prepared or approved by an authorised person.
Which channels work best for immigration firms?
Most firms rely on a mix of community recommendations, their website, search, social media in relevant languages, business networks for corporate work and bought leads. The right mix depends on whether you focus on individuals, employers or both. Word of mouth is often powerful for personal immigration work, while leads and search add volume you control.
Is SEO worth it for immigration firms?
It can be, especially for specific matter types and searches in particular languages or locations. Immigration terms can be competitive and results take time. Helpful general content checked by an authorised person tends to work best. Remember that rules change often, so content needs regular review to stay accurate and to avoid misleading readers.
Is paid search worth it for immigration?
It can bring enquiries quickly, but many immigration searches come from people looking for Home Office forms, free information or guidance on what they can do themselves. Careful keyword choice, location and language targeting and regular management are essential. Track the cost of each instruction, not each click, and compare it with your other channels.
How can we build community referrals?
Treat every client well, because recommendations follow good experiences. Some firms build links with community organisations, faith groups and cultural associations, and give general talks about how to choose a regulated adviser. Keep any talks within the rules on giving advice, and make sure any financial arrangement with a referrer is disclosed to clients in writing.
How do we market sponsor licence services to employers?
Through business networks, accountants, HR consultants, recruitment agencies and sector bodies, as well as search and bought leads. Employers value clear explanations of duties, realistic timescales and support after the licence is granted. Short guides for employers, checked by an authorised person, can position your firm as a trusted source.
Do reviews matter for immigration firms?
Yes. Clients often check reviews to reassure themselves that a firm is genuine and treats people well. Ask satisfied clients for a review once their matter has concluded, make it easy with a direct link and never pressure anyone. Respect clients who prefer privacy, and never publish details that could identify someone without their clear consent.
How should immigration firms present their fees?
Clearly and in writing, separating your professional fees from Home Office fees and other charges. Many firms offer fixed fees for defined pieces of work, such as preparing and submitting an application, and fixed fee consultations. Explain what is included, what might cost more and when payment is due. Clients who understand the cost up front are far more likely to instruct.
Should we charge for the first consultation?
Many immigration firms charge a fixed fee for a first consultation, which reflects the time and expertise involved and tends to attract clients who are serious about getting help. Others offer a short free call to understand the matter before booking a paid consultation. Whichever you choose, explain it clearly before booking so the client is not surprised.
How do we handle urgent immigration enquiries?
Check every new enquiry for a deadline as soon as it arrives. Refusals, expiry dates and appointments should go to the top of the list and, where needed, straight to a senior adviser. Offer the earliest consultation you can and explain clearly what the client should bring. A well organised response to an urgent enquiry builds trust quickly.
What should we ask clients to bring to a consultation?
In general terms, passports, any current or previous visas or permissions, and any letters or decisions from the Home Office are usually useful. You will know what else is relevant for each type of matter. Sending a short checklist when you book the consultation helps the client prepare and lets you make the most of the time together.
How can we reduce no-shows for immigration consultations?
Book a specific time, send a confirmation with the date, time, format and what to bring, and send a reminder the day before, in the client's language where you can. Offer video or phone as well as in person, especially for clients outside the UK. Taking payment for a fixed fee consultation at booking also tends to reduce no-shows.

What is a conveyancing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person who is buying, selling or remortgaging a property in England and Wales and has asked to be contacted by a firm about paid conveyancing. They have answered a short set of questions about the transaction, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm. We send that enquiry to you alone, as soon as it has been checked.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We do not send the same enquiry to several firms at once and we never resell a lead after it has been delivered. If you are the firm we match, you are the only firm that receives that person's details from us. The client can still choose to speak to other firms on their own, as anyone can.
How do you check the leads?
Every client confirms they want paid legal help with a property in England and Wales. We verify their UK mobile number and their email address, and we filter out enquiries that fall outside our scope or your criteria. People we cannot help are told politely and pointed elsewhere, so they never become a lead. Each lead then arrives with the client's answers and the consent record.
What details will I get for each enquiry?
You get the client's name, mobile and email, whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, the tenure, whether there is a mortgage, the target timescale and the location. There is also a short description in the client's own words, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the exact consent wording they agreed to.
Can I quote from the lead alone?
Usually you can give a provisional figure on the first call, because the lead covers the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether a mortgage is involved. You will still want to confirm the details with the client and send a written quote. Things like a leasehold management pack, a new build or a gifted deposit may change your figure once you know more.
Do you send leasehold purchases and sales?
Yes, if you want them. Every lead says whether the property is freehold or leasehold, and you can choose to receive leasehold matters, leave them out or ask for them only in certain areas. Are remortgage leads included?
Only if you ask for them. Remortgages, transfers of equity, purchases, sales and combined sale and purchase matters can each be switched on or off. Some firms take remortgages to keep a steady flow of shorter matters, while others prefer to concentrate on purchases. You can change the mix at any time by letting us know.
Do clients know a firm will call them?
Yes. Every client has agreed to be contacted by a firm about their matter, by phone, email or text, and most are expecting a call soon after they submit. It helps to say early in the call that you are getting in touch because of the enquiry they made through Leaflegal. That puts the client at ease and moves the conversation quickly on to their purchase, sale or remortgage.
