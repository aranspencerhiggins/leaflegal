Speed to lead is the time between a new enquiry reaching your firm and someone from your firm actually speaking to the person who made it. Not the time it takes to send an automatic email, and not the time it takes to assign the lead in your case management system. The clock stops when a real conversation starts.

For most legal enquiries that gap matters more than almost anything else in your intake process. A person who has just filled in a form about their divorce, their visa or their court date is thinking about the problem right now. They have their phone in their hand. They are often worried, and many of them are looking at more than one option at the same time. The firm that rings while that moment lasts gets to have the first useful conversation, and the first useful conversation is very often the one that leads to an instruction.

This guide is for solicitors, conveyancers, immigration advisers and the intake teams that support them. It looks at why response time matters, how to set up your people and systems so leads are called quickly, what to do when leads arrive out of hours or nobody answers, and how to measure whether it is working. None of it needs expensive software. Most of it is about deciding who does what and sticking to it.

What this guide is not

It is not a promise that calling quickly will win every matter. Some people are only comparing prices, some change their mind, and some cannot afford the fees once they hear them. Speed does not fix a poor first call or an unclear fee. What it does is make sure you get a fair chance to show what your firm can do before someone else does.

Where this fits with Leaflegal

Leaflegal sends each enquiry to one firm only, by email or straight into your CRM, as soon as we have checked it. Every lead includes the matter type, any deadline, the location, a short description in the client's own words, verified contact details, the best time to call and the consent record. That gives you everything you need to ring straight away. What happens after the lead lands is in your hands, and that is what this guide is about. You can read more about how delivery works.

There is no magic number of minutes that applies to every firm. But the reasons speed matters are easy to see once you think about the enquiry from the client's side.

The enquiry is at its warmest straight away

When someone submits a legal enquiry, they have just spent a few minutes describing their situation. They have answered questions about urgency, location and what they want help with. They have ticked a box agreeing that a firm can contact them. At that moment they remember doing it, they know why their phone is ringing and they want to talk. An hour later they may be in a meeting, collecting children or driving. A day later the urgency may have faded, or they may have found someone else.

People often look at more than one option

Leaflegal leads are exclusive, so the same enquiry is not sent to other firms by us. But clients do not stop searching just because they have filled in one form. Some will also ring a firm a friend recommended, call a local solicitor they pass every day or send another online enquiry. Whoever has a helpful conversation first starts to build trust, and it is hard for a second firm to undo that.

Anxiety makes speed feel like care

Family breakdown, visa refusals, driving bans and licence reviews are stressful. A quick call tells the client that your firm takes their problem seriously. A slow response, even a polite one, can feel like the firm is too busy for them. For many private clients, how quickly you respond is the first evidence they have of how you will handle their matter.

Deadlines are real

Many legal matters come with a date attached. A court hearing for a motoring offence, a refusal with a short window to act, a premises licence review, a completion date or a lease that needs extending before a sale. When the client has a fixed date, a fast first call is not just good service. It gives you more time to do the work properly.

Your cost per instruction depends on it

Whether you pay for leads, run your own ads or rely on SEO, every enquiry has a cost. Leads that are called late convert less often, which means each instruction costs more. Improving response time is one of the cheapest ways to get more value from the enquiries you already pay for.

Fast response is not about one keen fee earner checking their inbox. It is about a simple system that works on busy days, holidays and sick days too. These are the parts most firms need.

Send leads to a team, not a person

If leads go to one person's inbox, they will sit there whenever that person is in court, on leave or with a client. Use a shared intake inbox or route leads into your CRM where they can be seen by everyone who might pick them up. Leaflegal can send each lead to one or more email addresses or by webhook into your system.

Make new leads impossible to miss

Turn on mobile notifications for the shared inbox or CRM. Some teams use a separate ringtone or a pinned channel so a new lead stands out from routine email. The goal is that the right person knows about a lead within moments, wherever they are in the building.

Decide who calls

  • Name a first responder for each part of the day
  • Name a backup for when the first responder is on another call
  • Decide whether intake staff or fee earners make the first call
  • Agree who covers lunch, early mornings and late afternoons
  • Write it down and share it, so nobody assumes someone else has it

Call first, then email

An email can feel like a response, but it rarely starts a real conversation. Pick up the phone first. If there is no answer, follow with a short voicemail, an email and a text so the client has your name and number in front of them.

Have the basics ready

Slow calls often start slowly because the caller is hunting for information. Keep a short note for each practice area with your fee structure, typical next steps, what documents the client will need and how to book a consultation. Then whoever picks up the lead can have a useful conversation straight away. Our first call guide gives a simple structure.

Match volume to capacity

If you take on more leads than your team can call quickly, response times slip across the board. With Leaflegal you set a monthly cap, and the minimum is 10 leads a month. You can pause if your team is at capacity or someone is away, and restart when you are ready.

Many people deal with personal legal problems in the evening and at weekends, once work and family are done for the day. That means a share of enquiries will arrive when your office is closed. How you handle them depends on your practice areas and your team, but it helps to decide in advance rather than leaving it to chance.

Options firms use

  • A short evening window, such as until early evening, covered by one person on rotation
  • A first thing call list, so overnight leads are rung before anything else the next morning
  • Weekend cover for the most time sensitive areas only
  • An outsourced reception or intake service that can make a first contact and book a call back
  • A personal email or text sent from the fee earner in the evening, with a call the next morning

Prioritise by urgency

Not every out of hours lead needs the same speed. A motoring client with a hearing next week, an immigration client with a deadline or a licensing client facing a review usually needs a faster response than someone thinking about a remortgage in a few months. Each Leaflegal lead includes urgency and any known date, so your team can sort the morning list in the right order.

Respect the best time to call

Every lead tells you the client's preferred time to call. If someone says evenings only, a call at ten in the morning may go unanswered or come at an awkward moment. Use it to plan, and note it in your system so whoever follows up sees it too.

Be careful with late calls

There is a difference between being responsive and being intrusive. A call late at night or very early in the morning can feel inappropriate, particularly for family and immigration matters where the client may not be able to talk freely. A short text or email saying when you will call is often better than ringing at an odd hour.

Plenty of people do not answer calls from numbers they do not recognise, even when they are expecting one. A missed first call is normal. What matters is what you do next.

Leave a clear voicemail

Keep it short. Say your name, your firm, that you are calling about the enquiry they made through Leaflegal about their matter, and a number to call back. Speak slowly when you give the number. Avoid detail about the matter itself in case someone else hears the message.

Follow straight away by text and email

Send a short text and an email within a few minutes of the call. Include your name, direct line and a simple next step, such as a link to book a call or a reply with a good time. Many people who ignore an unknown number will reply to a text once they know who it is from.

Try again at a different time

If the first call was in the morning, try the afternoon. If it was during working hours, try just after. Check the best time to call on the lead. Varying the time of day gives you a better chance of catching someone who is free.

Keep a record

Log every attempt in your CRM or a simple spreadsheet, with the time and method. It stops two people calling the same client within minutes of each other, and it lets you see later how many attempts it usually takes to make contact.

Know when to stop

A planned sequence of a few attempts over about a week is reasonable for most firms. After that, a final polite message is usually enough. Our guide to following up sets out a simple sequence. If contact details turn out to be genuinely wrong, tell us within the review period in your partner agreement.

You cannot improve what you do not measure. The good news is that the measures that matter most are simple to track, even in a spreadsheet.

What to track

  • When each lead arrived
  • When the first call attempt was made
  • When you first spoke to the client
  • How many attempts it took
  • Who made the call
  • Whether a consultation was booked
  • Whether the client instructed you
  • If not, the reason, where you know it

Look at the gaps

Once you have a few weeks of data, look at the time between arrival and first attempt, and between arrival and first conversation. Then look at when the gaps are longest. Many firms find that response times are fine on weekday mornings but slip at lunchtime, late afternoon or on Fridays. That tells you exactly where to add cover.

Compare against outcomes

Compare how quickly leads were reached with whether they instructed. Your own numbers will tell you far more than any general figure, because they reflect your practice areas, your fees and your clients. Do not rely on statistics from other firms or other industries.

Share feedback with us

Tell us which leads you reached, which instructed and which did not, and why where you know. It helps us refine your practice areas, matter types and locations so we send more of the work you want.

Make it a habit

A short weekly look at response times, shared with the team, keeps speed on everyone's mind. It does not need to be formal. A quick review of the slowest leads that week and what got in the way is usually enough to keep improving.

Questions and answers

How quickly should we call?
Within minutes if you can, and within the hour at the latest.
What if leads arrive out of hours?
Call first thing the next working day, and consider evening cover for urgent areas like motoring and immigration.
What does speed to lead mean for a law firm?
It means the time between a new enquiry reaching your firm and the first real conversation with the person who made it. An automatic acknowledgement email does not count, and neither does assigning the lead to a fee earner. The measure that matters is how long the client waits before they speak to someone who can help them understand their options and the next steps.
How quickly should we call a new legal enquiry?
As quickly as you reasonably can. Within minutes is ideal during working hours, and within the hour is a sensible maximum for most practice areas. Urgent matters with a hearing, refusal or completion date deserve the fastest response. The right target for your firm depends on your team and your areas, but the aim should always be to speak to the client while the enquiry is fresh in their mind.
Does speed really make a difference to conversion?
In our experience and that of the firms we work with, it is one of the habits that most affects whether an enquiry becomes an instruction. The client is at their most engaged just after they enquire. We do not publish general statistics because results vary so much by firm and area. The best way to see the effect is to track your own response times against your own instructions.
Is speed more important for some practice areas?
Yes. Matters with a fixed date, such as motoring offences, licence reviews, immigration deadlines and conveyancing transactions, tend to reward the fastest response. Family clients often want to speak to someone the same day because they are anxious. Debt recovery clients are business owners who want action. Fast response helps in every area, but it matters most where the client has a clock running.
What if we are a small firm without an intake team?
Small firms can still respond quickly. Send leads to a shared inbox or phone that more than one person can see, turn on notifications and agree who calls at each part of the day. Even two people covering for each other is far better than relying on one. You can also set a lower monthly cap so you only take the volume you can call back quickly.
Should a fee earner or an intake assistant make the first call?
Either can work. A trained intake assistant can often respond faster and book a consultation with the right fee earner. A fee earner can answer more questions on the spot. Many firms use intake staff for the first call and fee earners for the consultation. What matters most is that whoever calls is quick, knowledgeable about fees and next steps, and able to book a clear next action.
Is an automatic reply email enough to start with?
An automatic reply can be a useful reassurance that the enquiry has arrived, especially out of hours. It is not a substitute for a call. Clients rarely instruct a firm because of an automated message, and a generic reply can feel cold to someone who has just described a personal problem. If you use one, keep it short, name the person who will call and say roughly when.
Does calling quickly make us look pushy?
Not if the call is helpful. The client has asked to be contacted about their matter and agreed to a firm calling them, so a prompt call is what most people expect. Pushiness comes from tone, not timing. Introduce yourself, explain how you were introduced, ask whether it is a good time to talk and offer to call back later if it is not.
Where should we send leads?
To a place more than one person can see. A shared intake inbox, a CRM or a case management system all work well. Avoid sending leads only to one fee earner's personal inbox, because leads will wait whenever that person is busy or away. Leaflegal can send each lead to one or more email addresses, a shared inbox or your CRM by webhook, so you can choose what suits your team.
Do we need special software to respond quickly?
No. Many firms respond quickly using nothing more than a shared inbox with mobile notifications and a clear rota. A CRM helps with tracking and follow-up, and some firms use tools that alert the intake team the moment a lead arrives. Start with a simple set-up that works, then add software if it solves a specific problem you have noticed.
How do we stop leads being missed at lunchtime?
Stagger lunch breaks so someone is always watching the intake inbox, or name a lunchtime responder on a rota. Lunchtime is often when clients have a moment to take a call, so it can be a good time to reach people. Check your own data to see whether leads arriving around midday are being called later than others.
What should we have ready before we call?
A short note for each practice area covering your fee structure, what usually happens next, what documents the client will need and how to book a consultation. Read the lead before dialling so you know the matter type, any deadline, the location and the client's description. Having this ready means the first call can be useful rather than a rushed introduction followed by a promise to call back.
How many leads should we take each month?
Only as many as your team can call back quickly and handle properly. Taking more than you can manage slows your response to every lead. With Leaflegal the minimum is 10 leads a month, and above that you tell us your cap. You can change it or pause when you need to, for example during holidays or when a key fee earner is away.
Can leads go to more than one person at the same time?
Yes. We can send each lead to several email addresses, a shared inbox or your CRM, where you can assign it. If several people receive the same lead, agree who owns it so that the client does not get three calls in five minutes from the same firm. A simple rule, such as the first person to log the lead takes it, usually works.
How do we keep response times up during holidays?
Plan cover before the holiday season starts. Check who is away and when, make sure the rota still has a first responder and a backup each day, and lower your monthly cap or pause leads for a short time if cover is thin. Busy periods such as school holidays can also bring more family enquiries, so think about which areas need the most attention.
What if the client does not answer the first call?
That is common, especially when the number is unfamiliar. Leave a short voicemail with your name, firm and number, then send a text and an email straight away so they know who called. Try again at a different time of day. Most firms find a planned sequence of a few attempts over about a week reaches far more clients than one call and an email.
What should we say in a voicemail?
Keep it brief and discreet. Give your name and firm, say you are calling about the enquiry they made through Leaflegal, and leave a direct number, said slowly. Avoid details of the matter in case someone else listens to the message. You might mention that you will also send a text and email so they have your details in writing.
Is it ever too early or too late to call?
Yes. Very early morning or late evening calls can feel intrusive, particularly in family or immigration matters where the client may not be able to talk privately. Use the best time to call on each lead as your guide. Out of hours, a short text or email saying when you will call is often more welcome than a phone call at an awkward moment.
How do we handle leads that arrive at the weekend?
Decide in advance. Some firms have one person check leads on weekend mornings for urgent areas only. Others send a short personal email at the weekend and call first thing on Monday. Each lead shows urgency and any known date, so you can prioritise those with a hearing, refusal or completion coming up and leave less urgent ones until the next working day.
Should we text before calling?
Usually it is better to call first, because a conversation moves things on faster than a message. But some firms find a short text saying who they are and that they will call in a few minutes helps people pick up an unknown number. Test both approaches with your own clients and keep whichever gets more people talking to you.
How many times should we try to reach someone?
A few attempts over about a week is reasonable for most matters. Use a mix of calls, texts and emails at different times of day. After that, a final polite message letting them know how to get in touch is usually enough. Keep a record of every attempt so the team does not over contact anyone and you can see what works.
What if the client says they are busy when we call?
Thank them, ask when would be a better time and book it there and then, ideally with a calendar invite or a confirming text. Keep the first call short if they only have a minute, and confirm what you will cover next time. A booked call back is a good outcome. It means the client has agreed to speak to you, which keeps your firm at the front of their mind.
Should we mention Leaflegal when we call?
Yes. The client made their enquiry through Leaflegal and agreed to be contacted by a firm we match them with, so mentioning it helps them place your call straight away. It also fits with your duty to tell clients about the referral arrangement. The SRA rules require that to be confirmed in writing, so many firms include it in their client care letter as well.
How quickly do Leaflegal leads reach us?
As soon as we have checked the enquiry. We confirm that each one meets our minimum criteria and yours, including verified UK mobile and email and confirmation that the client wants paid help, then send it by email or into your CRM. There is no platform to log in to and no waiting for a daily batch, so you can call while the client is still thinking about their matter.
What information do we get to help us call quickly?
Each lead includes the client's name, verified UK mobile and email, the practice area and matter type, a short description in their own words, urgency and any known deadline, their location and the best time to call. You also get the source, a timestamp and the consent record. That gives you enough to open the call with something useful straight away.
Is the same lead sent to other firms?
No. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only and is never resold. That means you are not racing other firms we have sent the same lead to. But clients can still contact other firms on their own, which is why a fast and helpful first call still matters even with exclusive leads.
Can we pause leads if we cannot respond quickly for a while?
Yes. If your team is at capacity, a fee earner is on leave or you have an office move, tell us and we will pause matching until you are ready. Pausing is better than receiving leads you cannot call back promptly. Your partner agreement sets out how pausing and restarting work alongside your monthly volume.
What if contact details turn out to be wrong?
Contact details are verified before a lead is sent, but if you find a lead is genuinely invalid, let us know within the review period set out in your partner agreement. We will deal with it as the agreement describes. A client simply not answering, or choosing another firm, does not make a lead invalid, which is another reason a good follow-up sequence matters.
Will you help us improve our response times?
We are happy to talk through how leads are being handled and share what tends to work for other firms in general terms. The most useful thing you can do is give us feedback on which leads you reached and which instructed. That helps us adjust your criteria and volume so you receive the leads your team is best placed to call quickly and convert.

What clients ask

What is a conveyancing lead from Leaflegal?
It is an enquiry from a person who is buying, selling or remortgaging a property in England and Wales and has asked to be contacted by a firm about paid conveyancing. They have answered a short set of questions about the transaction, confirmed their contact details and agreed to be contacted by one firm. We send that enquiry to you alone, as soon as it has been checked.
Are the leads exclusive?
Yes. Each enquiry is matched to one firm only. We do not send the same enquiry to several firms at once and we never resell a lead after it has been delivered. If you are the firm we match, you are the only firm that receives that person's details from us. The client can still choose to speak to other firms on their own, as anyone can.
How do you check the leads?
Every client confirms they want paid legal help with a property in England and Wales. We verify their UK mobile number and their email address, and we filter out enquiries that fall outside our scope or your criteria. People we cannot help are told politely and pointed elsewhere, so they never become a lead. Each lead then arrives with the client's answers and the consent record.
What details will I get for each enquiry?
You get the client's name, mobile and email, whether it is a purchase, sale, both or a remortgage, the rough property value, the tenure, whether there is a mortgage, the target timescale and the location. There is also a short description in the client's own words, the best time to call, the source, a timestamp and the exact consent wording they agreed to.
Can I quote from the lead alone?
Usually you can give a provisional figure on the first call, because the lead covers the transaction type, rough value, tenure and whether a mortgage is involved. You will still want to confirm the details with the client and send a written quote. Things like a leasehold management pack, a new build or a gifted deposit may change your figure once you know more.
Do you send leasehold purchases and sales?
Yes, if you want them. Every lead says whether the property is freehold or leasehold, and you can choose to receive leasehold matters, leave them out or ask for them only in certain areas. Firms with an experienced leasehold team sometimes prefer this work, while others choose to focus on freehold transactions. We set your matching to suit you.
Are remortgage leads included?
Only if you ask for them. Remortgages, transfers of equity, purchases, sales and combined sale and purchase matters can each be switched on or off. Some firms take remortgages to keep a steady flow of shorter matters, while others prefer to concentrate on purchases. You can change the mix at any time by letting us know.
Do clients know a firm will call them?
Yes. Every client has agreed to be contacted by a firm about their matter, by phone, email or text, and most are expecting a call soon after they submit. It helps to say early in the call that you are getting in touch because of the enquiry they made through Leaflegal. That puts the client at ease and moves the conversation quickly on to their purchase, sale or remortgage.
