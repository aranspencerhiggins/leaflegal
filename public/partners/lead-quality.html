What every lead includes

Each lead is the complete record of what the client told us, in the order they told us. There is no scraped data and no guesswork, just the answers someone gave when they asked to speak to a solicitor.

  1. Practice area and matter

    The area of law, the type of matter and a short description in the client’s own words.

  2. Urgency

    How soon they need help, including any deadline, hearing or court date they know about.

  3. Location

    Where they are, so we only send you clients in the areas you cover, or nationally if you work remotely.

  4. Paid help confirmed

    Confirmation that they are looking for paid legal help, not legal aid or free advice.

  5. Verified contact details

    Name, a UK mobile number and an email address on a real domain, plus the best time to call.

  6. Consent record

    The exact wording they agreed to and when, so you can contact them with confidence.

Lead quality is the question every firm asks before buying leads, and the one most lead sellers answer least clearly. At Leaflegal we try to be precise. A quality lead is a real person or business, with a real matter in a practice area, matter type and location your firm has chosen, who wants paid help, can be reached on the details they gave and has agreed to hear from a firm. That is what we provide.

A quality lead is not the same thing as a guaranteed instruction. Even the best enquiry can end with the client choosing another firm, deciding to wait, finding the fees too high or resolving the matter another way. We provide qualified enquiries, not guaranteed instructions, and we think it is important to say so plainly.

What shapes quality

Quality comes from several places at once. It starts with campaigns aimed at people who need a solicitor now rather than people browsing general information. It continues with an enquiry form that asks the right questions and filters out people we cannot help. It depends on checks of contact details and recorded consent. And it relies on matching each enquiry to a firm whose criteria it genuinely fits.

What your firm adds

Half of lead quality, in practice, is what happens after delivery. A lead that is called within minutes by someone who has read it, refers to the client's matter and explains fees clearly will often turn into an instruction. The same lead called two days later by someone who has not read it often will not. That is not a fault in the lead. It is the reality of how people choose a solicitor.

A shared interest

We want your firm to win work from the leads we send. Firms that win work stay with us and grow their volume. That gives us every reason to improve quality over time, and it is why we ask for feedback about which leads convert and which do not.

Quality differs by practice area

What a good lead looks like varies between areas of law. A good conveyancing lead is someone with an offer accepted who wants a quote today. A good family lead is someone who has decided to separate and wants to understand their options. A good debt recovery lead is a business with a clear, undisputed invoice. A good licensing lead is a business with a hearing date fixed. Telling us what a good lead looks like for your firm, in each area you take, helps us set your matter types to match.

Your partner agreement sets out what counts as a valid lead. The description below is the general position, and your agreement is what governs your firm.

The basic test

A valid lead has accurate contact details and matches the practice areas, matter types and locations agreed with your firm. If you can reach the client and their matter is the kind of work you asked to receive, in a place you asked to cover, the lead is valid.

Still valid if the client chooses someone else

A lead stays valid if the client decides to instruct another firm, chooses not to go ahead or cannot afford your fees. These outcomes are a normal part of handling private client enquiries. The lead did what it was meant to do, which was to give your firm a fair, exclusive chance to win the work.

Still valid if the first call is not answered

Many clients do not answer the first call. They may be at work, driving, looking after children or wary of unknown numbers. An unanswered call is not a sign of a bad lead. Leave a voicemail, send an email and a text and try again.

Still valid if the matter is described loosely

Clients are not lawyers. Someone might call a lease extension a remortgage problem or say divorce when their main worry is the children. If the client's real matter is still within the practice areas and matter types you chose, the lead is valid, even if their description is imperfect.

Still valid if the client is not ready yet

Some clients enquire before they are ready to instruct, perhaps before an offer is accepted or before they have had a difficult conversation at home. The lead is still genuine. Keeping in touch politely means your firm is the one they remember when they are ready.

Why the rules matter

Clear rules about validity mean your team knows what to report and what to work. They also mean disagreements are rare, because both sides agreed the test before the first lead arrived.

Occasionally a lead is not what it should be. Your partner agreement sets out how this is handled, including what counts as invalid and the review period for raising it.

Common examples

  • Wrong contact details, so the client cannot be reached on the number or email given
  • A matter outside the practice areas or matter types agreed with your firm
  • A client outside the locations agreed with your firm

Raising an invalid lead

Tell us within the review period in your partner agreement, with a short note of what was wrong. For example, the number connects to someone who has never heard of the client, or the matter is a personal injury claim that should have been filtered out. Where a lead is genuinely invalid, we deal with it as your agreement sets out.

Practical habits

Ask whoever handles new leads to flag anything that looks wrong on the first call. Collect flags in one place and send them to us promptly. A short, regular note is easier for everyone than a long list at the end of the month, and it means the details are still fresh.

Why we want to know

Invalid leads cost us as well as you. If one campaign, form or matter type keeps producing problems, we want to find out quickly so that we can fix it. Reports from firms are one of the most useful ways we have of improving quality for everyone.

Conflicts

A conflict of interest does not make a lead invalid in itself, because the client and matter are genuine. If you cannot act because of a conflict, let us know and we will treat the lead as your partner agreement sets out.

Every firm that buys leads sees some that do not turn into instructions. Understanding the usual reasons helps you separate normal attrition from a real problem.

The client chose another firm

Exclusive means Leaflegal only introduces the client to you. It does not stop them looking elsewhere. Some clients, especially with higher value matters, speak to more than one firm before deciding.

The call came too late

The most common avoidable reason. A client who enquired on Monday morning and heard nothing until Tuesday afternoon may have found help elsewhere or lost the urge to act.

Fees were a surprise

Every client has confirmed they want paid help, but they may have had no idea what legal work costs. Clients who hear a clear explanation of fees early, and why they are what they are, are more likely to go ahead than those who are left guessing.

The timing was not right

Some clients enquire early, before an offer is accepted, before they have told their partner they want to separate or before a hearing date is set. They may instruct later. Polite follow up keeps you in mind.

The matter resolved itself

A debtor pays up before any letter is sent. A licence review is withdrawn. A sale falls through, so the lease extension is no longer urgent. These things happen, and they are part of working with real people in real situations.

When it is a real problem

If many leads in a row fail for the same reason, such as clients consistently wanting work you do not offer, tell us. It may be that your criteria need adjusting or that something in our campaigns or form needs changing.

The firms that convert the most Leaflegal leads tend to do the same few things well. None of them are complicated.

Call within minutes

Speed is the single biggest factor most firms can control. Send leads to a shared inbox or CRM, switch on notifications and agree who calls at each point in the day.

Use what the client told us

Open with the matter type, any deadline and something from the client's own description. It shows you have listened and gets straight to the useful part of the conversation.

Explain the introduction

Tell the client they made an enquiry through Leaflegal and asked to speak to a solicitor. Mention the referral arrangement in the way your firm does, as the SRA Code of Conduct requires.

Be clear about fees

Fixed fees, written estimates and an honest view of the next steps build trust quickly. If you cannot give a figure on the first call, say when the client will receive one.

Agree a next step

End every call with something definite, such as a booked appointment, a list of documents or a quote to review. Confirm it by email the same day.

Follow up properly

A voicemail, an email and a text, then another call the next day and a helpful email a few days later, turns many silences and not yets into instructions. Be helpful rather than pushy. People going through a separation or a court case have a lot on their mind.

Review your calls

Every so often, listen in on or talk through a few first calls with the people who make them. Are they mentioning the matter straight away? Are they explaining fees clearly? Are they agreeing a next step? Small changes to how the first call is handled often make a bigger difference to results than any change to the leads themselves.

Feedback is how lead quality improves over time. The more you tell us about what happens to your leads, the better we can match enquiries to your firm.

What to tell us

  • Which leads turned into instructions
  • Which did not, and why if you know
  • Matter types that consistently suit you or do not
  • Locations that produce good or poor results
  • Any pattern in what clients ask for that you do not offer

What we do with it

We use your feedback to refine your matter types and locations, and to adjust campaigns so they reach the kinds of clients that firms like yours want. We do not share one firm's results with another.

Confidentiality

Share only what you are comfortable sharing under your duty of confidentiality. A simple outcome for each lead, such as instructed, not instructed or could not reach, is usually enough. We do not need details of advice or fees.

How often

Many firms find a short monthly update works well. Others prefer to mention patterns as they notice them. There is no portal or report to fill in, so an email or a call is the way to share it.

Feedback works both ways

If we notice something that might help your firm, such as a matter type that seems to produce enquiries you rarely win, or a location where you could reasonably widen your coverage, we will mention it. The decision about what to change is always yours.

Are your leads better than shared leads?

Clients are not lawyers, and descriptions are sometimes loose. If the real matter is still within the practice areas and matter types you chose, the lead is valid. If it is clearly outside them, raise it within your review period. Their own description usually makes the real issue clear, and your first conversation will confirm it. If you keep seeing the same confusion, tell us so we can look at the wording on our form.
What if I have a conflict?
A conflict does not make a lead invalid in itself, because the client and matter are genuine. Do not act, and let us know. We will treat the lead as your partner agreement sets out. Run your conflict checks before taking any detailed information about the matter. If you cannot act, it is courteous to let the client know quickly so they can look for help elsewhere.
Can I reject a lead because I do not like the matter?
If the matter is within the practice areas, matter types and locations you agreed to receive, the lead is valid even if it is not the kind of case you hoped for. The better answer is to change your criteria, so that you stop receiving that kind of matter in future. Talk to our team about it.
How can I convert more leads?
Call within minutes, use what the client told us, explain how you were introduced, be clear about fees, agree a definite next step and follow up properly. These habits make more difference than almost anything else. Telling us which leads convert also helps us refine your criteria. Small habits add up.
How quickly should I call?
Within minutes if you can during working hours, and first thing the next working day for leads that arrive overnight. The firms that do best call within minutes. Every hour that passes makes it more likely the client has spoken to someone else or lost momentum. Notifications make this easier.
What should I say on the first call?
Introduce yourself and your firm, say they made an enquiry through Leaflegal and asked to speak to a solicitor, and refer to their matter and any deadline. Explain the next step and your fees in plain English, and confirm anything you agree by email. Mention the referral arrangement in the way your firm does.
How many times should we follow up?
Four or five attempts over about a week is reasonable for a client you cannot reach. A call, voicemail, email and text on the first day, another call the next day, a helpful email a few days later and a final check in after a week. Be helpful, not pushy.
Which practice areas convert fastest?
Deadline driven matters, such as licence reviews with a hearing fixed or immigration refusals with a deadline, tend to convert quickly because the client has to act. Conveyancing and family matters can take longer, especially if the client enquired early. Debt recovery enquiries can also move quickly when the debt is clear and undisputed. Whatever the area, give leads time to mature before judging results.
Should we offer a free first consultation?
That is your firm's decision. Some firms offer a short free call and then a fixed fee meeting. Others charge from the start but make the cost very clear. What tends to matter most is that the client knows exactly what the first step involves and what it costs. Be clear and consistent.
Does mentioning Leaflegal on the call help?
Usually, yes. Clients sometimes forget the name of the service they used, especially if they enquired late at night. Saying that they made an enquiry through Leaflegal and asked to speak to a solicitor tells them straight away that the call is expected. It is also a natural moment to explain the referral arrangement.
Why do you want to know which leads convert?
Because it is the best way to improve what you receive. If certain matter types or locations consistently work well for you, we can focus your matching on them. If others do not, we can adjust or remove them. It also helps us improve our campaigns. Feedback is the main way quality improves.
What feedback should I give?
A simple outcome for each lead is usually enough, such as instructed, not instructed or could not reach, and a reason if you know it. Patterns are especially useful, such as clients repeatedly asking for work you do not offer. A short monthly email is enough for most firms. The more consistent your feedback, the easier it is for us to spot what is working and change what is not.
Will you share my results with other firms?
No. We do not share one firm's results, criteria or details with another. We may use what we learn in general terms to improve our campaigns, but never in a way that identifies your firm. Your criteria, volume and price are confidential too. Other partner firms never see them, and we treat their details in exactly the same way.
Is there a report I need to fill in?
No. There is no portal or form to complete. An email or a call is the way to share feedback. Many firms send a short update once a month. Others mention patterns as they notice them. The important thing is that the information reaches us, in whatever form suits your team.
Does sharing outcomes breach client confidentiality?
Share only what you are comfortable sharing under your duty of confidentiality. Whether a lead instructed and a general reason is usually enough. We do not need, and do not ask for, details of your advice, your fees or the client's affairs. If in doubt, check with your compliance officer before sharing anything beyond a simple outcome.
How many leads will I get?
We cannot guarantee exact volumes, because they depend on how many people enquire and how closely they match your criteria. We will give you an honest view of likely volumes when we agree your criteria, and tell you if your criteria are likely to limit them. Wider criteria usually mean steadier volumes.
What if I am not happy with quality?
Tell us. Look first at your records to see where leads are being lost, such as slow first calls, clients not answering or matters outside what you want. Then we can work out together whether the answer lies in your criteria, our campaigns or your intake process. Most issues can be fixed.
How long before I can judge quality?
A handful of leads is too few. Most firms want a couple of months of leads, with time for slower instructions to come through, before drawing conclusions. Look at results by practice area rather than as one overall figure. Some matters take weeks to instruct, so judging too early can make results look worse than they really are. Keep records and review them together with us.
Do clients know they will be contacted by a solicitor?
Yes. Every client ticks a box agreeing that their details can be shared with a firm we match them with and that the firm can contact them about their matter. We record the wording and time and send it with every lead. They are also told that Leaflegal is an introduction service, not a law firm, and that we are paid by the firms we introduce.
Can I change my criteria to improve quality?
Yes. If a matter type or location consistently produces enquiries that do not suit you, we can remove or narrow it. If another works well, we can give it more weight within your cap. Tell our team what you want to change and we will update your matching. Changes apply to new leads.

More questions people ask

What is a legal lead?
It is an enquiry from a person or business who wants to speak to a solicitor about their own matter and has agreed to be contacted by a firm. A good legal lead includes their contact details, the type of matter, how urgent it is, where they are and a record of their consent. It is an opportunity to win an instruction, not an instruction in itself.
Are legal leads the same as referrals?
They are similar in that someone introduces a client to your firm. The difference is that a traditional referral usually comes from a professional contact who knows the client, while a lead comes from a client who responded to marketing and asked to be introduced. In both cases, the SRA Code requires you to tell clients about any financial arrangement with the introducer.
Are legal leads worth buying?
For firms that call quickly and follow up properly, they can be a reliable source of new private clients. For firms that leave leads for a day or two, results tend to be disappointing whoever the supplier is. We will give you an honest view of likely volumes for your criteria before you start, and the minimum of 10 a month is enough to judge quality.
Why does freshness matter so much?
People who need a solicitor usually act on that need straight away. Within hours they may have asked a friend, found another firm or decided to wait. A lead that arrives as soon as it is checked and is called within minutes catches the client while they are focused on their matter and expecting a call.
What is the difference between a lead and a cold contact?
A lead is someone who asked to be contacted about their own matter. A cold contact is someone whose details were collected for another purpose, or bought from a list, and who is not expecting to hear from you. Leaflegal only produces leads. We never buy data or pass on cold contacts.
Do you sell legal leads in Scotland or Northern Ireland?
Not at the moment. Our leads in six practice areas are for matters in England and Wales. Immigration introductions can come from anywhere in the UK, because immigration law applies UK wide. If you are a firm outside England and Wales with an interest in immigration work, mention it when you apply and we will tell you honestly whether the service could work for you.
Will every legal lead become a client?
No. Some people are comparing firms, some decide not to proceed and some cannot afford the fees once they understand them. A lead is an opportunity, not a guaranteed instruction. Firms that call quickly, use the information in the lead and are clear about their fees tend to win the most work from the leads they receive.
How do people find Leaflegal?
Through our campaigns on social media, search and content. Our adverts and pages are aimed at people who have a legal need now, such as a house move, a separation, a licence review or a shortening lease. They complete a short enquiry form on our site and agree to be introduced to a firm.
Do you buy data from other companies?
No. Every Leaflegal lead comes from our own enquiry forms. We do not buy data, rent lists, scrape directories or take enquiries from other lead sellers. That is the only way we can be sure every client knows who we are and what they agreed to. It also keeps every lead exclusive.
How do you check contact details?
The mobile number is checked as a valid UK mobile number and the email address is checked for a real domain at the point of submission. This catches most mistakes and invented details. If a lead still turns out to have wrong details, tell us within the review period in your partner agreement.
Do you speak to clients before sending leads?
No. Leads are sent as soon as they are checked, so you are the first person to speak to the client about their matter. Calling them first would slow things down and mean the client had to explain their situation twice. Our checks run while the client fills in the form, so they do not hold the lead back.
How do you stop people who want free advice?
Every client confirms they are looking for paid legal help before their enquiry is submitted. People who only want legal aid or free advice are told we cannot help and pointed to other sources of support. This does not mean every client will accept your fees, but it does mean they expect to pay.
What information do clients give about their matter?
They choose the practice area and type of matter, say how urgent it is and whether there is a deadline or hearing, and write a short description in their own words. Some areas have extra questions, such as rough property value, years left on a lease or the amount of an unpaid debt.
Can I see the consent wording before I start?
Yes. We show you a sample lead on your first call, including the consent record, so you can see exactly what clients agree to and how it is presented. Your compliance officer is welcome to review it. The same wording and the time it was agreed are sent with every lead you receive.
