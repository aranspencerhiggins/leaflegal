About buy to let purchases

Buying a property to rent out involves the usual purchase work plus a few extra checks. Buy to let mortgages have their own conditions, there may be existing tenants, and buying through a limited company changes the paperwork.

Your conveyancer will also check for anything that affects letting, such as restrictions in a lease, and explain the Stamp Duty Land Tax position, which is often higher for additional properties.

A good conveyancer keeps you updated without you having to chase, explains anything unusual in the paperwork in plain English and works with your estate agent and mortgage broker to keep the chain moving. When we introduce you, the firm will explain how they work and what they need from you to get started.

Buy to let solicitor in brief

A buy to let solicitor handles the purchase of an investment property and the extra checks a buy to let lender asks for. Leaflegal introduces you to one regulated firm used to buy to let conveyancing, whether you buy in your own name or through a limited company.

Buy to let conveyancing often involves higher rate Stamp Duty Land Tax, existing tenancies to check and company paperwork for limited company buyers. Ask for a fixed fee that covers these points.

Buy to let purchases explained

Pick a topic to read more.

Buying a property to rent out uses the same basic conveyancing process as buying a home to live in. The title is checked, searches are ordered, enquiries are raised and the money changes hands at completion. What changes is the purpose. Because the property is an investment and will be lived in by someone else, there are extra legal questions to answer before you commit.

Some of these questions are about the property itself. Can it be let at all under the lease or title? Does it need a licence from the council? Is there already a tenant living there? Others are about you. Are you buying in your own name, jointly with someone, or through a limited company? Is your mortgage a buy to let mortgage, and what conditions does the lender attach?

A conveyancer who deals with landlords regularly will spot these points early. They are not there to tell you whether a property is a good investment, which is a commercial decision for you and perhaps your accountant or broker. Their job is to make sure you get good legal title and know about anything that affects your plans to let.

Timing also matters more for landlords. Every week a rental property sits empty is a week without rent, so many investors want to know how long the legal work will take and what might slow it down. A conveyancer cannot promise a date, but an experienced one will tell you early about anything likely to cause delay, such as missing licensing records or a lease that needs the freeholder's consent.

The broad stages of a buy to let purchase match an ordinary purchase. The extra work tends to happen during the investigation of the title and the enquiries. Your conveyancer will tell you exactly what applies to your purchase.

Instruction and checks

The firm checks your identity and the source of your funds. If you are buying through a company, it will also check the company, its directors and anyone who owns or controls it. Landlords with several properties may be asked for more background on where their money has come from.

Title and lease review

Your conveyancer reads the title for anything that restricts how the property can be used. For a leasehold flat, the lease may say whether letting is allowed, whether consent is needed, whether short term or holiday lets are banned and whether tenants must sign up to any rules. Freehold titles can also contain restrictive covenants that limit use.

Letting enquiries

Extra questions are raised with the seller about anything that affects letting. These can include licensing, gas and electrical safety records, the energy performance certificate and, where there is a tenant, the tenancy agreement and deposit.

Mortgage conditions

Buy to let mortgage offers often contain conditions about the type of tenancy allowed, who the tenants can be and how long tenancies may last. Your conveyancer acts for the lender as well as you and must report anything that conflicts with those conditions.

Exchange and completion

Once the checks are done and you are happy, contracts are exchanged and a completion date is fixed. If a tenant is in place, the seller's side should hand over the tenancy documents and deposit details at completion so you can take over as landlord.

After completion

Your conveyancer pays any Stamp Duty Land Tax and registers you or your company as owner. If the property is leasehold, notice of the purchase and of your mortgage is usually given to the landlord or managing agent, and tenants usually need to be told who their new landlord is.

One of the first decisions many landlords make is whether to buy in their own name or through a limited company. This is mainly a tax and finance decision, and it is one to discuss with an accountant or tax adviser before you make an offer. The legal work differs depending on which route you take.

Buying in your own name

Buying personally is the simplest route legally. You can buy alone or with others. Joint owners can hold as joint tenants or tenants in common, and a declaration of trust can record unequal shares. Rental income is then taxed as part of your personal income.

Buying through a company

Many landlords now use a limited company, often one set up only to hold property. The company is the buyer and owner. The conveyancer will need the company's details, its articles, information about its officers and owners, and evidence that the company has authority to buy and borrow. Most lenders also want personal guarantees from the directors.

Personal guarantees

A personal guarantee means a director can be personally liable for the company's mortgage debt. Lenders often want guarantors to receive independent legal advice before they sign, which may come from a different solicitor. Your conveyancer will explain what the lender requires.

Moving property into a company

Some landlords ask about transferring properties they already own into a company. This is a sale and purchase in legal terms and can have significant tax consequences. It needs specialist tax advice before any legal work starts.

Whatever route you choose, make sure your conveyancer knows from the start. Changing the buyer halfway through can mean starting parts of the work again and may need a new mortgage offer.

Landlords in England and Wales have legal duties that go beyond the purchase. Your conveyancer focuses on the purchase, but many of these duties affect what they check and what they ask the seller for. The rules change often, so it is worth checking GOV.UK and speaking to a letting agent or landlord specialist about your duties once you own the property.

Tenancy law

In England, the Renters' Rights Act 2025 is changing private renting, including ending section 21 no fault possession and moving most tenancies onto a rolling basis. The changes are being brought in over time. In Wales, renting is governed by the Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016, which uses occupation contracts instead of assured shorthold tenancies. Your conveyancer can explain how the rules affect a tenancy you are taking over.

Licensing

Some properties need a licence from the local council before they can be let. Larger houses in multiple occupation need a mandatory licence, and many councils run additional or selective licensing schemes covering whole areas. Your conveyancer can raise enquiries about licensing, but checking the council's current schemes is also wise.

Safety and energy

Landlords must meet duties on gas safety, electrical safety, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and minimum energy efficiency. Records such as gas safety certificates and electrical reports are usually requested from the seller where a tenant is in place.

Deposits and right to rent

Tenancy deposits must be protected in a government approved scheme within set time limits. In England, landlords must also check that tenants have the right to rent. If you take over a tenancy, the deposit protection needs to move into your name.

Planning

Converting a house into flats or a house in multiple occupation can need planning permission, and some areas remove the usual permitted development rights. Your searches and enquiries may reveal this, but a planning check is sensible if you plan any change of use.

Buy to let purchases tend to cost a little more in legal fees than buying a home to live in, because of the extra checks and the lender's requirements. The total cost also includes tax and other charges.

Legal fee

Many firms quote a fixed fee for a buy to let purchase. Expect extra charges for buying through a company, buying with a tenant in place, a leasehold property, or a lender who needs separate representation. Ask what is included before you instruct.

Disbursements

These include search fees, the HM Land Registry fee, identity check fees, bank transfer fees and, for a company buyer, company search fees. For a leasehold flat there may also be fees for the management pack and notices.

Stamp Duty Land Tax

Buying an additional residential property usually means paying higher rates of Stamp Duty Land Tax in England. Companies buying residential property usually pay the higher rates too. The rates and rules change, so check GOV.UK or ask your conveyancer. In Wales, Land Transaction Tax has its own higher rates for additional properties.

Ongoing costs

  • Landlord insurance and buildings insurance
  • Licensing fees where a licence is needed
  • Safety certificates and reports
  • Letting or management agent fees
  • Service charge and ground rent for leasehold property
  • Accountancy fees, especially for company owners

It is worth asking for a written estimate that sets out each of these items separately. If the firm finds something during the work that changes the price, such as a lease that turns out to need a deed of variation, a good firm will tell you before it does the extra work.

Many buy to let issues come from finding out late that the property cannot be used as planned. Raising the right questions early helps avoid wasted time and money.

Lease restrictions

Some leases ban letting, require the landlord's consent or forbid short lets. A lease might also stop a flat being shared by unrelated people. Finding this out after exchange can be costly.

Lender conditions

Buy to let lenders may restrict tenancies to certain types, ban lets to companies or students, or limit the tenancy length. If your plans do not match the offer, your lender needs to know.

Inheriting a problem tenancy

If you buy with a tenant in place, you take on the tenancy as it is. Missing paperwork, an unprotected deposit or rent arrears can become your problem. Your conveyancer will ask for the documents, but some risks are commercial ones for you to weigh up.

Unlicensed property

If a property should have a licence but does not, the landlord can face penalties and may have to repay rent. Check the licensing position before exchange.

Company paperwork

Company purchases are often delayed by missing documents such as board minutes, identity checks on directors or the lender's guarantee requirements. Getting these ready early helps keep things on track.

Service charges and major works

With a leasehold flat, a large bill for roof repairs, cladding or lift replacement can wipe out a year's rent. The management pack and replies from the managing agent should show any planned works and the state of the reserve fund. Your conveyancer will report on what they find, and you can then decide whether the figures still work for you.

Buying from overseas

Landlords who live abroad can buy property in England and Wales, but identity checks, signing documents and sending money from another country all take longer. A separate Stamp Duty Land Tax surcharge can apply to buyers who are not UK resident. Telling the firm at the start where you live helps it plan the work.

Buy to let purchases questions and answers

Is buy to let conveyancing different from a normal purchase?
The core work is the same, but there can be tenancies to review, lender conditions for letting and higher rate Stamp Duty Land Tax. Company purchases need extra documents.
Can I buy a rental property through a company?
Yes. The conveyancer will need company details and the lender will usually want personal guarantees from the directors.
Does buy to let cost more in Stamp Duty?
Additional properties usually attract a higher rate. Your conveyancer will confirm what applies to you.
Can I buy a flat with a tenant already in it?
Yes. The firm will check the tenancy agreement, the deposit protection and what happens on completion.
Does Leaflegal do the conveyancing?
No. Leaflegal is an introduction service. The firm we introduce you to does the legal work and is responsible for it.
Which areas do you cover?
Conveyancing enquiries for properties in England and Wales. Scotland and Northern Ireland have separate systems and are not covered at the moment.
How much does conveyancing cost?
It depends on the property, its value, whether it is leasehold and the firm. The firm will give you a written quote before you instruct them.
Is buying a rental property different from buying a home?
The core legal work is the same. Your conveyancer checks the title, orders searches, raises enquiries and handles the money. The difference is in the extra checks around letting, such as lease restrictions, licensing, existing tenancies and the conditions in a buy to let mortgage. The firm will also need to know from the start whether you are buying personally or through a company.
Do I need a buy to let mortgage?
If you plan to let the property and are borrowing, you will usually need a mortgage designed for letting. A standard residential mortgage normally forbids letting without the lender's consent. Letting in breach of your mortgage terms can have serious consequences. Your mortgage broker can explain the options, and your conveyancer will check the offer conditions.
Can I let my old home instead of selling it?
Many people do, sometimes called being an accidental landlord. You will usually need your lender's consent to let, or a new buy to let mortgage. Buying your next home while keeping the old one can mean paying higher rates of Stamp Duty Land Tax. Your conveyancer can explain how the tax applies, and a tax adviser can help with the wider picture.
Can I buy a property at auction to let?
Yes, but auction purchases move quickly. When the hammer falls, contracts are usually exchanged and completion often follows within a short fixed period. That means the legal checks need to happen before the auction. A conveyancer can review the legal pack for you in advance, and you will need your finance ready in time.
Can I buy a house in multiple occupation?
Yes, but these properties carry extra rules. A large house in multiple occupation needs a mandatory licence, and smaller ones may need one under local schemes. Planning permission can also be needed. Many lenders have specific products for these properties. Your conveyancer will raise enquiries about licensing and planning, and it is wise to check with the council too.
Does the conveyancer check whether the property is a good investment?
No. Your conveyancer checks the legal title and raises questions about anything that might affect your ownership or your plans. Whether the rent, price and location make sense as an investment is a commercial decision for you. Many landlords speak to letting agents, mortgage brokers and accountants alongside their conveyancer.
How long does a buy to let purchase take?
There is no fixed timescale. A simple purchase of a vacant freehold house with no chain can move quickly, while a leasehold flat with a tenant in place, a company buyer and a lender with strict conditions will usually take longer. Searches, the speed of the seller's side and the managing agent's response all play a part. Your conveyancer can give a better idea once they have seen the paperwork.
Can I buy through a limited company?
Yes. Many landlords now buy through a company set up to hold property. The company is the legal owner. Your conveyancer will need details of the company, its directors and its owners, and evidence that it can buy and borrow. Whether this route suits you is mainly a tax question for an accountant.
Will the lender want a personal guarantee?
Most lenders who lend to property companies want the directors to give personal guarantees. This means they can be personally liable for the company's mortgage debt. Lenders often require guarantors to have independent legal advice before signing, and this may have to come from a separate solicitor. Your conveyancer will explain the lender's requirements.
Does a company pay Stamp Duty?
Yes. A company buying residential property usually pays the higher rates of Stamp Duty Land Tax that apply to additional properties, and different rules can apply to higher value homes. Rates and reliefs change, so check GOV.UK or ask your conveyancer what applies to your purchase.
What documents will the firm need for a company purchase?
Typically the company's registration details, its articles of association, details of directors and people with significant control, identity documents for those people and evidence of where the purchase money is coming from. Some firms will also ask for board minutes approving the purchase and the mortgage. Having these ready at the start can save weeks.
Can I move my existing rental properties into a company?
It is possible, but the company is treated as buying the property, so it can trigger Stamp Duty Land Tax and capital gains tax, and you will usually need new mortgages. This is an area where specialist tax advice is essential before you start. Your conveyancer can then handle the legal transfers.
Can I buy through a company with a business partner?
Yes. Many property companies have two or more directors and shareholders. It is sensible for the owners to agree in writing how decisions are made, how profits are shared and what happens if one wants to leave, often in a shareholders' agreement. That is separate from the conveyancing, and a company or commercial solicitor usually prepares it. Your conveyancer will need identity checks on everyone involved.
Can I buy a property with a tenant already living there?
Yes. This is often called buying with a sitting tenant or tenanted. You take over the tenancy as it stands. Your conveyancer will ask for the tenancy agreement, deposit protection details, safety records and any notices that have been served. At completion the deposit and paperwork should be handed over so you can step into the seller's shoes.
What happens to the tenant's deposit when I buy?
The deposit usually needs to be transferred to you or your agent on completion and then protected in your name with an approved scheme. The tenant should be told who now holds it. Your conveyancer can deal with the handover on completion, but keeping the deposit protected after that is your duty as landlord.
Can the lease stop me letting a flat?
Yes. Some leases forbid letting, some need the freeholder's consent and some ban short term or holiday lets. A lease might also limit who can live there. Your conveyancer will read the lease and tell you what it says. If the lease does not allow what you plan, it is far better to know before exchange.
How are tenancy rules changing?
In England, the Renters' Rights Act 2025 is reforming private renting, including ending section 21 no fault possession and moving most tenancies onto a rolling basis. Changes are being introduced over time. Wales has its own system under the Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016. Your conveyancer can explain how the rules affect a tenancy you are taking over, and GOV.UK has current guidance for landlords.
Can I use the property as a holiday let?
It depends on the title, any lease, planning rules in the area and your mortgage. Some leases ban short lets and some councils need planning permission for them. Holiday lets usually need a different kind of mortgage and insurance. Your conveyancer can check the title and lease, but you may also need to speak to the council and your lender.
Do I need a licence to let the property?
Some properties do. Larger houses in multiple occupation always need a licence, and many councils run schemes that require licences for other rented homes in certain areas. Your conveyancer can ask the seller about licensing, and you can check the local council's website for current schemes.
What if the tenant is behind with the rent?
Rent arrears on a tenanted purchase are something to raise before exchange. The contract can say how arrears are dealt with, for example whether the seller keeps the right to recover them or the price is adjusted. Taking on a tenant who is already behind is a commercial risk you will want to weigh up. Your conveyancer will ask for a rent statement and explain what the contract says.
What about flats with cladding or fire safety problems?
Some blocks have fire safety defects that affect lending, insurance and service charges. The Building Safety Act 2022 limits what some leaseholders can be charged for certain repairs, but the protection depends partly on how many properties the owner holds, so landlords with several properties may be treated differently. Your conveyancer will ask the managing agent about the building and report on what they find, and your lender may have its own requirements.
Will I pay a higher rate of Stamp Duty?
Usually yes, if you already own a home or are buying through a company. Higher rates apply to most additional residential properties in England. The rates and the rules about when they apply change from time to time, so check GOV.UK or ask your conveyancer. In Wales, Land Transaction Tax has its own higher rates.
Can I reclaim the higher rate later?
In some cases, if you buy a new main home before selling your old one and then sell the old one within a set period, you may be able to claim back the extra tax. This does not usually apply to buy to let purchases where the new property will not be your home. Your conveyancer or a tax adviser can explain whether a refund could apply.
Why does the firm need so much detail about my funds?
Conveyancers must carry out anti money laundering checks on every purchase. Landlords often use money from several sources, such as savings, remortgages, sale proceeds or company funds. Each source needs to be explained and evidenced. Providing a clear trail early is the best way to avoid delays.
Who should I speak to about tax?
Your conveyancer will deal with the Stamp Duty Land Tax return for the purchase. Wider questions, such as income tax on rent, mortgage interest relief, capital gains tax and whether to use a company, are for an accountant or tax adviser. Many landlords speak to one before they make an offer.
Do I need insurance in place before completion?
If the property is freehold, you usually become responsible for insuring it from exchange of contracts, and your lender will expect cover to be in place. Landlords need a policy that covers letting, as ordinary home insurance often does not. For a leasehold flat, the building is usually insured by the freeholder, but you may still want cover for contents and liability. Your conveyancer will confirm what the lender requires.
Do I need a conveyancer who specialises in buy to let?
Any regulated conveyancer can handle a buy to let purchase, but one who deals with landlords regularly will know what to look for. Ask whether the firm acts for company buyers, buys with tenants in place and is on your lender's panel. Some buy to let lenders have smaller panels than residential lenders.
Can the same firm act on several purchases?
Yes. Many landlords use the same conveyancer for each purchase as they build a portfolio. The firm will already have your identity and company documents, which can speed things up. Each purchase is still a separate transaction with its own fee.
Does Leaflegal charge me anything?
No. Leaflegal is free for landlords to use. The firm we introduce you to pays us for the introduction, and that does not change what you pay. You are under no obligation to instruct them.
How quickly will the firm contact me?
The firm usually gets in touch within one working day of your enquiry, often sooner. It will help to have the property address, price, whether you are buying personally or through a company and your mortgage details ready.
Can I buy a rental property while living abroad?
Yes. Many landlords buy from overseas. The firm will need to verify your identity, which can take longer from abroad, and documents may need to be signed in front of a suitable witness where you live. A separate Stamp Duty Land Tax surcharge can apply to non-resident buyers. Tell the firm at the outset so it can plan the work and explain what applies.
What is a reservation fee?
It is a payment to the developer that takes a plot off the market for a set time while you arrange your mortgage and legal work. It is usually deducted from the price on completion. Whether you get it back if you pull out depends on the reservation agreement, so read it carefully. Developers registered under the New Homes Quality Code must follow rules about how reservations work.
When should I instruct a conveyancer?
Ideally on the day you reserve, or before. Developers often give a short time to exchange, and every day counts. If you have a firm ready, the developer's solicitor can send the sales pack straight away and searches can be ordered immediately. Waiting even a week can make a tight deadline much harder to meet.
Can I use the developer's recommended solicitor?
You can, but you do not have to. Some developers offer to pay legal fees or other incentives if you use a firm on their list. A firm you choose yourself acts only for you and has no connection to the developer. Whoever you choose, check that the firm is regulated, experienced with new builds and on your lender's panel.
Can I negotiate the price on a new build?
Developers often prefer to offer incentives rather than reduce the headline price, though both are possible. Any incentive must be disclosed to your lender, and some lenders treat incentives as reducing the price for lending purposes. Your conveyancer will check the incentives are recorded properly in the paperwork.
What is the New Homes Quality Code?
It is a code of practice for developers who have registered with the New Homes Quality Board. It sets standards for how homes are marketed and sold, what information buyers are given, how reservations work and how complaints are handled. Registered developers can be referred to the New Homes Ombudsman. Your conveyancer can check whether your developer is registered.
Can I buy a new build home off plan?
Yes. Buying off plan means exchanging before the home is built, often based on plans, a show home and a specification. It can mean a long wait before completion. Your conveyancer will look closely at the long stop date, the developer's right to make changes and what happens to your deposit if the developer runs into problems.
What is part exchange?
Some developers offer to buy your current home as part of the deal, so you do not need to find a buyer yourself. The developer usually values your home and makes an offer, which may be lower than you would get on the open market in exchange for speed and certainty. Your conveyancer would handle the sale to the developer and the new purchase together, and will explain how the two are linked in the contract.
Why are new build deadlines so short?
Developers want sales secured so they can plan their finances and build programme. A deadline to exchange within a few weeks is common. It is not a legal requirement, it is a commercial term. Your conveyancer will tell you what is needed to meet it and let you know early if it looks unrealistic.
